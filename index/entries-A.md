# Name index — gold-standard entries (letter A)

Five hand-written model entries that fix the format for the Phase 3 writers.
Each follows the schema in `canon.md`, resolves aliases from the canonicalization
table, and cites real `book.line` refs from `occurrences.json`. Read these before
writing any others.

---

**Aegisthus** (Αἴγισθος) · MORTAL
Son of Thyestes and cousin of Agamemnon; while Agamemnon is at Troy he seduces
Clytemnestra, murders the king at the homecoming feast, and rules Mycenae until
Orestes kills him in vengeance. Zeus opens the poem with him (1.35–43) as proof
that mortals earn their own ruin "beyond their portion," and his story shadows
Odysseus's return throughout — the homecoming that went wrong.
*Epithets:* "blameless Aegisthus" (ἀμύμων Αἴγισθος, 1.29 — the notorious clash of
a moral honorific with an adulterer-murderer); "Aegisthus of the crooked
counsels" (Αἴγισθος δολόμητις, 1.300).
*Kin:* son of Thyestes (brother of Atreus); paramour of Clytemnestra.
**Refs:** 1.29, 1.35–43, 1.300, 3.194–198, 3.256–275 (Nestor's fuller telling),
4.524–537, 11.409, 24.96. Books 1, 3–4, 11, 24.
*See also:* Agamemnon, Clytemnestra, Orestes.

---

**Agamemnon** (Ἀγαμέμνων) · MORTAL
Overlord of the Achaean host at Troy, elder son of Atreus and brother of
Menelaus. Murdered by Aegisthus and Clytemnestra at his homecoming; his shade
meets Odysseus in the underworld and warns him against trusting his own wife
(11.441–456) — advice the poem then spends twelve books disproving in Penelope.
He is the poem's standing example of the ruined return.
*Epithets:* "shepherd of the people" (ποιμὴν λαῶν, 3.156).
*Also called:* "son of Atreus" / "Atreides" — but so is Menelaus; the referent is
Agamemnon in the underworld and homecoming-paradigm scenes, Menelaus in the
Telemachy (see `canon.md`).
*Kin:* son of Atreus; husband of Clytemnestra; father of Orestes; brother of
Menelaus.
**Refs:** 1.30 (first, in Zeus's speech), 3.143–150, 3.234–235, 4.512–537
(Proteus tells the death), 11.387–464 (his shade), 24.20–97, 24.186. Books 1,
3–4, 8–9, 11, 13–14, 24.
*See also:* Aegisthus, Menelaus, Orestes, Atreus.

**Alcinous** (Ἀλκίνοος) · MORTAL
King of the Phaeacians on Scheria, and Odysseus's last host before Ithaca. He
receives the shipwrecked stranger, presides over the games and feast at which
Odysseus is moved to tell his wanderings (the Apologoi, Books 9–12), and sends
him home asleep on a Phaeacian ship, laden with gifts — the act for which
Poseidon later turns that ship to stone.
*Epithets:* "great-hearted Alcinous" (μεγαλήτωρ Ἀλκίνοος, 6.17); "the sacred
strength of Alcinous" (ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο, 7.167).
*Kin:* son of Nausithous (himself a son of Poseidon), brother of Rhexenor;
marries his niece Arete (Rhexenor's daughter); father of Nausicaa and Laodamas.
**Refs:** 6.12 (first named), 7.55–66 (the genealogy), 7.167, 8.*, 9.2, 11.362,
13.20–23 (the send-off). Books 6–9, 11, 13.
*See also:* Arete, Nausicaa, Phaeacians, Scheria, Poseidon.

**Antinous** (Ἀντίνοος) · MORTAL
Son of Eupeithes and the most violent of Penelope's suitors; with Eurymachus he
leads them, openly hostile where Eurymachus is smooth. He masterminds the ambush
laid for Telemachus (4.660–674, 16.363–392) and hurls a stool at the disguised
Odysseus (17.462–465). He is the first suitor to die: Odysseus's opening arrow
takes him through the throat as he lifts a golden cup, "and slaughter was not in
his thoughts" (22.8–16).
*Also called:* one of "the lordly suitors" (μνηστῆρες ἀγήνορες) collectively.
*Kin:* son of Eupeithes, who leads the suitors' kin in the failed revenge of
Book 24.
**Refs:** 1.383 (first, silencing Telemachus), 2.301–336, 4.660–674, 16.363–392,
17.462–465, 18.34–42, 20.275, 21.140–166, 22.8–16 (death), 24.424 (Eupeithes
mourns him). Books 1–2, 4, 16–18, 20–22, 24.
*See also:* Eupeithes, Eurymachus, Penelope, the suitors.

**Athena** (Ἀθήνη) · GOD
Daughter of Zeus and Odysseus's divine patron; the intelligence behind the whole
plot. She wins his release from Calypso at the gods' council (1.44–95), rouses
Telemachus to act and sail (Books 1–2), guides him to Pylos and Sparta,
transforms and shields Odysseus on Ithaca (13.221–440), fights at his side in the
hall (22.205–309), and imposes the final peace (24.529–548). More than any other
god she moves in disguise.
*Epithets:* "gray-eyed Athena" (γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη, 1.44); "the goddess, gray-eyed
Athena."
*Also called:* Pallas / Pallas Athena (1.125); "daughter of Zeus." *Disguises:*
Mentes, leader of the Taphians (1.105); Mentor, Odysseus's old friend
(2.268, 22.206, 24.503); and various unnamed mortals (a young girl at 7.20, a
herald, a shepherd at 13.221).
*Kin:* daughter of Zeus.
**Refs:** 1.44–95, 1.105 (as Mentes), 2.267–295 (as Mentor), 3.12–13, 6.13–40,
13.221–440, 16.155–177, 22.205–309, 24.529–548 …and throughout. Books 1–9, 11,
13–24.
*See also:* Zeus, Odysseus, Telemachus, Mentor.

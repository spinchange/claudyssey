Formula assignments used in this book:

- Finite ὣς φάτο family (ὣς ἔφαθʼ / ὣς ἔφατʼ / ὣς φάτο) → **"So he spoke," / "So she spoke,"**
- Participial ὣς εἰπών / ὣς εἰποῦσα / ὣς ἄρα μιν φαμένη → **"So having spoken,"**
- Iterative ὣς ἄρα τις εἴπεσκε → **"So would one speak;"** (13.170, unchanged)

## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**13.1** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, οἱ δʼ ἄρα πάντες ἀκὴν ἐγένοντο σιωπῇ
- OLD: So he had spoken, and all of them then fell hushed into silence,
- NEW: So he spoke, and all of them then fell hushed into silence,
- Pluperfect "had spoken" broke the formula; the aorist is the ruling's form. Dropping
  the auxiliary frees a syllable, so the opening feet rebalance without padding.

**13.16** ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀλκίνοος, τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιὴνδανε μῦθος.
- OLD: So Alcinous spoke, and his saying gave pleasure to all of them.
- NEW: So he spoke, Alcinous, and his word was pleasing to all of them.
- The Greek names the subject, so the name is kept — but as an appositive after the
  fixed opening, not folded into it. μῦθος is "word"; ἐπιήνδανε is "was pleasing."

**13.47** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, οἱ δʼ ἄρα πάντες ἐπῄνεον ἠδʼ ἐκέλευον
- OLD: So he had spoken, and all gave assent and urged one another
- NEW: So he spoke, and all of them gave assent and urged one another
- Same pluperfect fix as 13.1. πάντες recovers its "of them" now that the auxiliary
  no longer occupies the second foot.

**13.63** ὣς εἰπὼν ὑπὲρ οὐδὸν ἐβήσετο δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς,
- OLD: Having spoken thus, noble Odysseus stepped over the threshold.
- NEW: So having spoken, noble Odysseus stepped over the threshold.
- Participial family standardized. Also removes a line-initial inversion that read as
  a "Thus" variant.

**13.217** ὣς εἰπὼν τρίποδας περικαλλέας ἠδὲ λέβητας
- OLD: So he spoke, and he counted the splendid tripods before him,
- NEW: So having spoken, he counted the splendid tripods before him,
- This is ὣς εἰπών, not ὣς φάτο: the old line flattened the participial formula into
  the finite one. Distinct Greek, distinct English.

**13.352** ὣς εἰποῦσα θεὰ σκέδασʼ ἠέρα, εἴσατο δὲ χθών·
- OLD: So the goddess spoke, and scattered the mist, and the country appeared clear.
- NEW: So having spoken, the goddess scattered the mist, and the land lay open.
- Participial standardized; the old line also ran long. εἴσατο δὲ χθών is "and the land
  showed itself" — "lay open" carries it and supplies the trochaic close.

**13.366** ὣς εἰποῦσα θεὰ δῦνε σπέος ἠεροειδές,
- OLD: So the goddess spoke, and entered the cave wrapped over in dimness,
- NEW: So having spoken, the goddess entered the cave wrapped in dimness,
- Participial standardized; "over" was padding the longer opening required.

**13.429** ὣς ἄρα μιν φαμένη ῥάβδῳ ἐπεμάσσατʼ Ἀθήνη.
- OLD: So then having spoken, Athena touched him there with her wand.
- NEW: So having spoken to him, Athena touched him with her wand.
- Participial standardized, and the unwarranted line-final "there" removed. μιν is the
  object of φαμένη ("having so spoken **to him**"), so the pronoun moves to where the
  Greek actually puts it and pays for the cut filler.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**13.12** δῶρʼ, ὅσα Φαιήκων βουληφόροι ἐνθάδʼ ἔνεικαν·
- KEPT: all that the Phaeacian counselors brought and bestowed here.
- ἐνθάδε is in the Greek — a true deictic ("hither"), Alcinous pointing at the gifts in
  his own hall. Warranted; not filler.

## Residual "Thus" openings (justified)

**13.167** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον·
- KEPT: Thus would a man speak out as he looked at the man close beside him:
- ὧδε genuinely means "in this way" and introduces the quoted speech that follows. This
  is a *different* Greek word from the ὣς of 13.170; the pair is deliberately not merged.

**13.185** ὣς οἱ μέν ῥʼ εὔχοντο Ποσειδάωνι ἄνακτι
- KEPT: Thus they were praying then to Poseidon the lord and the ruler,
- Summative ὣς ὁ/οἱ μέν, the scene-closing "so it was that…" construction, rendered
  "Thus" consistently across the poem (cf. 4.15, 4.620, 15.493, 20.172, 24.412). Not a
  speech-close formula.

# Book 14 de-filler — all 47 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened to a feminine close).
2. No reworked line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then*; none of the 47 Greek lines
   carries a line-final locative/temporal that would warrant one. Where the Greek
   does carry ἔπειτα (233, 245, 420, 490), it is rendered honestly mid-line or as
   "thereafter."
3. The preferred fix throughout is reordering so a naturally trochaic word lands
   last (participles, Odysseus at 76, plurals, "-ward" compounds at 277/280).
4. Masculine endings used: 0 (budget 3).
5. Formulas: the Greek half-line δέ οἱ ἔκπεσε χειρός recurs at 31 and 34; the
   English now repeats verbatim ("dropped out of his fingers"). 409 and 453 are
   poem-wide formula lines and now match the renderings already standard in
   Books 17 and 20 (409 = 17.166, 20.240; 453 = 17.98).
6. Voice rule: all reworked dialogue is mortal speech (Eumaeus, the disguised
   Odysseus); no god speaks in any reworked line.

Adjacent lines edited consequentially: 427 (singe restored to 426 where the Greek
has it) and 448 (received "in the hands" from 447).

**14.11** σταυροὺς δʼ ἐκτὸς ἔλασσε διαμπερὲς ἔνθα καὶ ἔνθα,
- OLD: and outside he had driven stakes all around on both sides there,
- NEW: and outside he had driven the stakes right through, this way and that way,
- ἔνθα καὶ ἔνθα ("this way and that") now closes the line itself; διαμπερές = "right through."

**14.13** ἔντοσθεν δʼ αὐλῆς συφεοὺς δυοκαίδεκα ποίει
- OLD: Inside the yard he had fashioned a dozen pens for the swine there,
- NEW: A dozen pens for the swine he had fashioned inside the courtyard,
- "she-swine" avoided at the close: line 14 already carries it.

**14.15** πεντήκοντα σύες χαμαιευνάδες ἐρχατόωντο,
- OLD: and in each pen fifty sows that lay upon the ground there
- NEW: and in each pen fifty sows that couched on the ground for their sleeping
- χαμαιευνάδες = "ground-sleeping"; the close now renders the compound's second half.

**14.19** αἰεὶ ζατρεφέων σιάλων τὸν ἄριστον ἁπάντων·
- OLD: sent them the best of the well-fattened hogs of the drove there;
- NEW: sent them the best of them all, of the hogs most richly fattened;
- ἁπάντων ("of all") restored; "of the drove" was the old line's invention.

**14.26** οἱ τρεῖς· τὸν δὲ τέταρτον ἀποπροέηκε πόλινδε
- OLD: swine, the three; but the fourth he had sent to the city perforce there,
- NEW: swine, the three; but the fourth one he had sent forth to the city,
- "perforce" was a second invention in the old line; both fillers gone.
  ἀποπροέηκε = "sent forth away."

**14.28** ὄφρʼ ἱερεύσαντες κρειῶν κορεσαίατο θυμόν.
- OLD: so that, after the slaughter, their hearts might be glutted with meat there.
- NEW: so that, after the slaughter, with meat their hearts might be glutted.

**14.31** ἕζετο κερδοσύνῃ, σκῆπτρον δέ οἱ ἔκπεσε χειρός.
- OLD: sat himself down in his craft, and his staff fell loose from his hand there.
- NEW: sat himself down in his craft, and the staff dropped out of his fingers.
- Formula: δέ οἱ ἔκπεσε χειρός recurs at 14.34; the English is now verbatim in both.
  χειρός rendered "fingers" for the cadence (pilot precedent, 21.75).

**14.34** ἔσσυτʼ ἀνὰ πρόθυρον, σκῦτος δέ οἱ ἔκπεσε χειρός.
- OLD: darting through the foredoor in haste, and the hide dropped out of his hand there.
- NEW: darting forth through the foredoor, and the hide dropped out of his fingers.
- "in haste" doubled 33's "quickly"; cut. Verbatim echo of 31's close, as in the Greek.

**14.38** ἐξαπίνης, καί κέν μοι ἐλεγχείην κατέχευας.
- OLD: you all at once, and on me you would have poured down shame and reproach there.
- NEW: you all at once, and on me you would have poured down reproaches.
- ἐλεγχείην is one noun; the old doubling "shame and reproach" is gone. The plural
  "reproaches" supplies the feminine close (as at pilot 21.333).

**14.46** σίτου καὶ οἴνοιο κορεσσάμενος κατὰ θυμόν,
- OLD: so that you too, when your spirit has taken its fill of food and wine there,
- NEW: so that you too, when with food and wine your spirit is sated,

**14.50** ἐστόρεσεν δʼ ἐπὶ δέρμα ἰονθάδος ἀγρίου αἰγός,
- OLD: and over it spread the hide of a shaggy and wild mountain goat there,
- NEW: and the hide of a shaggy wild goat he spread out upon it,
- "mountain" was the old line's invention (ἀγρίου = wild); cut.

**14.73** βῆ δʼ ἴμεν ἐς συφεούς, ὅθι ἔθνεα ἔρχατο χοίρων.
- OLD: then he went to the sties where the gathered litters of piglets were penned there.
- NEW: then he went on his way to the sties, where the tribes of the piglets were penned in.
- ἔθνεα = "tribes," restored; "gathered litters" was paraphrase.

**14.74** ἔνθεν ἑλὼν δύʼ ἔνεικε καὶ ἀμφοτέρους ἱέρευσεν,
- OLD: From there he took out two and brought them, and slaughtered them both there,
- NEW: From there he took out two and brought them, and slaughtered the pair of them,
- ἔνθεν keeps its true position at the head of the line.

**14.76** ὀπτήσας δʼ ἄρα πάντα φέρων παρέθηκʼ Ὀδυσῆϊ
- OLD: Then when he had roasted all, he brought it and set it by him there,
- NEW: Then when he had roasted it all, he brought it and set it before Odysseus,
- Ὀδυσῆϊ is in the Greek; the old line dropped the name. Restored, and the name
  supplies the trochaic close.

**14.89** οἵδε δὲ καί τι ἴσασι, θεοῦ δέ τινʼ ἔκλυον αὐδήν,
- OLD: But these men also know something; they heard some voice of a god there,
- NEW: But these men also know something; some god’s voice came to their hearing,
- ἔκλυον ("they heard") flipped to "came to their hearing" for the close; the
  voice still "tells" in 90.

**14.109** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ ἐνδυκέως κρέα τʼ ἤσθιε πῖνέ τε οἶνον
- OLD: So he spoke, and the other kept eating the meat and the wine there
- NEW: So he spoke, and the other ate meat and drank of the wine, attentive,
- Fidelity recovered twice: πῖνε ("drank") was dropped by the old zeugma, and
  ἐνδυκέως ("attentively") was dropped outright. "attentive, / eagerly" now
  mirrors the Greek's paired adverbs ἐνδυκέως / ἁρπαλέως across the line-break.

**14.136** κεῖται ἐπʼ ἠπείρου ψαμάθῳ εἰλυμένα πολλῇ.
- OLD: covered around and concealed in a multitude deep of the sand there.
- NEW: covered around and concealed in a multitude deep of the sand-drift.
- "sand-drift" concretizes ψαμάθῳ by one degree for the cadence; flagged.

**14.154** ἕσσαι με χλαῖνάν τε χιτῶνά τε, εἵματα καλά·
- OLD: clothe me then in a mantle and tunic, fair garments to wear there.
- NEW: clothe me then in a mantle and tunic, in beautiful garments.
- "to wear" was padding; καλά = beautiful.

**14.201** υἱέες ἐν μεγάρῳ ἠμὲν τράφεν ἠδʼ ἐγένοντο
- OLD: grew up and were born in his halls, true sons by his wedded wife there.
- NEW: grew up and were born in his halls, true sons of the wife he had wedded.
- The possessive-participle close ("he had wedded") carries κουριδίῃ/γνήσιοι.

**14.233** λάγχανον· αἶψα δὲ οἶκος ὀφέλλετο, καί ῥα ἔπειτα
- OLD: used to fall to me by lot; so quickly my household increased then,
- NEW: used to fall to my share by lot; and quickly my household prospered,
- ἔπειτα is not dropped: 234's "and thereafter" already renders it; the old "then"
  was a doubling. ὀφέλλετο = "was enriched" → "prospered."

**14.235** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ τήν γε στυγερὴν ὁδὸν εὐρύοπα Ζεὺς
- OLD: But when at last Zeus of the wide-seeing counsel devised there
- NEW: But when at last Zeus of the wide-seeing counsel had purposed
- "purposed" (ἐφράσατο, from 236) keeps the verb out of collision with 243's
  "plotted evils."

**14.245** κουριδίῃ τʼ ἀλόχῳ καὶ κτήμασιν· αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα
- OLD: in my lawful wife and my wealth; but after that time then
- NEW: in my lawful wedded wife and my wealth; but then, thereafter,
- αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα is genuinely line-final in the Greek; "thereafter" renders it
  honestly (a real temporal, not padding). "wedded" fills out κουριδίῃ.

**14.258** στῆσα δʼ ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ ποταμῷ νέας ἀμφιελίσσας.
- OLD: and there in the river of Egypt I moored my curving ships there.
- NEW: and there in the river of Egypt I moored my ships, double-curving.
- The first "there" (στῆσα δʼ ἐν...) stands; the doubled final one is gone.
  ἀμφιελίσσας = "curved at both ends" — "double-curving" is the exact sense, and
  avoids repeating 260's "vessels."

**14.266** οἱ δὲ βοῆς ἀΐοντες ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφιν
- OLD: Hearing the shouting, as soon as the dawn was beginning to show there,
- NEW: Hearing the shouting, as soon as the dawn came forth in her shining,
- φαινομένηφιν = "showing forth" → "came forth in her shining."

**14.276** αὐτίκʼ ἀπὸ κρατὸς κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκα
- OLD: straightway I lifted the well-wrought helmet down from my head then,
- NEW: straightway down from my head I lifted the well-wrought helmet,

**14.277** καὶ σάκος ὤμοιϊν, δόρυ δʼ ἔκβαλον ἔκτοσε χειρός·
- OLD: and the shield from my shoulders, and cast the spear from my hand there.
- NEW: and the shield from my shoulders, and cast the spear from my hand outward.
- ἔκτοσε ("out, outward") was dropped by the old line; it now supplies the close.

**14.280** ἐς δίφρον δέ μʼ ἕσας ἄγεν οἴκαδε δάκρυ χέοντα.
- OLD: set me into his car and carried me home while I wept there.
- NEW: set me into his car and carried me, weeping, homeward.
- οἴκαδε = "homeward" (the poem's own idiom); δάκρυ χέοντα agrees with "me," so
  "me, weeping" keeps the weeper unambiguous.

**14.286** χρήματʼ ἀνʼ Αἰγυπτίους ἄνδρας· δίδοσαν γὰρ ἅπαντες.
- OLD: much wealth among Egyptian men, for all of them gave me gifts there.
- NEW: much wealth among Egyptian men, for all of them kept giving.
- "gifts" was the old line's addition (δίδοσαν takes no object); the imperfect
  is now rendered ("kept giving").

**14.288** δὴ τότε Φοῖνιξ ἦλθεν ἀνὴρ ἀπατήλια εἰδώς,
- OLD: then there came a Phoenician man well versed in deceits there,
- NEW: then indeed there came a Phoenician man, well versed in deceptions,
- δή regains "indeed"; the mid-line "then there came" renders δὴ τότε ἦλθεν itself.

**14.292** ἔνθα παρʼ αὐτῷ μεῖνα τελεσφόρον εἰς ἐνιαυτόν.
- OLD: There I remained beside him through the full course of a year there.
- NEW: There I remained beside him until a full year was completed.
- Opening "There" = ἔνθα (warranted, kept); the echo at the close is gone.
  τελεσφόρον ("completion-bringing") → "completed."

**14.308** οἱ δὲ κορώνῃσιν ἴκελοι περὶ νῆα μέλαιναν
- OLD: Like to the sea-crows they drifted around the black ship there,
- NEW: And they, like to the sea-crows, around the black ship were drifting,
- οἱ δέ ("and they") restored at the head of the line.

**14.310** αὐτὰρ ἐμοὶ Ζεὺς αὐτός, ἔχοντί περ ἄλγεα θυμῷ,
- OLD: But to me Zeus himself, though I had pain in the heart there,
- NEW: But to me Zeus himself, though I carried griefs in my spirit,
- ἄλγεα is plural ("griefs"), restored; the plural is honest, the close is "spirit."

**14.314** ἐννῆμαρ φερόμην, δεκάτῃ δέ με νυκτὶ μελαίνῃ
- OLD: Nine days I was borne, and on the tenth in the blackness of night then
- NEW: Nine days I was borne, and then on the tenth, in the night’s blackness

**14.345** ἔνθʼ ἐμὲ μὲν κατέδησαν ἐϋσσέλμῳ ἐνὶ νηῒ
- OLD: There they bound me fast in the well-benched ship where I sat there,
- NEW: There they bound me fast within the well-benched vessel,
- "where I sat" was pure padding; cut. Opening "There" = ἔνθα, kept.

**14.369** τῷ κέν οἱ τύμβον μὲν ἐποίησαν Παναχαιοί,
- OLD: Then the Panachaeans would have made him a burial mound there,
- NEW: Then would the Panachaeans have fashioned his burial barrow,
- τύμβον = "barrow"; opening "Then" = τῷ ("in that case"), kept in place.

**14.402** ξεῖνʼ, οὕτω γάρ κέν μοι ἐϋκλείη τʼ ἀρετή τε
- OLD: “Stranger, that surely would win me fair glory and noble repute there
- NEW: “Stranger, that surely would win me noble repute and fair glory
- The two nouns swap places; "glory" lands the feminine close.

**14.409** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: Thus then the two of them spoke such words to one another there.
- NEW: Thus they were speaking such things in turn to one another.
- Poem-wide formula line, now verbatim with 17.166 and 20.240.

**14.419** οἱ δʼ ὗν εἰσῆγον μάλα πίονα πενταέτηρον.
- OLD: They then brought in a hog, very fat, of five years of age there.
- NEW: Then they led in a five-year-old hog, exceedingly fattened.
- εἰσῆγον = "led in"; μάλα πίονα = "exceedingly fattened."

**14.420** τὸν μὲν ἔπειτʼ ἔστησαν ἐπʼ ἐσχάρῃ· οὐδὲ συβώτης
- OLD: Him they next set beside the hearth; and the swineherd did not then
- NEW: Him thereafter they set by the hearth; nor did the swineherd
- ἔπειτα moves to its honest mid-line place ("thereafter"); οὐδὲ συβώτης now ends
  the line exactly as in the Greek, and "swineherd" is the trochaic close.

**14.426** τὸν δʼ ἔλιπε ψυχή. τοὶ δʼ ἔσφαξάν τε καὶ εὗσαν·
- OLD: he struck, and the life left the boar; and the rest slit the throat there.
- NEW: he struck, and the life left the boar; and they slit his throat and singed him.
- εὗσαν ("singed") restored to this line, where the Greek has it (the old text had
  displaced it into 427). Forced the consequential edit below.

**14.427** αἶψα δέ μιν διέχευαν (adjacent line, consequential edit)
- OLD: Quickly they singed him and cut him apart; and the swineherd then made ready
- NEW: Quickly they cut him apart; and the swineherd then made ready
- The displaced "singed" removed; 426–427 now track the Greek sequence
  (throat-cut, singe, then cut apart).

**14.431** ὤπτησάν τε περιφραδέως ἐρύσαντό τε πάντα,
- OLD: roasted them carefully through, and when all was done drew them off there,
- NEW: roasted them carefully through, and drew them off, every morsel,
- "when all was done" was a second filler; cut. πάντα → "every morsel" is a
  one-degree concretization for the close ("pieces" would collide with 430); flagged.

**14.447** σπείσας δʼ αἴθοπα οἶνον Ὀδυσσῆϊ πτολιπόρθῳ
- OLD: then he poured out the flaming wine and set it in the hands there
- NEW: then, when he had poured the flaming wine in libation, he set it
- σπείσας is properly "poured as libation" — now explicit. "in the hands" moves
  to 448 (below), preserving the "sacker of cities" formula there intact.

**14.448** ἐν χείρεσσιν ἔθηκεν· ὁ δʼ ἕζετο ᾗ παρὰ μοίρῃ (adjacent line, consequential edit)
- OLD: of Odysseus, sacker of cities; then sat by his portion.
- NEW: in the hands of Odysseus, sacker of cities; then sat by his portion.
- Receives ἐν χείρεσσιν from 447; scans as six falling beats with feminine close.

**14.453** οἱ δʼ ἐπʼ ὀνείαθʼ ἑτοῖμα προκείμενα χεῖρας ἴαλλον.
- OLD: Then to the good fare lying before them they stretched out their hands there.
- NEW: Then they reached out their hands to the good things laid ready before them.
- The poem-wide feast formula, now verbatim with 17.98.

**14.456** σίτου καὶ κρειῶν κεκορημένοι ἐσσεύοντο.
- OLD: filled full with bread and with meat, were turning and moving to bed there.
- NEW: full-sated both with the bread and the meat, were hastening.
- "to bed" doubled 455's "toward sleeping" (πρὸς κοῖτον, already rendered there);
  ἐσσεύοντο = "were hastening."

**14.479** εὗδον δʼ εὔκηλοι, σάκεσιν εἰλυμένοι ὤμους·
- OLD: and at their ease they slept with their shoulders wrapped under their shields there.
- NEW: and they slept all at their ease, with shields enwrapping their shoulders.

**14.490** ὣς ἐφάμην, ὁ δʼ ἔπειτα νόον σχέθε τόνδʼ ἐνὶ θυμῷ,
- OLD: So I spoke, and at once in his heart he conceived this design there,
- NEW: So I spoke, and at once this design he conceived in his spirit,
- "at once" (inherited) stands for ἔπειτα mid-line.

**14.501** βῆ δὲ θέειν ἐπὶ νῆας· ἐγὼ δʼ ἐνὶ εἵματι κείνου
- OLD: and ran on his way to the ships; and I in that man’s cloak there
- NEW: and ran on his way to the ships; and I in that man’s garment
- εἵματι = "garment" (the cloak-word χλαῖναν belongs to 500, where it stands).

## Tally

- 47 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then/here*.
- 0 masculine endings used (budget was 3).
- Formulas restored to verbatim echo: 31/34 (δέ οἱ ἔκπεσε χειρός); 409 aligned
  with 17.166/20.240; 453 aligned with 17.98.
- Fidelity recovered along the way: Ὀδυσῆϊ at 76; πῖνε and ἐνδυκέως at 109;
  ἁπάντων at 19; ἔθνεα at 73; ἔκτοσε at 277; οἴκαδε at 280; δή at 288; οἱ δέ at
  308; the plural ἄλγεα at 310; εὗσαν restored to 426; σπείσας made explicit at
  447; εἰσῆγον at 419. Inherited inventions cut: "perforce" (26), "mountain" (50),
  "gifts" (286), "to wear" (154), "where I sat" (345), "when all was done" (431),
  "to bed" (456).
- Honest costs incurred: χειρός → "fingers" at 31/34 (pilot precedent); "sand-drift"
  at 136; πάντα → "every morsel" at 431; ἐλεγχείην singular → "reproaches" at 38;
  ἔκλυον flipped to "came to their hearing" at 89. Total added-word residue is far
  below what the old lines carried in filler.
- Two adjacent lines edited consequentially: 427 and 448 (both scanned and logged
  above).
- Self-lint: no verse line in book-14.md now ends in *there/now/then*. Eight
  pre-existing lines end in "here" (66, 124, 161, 169, 188, 407, 462, 516); none
  were in the extraction's target set, and the checked cases are warranted deictics
  (161 ἐνθάδε "hither"; 407 ἔνδον "within"). A separate "here" pass could vet the
  remainder.

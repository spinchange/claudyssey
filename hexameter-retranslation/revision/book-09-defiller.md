# Book 09 de-filler — all 33 filler-final lines resolved

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling line;
no line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek carries the sense (one
case: 09.193, αὐτοῦ in the run-over clause of 194); the preferred fix is reordering
so a naturally trochaic word lands last; masculine endings budgeted at 3, used once
(09.431); formulas normalized where the Greek repeats (09.109 = 09.123 ἄσπαρτα καὶ
ἀνήροτα → "unsown and unplowed"; 09.409 joins the poem-wide "and these words took
wing as they uttered" formula for ἔπεα πτερόεντα).

---

**09.33** ἀλλʼ ἐμὸν οὔ ποτε θυμὸν ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν ἔπειθον.
- OLD: Yet they could never persuade the heart in my breast to remain there.
- NEW: Yet they could never persuade the heart that was housed in my bosom.
- "housed" is the one light added word (logged); "to remain" was itself supplied.

**09.40** Ἰσμάρῳ. ἔνθα δʼ ἐγὼ πόλιν ἔπραθον, ὤλεσα δʼ αὐτούς·
- OLD: into Ismarus; there I sacked the town and destroyed all the men there.
- NEW: into Ismarus; there I sacked the city and slaughtered the people.
- The line-medial "there" is kept: it renders ἔνθα itself; only the final filler goes.

**09.72** καὶ τὰ μὲν ἐς νῆας κάθεμεν, δείσαντες ὄλεθρον,
- OLD: Down in the ships we lowered the sails in our terror of death there,
- NEW: Down in the ships we lowered the sails in our dread of destruction,
- δείσαντες ὄλεθρον = fearing destruction; the noun now closes the line unstressed.

**09.89** οἵ τινες ἀνέρες εἶεν ἐπὶ χθονὶ σῖτον ἔδοντες
- OLD: what kind of men in the country were eating their bread on the earth there.
- NEW: what kind of men were eating their bread on the earth of that country.
- The old line doubled ἐπὶ χθονί ("in the country ... on the earth"); the doubling
  is folded into one phrase and the doubled locative gives the close.

**09.93** ἡμετέροις, ἀλλά σφι δόσαν λωτοῖο πάσασθαι.
- OLD: rather they gave my companions some lotus flower to taste there.
- NEW: rather they gave my companions a taste of the flower of lotus.

**09.101** σπερχομένους νηῶν ἐπιβαινέμεν ὠκειάων,
- OLD: all of them boarding the swift-faring ships with no further delay there,
- NEW: all of them making their haste to embark on the swift-faring vessels,
- σπερχομένους = hastening ("making their haste"); ἐπιβαινέμεν = to embark.

**09.109** ἀλλὰ τά γʼ ἄσπαρτα καὶ ἀνήροτα πάντα φύονται,
- OLD: everything grows for the people without being planted or plowed there,
- NEW: everything grows of itself, unsown and unplowed for the people,
- Formula: ἄσπαρτα καὶ ἀνήροτα recurs at 09.123, already "unsown and unplowed"
  there; now verbatim here too. "of itself" is the logged light addition (the sense
  of unaided growth); "for the people" is Murray's own supplied dative ("spring up
  for them"), carried over from the old line.

**09.114** ἐν σπέσσι γλαφυροῖσι, θεμιστεύει δὲ ἕκαστος
- OLD: dwelling in hollow caves, and every man gives the law there
- NEW: dwelling in hollow caves, and each man renders his judgments
- θεμιστεύει = gives judgments; "renders his judgments / over his children and
  wives" keeps the run-over into 115 grammatical, which the draft's "governs his
  household" broke.

**09.185** ὑψηλὴ δέδμητο κατωρυχέεσσι λίθοισι
- OLD: ran all around it, constructed of stones dug deep in the earth there,
- NEW: ran all around it, constructed of stones in the earth deep-bedded,
- κατωρυχέεσσι λίθοισι = stones deep-bedded in the earth (Murray's own word); the
  draft's "quarried and buried" said something the Greek does not.

**09.189** πωλεῖτʼ, ἀλλʼ ἀπάνευθεν ἐὼν ἀθεμίστια ᾔδη.
- OLD: standing aloof, for the heart in his body was stranger to law there.
- NEW: standing aloof, and the heart in his body knew nothing of justice.
- Keeps the poem's "heart in his body" formula (cf. 413, 459, 480); ἀθεμίστια ᾔδη
  closes on "justice."

**09.193** δὴ τότε τοὺς ἄλλους κελόμην ἐρίηρας ἑταίρους
- OLD: Then I commanded the rest of my faithful companions to stay there,
- NEW: (unchanged — warranted)
- The final "to stay there" translates the run-over clause of 194, μένειν αὐτοῦ
  πὰρ νηί, "to remain right there beside the ship": αὐτοῦ is exactly the licensed
  locative class. The draft's rewrite dropped the infinitive and broke the join
  with 194 ("close to the ship, and provide their defense…"). Kept verbatim.

**09.196** βῆν· ἀτὰρ αἴγεον ἀσκὸν ἔχον μέλανος οἴνοιο
- OLD: Thus I went forward, and carried a goatskin filled with dark wine there,
- NEW: Thus I went forward, and carried the dark wine held in a goatskin,
- The draft's "goatskin of dark wine" closed on the stress; reordered so "goatskin"
  closes, and "wine … / sweet wine given to me by Maron" now apposes cleanly.

**09.208** τὸν δʼ ὅτε πίνοιεν μελιηδέα οἶνον ἐρυθρόν,
- OLD: Whenever they drank of this honey-sweet red-colored wine there,
- NEW: Whenever they drank of this honey-sweet, red-colored vintage,
- "vintage" is the logged synonym for the cadence (pilot precedent: "the weapon"
  for τόξου at 21.113); the draft's "of the" is restored to "of this" (τόν).

**09.220** ἀρνῶν ἠδʼ ἐρίφων· διακεκριμέναι δὲ ἕκασται
- OLD: filled with the lambs and the kids, each kind being folded apart there,
- NEW: filled with the lambs and the kids, each kind held apart from the others,
- διακεκριμέναι = kept distinct. The draft's "penned separately" both trailed a
  three-syllable tail and collided with 221's line-end "separate"; "held" also
  avoids tripling 221–222's "kept."

**09.222** χωρὶς δʼ αὖθʼ ἕρσαι. ναῖον δʼ ὀρῷ ἄγγεα πάντα,
- OLD: newborn kept to themselves, and the vessels were swimming with whey there,
- NEW: newborn kept to themselves, and with whey every vessel was swimming,
- ἄγγεα πάντα = every vessel; the draft's "milk pails" duplicated "milking" in 223.

**09.242** ἐσθλαὶ τετράκυκλοι ἀπʼ οὔδεος ὀχλίσσειαν·
- OLD: each with four wheels, could have lifted that stone from the ground there,
- NEW: each with four wheels, could have lifted that stone from the floor of the cavern,
- οὔδεος is the cave floor; "floor of the cavern" concretizes it (pilot precedent:
  21.82 "floor-boards"). The draft's "boulder … pavement" changed two words for no
  fidelity gain.

**09.246** αὐτίκα δʼ ἥμισυ μὲν θρέψας λευκοῖο γάλακτος
- OLD: Straightway then he curdled one half of the gleaming white milk there,
- NEW: Straightway then one half of the gleaming white milk he curdled,
- Plain "half" (not the draft's "half-part") keeps the μέν/δέ echo with 248's
  "half he positioned in vessels."

**09.289** σὺν δὲ δύω μάρψας ὥς τε σκύλακας ποτὶ γαίῃ
- OLD: snatching up two at a time, and like puppies he dashed them on ground there;
- NEW: snatching up two at a time, and dashed them like puppies to earthward;
- ποτὶ γαίῃ closes as a "-ward" compound in the poem's idiom.

**09.292** ἤσθιε δʼ ὥς τε λέων ὀρεσίτροφος, οὐδʼ ἀπέλειπεν,
- OLD: just like a lion bred in the mountains, and nothing was left there,
- NEW: just like a lion bred in the mountains, leaving no remnant,
- οὐδʼ ἀπέλειπεν is active — "he left nothing"; the participle keeps it so and
  hands straight into 293's "neither the entrails nor flesh nor the bones." The
  draft's "left behind him" ran to seven beats.

**09.300** ἆσσον ἰών, ξίφος ὀξὺ ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ,
- OLD: going up close and unsheathing the sharpened sword at my thigh there,
- NEW: going up close, and drawing the sharp-edged sword from my thigh-side,
- παρὰ μηροῦ = from beside the thigh; ἐρυσσάμενος = drawing.

**09.329** καὶ τὸ μὲν εὖ κατέθηκα κατακρύψας ὑπὸ κόπρῳ,
- OLD: Carefully then I laid it away and concealed it beneath all the dung there,
- NEW: Carefully then I laid it away, concealed in the dung-bed,
- The draft's "dung-heaps" duplicated 330's line-end "heap"; "dung-bed" keeps the
  noun adjacent so 330's "litter that lay…" still apposes to it.

**09.360** ὣς φάτʼ, ἀτάρ οἱ αὖτις ἐγὼ πόρον αἴθοπα οἶνον.
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and again I provided the fire-bright wine there.
- NEW: Thus did he speak, and again the fire-bright wine I provided.

**09.382** οἱ μὲν μοχλὸν ἑλόντες ἐλάινον, ὀξὺν ἐπʼ ἄκρῳ,
- OLD: Taking the stake of the olive, sharpened to end in a point there,
- NEW: Taking the stake of the olive, brought to a point at the far end,
- ὀξὺν ἐπʼ ἄκρῳ; "far end" is the poem's own phrase for this same sharpened end
  (327 "I sharpened the far end"), whose trochaic close it already established.
  The draft's placeholder ("a sharp point") repeated "sharp" and closed stressed.

**09.396** ἡμεῖς δὲ δείσαντες ἀπεσσύμεθʼ· αὐτὰρ ὁ μοχλὸν
- OLD: rang; we withdrew in our terror; he wrenched the great stake from his eye there,
- NEW: rang; we withdrew in our terror; he wrenched the great stake outward,
- Greek 396 has no eye-word (ὀφθαλμοῖο belongs to 397, whose English already ends
  "eyeball" — the draft's close duplicated it). "outward" renders the ἐξ- of 397's
  ἐξέρυσε, pulled with the verb into this line.

**09.398** τὸν μὲν ἔπειτʼ ἔρριψεν ἀπὸ ἕο χερσὶν ἀλύων,
- OLD: hurled it away, while his arms in a furious agony flailed there,
- NEW: hurled it away, while his arms flailed round in a furious anguish,
- χερσὶν ἀλύων; "round" is the logged light word (the flailing-about of ἀλύων);
  "anguish" is the poem's word (440 "terrible anguish") and closes unstressed
  where "agony" would trail a three-syllable tail.

**09.409** οἱ δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενοι ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευον·
- OLD: Then in their turn they responded to him with the words that took wing there:
- NEW: Then in their turn they answered, and these words took wing as they uttered:
- Normalized to the poem-wide ἔπεα πτερόεντα formula ("and these words took wing
  as … uttered:", cf. 4.550, 5.117, 5.172, etc.); the draft's close on "wing" was
  a fourth masculine ending the budget does not have.

**09.419** οὕτω γάρ πού μʼ ἤλπετʼ ἐνὶ φρεσὶ νήπιον εἶναι.
- OLD: such was the fool he expected, perhaps, he would find me to be there.
- NEW: such was the fool, I suppose, that his heart expected to find me.
- πού = "I suppose" (the poem's rendering at 262); ἐνὶ φρεσί restored ("his
  heart"), which the draft dropped; the draft also closed on an unwarranted "still."

**09.431** τρεῖς δὲ ἕκαστον φῶτʼ ὄιες φέρον· αὐτὰρ ἐγώ γε—
- OLD: Thus every man had three, but for me there remained a great ram there,
- NEW: Thus every man had three, but for me there remained the great ram,
- Licensed masculine ending #1: αὐτὰρ ἐγώ γε— breaks off on the emphatic reserve
  of the best ram, the instrument of the escape; closing on it is the emphasis the
  Greek's own aposiopesis carries. The medial "there remained" is the existential
  idiom, not a locative. The draft's "ram-leader" added a rank the Greek gives
  only in 432.

**09.434** κείμην· αὐτὰρ χερσὶν ἀώτου θεσπεσίοιο
- OLD: belly and waited, my hands in the marvelous fleece of the ram there
- NEW: belly and waited, and both of my hands in the marvelous fleeces
- χερσίν = with both hands; ἀώτου θεσπεσίοιο = the marvelous choice wool; the
  poetic plural "fleeces" is the poem's own (426 "dark-violet fleeces"). The
  draft's "fleece of the ram-flock" made the fleece the flock's. Runs on into
  435 "twisted and tightly held" unchanged.

**09.447** κριὲ πέπον, τί μοι ὧδε διὰ σπέος ἔσσυο μήλων
- OLD: “Sweetheart, my old ram, why are you last out of all of the flock now,
- NEW: “Sweetheart, my old ram, why are you last of the whole of the sheep-flock,
- μήλων closes as "sheep-flock," the poem's compound (167, 418); the draft's
  "the whole flock" closed on the stress.

**09.468** ἀλλʼ ἐγὼ οὐκ εἴων, ἀνὰ δʼ ὀφρύσι νεῦον ἑκάστῳ,
- OLD: This I allowed not; frowning, I held back the tears of each man there,
- NEW: This I allowed not; frowning, I checked every man from his weeping,

**09.501** ἀλλά μιν ἄψορρον προσέφην κεκοτηότι θυμῷ·
- OLD: once more I called to the monster, with anger at heart as I spoke there:
- NEW: once more I called to the monster, my heart still angered, and spoke thus:
- κεκοτηότι θυμῷ; "thus" is deictic to the quoted speech, not metrical padding.

**09.535** νηὸς ἐπʼ ἀλλοτρίης, εὕροι δʼ ἐν πήματα οἴκῳ.
- OLD: there on another man’s ship, and discover affliction at home there.”
- NEW: there on another man’s ship, and find in his house affliction.”
- The line-initial "there" carries over from 534's clause and stays; εὕροι δʼ ἐν
  πήματα οἴκῳ closes on "affliction."

# Οὖτις / μῆτις wordplay pass — Books 2, 3, 5, 8, 9

Targeted pass to carry the Book 9 name-pun (Οὖτις ≈ οὔ τις "nobody";
μή τις ≈ μῆτις "cunning"; οὐτιδανός "worthless nothing") as far as English
allows, and to align the poem's deliberate echoes of it. The ~50 other
οὔ τις / μή τις lines are unmarked idiom in the Greek and were left alone;
"nobody" everywhere would make the word marked in English, the opposite of
the Greek effect.

- Verses changed: **11** (2.232–233, 3.119–121, 5.11, 8.552, 9.406, 9.410, 9.414, 9.460)
- Scansion lines updated in the `-performance.md` files: **11**
- Verse counts: unchanged in every book
- Metrical note: the line-final dactyl at 9.460 (NÓBODY) follows existing
  practice — ÍTHACA ×14, HÓMECOMING ×11, WÁNDERING ×6, SÚFFERING ×5,
  CÝCLOPES ×5, ÉVENING ×5 (incl. 9.451 in the same speech).
- Audio: Books 2, 3, 5, 8, 9 chunks containing these lines are now stale.

Anchor for μῆτις is **cunning** (20.20, the poem's explicit callback:
"till your cunning / led you out from the cave"). 2.279, 9.422, 10.193
(μῆτις as "intelligence" / "device" / "plan") were left as optional.

---

**02.232** punctuation only — Greek 233 opens with causal ὡς, so 232 must not close the sentence
- OLD: rather, forever be cruel in mind and accomplish injustice.
- NEW: rather, forever be cruel in mind and accomplish injustice,

**02.233** ὡς οὔ τις μέμνηται Ὀδυσσῆος θείοιο — "now in the country" was filler (λαῶν is carried by 234)
- OLD: No one remembers the godlike Odysseus now in the country,
- NEW: since nobody any longer remembers the godlike Odysseus,

**03.119** παντοίοισι δόλοισι — δόλος moved off "cunning" so that 120 can use it for μῆτις
- OLD: every conceivable cunning, and Cronos' son scarcely ended it.
- NEW: every conceivable trickery, and Cronos' son scarcely ended it.

**03.120** ἔνθʼ οὔ τίς ποτε μῆτιν ὁμοιωθήμεναι ἄντην — both halves of the pun in one line, about Odysseus; ἤθελ' (121) absorbed as "willing"
- OLD: No man there would openly set his intelligence against that man's counsel,
- NEW: Nobody there was ever willing to match him in cunning

**03.121** ἤθελʼ, ἐπεὶ μάλα πολλὸν ἐνίκα δῖος Ὀδυσσεὺς — ἄντην moved here as "face to face"; "no man who wished to succeed" was padding
- OLD: no man who wished to succeed, for greatly surpassed them Odysseus
- NEW: face to face, since by far the greatest was godlike Odysseus

**05.11** verbatim repeat of 2.233 in the Greek; now identical in English
- OLD: since not one of the people remembers the godlike Odysseus,
- NEW: since nobody any longer remembers the godlike Odysseus,

**08.552** οὐ μὲν γάρ τις πάμπαν ἀνώνυμός ἐστʼ ἀνθρώπων — Alcinous's cue for "I am Odysseus"; "in humanity" was filler, ἀνθρώπων is carried by 553 "among people"; γάρ kept
- OLD: No mortal man in humanity ever is wholly without name,
- NEW: For there is nobody living at all who is wholly nameless,

**09.406** ἦ μή τίς σʼ αὐτὸν κτείνει δόλῳ ἠὲ βίηφιν; — near-identical to 408 in the Greek; now mirrors 408's "kills … by cunning … power" so "Nobody" is heard as the neighbours' "nobody"
- OLD: Surely no one destroys you by cunning or violence against you?”
- NEW: Surely nobody kills you by cunning, or else by his power?”

**09.410** εἰ μὲν δὴ μή τίς σε βιάζεται — the punchline μή τις
- OLD: “If no one forces you now, when you sit all alone in the cavern,
- NEW: “If nobody forces you now, when you sit all alone in the cavern,

**09.414** ὡς ὄνομʼ ἐξαπάτησεν ἐμὸν καὶ μῆτις ἀμύμων — "false" is not in the Greek (the name is not false, that is the joke); μῆτις → "cunning" to match 20.20
- OLD: seeing my false name and flawless design had completely deceived him.
- NEW: seeing the way that my name and my flawless cunning had fooled him.

**09.460** τά μοι οὐτιδανὸς πόρεν Οὖτις — οὐτιδανός is built on the same root as Οὖτις and the speech ends on the name; "nothing … Nobody" reproduces the sound-play and the final-word punch
- OLD: brought on me now by this worthless and weakling Nobody creature.”
- NEW: brought on me now by that worthless nothing, that weakling Nobody.”

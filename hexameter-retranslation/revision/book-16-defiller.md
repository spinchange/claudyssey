# Book 16 de-filler — all 46 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling
line; no line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then*/*here* unless the Greek warrants
it (two cases: 16.181 νέον "just now", 16.355 ἔνδον "here"); the preferred fix is
reordering onto a naturally trochaic word; masculine endings budgeted (two used:
16.355, 16.367); verbatim Greek repetition kept verbatim in English (16.98 =
16.116). One adjacent line (16.191) was edited as a consequence of the 16.190 fix.

---

**16.15** κύσσε δέ μιν κεφαλήν τε καὶ ἄμφω φάεα καλὰ
- OLD: kissed his head, and both of his beautiful radiant eyes there,
- NEW: kissed his head, and both of his eyes, so beautiful, radiant,
- ἄμφω ("both") retained, paralleling 16's "and both of his hands"; "so" is the
  one light addition, logged.

**16.29** ἀνδρῶν μνηστήρων ἐσορᾶν ἀΐδηλον ὅμιλον.
- OLD: gazing upon that ruinous crowd of the suitor-men there.”
- NEW: gazing upon that ruinous throng of the men who are suitors.”

**16.31** ἔσσεται οὕτως, ἄττα· σέθεν δʼ ἕνεκʼ ἐνθάδʼ ἱκάνω,
- OLD: “It shall be so, old father; for your sake I have come here now,
- NEW: “It shall be so, old father; for your sake I have come hither,
- ἐνθάδʼ rendered as "hither" (the poem's own word at 16.57, 16.222): the
  locative is kept and the close is feminine; the unwarranted "now" is gone.

**16.34** ἀνδρῶν ἄλλος ἔγημεν, Ὀδυσσῆος δέ που εὐνὴ
- OLD: some other man has married her, while Odysseus’ bed there
- NEW: some other man has married her, while perhaps the bed of Odysseus
- που ("perhaps"), untranslated in the old line, is restored — it supplies the
  missing beat honestly and the name closes the line.

**16.41** αὐτὰρ ὅ γʼ εἴσω ἴεν καὶ ὑπέρβη λάϊνον οὐδόν.
- OLD: then he himself went inward and crossed the threshold of stone there.
- NEW: then he himself went inward, and stepped across the stone threshold.
- ὑπέρβη is literally "stepped over." Book 17.30 repeats this formula and should
  be matched to this line when its turn comes.

**16.46** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ αὖτις ἰὼν κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετο· τῷ δὲ συβώτης
- OLD: So he spoke, and Odysseus went back and sat in his place then;
- NEW: So he spoke, and Odysseus, turning back, went and was seated;

**16.49** τοῖσιν δʼ αὖ κρειῶν πίνακας παρέθηκε συβώτης
- OLD: Then the swineherd set before them platters of roasted meats there,
- NEW: Then the swineherd set platters of roasted meats down beside them,
- "beside them" is παρέθηκε's own preposition — and avoids "before them"
  colliding with 50's "left over before" in the very next line.

**16.52** ἐν δʼ ἄρα κισσυβίῳ κίρνη μελιηδέα οἶνον·
- OLD: and in an ivy-wood cup he mixed the honey-sweet wine there;
- NEW: and he mixed in the ivy-wood cup the wine sweet as honey;
- μελιηδέα unpacked to "sweet as honey" so the line closes feminine without any
  bolted-on word.

**16.54** οἱ δʼ ἐπʼ ὀνείαθʼ ἑτοῖμα προκείμενα χεῖρας ἴαλλον.
- OLD: And to the good things prepared and set out they reached with their hands then.
- NEW: Then they reached out their hands to the good things laid ready before them.
- Matched verbatim to the feast formula fixed in the Book 14 and 17 revisions
  (14.453, 17.98) — the Greek line is identical.

**16.98** μαρναμένοισι πέποιθε, καὶ εἰ μέγα νεῖκος ὄρηται.
- OLD: there in the clash of battle, even if great strife should arise there.
- NEW: there in the clash of battle, even should great strife be arising.
- Sentence ends here (99 begins "If only…"), so the period is kept.

**16.105** εἰ δʼ αὖ με πληθυῖ δαμασαίατο μοῦνον ἐόντα,
- OLD: And if they by their numbers subdued me, being all alone there,
- NEW: And if they should overcome me alone by the weight of their numbers,
- "weight of" is a light gloss on πληθυῖ's instrumental force, logged; "alone"
  now attaches to *me* (μοῦνον ἐόντα), where the draft's participle dangled.

**16.107** τεθνάμεν ἢ τάδε γʼ αἰὲν ἀεικέα ἔργʼ ὁράασθαι,
- OLD: than forever keep seeing these shameful deeds before my eyes there,
- NEW: than forever to go on seeing these shameful deeds before me,
- "before me" is smaller than the old line's invented "before my eyes"; logged.

**16.110** καὶ οἶνον διαφυσσόμενον, καὶ σῖτον ἔδοντας
- OLD: and the wine being drained and squandered, and men eating the food there
- NEW: and the wine being drawn off and wasted, and the food being eaten
- διαφυσσόμενον is literally "drawn off." Continues into 111 unchanged.

**16.116** μαρναμένοισι πέποιθε, καὶ εἰ μέγα νεῖκος ὄρηται.
- OLD: there in the clash of battle, even if great strife should arise there.
- NEW: there in the clash of battle, even should great strife be arising.
- Verbatim with 16.98, as the Greek is.

**16.145** ἧσται ὀδυρόμενος, φθινύθει δʼ ἀμφʼ ὀστεόφι χρώς.
- OLD: sits there and mourns, and the flesh all wastes from around his bones there.”
- NEW: sits there in mourning, and all of the flesh from his bones is wasting.”
- The non-final "sits there" is kept (ἧσται's settled posture).

**16.154** ἦ ῥα καὶ ὦρσε συφορβόν· ὁ δʼ εἵλετο χερσὶ πέδιλα,
- OLD: So he spoke and stirred up the swineherd; he took in his hands then
- NEW: So he spoke and stirred up the swineherd; and into his hands he was taking
- Enjambment into 155 ("both of his sandals…") preserved.

**16.162** ἀλλʼ Ὀδυσεύς τε κύνες τε ἴδον, καί ῥʼ οὐχ ὑλάοντο
- OLD: But Odysseus saw her, and so did the dogs, yet they did not bark there,
- NEW: But Odysseus saw her, and so did the dogs, yet they kept from barking,
- "they" stays plural so 163's "shrank" keeps its subject (the draft's "not one
  was barking" stranded it); "kept from" carries a faint shade of restraint,
  logged — the dogs whimper in fear rather than bark.

**16.176** κυάνεαι δʼ ἐγένοντο γενειάδες ἀμφὶ γένειον.
- OLD: and blue-black grew the beard all around on the line of his chin there.
- NEW: and blue-black grew the beard that encircled the whole of his jawline.
- "encircled the whole of" redistributes ἀμφὶ's "all around."

**16.179** ταρβήσας δʼ ἑτέρωσε βάλʼ ὄμματα, μὴ θεὸς εἴη,
- OLD: Fearful, he turned his eyes away, in dread lest it might be a god there,
- NEW: Fearful, he turned his eyes elsewhere, in dread it might be immortal,
- ἑτέρωσε = "elsewhere"; "immortal" for θεὸς matches the poem's own usage
  ("the immortals," 16.187).

**16.181** ἀλλοῖός μοι, ξεῖνε, φάνης νέον ἠὲ πάροιθεν,
- KEPT: “Stranger, to me you appear quite changed from the man you were just now.
- The close is νέον itself — "just now" is the literal Greek. Warranted; no
  change made.

**16.190** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας υἱὸν κύσε, κὰδ δὲ παρειῶν
- OLD: So he spoke, and he kissed his son; from his cheeks to the ground then
- NEW: So he spoke, and he kissed his son; and down from his cheeks, earthward,
- χαμᾶζε (191, "to the ground"), which the draft dropped, is kept as "earthward"
  — a close the project's own rule-list endorses — and no masculine ending is
  spent. This forced one consequential edit next door:
  - **16.191** δάκρυον ἧκε χαμᾶζε· πάρος δʼ ἔχε νωλεμὲς αἰεί
  - OLD: tears fell down, though before he had always held back his weeping.
  - NEW: tears fell, though before he had always held back his weeping.
  - "down" removed (now carried by 190's "down… earthward"); the line still
    scans six.

**16.207** αὐτάρ τοι τόδε ἔργον Ἀθηναίης ἀγελείης,
- OLD: Yet this is the work of Athena, the driver and giver of spoil there,
- NEW: Yet this is the work of Athena, the driver and giver of plunder,
- ἀγελείης appears nowhere else in the project's books, so the synonym swap
  breaks no formula.

**16.223** ἤγαγον εἰς Ἰθάκην; τίνες ἔμμεναι εὐχετόωντο;
- OLD: into Ithaca? Who did they claim that they were among men there?
- NEW: into Ithaca? Who did they claim to be, what manner of people?
- "what manner of people" replaces the old line's own filler "among men,"
  logged. (16.57–59 carries the same Greek question with different enjambment;
  that divergence predates this pass.)

**16.249** ἐκ δὲ Σάμης πίσυρές τε καὶ εἴκοσι φῶτες ἔασιν,
- OLD: out of Same there are four and twenty men in all there,
- NEW: and out of Same there are four and twenty men altogether,
- The opening "and" is δὲ. Mid-line "there are" kept, as in 247 and 250.

**16.262** τὸν δʼ αὖ Τηλέμαχος πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα·
- OLD: Then clear-headed Telemachus answered him face to face there:
- NEW: Then clear-headed Telemachus answered and spoke to him thuswise:
- This Greek line is the same speech-formula the book already renders at 112
  and 146; the repetition is now verbatim with them rather than a new variant.
  (30/68 and 240 carry yet other renderings of the same formula — a pre-existing
  inconsistency left for a separate pass.)

**16.280** πείσονται· δὴ γάρ σφι παρίσταται αἴσιμον ἦμαρ.
- OLD: will they obey you, for now their destined day is at hand there.
- NEW: will they obey you, for now their day of destiny nears them.
- σφι rendered literally as the closing pronoun.

**16.295** νῶϊν δʼ οἴοισιν δύο φάσγανα καὶ δύο δοῦρε
- OLD: But for us two alone leave two swords and likewise two spears there,
- NEW: But leave for the two of us only two swords, and two spears likewise,

**16.296** καλλιπέειν καὶ δοιὰ βοάγρια χερσὶν ἑλέσθαι,
- OLD: and two shields of oxhide for us to take up in our hands then,
- NEW: and a pair of oxhide shields as well, for our hands to lay hold of,
- δοιὰ = "a pair," καὶ = "as well," χερσὶν ἑλέσθαι = "for our hands to lay hold
  of" — nothing added; the preposition close has pilot precedent (21.53).

**16.318** ἀνδρῶν δʼ οὐκ ἂν ἐγώ γε κατὰ σταθμοὺς ἐθέλοιμι
- OLD: But the men on the farms I for my part should not wish that we test now,
- NEW: But the men at the farmsteads I for my part would not have us testing,
- σταθμούς is the poem's "farmstead" (16.66, 82); ἐγώ γε keeps its contrastive
  stress; "would not have us" = οὐκ ἂν ἐθέλοιμι with the old line's "we."

**16.335** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή ῥʼ ἵκοντο δόμον θείου βασιλῆος,
- OLD: But when they came to the house of the godlike king there,
- NEW: But when they came at last to where the godlike king was dwelling,
- "at last" carries ὅτε δή's force (as at 324, "When at last").

**16.341** βῆ ῥʼ ἴμεναι μεθʼ ὕας, λίπε δʼ ἕρκεά τε μέγαρόν τε.
- OLD: then he went on his way to the swine, and left court and hall there.
- NEW: then he went his way to the swine, leaving court and hall behind him.
- The draft's swap to "hall and court" bought nothing metrically, so the Greek
  order (ἕρκεά… μέγαρόν) stands; sentence-final period kept for 342's "Then."

**16.345** τοῖσιν δʼ Εὐρύμαχος, Πολύβου πάϊς, ἦρχʼ ἀγορεύειν·
- OLD: Then Eurymachus, son of Polybus, started to speak there:
- NEW: Then to them Eurymachus, son of Polybus, thus began speaking:
- τοῖσιν ("to them") restored.

**16.353** ἱστία τε στέλλοντας ἐρετμά τε χερσὶν ἔχοντας.
- OLD: as they were furling the sail and holding the oars in their hands there.
- NEW: as they were both furling the sail and holding the oars in their fingers,
- τε… τε gives "both… and" its sixth beat honestly; χερσὶν as "fingers" follows
  the pilot (21.75). Ends with a comma: the sentence runs on from 351–52.

**16.355** μή τινʼ ἔτʼ ἀγγελίην ὀτρύνομεν· οἵδε γὰρ ἔνδον.
- OLD: “Let us no longer press any message on others; they are here now.
- NEW: “Let us no longer press any message on others; they are here.
- ἔνδον warrants the close; "they are HERE" is the whole point of the speech, so
  the masculine ending earns its keep (licensed masculine #1).

**16.358** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, οἱ δʼ ἀνστάντες ἔβαν ἐπὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης,
- OLD: So he spoke, and they rose and went to the shore of the sea there.
- NEW: So he spoke; and rising up they made their way to the sea-shore.
- ἀνστάντες kept as the participle "rising up" — no "at once" needed.

**16.367** οὔ ποτʼ ἐπʼ ἠπείρου νύκτʼ ἄσαμεν, ἀλλʼ ἐνὶ πόντῳ
- OLD: never on shore did we spend the night, but out on the sea there
- NEW: never on shore did we spend the night, but out on the sea
- Licensed masculine #2: the shore/sea contrast is the sentence's whole point,
  the Greek line itself ends πόντῳ, and 368 ("sailing our swift ship onward…")
  takes the enjambment cleanly.

**16.397** ἡγεῖτο μνηστῆρσι, μάλιστα δὲ Πηνελοπείῃ
- OLD: led the suitors, and most of them all he was pleasing to her there,
- NEW: led the suitors, and far more than all of the rest he was pleasing
- The old line's redundant "to her" (398 opens "to Penelope") is gone.

**16.408** ἐλθόντες δὲ καθῖζον ἐπὶ ξεστοῖσι θρόνοισιν.
- OLD: and when they had entered they sat on the polished chairs there.
- NEW: and when they had entered, they sat themselves down on chairs well-polished.
- Postposed adjective; "sat themselves down" as at 344.

**16.414** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ μνηστῆρας ἀφίκετο δῖα γυναικῶν,
- OLD: But when among suitors the godlike queen of women had come there,
- NEW: But when among the suitors had come the godlike queen of women,
- Now closes on γυναικῶν, the Greek line's own last word.

**16.425** δῆμον ὑποδείσας; δὴ γὰρ κεχολώατο λίην,
- OLD: fearing the people? For bitterly angry indeed they had grown then,
- NEW: in fear of the people? For so bitterly angry were they growing,
- The question mark stays — it closes 424's "Do you not know…?", which the
  draft had left dangling. "so" renders λίην.

**16.442** ἡμετέρῳ, ἐπεὶ ἦ καὶ ἐμὲ πτολίπορθος Ὀδυσσεὺς
- OLD: that man's; for city-sacking Odysseus often before now
- NEW: that man’s; for in truth city-sacking Odysseus often
- ἦ = "in truth." The draft's "for me too" would have doubled 443's "sat me,"
  which already carries ἐμὲ; "often" (kept from the old line, not literal) keeps
  the habitual color of the reminiscence — logged. Apostrophe normalized to the
  poem's curly form.

**16.444** ἐν χείρεσσιν ἔθηκεν, ἐπέσχε τε οἶνον ἐρυθρόν.
- OLD: and held out red wine to me and let me drink from the cup there.
- NEW: and held out to me the ruddy wine for my drinking.
- The old line's invented "the cup" is gone; "ruddy" for ἐρυθρόν has project
  precedent (13.69). The draft's second "always" (duplicating its 442) dropped.

**16.453** ἤλυθεν· οἱ δʼ ἄρα δόρπον ἐπισταδὸν ὡπλίζοντο,
- OLD: and his son; and the two were making ready the evening meal there,
- NEW: and his son; and eagerly they were making the evening meal ready,
- ἐπισταδὸν rendered as "eagerly."

**16.462** ἦ ῥʼ ἤδη μνηστῆρες ἀγήνορες ἔνδον ἔασιν
- OLD: Are the proud suitors already inside the hall and returned now
- NEW: Are the proud suitors already at home, come back to the palace,
- ἤδη rendered once, not twice; ἔνδον = "at home."

**16.472** ἦα κιών, ὅτε νῆα θοὴν ἰδόμην κατιοῦσαν
- OLD: I was going along when I saw a swift ship making descent there
- NEW: I was walking along, when I saw a swift ship that was descending
- κατιοῦσαν as the participle; enjambs into 473 ("into our harbor…").

**16.476** ὣς φάτο, μείδησεν δʼ ἱερὴ ἲς Τηλεμάχοιο
- OLD: So he spoke, and the sacred strength of Telemachus smiled there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and there smiled the sacred strength of Telemachus,
- The inversion's "there" is a syntactic expletive ("there smiled…"), not the
  banned locative close; the name closes the line, and no "at once" is added.

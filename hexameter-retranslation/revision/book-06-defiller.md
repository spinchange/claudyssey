# Book 06 de-filler — all 12 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling
line; no line ends in *there/now/then* (no warranted keeper was needed in this
book — no target line's Greek carries the locative/temporal sense at the close);
the preferred fix is reordering onto a naturally trochaic word; 0 masculine
endings used (budget 3); ἀμφίπολος stays "serving woman(-en)" as everywhere else
in the book; ἔπειτα at 06.314 is kept as "then" but moved inside the clause.

Adjacent lines were never touched; joins were checked against 37/39, 43/46,
75/77, 99/101, 103/105, 114/116, 146/148, 166/168, 219/221, 313/315, 328/330.

---

**06.38** ζῶστρά τε καὶ πέπλους καὶ ῥήγεα σιγαλόεντα.
- OLD: also the shining blankets and covers that need to be washed there.
- NEW: also the shining blankets and coverlets needing a washing.
- Sashes and robes are already carried by line 37; "needing a washing" keeps
  the old line's errand-context without the dangling close.

**06.44** δεύεται οὔτε χιὼν ἐπιπίλναται, ἀλλὰ μάλʼ αἴθρη
- OLD: rain; neither snow draws near, but the clear upper sky without cloud there
- NEW: rain; neither snow draws near, but an utterly clear upper heaven
- Fixed as a couplet with 45, matching the Greek enjambment (αἴθρη the subject,
  πέπταται its verb across the line-break). "utterly" renders μάλα.

**06.45** πέπταται ἀνέφελος, λευκὴ δʼ ἐπιδέδρομεν αἴγλη·
- OLD: stretches around, and a radiant whiteness continually shines there.
- NEW: spreads out unclouded around, and a radiant whiteness keeps shining.
- ἀνέφελος now sits in 45 where the Greek puts it, not in 44.

**06.76** μήτηρ δʼ ἐν κίστῃ ἐτίθει μενοεικέʼ ἐδωδὴν
- OLD: Mother prepared and filled up a chest with the heartening food there,
- NEW: Mother packed in a chest the food that would gladden the spirit,
- μενοεικέʼ ἐδωδήν rendered periphrastically; "prepared and filled up" was
  itself padding for ἐτίθει.

**06.100** σφαίρῃ ταὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ἔπαιζον, ἀπὸ κρήδεμνα βαλοῦσαι·
- OLD: they cast off the veils from their heads and played with a ball there,
- NEW: casting the veils away from their heads, with a ball they were playing,
- Lowercase: the sentence continues from 99's "was put from their bodies,".
  The participle now mirrors βαλοῦσαι and supplies the feminine close.

**06.104** τερπομένη κάπροισι καὶ ὠκείῃς ἐλάφοισι·
- OLD: taking delight in the boars and the rapidly running red deer there;
- NEW: taking delight in the boars and the deer that are swiftly running;
- "red" dropped (not in ὠκείῃς ἐλάφοισι); "wild boars" was rejected to avoid
  colliding with 105's "nymphs of the wilds."

**06.115** σφαῖραν ἔπειτʼ ἔρριψε μετʼ ἀμφίπολον βασίλεια·
- OLD: Then at a serving woman the princess cast the round ball there;
- NEW: Then the princess flung the round ball at a serving woman;
- "serving woman" kept as the book's standing word for ἀμφίπολος (52, 80, 84);
  116's "missing the woman" echoes it as the Greek repeats ἀμφιπόλου. ἔρριψε
  ("hurled") is "flung."

**06.147** μή οἱ γοῦνα λαβόντι χολώσαιτο φρένα κούρη.
- OLD: lest the young maiden be angered at heart if he clasped at her knees there.
- NEW: lest, if he clasped at her knees, the maiden be angered in spirit.
- Fronting the clause keeps the line to clean dactyls; "young" dropped
  (κούρη is simply "maiden").

**06.167** δήν, ἐπεὶ οὔ πω τοῖον ἀνήλυθεν ἐκ δόρυ γαίης,
- OLD: since no such sapling had ever arisen before from the soil there,
- NEW: since no such sapling had ever before from the soil arisen,
- "ever before" together render οὔ πω ("never yet"); δήν is carried by 166's
  "a long time."

**06.220** χρίσομαι· ἦ γὰρ δηρὸν ἀπὸ χροός ἐστιν ἀλοιφή.
- OLD: oil has been far from my skin for a terribly long span of time now.
- NEW: oil has been long a stranger to all of the skin of my body.
- 219 already closes on "for truly" (ἦ γάρ), so the new line must not repeat
  it. "a stranger to" is a periphrasis for ἀπὸ χροός ἐστιν; "all of ... of my
  body" is the added-word residue (logged), expanding χροός for the cadence.

**06.314** ἐλπωρή τοι ἔπειτα φίλους τʼ ἰδέειν καὶ ἱκέσθαι
- OLD: there is a hope that your friends and your family still shall be seen there,
- NEW: there is a hope that then you shall see all your friends and your kinfolk,
- ἔπειτα kept as "then," moved off the close into the clause. ἱκέσθαι is left,
  as before, to 315's "entering into your well-built house." "all" is a minor
  added quantifier (logged).

**06.329** αὐτῷ δʼ οὔ πω φαίνετʼ ἐναντίη· αἴδετο γάρ ῥα
- OLD: Yet she did not appear to him face to face in the grove there;
- NEW: Still, she did not yet show herself face to face before him;
- The old "in the grove" is not in this Greek line (the grove stands at 322);
  οὔ πω regains its "not yet." αἴδετο γάρ ῥα is carried, as before, by 330's
  "awe of her father's own brother restrained her." Unstressed-pronoun close
  as at 21.129.

## Tally

- 12 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*.
- 0 masculine endings used (budget of 3 untouched).
- 0 warranted filler-class keepers needed (06.314's ἔπειτα survives as
  mid-line "then"; 06.115's ἔπειτʼ as the line-opening "Then").
- Fidelity recovered along the way: μάλα at 44, ἀνέφελος restored to 45's
  position, οὔ πω at 167 and 329, ἔρριψε sharpened at 115; over-specification
  cut: "red" at 104, "young" at 147, "in the grove" at 329.
- Honest costs incurred: "needing a washing" carried over at 38 (errand
  context, not in the Greek line itself); "all of ... of my body" at 220;
  "all" at 314. Total added-word residue is far below the filler removed.
- Not targets, left standing: the book's other line-final "here"s (153, 177,
  194, 203, 244) and mid-line locatives (e.g. 106 "play there beside her"),
  which the extraction did not flag.

## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**16.40** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας οἱ ἐδέξατο χάλκεον ἔγχος·
- OLD: So saying, he took from his hand the bronze-shining spearshaft;
- NEW: So having spoken, he took from his hand the bronze-shining spearshaft;
- "So saying" was the book's minority participial variant (4 instances poem-wide
  against 20 for "So having spoken"); standardized to the dominant form. Same Greek
  verse as 15.282, which now matches.

**16.154** ἦ ῥα καὶ ὦρσε συφορβόν· ὁ δʼ εἵλετο χερσὶ πέδιλα,
- OLD: So he spoke and stirred up the swineherd; and into his hands he was taking
- NEW: He spoke, and he roused up the swineherd; and into his hands he was taking
- ἦ ῥα is not ὣς φάτο. Recast to the bare "He spoke," matching the same correction
  at 15.539, so the ruled formula is reserved for the ὣς φάτο family alone.

**16.172** ἦ καὶ χρυσείῃ ῥάβδῳ ἐπεμάσσατʼ Ἀθήνη.
- OLD: So she spoke, and Athena then touched him there with her golden wand.
- NEW: She spoke, and Athena reached out and touched him with her golden wand.
- ἦ καί, again not the formula. The recast also retires a line-medial filler
  "then … there" and gives ἐπεμάσσατο ("laid on, touched upon") the reaching
  gesture the bare "touched" had lost.

**16.190** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας υἱὸν κύσε, κὰδ δὲ παρειῶν
- OLD: So he spoke, and he kissed his son; and down from his cheeks, earthward,
- NEW: So having spoken, he kissed his son; and down from his cheeks, earthward,

**16.213** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετο, Τηλέμαχος δὲ
- OLD: So he spoke, and he sat him down; and Telemachus, flinging his arms round
- NEW: So having spoken, he sat down; and Telemachus, flinging his arms round
- "sat him down" → "sat down": the reflexive was padding, and cutting it pays for
  the longer participial opening exactly.

**16.345** τοῖσιν δʼ Εὐρύμαχος, Πολύβου πάϊς, ἦρχʼ ἀγορεύειν·
- OLD: Among them Eurymachus, son of Polybus, thus began speaking:
- NEW: Among them Eurymachus, son of Polybus, first began speaking:
- No ὣς in the Greek at all; the *thus* was imported. ἦρχ᾿ ἀγορεύειν is "began
  (first) to speak" — "first" renders the ἄρχω that "thus" had been squatting on.

**16.406** ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀμφίνομος, τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιήνδανε μῦθος.
- OLD: So spoke Amphinomus, and his counsel was pleasing among them.
- NEW: So he spoke, Amphinomus, and his counsel was pleasing among them.
- Finite ὣς ἔφατ᾿ with a named subject. The ruling requires the opening be the
  formula verbatim, so the inverted "So spoke NAME" yields to the poem's other
  established shape for this exact case — "So he spoke, NAME," — which is already
  used at 1.42, 3.420, 5.146, 6.296, 8.16.

**16.219** — *examined, left unchanged*: ὣς ἄρα τοί γ᾿ … "So from beneath their
brows…" answers the simile at 216–218 ("more ceaselessly even than wild birds…").
This is correlative ὥς ("even so"), not a speech formula; its "So" is correct.

**16.321** — *already correct*: "So in such words they spoke and addressed one
another together." 15.493 has now been brought into line with it.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**16.9** ἢ καὶ γνώριμος ἄλλος, ἐπεὶ κύνες οὐχ ὑλάουσιν,
- OLD: or some other familiar man, since the dogs do not bark here,
- NEW: or some other familiar man, since the dogs are not barking,
- No deictic. The Greek present ὑλάουσιν is progressive in force — the dogs are
  not barking *at this moment* — so the progressive supplies the trochaic close
  that "here" was faking.

**16.128** οἶκον ἐμόν· τάχα δή με διαρραίσουσι καὶ αὐτόν.
- OLD: Soon enough they will rend me apart and destroy me as well here.
- NEW: Soon enough they will rend me apart and will utterly destroy me.
- No deictic. καὶ αὐτόν is "me myself as well" — intensive, not locative; "utterly"
  carries the intensity to a trochaic close.

**16.254** τῶν εἴ κεν πάντων ἀντήσομεν ἔνδον ἐόντων,
- OLD: If we two should encounter all these while they are inside here,
- NEW: If we two should encounter them all while they are within doors,
- ἔνδον is "indoors," not "here"; "within doors" renders it and closes trochaically.

**16.268** φυλόπιδος κρατερῆς, ὁπότε μνηστῆρσι καὶ ἡμῖν
- OLD: of the strong battle, whenever among the suitors and us here
- NEW: of the strong battle, whenever between the suitors and us two
- No deictic. ἡμῖν here is the father-and-son pair, and the dual is the whole point
  of the speech; "us two" restores it and ends the line honestly.

**16.300** εἰ ἐτεόν γʼ ἐμός ἐσσι καὶ αἵματος ἡμετέροιο,
- OLD: if truly you are my son and are sprung from the blood that is ours here,
- NEW: if truly you are my son and are sprung of the blood of our kindred,
- No deictic; "that is ours here" was a five-syllable evasion of the genitive.

**16.301** μή τις ἔπειτʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἀκουσάτω ἔνδον ἐόντος,
- OLD: let no one thereafter hear that Odysseus is present indoors here,
- NEW: let no one thereafter hear that Odysseus is within the dwelling,
- ἔνδον again; "present indoors here" was triple-rendering one word.

**16.355** μή τινʼ ἔτʼ ἀγγελίην ὀτρύνομεν· οἵδε γὰρ ἔνδον.
- OLD: “Let us no longer press any message on others; they are here.
- NEW: “Let us send out no further message; these men are within doors.
- ἔνδον, third instance — now rendered "within doors" consistently with 254 and 301.
  οἵδε ("these men") is the demonstrative, and it survives; the locative does not
  need to be "here."

**16.439** ζώοντός γʼ ἐμέθεν καὶ ἐπὶ χθονὶ δερκομένοιο.
- OLD: while I am still alive and looking upon the light of the earth here.
- NEW: while I am living yet and upon the earth still am gazing.
- No deictic. The old line also mistranslated: ἐπὶ χθονὶ δερκομένοιο is "seeing
  *upon* the earth" (i.e. still above ground and with sight), not "looking upon
  the light *of* the earth." The fix restores the sense and lands on a trochee.

**16.181** ἀλλοῖός μοι, ξεῖνε, φάνης νέον ἠὲ πάροιθεν,
- OLD: “Stranger, to me you appear quite changed from the man you were just now.
- NEW: “Stranger, you seem to me changed from the man that you were aforetime.
- Caught by the same sweep as a *now*-final line. πάροιθεν is the contrast word —
  "before, formerly" — so "aforetime" renders what the Greek actually puts at the
  line's end, and the filler "quite" goes with it.

  Consequential edit, **16.180** καί μιν φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
  - OLD: and lifting his voice he addressed him in winged words and spoke thus:
  - NEW: and lifting his voice he addressed him in winged words, saying:
  - The old close "and spoke thus" doubled φωνήσας, which "lifting his voice"
    already renders. Normalized to the poem's commonest form of this very common
    formula ("winged words, saying:", 12 instances).

**16.132** — *reworked, deictic kept*
αὐτὰρ ἐγὼν αὐτοῦ μενέω, σὺ δὲ δεῦρο νέεσθαι,
- OLD: I myself will remain here; but you must come back to this place here
- NEW: I myself will remain in this place; but you must come back here
- **δεῦρο is genuine and is kept**, but the old line spent "here" twice — once for
  αὐτοῦ ("in that same spot") and once for δεῦρο. Swapping them puts each Greek
  word on its own English word: αὐτοῦ → "in this place," δεῦρο → the line-final
  "here" it actually licenses.

## Job 3 — residual *Thus*

Both remaining instances are mid-line and genuinely mean "in this way"; both kept
and counted as justified.

- **16.64** ὣς γάρ οἱ ἐπέκλωσαν θεοὶ αἶσαν — "for thus has some god spun the thread
  of it for him." Manner, not connective.
- **16.440** ὧδε γὰρ ἐξερέω — "For thus I will speak." ὧδε is explicitly "in this
  way / as follows," pointing forward to the oath that follows.

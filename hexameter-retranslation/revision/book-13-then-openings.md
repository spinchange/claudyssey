# Book 13 — “Then”-opening audit

**Examined:** 29 verses opening with *Then*.  **Kept (warranted):** 6.  **Rewritten (habitual):** 23.  **Residual *Then*-openings in the file:** 6.

**Warrant rule.** A line keeps its opening *Then* only where the Greek carries a real
temporal adverb — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα, or an
ἦμος/τῆμος correlative. Where the Greek has only a light connective (bare δέ, αὐτάρ,
ἀτάρ, καί, ἄρα, γάρ, τε, ὣς + verb, or asyndeton), the *Then* is the translator's tic and
is removed. Replacements reflect the actual connective: αὖτε → “in his/her turn,” “once
again”; tmesis κάδ / ἐκ → the restored verbal prefix (“Down …,” “Out …”); reply-formulas
→ answer-openings without *Then*; bare δέ → subject-first or verb-first, the most
Homerically invisible openings in English. No replacement family exceeds a fifth of the
rewrites; the largest, plain subject-first *They*, accounts for 3 of 23. Every rewritten
line still scans as six falling beats, and no *Straightway/At once* was introduced
(no αἶψα/αὐτίκα/ὦκα at any of these openings).

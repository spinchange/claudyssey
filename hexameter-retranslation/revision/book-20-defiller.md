# Book 20 de-filler — all 42 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek itself carries the
   sense at that position (one case: 20.135, νῦν).
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, names, plurals, compounds), not adding new words.
4. Masculine endings used twice, where the Greek's own line-final word earns the
   stress: 20.252 (οἶνον) and 20.302 (τοῖχον).
5. Formula: 20.172 = 20.240 (ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον); 172 now
   repeats 240's existing English verbatim.

**20.22** ὣς ἔφατʼ, ἐν στήθεσσι καθαπτόμενος φίλον ἦτορ·
- OLD: So he spoke, as there in his breast he upbraided his own dear heart there;
- NEW: So he spoke, in his breast his own dear heart rebuking;

**20.32** στῆ δʼ ἄρʼ ὑπὲρ κεφαλῆς καί μιν πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπε·
- OLD: Then she stood overhead by his head and addressed him in speech there:
- NEW: Then over his head she stood, and she spoke to him, saying:

**20.40** μοῦνος ἐών· οἱ δʼ αἰὲν ἀολλέες ἔνδον ἔασι.
- OLD: being alone by myself; and they are forever assembled within there.
- NEW: being alone by myself, while indoors they are forever assembled.

**20.51** καί κεν τῶν ἐλάσαιο βόας καὶ ἴφια μῆλα.
- OLD: still from them you would drive off cattle and sturdy fat sheep there.
- NEW: still you would drive off from them their cattle and sturdy fat sheep-flocks.

**20.53** πάννυχον ἐγρήσσοντα, κακῶν δʼ ὑποδύσεαι ἤδη.
- OLD: all through the night without rest, and from evils you soon will emerge now.”
- NEW: wakeful the whole night through; but soon from your evils you shall be emerging.”
- ἐγρήσσοντα ("staying awake") restored; Athena's voice keeps the uncontracted forms.

**20.81** ὀσσομένη καὶ γαῖαν ὕπο στυγερὴν ἀφικοίμην,
- OLD: seeing him, I might even go down under the hateful earth there,
- NEW: seeing him, I might even go down beneath the earth that is hateful,
- καί ("even") retained — the drafter had dropped it.

**20.91** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτίκα δὲ χρυσόθρονος ἤλυθεν Ἠώς.
- OLD: So she spoke, and at once came Dawn on her golden high throne there.
- NEW: So she spoke, and at once came Dawn on her throne that was golden.
- ἤλυθεν = "came," kept over the drafter's "appeared."

**20.94** ἤδη γιγνώσκουσα παρεστάμεναι κεφαλῆφι.
- OLD: that she, knowing him now, had come and stood close over his head there.
- NEW: that already knowing him, close to his head she had come and was standing.

**20.125** εἵματα ἑσσάμενος· περὶ δὲ ξίφος ὀξὺ θέτʼ ὤμῳ·
- OLD: put on his clothes, and about his shoulder he slung his sharp sword there;
- NEW: put on his clothes, and slung his sharp sword about his shoulder;

**20.127** εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος, ἀκαχμένον ὀξέι· χαλκῷ·
- OLD: took up a mighty spear with its point made keen by sharp bronze there,
- NEW: took up a mighty spear, its point with keen bronze sharpened,

**20.135** οὐκ ἄν μιν νῦν, τέκνον, ἀναίτιον αἰτιόῳο.
- OLD: “Child, do not now accuse the guiltless woman without a cause there.
- NEW: “Child, do not blame the guiltless woman without cause now.
- The one retained temporal close in this book: νῦν is in the Greek. "Without
  cause" keeps the ἀναίτιον/αἰτιόῳο wordplay; final foot "cause now" is trochaic.

**20.163** τρεῖς σιάλους κατάγων, οἳ ἔσαν μετὰ πᾶσιν ἄριστοι.
- OLD: came driving three fat hogs, which were the best of them all there.
- NEW: came driving three fat hogs, which of all the herd were the finest.
- "Herd" supplied as the understood referent of μετὰ πᾶσιν.

**20.167** ἦέ σʼ ἀτιμάζουσι κατὰ μέγαρʼ, ὡς τὸ πάρος περ;
- OLD: or do they still disgrace you all through the halls, as before now?”
- NEW: or, just as before, do they still hold you through the halls in dishonor?”

**20.172** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: So they were speaking such things to each other in turn there;
- NEW: Thus they were speaking such things in turn to one another;
- Verbatim with 20.240, whose Greek is the identical line; the English now
  repeats where Homer repeats.

**20.173** ἀγχίμολον δέ σφʼ ἦλθε Μελάνθιος, αἰπόλος αἰγῶν.
- OLD: then Melanthius came near to them, the goatherd of goats there,
- NEW: then there came near to them the goatherd of goats, Melanthius,
- Reordered to close on the name; "there came" is existential, not locative padding.

**20.185** τοῖσι δʼ ἐπὶ τρίτος ἦλθε Φιλοίτιος, ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν,
- OLD: Then there came as the third one Philoetius, leader of men there,
- NEW: Then as the third one after them, leader of men, came Philoetius,

**20.187** πορθμῆες δʼ ἄρα τούς γε διήγαγον, οἵ τε καὶ ἄλλους
- OLD: Ferrymen brought them across, the same men who also convey there
- NEW: Ferrymen brought them across, the very same men who also carry
- "Very same" renders the γε emphasis; "carry" closes trochaic and 188's
  "all other travelers too" remains its object.

**20.221** βουσὶν ἐπʼ ἀλλοτρίῃσι καθήμενον ἄλγεα πάσχειν.
- OLD: here to endure the grief while one sits by cattle not one's own now.
- NEW: here to sit by the cattle of others, suffering sorrows.
- 220 already ends "staying," so the drafter's "here to remain" would have
  doubled it; καθήμενον ("sitting") restored, ἄλγεα plural as "sorrows."

**20.231** ἱστίη τʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἀμύμονος, ἣν ἀφικάνω,
- OLD: and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come now,
- NEW: and the hearth of noble Odysseus, whereto I have made my coming,
- The drafter's "and now I have come to the hearth…" broke the oath's appositive
  chain (Zeus — guest-table — hearth); the relative clause is restored. "Made my
  coming" is a periphrasis for ἀφικάνω, the line's one licensed expansion.

**20.233** σοῖσιν δʼ ὀφθαλμοῖσιν ἐπόψεαι, αἴ κʼ ἐθέλῃσθα,
- OLD: With your own eyes you will see it, if that is indeed your desire there:
- NEW: With your own eyes you shall see it, if only you are willing:
- "Only" is a light intensifier for the αἴ κε wish-construction.

**20.242** ἤρτυον· αὐτὰρ ὁ τοῖσιν ἀριστερὸς ἤλυθεν ὄρνις,
- OLD: but to them on the left there came as a sign a great bird then,
- NEW: but to them on the left-hand side there came a bird-omen,
- "Great" (not in the Greek) dropped. ὄρνις in this position is precisely the
  omen-bird, so the compound "bird-omen" translates the word's own force rather
  than adding a gloss; ἀριστερός = the ill-boding left.

**20.243** αἰετὸς ὑψιπέτης, ἔχε δὲ τρήρωνα πέλειαν.
- OLD: an eagle high in flight, and he held in his talons a dove there.
- NEW: an eagle high in flight, and he held a dove that was trembling.
- "In his talons" (not in ἔχε) dropped; τρήρωνα, from τρέω, rendered "trembling."

**20.249** χλαίνας μὲν κατέθεντο κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε,
- OLD: and they laid down their cloaks on the benches and chairs in the hall there.
- NEW: and they laid their cloaks down across the chairs and the benches.
- "In the hall" (not in this Greek line) dropped; 248 ends with a comma, so the
  line keeps its lower-case continuation.

**20.251** ἵρευον δὲ σύας σιάλους καὶ βοῦν ἀγελαίην·
- OLD: slaughtered as well sleek swine and a heifer brought from the herd there.
- NEW: slaughtered as well sleek swine, and out of the herd a heifer.

**20.252** σπλάγχνα δʼ ἄρʼ ὀπτήσαντες ἐνώμων, ἐν δέ τε οἶνον
- OLD: Then they roasted the innards and shared them about, and the wine then
- NEW: Then they roasted the innards and shared them about, and the wine
- Licensed masculine ending #1: οἶνον is the Greek's own line-final word,
  enjambing into 253's mixing-bowls; the join is kept intact.

**20.255** καλοῖς ἐν κανέοισιν, ἐῳνοχόει δὲ Μελανθεύς.
- OLD: set in beautiful baskets, and Melanthius poured the wine there.
- NEW: set in beautiful baskets; and pouring the wine was Melanthius.
- Inverted-active keeps ἐῳνοχόει's imperfect ("was pouring") and closes on the name.

**20.260** πὰρ δʼ ἐτίθει σπλάγχνων μοίρας, ἐν δʼ οἶνον ἔχευεν
- OLD: By him he set portions of innards and poured out the wine then
- NEW: By him he set portions of innards, and wine he was pouring
- ἔχευεν enjambs into 261's golden cup, as in the Greek.

**20.278** ἄλσος ὕπο σκιερὸν ἑκατηβόλου Ἀπόλλωνος.
- OLD: under the shady grove of Apollo who strikes from afar there.
- NEW: under the shady grove of him who strikes from afar, Apollo.
- The drafter's "the archer who strikes from afar" rendered ἑκατηβόλου twice;
  once is enough, and the name takes the close.

**20.279** οἱ δʼ ἐπεὶ ὤπτησαν κρέʼ ὑπέρτερα καὶ ἐρύσαντο,
- OLD: Then when they had roasted the outer flesh and drawn it away there,
- NEW: Then, when they had drawn away the outer flesh they had roasted,

**20.281** πὰρ δʼ ἄρʼ Ὀδυσσῆϊ μοῖραν θέσαν οἳ πονέοντο
- OLD: And a portion beside Odysseus the servants set down there,
- NEW: And beside Odysseus a portion was set by those who were serving,
- οἳ πονέοντο = "those who were laboring/serving," now rendered as its own clause.

**20.284** μνηστῆρας δʼ οὐ πάμπαν ἀγήνορας εἴα Ἀθήνη
- OLD: Yet Athena did not let the haughty suitors at all then
- NEW: Yet Athena did not at all let the haughty suitors

**20.290** μνάσκετʼ Ὀδυσσῆος δὴν οἰχομένοιο δάμαρτα.
- OLD: wooed the wife of Odysseus, though long he had been gone from home there.
- NEW: wooed the wife of Odysseus, who had for so long been departed.
- "From home" (not in οἰχομένοιο) dropped.

**20.301** ἦκα παρακλίνας κεφαλήν, μείδησε δὲ θυμῷ
- OLD: dodged it by bending his head a little aside, and he smiled then
- NEW: dodged it by bending his head a little aside, and was smiling
- The drafter's added "just" was new padding; ἦκα is already "a little."

**20.302** σαρδάνιον μάλα τοῖον· ὁ δʼ εὔδμητον βάλε τοῖχον.
- OLD: inwardly with a sardonic smile; and it struck the strong wall there.
- NEW: inwardly, a smile most sardonic; and it struck the strong wall.
- Licensed masculine ending #2: τοῖχον is the Greek's own line-final word, and
  the blow lands on it. "Most sardonic" carries μάλα τοῖον, which the old line lost.

**20.316** καί κε τὸ βουλοίμην, καί κεν πολὺ κέρδιον εἴη
- OLD: even that I would choose, and it would be far better for me then
- NEW: even that I would choose, and by far it would be the better,
- πολύ ("by far") retained — the drafter's "surely" had replaced it. "For me,"
  not lexically present, is dropped.

**20.319** ῥυστάζοντας ἀεικελίως κατὰ δώματα καλά.
- OLD: dragged in disgrace through the beautiful rooms of the house there.”
- NEW: dragged about shamefully all through the beautiful chambers.”
- ῥυστάζοντας is frequentative ("dragged about"); ἀεικελίως = "shamefully";
  δώματα καλά needs no separate house-word.

**20.335** γήμασθʼ ὅς τις ἄριστος ἀνὴρ καὶ πλεῖστα πόρῃσιν,
- OLD: let her wed whatever man is best and offers the most there,
- NEW: let her wed the noblest man there is, and the greatest giver,

**20.343** αἰδέομαι δʼ ἀέκουσαν ἀπὸ μεγάροιο δίεσθαι
- OLD: But I am ashamed to send her away from the hall against her will there
- NEW: But I am ashamed to be driving her out of the hall unwilling
- δίεσθαι = "to drive off"; ἀέκουσαν ("her unwilling") sits in epic object-
  predicate position, and 344's "by a compelling word" still attaches to the verb.

**20.348** αἱμοφόρυκτα δὲ δὴ κρέα ἤσθιον· ὄσσε δʼ ἄρα σφέων
- OLD: and they were eating flesh that was smeared all over with blood there;
- NEW: and blood-defiled indeed was the flesh that they now were eating;
- αἱμοφόρυκτα kept as a compound in the poem's idiom; "indeed … now" together
  render δὴ (emphatic "by now") with the imperfect.

**20.354** αἵματι δʼ ἐρράδαται τοῖχοι καλαί τε μεσόδμαι·
- OLD: walls are bespattered with blood, and the beautiful beams in the house there.
- NEW: and with blood are the walls all bespattered, and the roof-beams so beautiful.
- "In the house" (not in the Greek — μεσόδμαι are the crossbeams themselves)
  dropped; "all" carries the perfect ἐρράδαται.

**20.387** ἡ δὲ κατʼ ἄντηστιν θεμένη περικαλλέα δίφρον
- OLD: Meanwhile she, in the facing place, had set down her beautiful chair there,
- NEW: Meanwhile she, in the facing place, had set down her chair of great beauty,
- The drafter's version ran to seven beats; "of great beauty" renders the
  περι- intensive and restores six.

**20.389** ἀνδρῶν ἐν μεγάροισιν ἑκάστου μῦθον ἄκουεν.
- OLD: and she heard in the hall the speech of each one of the men there.
- NEW: and to the words of each one of the men in the hall she was listening.

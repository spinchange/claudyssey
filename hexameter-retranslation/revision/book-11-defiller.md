# Book 11 de-filler — all 49 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek itself carries the
   locative/temporal sense at that position (one case: 11.372, αὐτοῦ "on the spot,"
   kept unchanged).
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last,
   not adding new words.
4. Masculine endings budgeted at 3 per book. Used three times: 11.120 (bronze),
   11.224 (wife), 11.265 (Thebes).
5. Formulas: the seven ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον lines
   (79, 138, 163, 435, 462, 477, 504) share one verbatim frame, with only the
   referent slot varying (spirit / prophet / my mother) as the old translation
   established for μιν; likewise 347 = 362 (Alcinous) and 354 = 377 (Odysseus,
   fully verbatim). 11.114–115 now echo the already-fixed 9.534–535 — Tiresias
   repeating Polyphemus’ curse — with only the person shifted.

Adjacent lines are edited only where a fix forced it (one case: 11.497).

**11.20** νῆα μὲν ἔνθʼ ἐλθόντες ἐκέλσαμεν, ἐκ δὲ τὰ μῆλα
- OLD: Coming ashore, we beached the vessel and unloaded the sheep there;
- NEW: Coming ashore, we beached the vessel and unloaded the sheep-flocks;
- "sheep-flocks" is the poem’s own compound for μῆλα (11.108 "sturdy sheep-flocks").

**11.24** ἔσχον· ἐγὼ δʼ ἄορ ὀξὺ ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ
- OLD: Drawing the sharp-edged sword that hung from beside my strong thigh there,
- NEW: Drawing the sharp-edged sword that beside my strong thigh was hanging,

**11.48** αὐτὸς δὲ ξίφος ὀξὺ ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ
- OLD: Drawing for myself the sharp-edged sword from beside my strong thigh there,
- NEW: Drawing for myself the sharp-edged sword at my strong thigh hanging,
- "for myself" keeps αὐτὸς δέ; parallel to 11.24 but not identical, as the Greek
  verbs differ (ἄορ/ξίφος).

**11.74** ἀλλά με κακκῆαι σὺν τεύχεσιν, ἅσσα μοι ἔστιν,
- OLD: Burn me together with all of the armor that still is my own there;
- NEW: Burn me together with all of the armor that is my possession;

**11.79** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον·
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and I answered the spirit and said in response then:
- NEW: Thus did he speak, and in answer I spoke to the spirit, responding:

**11.88** ἀλλʼ οὐδʼ ὣς εἴων προτέρην, πυκινόν περ ἀχεύων,
- OLD: yet, though mourning her bitterly, I did not allow her to come there
- NEW: yet, though mourning her bitterly, I did not let her come forward
- προτέρην ("going first") lands as "forward"; 11.89 still carries "near to the blood."

**11.114** ὀψὲ κακῶς νεῖαι, ὀλέσας ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους,
- OLD: late will you come and in evil condition, with all comrades lost there,
- NEW: late will you come and in evil condition, with all comrades perished,
- Matches 9.534 ("late may he come and in evil condition, with all comrades
  perished") — this is Polyphemus’ curse fulfilled, person shifted.

**11.115** νηὸς ἐπʼ ἀλλοτρίης· δήεις δʼ ἐν πήματα οἴκῳ,
- OLD: borne in another man’s vessel, and finding affliction at home there:
- NEW: there on another man’s ship, and shall find in your house affliction:
- Matches 9.535 ("there on another man’s ship, and find in his house affliction");
  "there" is now line-initial, as in Book 9.

**11.120** κτείνῃς ἠὲ δόλῳ ἢ ἀμφαδὸν ὀξέι χαλκῷ,
- OLD: whether by cunning or openly striking with sharp-edged bronze there,
- NEW: whether by cunning you kill them, or openly, with sharp-edged bronze,
- Licensed masculine ending #1: bronze is the emphatic instrument of the foretold
  vengeance. "you kill them" is κτείνῃς, which stands in this Greek line.

**11.123** ἀνέρες, οὐδέ θʼ ἅλεσσι μεμιγμένον εἶδαρ ἔδουσιν·
- OLD: people who never eat food with the salt of the sea intermixed there,
- NEW: people who never eat food with the salt of the sea intermingled,

**11.127** ὁππότε κεν δή τοι συμβλήμενος ἄλλος ὁδίτης
- OLD: When another traveler meets you abroad on the road there,
- NEW: When on the road another wayfarer happens to meet you,

**11.138** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον·

**11.151** Τειρεσίαο ἄνακτος, ἐπεὶ κατὰ θέσφατʼ ἔλεξεν·
- OLD: into the house of Hades, when all of the fates were revealed there.
- NEW: into the house of Hades, when all of the fates had been spoken.
- ἔλεξεν: the fates are spoken, not merely revealed.

**11.163** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον·

**11.191** ἐν κόνι ἄγχι πυρός, κακὰ δὲ χροῒ εἵματα εἷται·
- OLD: close to the fire in the ashes, and dresses his body in rags there;
- NEW: close to the fire in the ashes, his body in wretched garments;
- χροΐ ("on his flesh") kept as "his body"; verbless clause after "he sleeps" (190).

**11.194** φύλλων κεκλιμένων χθαμαλαὶ βεβλήαται εὐναί.
- OLD: beds of the fallen leaves are scattered along on the earth there.
- NEW: low to the earth are his beds, all strewn with the leaves that have fallen.
- χθαμαλαί restored to "low"; "to the earth" rather than "to the ground," which
  11.193 already carries ("rising ground").

**11.199** οἷς ἀγανοῖς βελέεσσιν ἐποιχομένη κατέπεφνεν,
- OLD: pouring her gentle arrows upon me and taking my life there,
- NEW: coming upon me and slaying me with her gentle arrows,
- ἐποιχομένη κατέπεφνεν rendered in Greek order: visiting, then slaying.

**11.223** ἀλλὰ φόωσδε τάχιστα λιλαίεο· ταῦτα δὲ πάντα
- OLD: Hasten toward light, however, as quickly as ever you can now;
- NEW: Hasten, however, toward light as quickly as ever you are able;

**11.224** ἴσθʼ, ἵνα καὶ μετόπισθε τεῇ εἴπῃσθα γυναικί.
- OLD: mark these things, so you may hereafter report them all to your wife there.”
- NEW: mark these things, that hereafter you may report them all to your wife.”
- Licensed masculine ending #2: the final word of Anticleia’s whole speech to her
  son, as γυναικί closes the Greek line. "them all" keeps 223’s ταῦτα δὲ πάντα.

**11.231** σπασσάμενος τανύηκες ἄορ παχέος παρὰ μηροῦ
- OLD: drawing the long-edged sword that hung from beside my strong thigh there,
- NEW: drawing the long-edged sword that beside my strong thigh was hanging,
- παχέος ("stout") warrants "strong" here, unlike 24/48 where it is inherited.

**11.263** οἳ πρῶτοι Θήβης ἕδος ἔκτισαν ἑπταπύλοιο,
- OLD: first to establish the site of the seven-gated city of Thebes there,
- NEW: first to establish the site of Thebes, the seven-gated city,

**11.265** ναιέμεν εὐρύχορον Θήβην, κρατερώ περ ἐόντε.
- OLD: powerful though they were, in the broad-dancing spaces of Thebes there.
- NEW: powerful though they both were, to inhabit broad-dancing Thebes.
- Licensed masculine ending #3: Thebes, a monosyllabic proper name, is the emphatic
  object of the whole sentence (263–265); "both" carries the dual ἐόντε.

**11.295** ἂψ περιτελλομένου ἔτεος καὶ ἐπήλυθον ὧραι,
- OLD: after the year turned round and the seasons again had returned there,
- NEW: after the year had turned round again, and the seasons came onward,
- ἂψ ("back/again") moved to the year’s revolution, where the Greek has it;
  ἐπήλυθον is "came on," not "returned."

**11.303** ἄλλοτε μὲν ζώουσʼ ἑτερήμεροι, ἄλλοτε δʼ αὖτε
- OLD: living in turn every other day and then dying in turn there,
- NEW: living in turn, every other day, then again in turn dying,
- "in turn ... then again in turn" mirrors ἄλλοτε μὲν ... ἄλλοτε δʼ αὖτε.

**11.347** τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἀλκίνοος ἀπαμείβετο φώνησέν τε·
- OLD: Then Alcinous answered the elder and said in response there:
- NEW: Then Alcinous made his reply to the elder, responding:

**11.354** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς·
- OLD: Then resourceful Odysseus addressed him in words of response there:
- NEW: Then resourceful Odysseus made his response to him, replying:
- Verbatim with 11.377, per the identical Greek formula.

**11.362** τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἀλκίνοος ἀπαμείβετο φώνησέν τε·
- OLD: Then Alcinous answered the hero and said in response there:
- NEW: Then Alcinous made his reply to the hero, responding:
- Frame verbatim with 11.347; the referent slot (elder/hero) follows the old

**11.372** Ἴλιον εἰς ἅμʼ ἕποντο καὶ αὐτοῦ πότμον ἐπέσπον.

**11.411** δειπνίσσας, ὥς τίς τε κατέκτανε βοῦν ἐπὶ φάτνῃ.

**11.413** νωλεμέως κτείνοντο σύες ὣς ἀργιόδοντες,

**11.424** βάλλον ἀποθνήσκων περὶ φασγάνῳ· ἡ δὲ κυνῶπις

**11.496** ἦ μιν ἀτιμάζουσιν ἀνʼ Ἑλλάδα τε Φθίην τε,
- OLD: or is dishonored throughout both Hellas and Phthia because now
- NEW: or is he held in dishonor throughout both Hellas and Phthia,
- "both" keeps τε ... τε; the displaced οὕνεκα moves to 497.

**11.497** οὕνεκά μιν κατὰ γῆρας ἔχει χεῖράς τε πόδας τε
- OLD: terrible old age captures his hands and his feet and confines him.
- NEW: now that old age has captured his hands and his feet and confines him.
- Adjacent verse, edited per rule 7: takes the causal οὕνεκα ("now that") displaced
  from 496. "terrible" is dropped — it has no warrant in the Greek, and the line
  cannot hold both it and the conjunction in six beats.

**11.502** τῷ κέ τεῳ στύξαιμι μένος καὶ χεῖρας ἀάπτους,
- OLD: such as I was, I would make my rage and unconquerable hands there
- NEW: such as I was, then my rage and unconquerable hands I would render
- ἀάπτους stays attributive on "hands"; "render / dreadful" (with 503) carries
  στύξαιμι. "then" is τῷ κε.

**11.504** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον·

**11.513** αὐτὰρ ὅτʼ ἐν πεδίῳ Τρώων μαρναίμεθα χαλκῷ,
- OLD: Whenever we fought on the plain of the Trojans with bronze in our hands there,
- NEW: Yet whenever we fought with our bronze on the plain of the Trojans,
- αὐτάρ restored as "Yet" (the old line had dropped it); "in our hands" was padding.

**11.583** ἑστεῶτʼ ἐν λίμνῃ· ἡ δὲ προσέπλαζε γενείῳ·
- OLD: standing in water, the pool lapping up to the point of his chin there;
- NEW: standing in water; up to his chin the pool kept on lapping;
- The participle keeps ἑστεῶτα in agreement with 582’s "Tantalus... I saw";
  "kept on lapping" renders the imperfect προσέπλαζε.

**11.600** ἔρρεεν ἐκ μελέων, κονίη δʼ ἐκ κρατὸς ὀρώρει.
- OLD: streamed, and the dust from the labor arose from his head in a cloud there.
- NEW: streamed, and up from the crown of his head the dust was arising.
- ἐκ μελέων is already carried by 599’s "from his members"; "from the labor" and
  "in a cloud" were padding; κρατός is the crown of the head.

**11.609** σμερδαλέος δέ οἱ ἀμφὶ περὶ στήθεσσιν ἀορτὴρ
- OLD: Dreadful to see was the baldric that crossed the great breadth of his chest there,
- NEW: Terrible round him, across his great chest, hung the baldric,
- ἀμφὶ περί doubled as "round him, across."

**11.628** αὐτὰρ ἐγὼν αὐτοῦ μένον ἔμπεδον, εἴ τις ἔτʼ ἔλθοι
- OLD: Yet I remained where I stood in the hope some other would come there,
- NEW: Yet I remained there steadfast, in hope some other was coming,
- αὐτοῦ kept mid-line as "there"; ἔμπεδον ("steadfast"), dropped by the old line,
  is restored. "some other" still apposes 629’s "one of the hero men."

**11.637** αὐτούς τʼ ἀμβαίνειν ἀνά τε πρυμνήσια λῦσαι.
- OLD: all to embark and release every cable that fastened the stern there.
- NEW: all to embark, and to loose from their moorings the stern-cables.
- "from their moorings" unpacks the ἀνά of ἀνά ... λῦσαι ("loose off").

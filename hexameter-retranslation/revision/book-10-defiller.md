# Book 10 de-filler — all 34 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied (as in the Book 21 pilot):

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek carries the sense at
   that position (no such case survived to line-end in this book; warranted
   locatives/temporals were retained mid-line: αὖθι at 165, αὐτοῦ at 271,
   μηκέτʼ ἔπειτα at 297).
3. Preferred fix throughout was reordering onto a naturally trochaic close
   (participles, Circe, compounds in the poem's own idiom: townward, earthward,
   bread-loaves).
4. Masculine endings licensed twice, within the 3/book budget: 10.323 (knees),
   10.343 (oath).
5. Formula echoes restored where the Greek repeats within the book: 265 = 324
   (ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, now verbatim but for pronoun gender, in the shape
   the poem already uses at 377/418/430/482); 299/343 share "to swear … the great
   oath" exactly as far as their Greek shares μέγαν ὅρκον ὀμόσσαι; 535 now echoes
   the poem's own 294 for the repeated sword-drawing formula.
6. Voice rule verified: Hermes (290, 296, 297, 299) and Circe (535) use no
   lexical contractions.

Of the junior draft's 34 entries, 18 were rewritten or corrected here (beat
overruns or shortfalls, an inverted-object close, one fidelity error, one
duplication with a neighbor, one broken join, two formula mismatches); notes
below defend each intervention.

---

**10.24**  ἀργυρέῃ, ἵνα μή τι παραπνεύσῃ ὀλίγον περ·
- OLD: cord, so not even the smallest of breaths might escape from the bag there.
- NEW: cord, so that never a breath might blow past it, however little.
- Rewritten from the draft ("…from the bag might be escaping"), whose close left
  "might be es-" slack before the beat. "blow past" is παραπνεύσῃ literally;
  "however little" is ὀλίγον περ; "from the bag" was a supplement in the old line
  ("it," the bag secured in 23, now carries it).

**10.32**  αἰεὶ γὰρ πόδα νηὸς ἐνώμων, οὐδέ τῳ ἄλλῳ
- OLD: all that time I had managed the sheet of the ship by myself there,
- NEW: always alone I continued to manage the sheet of the vessel,
- αἰεί fronted; οὐδέ τῳ ἄλλῳ is carried whole by 33's "giving it over to no
  other comrade."

**10.45**  ὅσσος τις χρυσός τε καὶ ἄργυρος ἀσκῷ ἔνεστιν.
- OLD: learning how much of the gold and the silver is held in the bag there.”
- NEW: learning how much of the gold and the silver the bag was containing.”

**10.46**  ὣς ἔφασαν, βουλὴ δὲ κακὴ νίκησεν ἑταίρων·
- OLD: Thus they conversed, and the evil advice of my comrades prevailed there.
- NEW: Thus they conversed, and prevailed the wicked counsel of comrades.
- Draft's "of my comrades" left three slack syllables before the last beat;
  ἑταίρων has no possessive, and the anarthrous genitive matches the poem's own
  "country of fathers" (420, 462). The verb-first inversion has the poem's
  precedent at 541 ("came Dawn").

**10.53**  ἀλλʼ ἔτλην καὶ ἔμεινα, καλυψάμενος δʼ ἐνὶ νηὶ
- OLD: I stayed and I endured; and covering over my head there,
- NEW: Yet I endured it and stayed, and covered my own head over,
- ἀλλά regained ("Yet"); "own" is the one added word (logged); ἐνὶ νηί stays
  deferred to 54's "down in the ship I lay," as before.

**10.76**  ὣς εἰπὼν ἀπέπεμπε δόμων βαρέα στενάχοντα.
- OLD: Thus having spoken, he sent me away while I heavily groaned there.
- NEW: Thus having spoken, he sent me from his halls, heavily groaning.
- δόμων restored (the old line dropped it). The draft kept "away" alongside
  "from his hall" and ran to seven beats; "from his halls" alone carries
  ἀπέπεμπε δόμων. Known cost: "me from his" is a light-syllable run — the least
  damage of the available shapes.

**10.101**  οἵ τινες ἀνέρες εἶεν ἐπὶ χθονὶ σῖτον ἔδοντες,
- OLD: what kind of men in the country were eating their bread on the earth there.
- NEW: what kind of men upon the earth were eating their bread-loaves.
- The old line's own doublet ("in the country" / "on the earth") is gone; χθονί
  appears once in the Greek.

**10.108**  Ἀρτακίην· ἔνθεν γὰρ ὕδωρ προτὶ ἄστυ φέρεσκον·
- OLD: spring, out of which all the people were carrying water to town there.
- NEW: spring, out of which all the people were carrying water townward.
- προτὶ ἄστυ as the poem's -ward idiom.

**10.142**  ἔνθα τότʼ ἐκβάντες δύο τʼ ἤματα καὶ δύο νύκτας
- OLD: There we departed the ship, and for two days long and two nights there
- NEW: There we disembarked from the ship, for two days and two nights together,
- Line-initial "There" is ἔνθα, kept. "together" is the one added word (logged):
  the duration clause has no verb of its own — "down we lay" belongs to 143.

**10.161**  τὸν δʼ ἐγὼ ἐκβαίνοντα κατʼ ἄκνηστιν μέσα νῶτα
- OLD: Then as he stepped from the woodland, I struck in the middle of back there;
- NEW: Then, as he stepped from the woodland, I struck him full in the backbone;
- κατʼ ἄκνηστιν μέσα νῶτα compressed into "full in the backbone"; τόν restored
  as "him."

**10.165**  εἰρυσάμην· τὸ μὲν αὖθι κατακλίνας ἐπὶ γαίῃ
- OLD: out of the wound, and I laid it aside on the ground where I stood there.
- NEW: out of the wound, and just where I stood I laid it earthward.
- αὖθι kept mid-line as "just where I stood"; ἐπὶ γαίῃ as "earthward"
  (the pilot's own move at 21.136).

**10.179**  ἐκ δὲ καλυψάμενοι παρὰ θῖνʼ ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο
- OLD: taking the cloaks from their heads by the shore of the barren salt sea there,
- NEW: taking the cloaks from their heads, by the shore of the sea that was barren,

**10.181**  αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ τάρπησαν ὁρώμενοι ὀφθαλμοῖσιν,
- OLD: Then, when they took in their hearts full delight in the sight of the stag there,
- NEW: Then, when their hearts had taken full pleasure in gazing upon it,
- "the stag" (already named in 180) yields to the pronoun; "gazing" carries
  ὁρώμενοι ὀφθαλμοῖσιν.

**10.213**  τοὺς αὐτὴ κατέθελξεν, ἐπεὶ κακὰ φάρμακʼ ἔδωκεν.
- OLD: beasts that the goddess herself had bewitched when she gave them her evil drugs there.
- NEW: beasts she herself had bewitched by the evil drugs she was giving.
- Draft kept the old line's supplement "the goddess" and ran to seven beats;
  αὐτή is just "she herself," so dropping the supplement fixes both fidelity
  and meter at once.

**10.221**  Κίρκης δʼ ἔνδον ἄκουον ἀειδούσης ὀπὶ καλῇ,
- OLD: inside they heard Circe singing aloud with a beautiful voice there,
- NEW: inside they heard the voice of Circe, beautifully singing,
- Now consonant with 227's "beautifully singing" (both of Circe's ὀπὶ καλῇ).

**10.243**  ἔδμεναι, οἷα σύες χαμαιευνάδες αἰὲν ἔδουσιν.
- OLD: such as are eaten by pigs that continually wallow on earth there.
- NEW: such food as pigs who lie on the ground are continually eating.
- χαμαιευνάδες is "ground-lying," not "wallowing" — a small fidelity gain.

**10.248**  δακρυόφιν πίμπλαντο, γόον δʼ ὠίετο θυμός.
- OLD: while in his spirit was nothing but sorrow and grieving lament there.
- NEW: while his spirit was set upon nothing besides lamentation.
- Rewritten from the draft's "could think of nothing except of lamenting"
  (ungrammatical "except of"). "was set upon" renders ὠίετο; the tears are
  already in 247's "eyes were filled up with teardrops."

**10.263**  τὸν δʼ ἂψ ἠνώγεα αὐτὴν ὁδὸν ἡγήσασθαι.
- OLD: lead me again on the selfsame road by which he had come there.
- NEW: lead me again by the selfsame road on which he had traveled.

**10.265**  καί μʼ ὀλοφυρόμενος ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
- OLD: wailing, he spoke to me then, and the words that he uttered took wing there:
- NEW: wailing aloud, he addressed me, and these words took wing as he uttered:
- Draft's bare "wailing, he addressed me" scanned five beats; "wailing aloud"
  restores the sixth in the exact shape the poem already uses for this formula
  at 418 ("Wailing aloud, they addressed me…"). Verbatim with 324 but for
  pronoun gender.

**10.271**  Εὐρύλοχʼ, ἦ τοι μὲν σὺ μένʼ αὐτοῦ τῷδʼ ἐνὶ χώρῳ
- OLD: “Lord Eurylochus, stay in this place and remain where you are now,
- NEW: “Eurylochus, stay here yourself, remain right where you are standing,
- "Lord" (not in the vocative) dropped; αὐτοῦ kept mid-line as "here …
  right where you are."

**10.276**  Κίρκης ἵξεσθαι πολυφαρμάκου ἐς μέγα δῶμα,
- OLD: there at the mighty palace of Circe, the goddess of many drugs there,
- NEW: at the mighty palace of Circe, the mistress of manifold potions,
- Both unwarranted "there"s removed. Rewritten from the draft's "drug-working
  goddess," which lost the πολυ- ("many") of πολυφαρμάκου and leaned on a
  secondary stress for its sixth beat; "manifold" is the poem's own word
  (330, 372).

**10.290**  τεύξει τοι κυκεῶ, βαλέει δʼ ἐν φάρμακα σίτῳ.
- OLD: She shall prepare you a potion and cast in the dish many drugs there;
- NEW: She shall prepare you a potion and mingle her drugs in your eating;
- Hermes speaking — no contractions. Rewritten from the draft's "potion of
  drink … drugs in your rations" (seven beats, and "rations" is not the poem's
  register); "your eating" for σίτῳ follows the poem's own noun at 373
  ("urging my eating").

**10.296**  ἡ δέ σʼ ὑποδείσασα κελήσεται εὐνηθῆναι·
- OLD: She shall be seized with terror and ask you to enter her bed there.
- NEW: She, overcome with terror, will urge you to come to her bedside.
- Hermes speaking — no contractions. Draft's mid-line "then" (pure syllable
  padding) removed.

**10.297**  ἔνθα σὺ μηκέτʼ ἔπειτʼ ἀπανήνασθαι θεοῦ εὐνήν,
- OLD: Do not refuse to lie in the bed of the goddess when asked there,
- NEW: Do not, from then on, refuse to lie in the bed of the goddess,
- μηκέτʼ ἔπειτα kept mid-line as "from then on."

**10.299**  ἀλλὰ κέλεσθαί μιν μακάρων μέγαν ὅρκον ὀμόσσαι,
- OLD: yet you must order the goddess to swear by the gods’ mighty oath there
- NEW: yet you must bid her to swear the great oath of the blessed immortals
- Hermes speaking — no contractions. μακάρων as "the blessed immortals,"
  echoing the poem's 74.

**10.323**  ἡ δὲ μέγα ἰάχουσα ὑπέδραμε καὶ λάβε γούνων,
- OLD: Shrieking, she ran underneath the great blade and embraced both my knees there;
- NEW: Shrieking, she ran underneath the great blade and embraced both my knees;
- Licensed masculine ending #1. The draft's "my knees clasping" inverted the
  object unnaturally and still ran long; closing hard on "knees" — the suppliant
  gesture the whole scene pivots on — earns the stress.

**10.324**  καί μʼ ὀλοφυρομένη ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
- OLD: wailing, she spoke to me then, and the words that she uttered took wing there:
- NEW: wailing aloud, she addressed me, and these words took wing as she uttered:
- Verbatim echo of 265 (identical Greek formula), feminine pronoun.

**10.343**  εἰ μή μοι τλαίης γε, θεά, μέγαν ὅρκον ὀμόσσαι
- OLD: not till you bring yourself, goddess, to swear by the gods’ mighty oath there
- NEW: not till you bring yourself, goddess, to swear me the great oath
- Licensed masculine ending #2: the single word this whole exchange turns on.
  The draft dropped τλαίης ("bring yourself") and imported "of the blessed
  immortals" from 299 — but 343's Greek has no μακάρων; the two lines share only
  μέγαν ὅρκον ὀμόσσαι, so the English now shares exactly "to swear … the great
  oath" and no more. μοι kept ("swear me").

**10.388**  ὣς ἐφάμην, Κίρκη δὲ διὲκ μεγάροιο βεβήκει
- OLD: Thus did I speak; and the goddess went out through the hall with her wand there,
- NEW: Thus did I speak; and holding her wand, through the hall went Circe,
- The draft deleted "with her wand" as unwarranted — but the wand is in the
  Greek of the next verse (389 ῥάβδον ἔχουσʼ ἐν χειρί), which the old
  translation had folded into this line; deleting it here would erase ῥάβδον
  from the passage entirely. Kept as "holding her wand" (the participle ἔχουσα),
  and the line now closes on the restored name Κίρκη.

**10.392**  ἐρχομένη προσάλειφεν ἑκάστῳ φάρμακον ἄλλο.
- OLD: Circe anointed each man with a different magical drug there.
- NEW: Circe anointed each one of the men with a different potion.
- The draft's "Circe moved all among them" duplicated ἐρχομένη, which the poem
  already renders at the end of 391 ("and moving among them,"). "magical"
  (not in the Greek) also removed.

**10.440**  τῷ οἱ ἀποπλήξας κεφαλὴν οὖδάσδε πελάσσαι,
- OLD: sever his head with a stroke, and bring it to earth in the dust there,
- NEW: sever his head with a blow of the weapon, and send it earthward,
- οὖδάσδε is literally "groundward," so "earthward" is the closer fit. The
  draft's "stroke of the blade" collided with 439's line-final "sword blade";
  "the weapon" (the poem's own word at 532) re-references τῷ without the echo.

**10.452**  δαινυμένους δʼ ἐὺ πάντας ἐφεύρομεν ἐν μεγάροισιν.
- OLD: All of the men we discovered at generous feasting within there.
- NEW: All of the men we discovered, well feasting, inside of the palace.
- ἐν μεγάροισιν made concrete. The draft ended with a comma; the period is
  restored — 453 begins a new sentence ("When they beheld…").

**10.514**  Κώκυτός θʼ, ὃς δὴ Στυγὸς ὕδατός ἐστιν ἀπορρώξ,
- OLD: also Cocytus, a stream that is branched from the water of Styx there;
- NEW: also Cocytus, whose stream from the water of Styx has its outflow;
- Circe speaking — no contractions. "whose stream" for the draft's "a stream
  that," which stacked three slack syllables before the beat.

**10.535**  αὐτὸς δὲ ξίφος ὀξὺ ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ
- OLD: Draw for yourself the sharp-edged sword from beside your strong thigh there;
- NEW: Draw from beside your thigh the sharp-edged sword that you carry;
- Circe speaking — no contractions. The Greek repeats 294's ξίφος ὀξὺ
  ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ nearly verbatim, so the English now echoes the poem's
  own 294 ("draw from beside your thigh the sharp-edged sword that you carry")
  rather than the draft's "broadsword," which added "broad-" and broke the echo.

## Tally

- 34 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*.
  No warranted filler-class ending was needed at line-end; the warranted words
  live mid-line (αὖθι at 165, αὐτοῦ at 271, μηκέτʼ ἔπειτα at 297, ἔνθα at 142's
  line-initial "There").
- 2 masculine endings licensed (10.323 "knees," 10.343 "oath"), within the
  3/book budget.
- Formula work: 265 = 324 verbatim (per the poem's 418 shape); 299/343 now share
  exactly what their Greek shares (μέγαν ὅρκον ὀμόσσαι), no more; 535 echoes the
  poem's own 294; 221 consonant with 227.
- Fidelity recovered along the way: δόμων at 76, ἀλλά at 53, τόν at 161, τλαίης
  at 343, Κίρκη at 388, the wand (389's ῥάβδον) kept at 388, χαμαιευνάδες at
  243; unwarranted supplements removed at 213 ("the goddess"), 271 ("Lord"),
  276 (both "there"s), 296 ("then"), 392 ("magical"), 343 (the imported
  "blessed immortals").
- Honest costs incurred (added words, all logged): "own" at 53, "together" at
  142, "alone" carried over at 32, "mistress" at 276, "aloud" at 265/324 (the
  poem's own 418 wording). "me from his" at 76 is the one light-syllable run
  knowingly kept.
- 18 of the 34 draft entries required rewriting or correction; the draft's other
  16 stood as submitted.
- Out of scope but flagged: verse 281 ends "…all alone here," — "here" was not
  in this pass's extraction pattern (there/now/then) and has no αὐτοῦ in the
  Greek; a candidate for a future "here" sweep.

The main finding in this book: **two participial ὣς εἰπών lines had been flattened into the finite
"So he spoke,"** (219, 487), collapsing two distinct Greek formulas into one English line. Both are
restored to the poem's dominant participial rendering, "So having spoken" (53 occurrences poem-wide).

**24.64** κλαίομεν ἀθάνατοί τε θεοὶ θνητοί τʼ ἄνθρωποι· — no deictic
- OLD: both we immortal gods and mortals that die upon earth here;
- NEW: both we the immortal gods and the mortal men who must perish;
- The Greek's antithesis is ἀθάνατοι / θνητοί — deathless against dying — with no locative at all. Restoring the paired articles makes the antithesis carry the line and the filler *here* becomes unnecessary.

**24.98** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: Thus they spoke such things in turn, addressing each other,
- NEW: So they were speaking such things in turn to one another,
- Fixed rendering for the recurring formula; identical now at 24.98 / 203 / 383 and 23.288.

**24.203** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: So the two of them spoke such words to one another, conversing,
- NEW: So they were speaking such things in turn to one another,
- Same Greek verse as 98 and 383; now the same English. "conversing" was padding beside "spoke."

**24.219** ὣς εἰπὼν δμώεσσιν ἀρήϊα τεύχεʼ ἔδωκεν. — **participial, not finite**
- OLD: So he spoke, and into the hands of the thralls he gave over the war-gear.
- NEW: So having spoken, he gave to the thralls the armor of battle.
- The old line read as ὣς φάτο and lost the participle's subordination. "into the hands of" also expanded a plain dative (δμώεσσιν).

**24.361** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσαντε βάτην πρὸς δώματα καλά. — participial, dual
- OLD: So after speaking thus, they went to the beautiful dwelling.
- NEW: So having spoken, the two went on to the beautiful dwelling.
- Brought into the standard participial shape; the dual φωνήσαντε / βάτην is recovered by "the two," which the old line had dropped.

**24.383** ὣς οἷ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον.
- OLD: Thus then the two of them spoke such things to one another.
- NEW: So they were speaking such things in turn to one another.
- Third instance of the formula; now verbatim with 98 and 203.

**24.412** ὣς οἱ μὲν περὶ δεῖπνον ἐνὶ μεγάροισι πένοντο· — a *different* formula (πένοντο, not ἀγόρευον)
- OLD: Thus they in the halls were intent on the meal before them.
- NEW: So they were busy about their meal in the halls of the dwelling.
- Line-initial *Thus* recast per the connective. Deliberately kept distinct from the ἀγόρευον line above: πένοντο is "were busy at," and περὶ δεῖπνον is "about the meal," which the new line renders literally.

**24.487** ὣς εἰπὼν ὤτρυνε πάρος μεμαυῖαν Ἀθήνην, — **participial, not finite**
- OLD: So he spoke, and he roused Athena, already eager before this;
- NEW: So having spoken, he urged on Athena, eager already;
- Second flattened participle restored. ὤτρυνε is "urged on"; πάρος μεμαυῖαν is carried by "eager already," so the old line's "already … before this" doubling is cut.

## Left as house practice

**24.533, 24.545** ὣς φάτ᾿ Ἀθηναίη — the Greek names the speaker inside the formula line. The poem's
established variant for that case is "So spoke [Name]" (cf. 12.294, 12.352, 17.147, 18.50, 18.290),
which preserves the named subject the ruling's pronoun form would discard. Flagged for the editor
rather than changed.

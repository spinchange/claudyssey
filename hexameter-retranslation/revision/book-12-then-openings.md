# Book 12 — "Then"-opening audit

36 verses in Book 12 opened with *Then/then*. **16 kept** (the Greek carries a real
temporal adverb), **20 rewritten** (the Greek carries only a light connective).

**Warrant rule.** A "Then" stands only where the Greek line has τότε, δὴ τότε,
καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα / αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα, or a ἦμος…(τῆμος) correlative.
Bare δέ, τε, καί, γάρ, ἀλλά, αὐτάρ/ἀτάρ, ὣς + verb, the reply-formulas
(τὸν δ᾿ ἀπαμειβόμενος, ἠμείβετο μύθῳ) and plain asyndeton do not warrant one.
αὐτίκα is a real adverb but means *straightway/at once*, not *then* — those three
lines (151, 278, 376) are rewritten to the adverb the Greek actually has.

No crowding demotions were needed: nowhere in the book do three warranted
"Then"-openings fall inside ten lines (the nearest pair is 317 / 319).

Replacement openings are spread deliberately — subject-first ×5, participle-first ×4,
prepositional/object-first ×5, verb-first ×2, αὐτίκα-adverbs ×3, "but" ×2, "And" ×1.
No single opening exceeds a fifth of the rewrites.

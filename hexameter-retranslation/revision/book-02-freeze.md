Formula renderings now fixed in this book:

- finite ὣς φάτο family (ὣς φάτο, ὣς ἔφατʼ, ὣς φάθʼ, ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη) →
  **“So he spoke,” / “So she spoke,” / “So they spoke,”**
- finite ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν → **“So he spoke,”** (following the Book 21 pilot's
  treatment of the same formula at 21.386)
- participial ὣς εἰπών / ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας(ʼ) → **“Thus having spoken,”**
- iterative εἴπεσκε → **“So would … be speaking:”**
- ἦ ῥα → **“He spoke, …”**, deliberately distinct from the ὣς φάτο formula

## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**2.35** ὣς φάτο, χαῖρε δὲ φήμῃ Ὀδυσσῆος φίλος υἱός — finite
- OLD: Thus he spoke; and in that word's omen Odysseus' son was rejoicing.
- NEW: So he spoke, and the dear son of Odysseus took joy in the omen,
- φίλος υἱός (“dear son”) restored; the line now closes on φήμῃ, the omen itself.

**2.80** ὣς φάτο χωόμενος, ποτὶ δὲ σκῆπτρον βάλε γαίῃ — finite
- OLD: Angrily speaking, he dashed down the scepter and burst into weeping.
- NEW: So he spoke in his wrath, dashed the scepter to earth, and was weeping,
- ποτὶ γαίῃ (“to the ground”) recovered, which the old “dashed down” had elided.

**2.103** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, ἡμῖν δʼ αὖτʼ ἐπεπείθετο θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ — finite; speaker is
Penelope inside Antinous' quotation, hence the feminine subject
- OLD: Thus she addressed us, and easily won our imperious spirits.
- NEW: So she spoke, and once more our imperious spirits were yielding.
- αὖτε (“once more”) restored; “easily” was not in the Greek.

**2.146** ὣς φάτο Τηλέμαχος, τῷ δʼ αἰετὼ εὐρύοπα Ζεὺς — finite
- OLD: Thus spoke Telemachus. Zeus of the thunderous voice sent two eagles,
- NEW: So he spoke, Telemachus. Zeus of the wide voice sent forth two eagles,
- εὐρύοπα is “wide-voiced,” not “thunderous”; the re-metering paid for the fix.

**2.224** ἦ τοι ὅ γʼ ὣς εἰπὼν κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετο — participial
- OLD: Thus he spoke and sat down; then Mentor arose in the council,
- NEW: Thus having spoken, he sat down; then Mentor arose in the council,

**2.257** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν, λῦσεν δʼ ἀγορὴν αἰψηρήν — finite ἐφώνησεν
- OLD: Thus he spoke and at once brought the people's assembly to ending.
- NEW: So he spoke, and dissolved in its swiftness the people's assembly.
- αἰψηρήν modifies ἀγορήν (the quickly-dispersing assembly), not the verb.

**2.267** ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, σχεδόθεν δέ οἱ ἦλθεν Ἀθήνη — finite
- OLD: Thus he prayed, and Athena approached and was standing beside him,
- NEW: So he spoke as he prayed, and Athena approached and drew near him,
- εὐχόμενος is the participle; the finite verb is ἔφατο, and now says so.
  ἦλθεν (“came”) recovered from the old static “was standing.”

**2.296** ὣς φάτʼ Ἀθηναίη κούρη Διός — finite, feminine subject
- OLD: Thus spoke Athena, the daughter of Zeus; and Telemachus lingered
- NEW: So she spoke, Athena, the daughter of Zeus; Telemachus lingered

**2.321** ἦ ῥα, καὶ ἐκ χειρὸς χεῖρα σπάσατʼ Ἀντινόοιο — **not** ὣς φάτο
- OLD: Thus he spoke and easily wrenched his hand from Antinous' clutches.
- NEW: He spoke, and he easily wrenched his hand from Antinous' clutches.
- ἦ ῥα gets its own bare rendering, kept clear of the ὣς φάτο formula so the two
  Greek formulas stay visibly distinct in English.

**2.324** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων — iterative
- OLD: Thus would one arrogant youth make a speech to the rest of his fellows:
- NEW: So would a man among those arrogant young men be speaking:

**2.331** ἄλλος δʼ αὖτʼ εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων — iterative, same shape
- OLD: Thus would another arrogant youth make a speech to his fellows:
- NEW: So would another among those arrogant young men be speaking:
- 324 and 331 now share the frame the Greek shares, differing only where the
  Greek differs (τις / ἄλλος). “make a speech to his fellows” was invented; the
  Greek has only εἴπεσκε.

**2.337** ὣς φάν, ὁ δʼ ὑψόροφον θάλαμον κατεβήσετο πατρὸς — finite, plural
- OLD: Thus they spoke, but he entered the lofty room of his father,
- NEW: So they spoke, but he entered the lofty room of his father,

**2.361** ὣς φάτο, κώκυσεν δὲ φίλη τροφὸς Εὐρύκλεια — finite
- OLD: Thus he spoke; Eurycleia, his nurse from his infancy, wailed,
- NEW: So he spoke, and Eurycleia, the nurse he had loved, was lamenting,
- φίλη τροφός is “dear nurse”; “from his infancy” belonged to 435 of Book 1, not
  to this line.

**2.377** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη, γρῆυς δὲ θεῶν μέγαν ὅρκον ἀπώμνυ — finite ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη
- OLD: Thus he spoke; and the old woman swore the great oath of immortals.
- NEW: So he spoke, and the old woman swore the great oath of immortals.

**2.101** μή τίς μοι κατὰ δῆμον Ἀχαιϊάδων νεμεσήσῃ — residual *Thus*
- OLD: Thus no Achaean woman around us can censure or shame me
- NEW: lest any Achaean woman among this people should blame me
- The Greek is a negative purpose clause (μή … νεμεσήσῃ), not a demonstrative;
  κατὰ δῆμον (“through the district”) replaces the invented “around us,” and the
  doubled “censure or shame” collapses back to the single νεμεσήσῃ.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

Neither line had a Greek deictic at the close. Both are filler.

**2.73** τῶν μʼ ἀποτινύμενοι κακὰ ῥέζετε δυσμενέοντες — no deictic
- OLD: and in return you maliciously injure the son he has left here,
- NEW: and in return you maliciously injure me, taking requital,
- Restores μʼ — Telemachus says *you injure me*, in the first person; “the son he
  has left” was a third-person paraphrase invented for the cadence.

**2.238** οἶκον Ὀδυσσῆος, τὸν δʼ οὐκέτι φασὶ νέεσθαι — no deictic
- OLD: all of Odysseus' house in the faith he shall never return here.
- NEW: all of Odysseus' house, and they say he shall never come homeward.
- φασί (“they say”) recovered; “in the faith” had turned reported speech into
  the suitors' inner conviction.

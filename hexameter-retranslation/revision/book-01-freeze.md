Formula renderings now fixed in this book:

- finite ὣς φάτο family → **“So he spoke,” / “So she spoke,”** (subject per the Greek)
- participial ὣς εἰπών / ὣς εἰποῦσα → **“Thus having spoken,”** (the poem's dominant

## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**1.42** ὣς ἔφαθʼ Ἑρμείας — finite ὣς ἔφατο, subject Hermes
- OLD: Hermes advised him, intending his good; but he never persuaded
- NEW: So he spoke, Hermes, intending his good; but he never persuaded
- The old line had swallowed the formula entirely. Line 43 (“Aegisthus' heart…”)
  is untouched, so the enjambment still lands.

**1.96** ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ὑπὸ ποσσὶν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα — participial
- OLD: Speaking, she fastened beneath her the beautiful sandals immortal,
- NEW: Thus having spoken, beneath her she bound on the beautiful sandals,
- “immortal” was doing duty for 97's ἀμβρόσια, which 97 already renders; dropping
  it here removes a duplication rather than losing anything.

**1.125** ὣς εἰπὼν ἡγεῖθʼ — participial
- OLD: Speaking, he led her within, and Pallas Athena came with him.
- NEW: Thus having spoken, he led, and Pallas Athena came with him.
- ἡγεῖτο absolute (“he led”), matching the poem's other “Thus having spoken, he led”
  lines.

**1.319** ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ἀπέβη γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη — participial
- OLD: Speaking these words, the gray-eyed Athena departed and vanished,
- NEW: Thus having spoken, the gray-eyed Athena departed and vanished,

**1.381** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, οἱ δʼ ἄρα πάντες ὀδὰξ ἐν χείλεσι φύντες — finite
- OLD: Thus he addressed them; they all bit their lips in their anger and marveled,
- NEW: So he spoke, and they all bit their lips in their anger and marveled,

**1.420** ὣς φάτο Τηλέμαχος, φρεσὶ δʼ ἀθανάτην θεὸν ἔγνω — finite
- OLD: Thus spoke Telemachus, knowing within that she was an immortal.
- NEW: So he spoke, Telemachus, knowing within that the goddess was deathless.
- ἀθανάτην θεὸν ἔγνω is “recognized [her as] a deathless goddess”; the recast
  absorbs the extra opening syllables into the predicate instead of padding.

**1.47** ὡς ἀπόλοιτο καὶ ἄλλος, ὅτις τοιαῦτά γε ῥέζοι — residual *Thus*
- OLD: Thus may another be ruined, whoever accomplishes such things.
- NEW: May any other be ruined as well, who accomplishes such things.
- The ὡς here is the wish-particle, not “in this way”; the old “Thus” was reading
  it as a demonstrative. καὶ ἄλλος (“another **also**”) is now rendered.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

No line in this book had a Greek deictic at the close. All five are filler.

**1.78** ὃν χόλον· οὐ μὲν γάρ τι δυνήσεται ἀντία πάντων — no deictic
- OLD: never alone can he strive and contend with the will of us all here,
- NEW: never alone can he strive and contend with the whole of our number,
- ἀντία πάντων (“against all”) now closes the line on its own noun.

**1.123** χαῖρε, ξεῖνε, παρʼ ἄμμι φιλήσεαι — no deictic
- OLD: “Welcome, my stranger; among us as friend you shall first be received here;
- NEW: “Welcome, my stranger; among us as friend you shall find entertainment;
- φιλήσεαι is “you will be treated as a guest-friend”; “first” was filler too.

**1.225** τίς δαίς, τίς δὲ ὅμιλος ὅδʼ ἔπλετο; τίπτε δέ σε χρεώ; — no locative
- OLD: What is the feast, what assembly is this, and what is your need here?
- NEW: What is the feast, what assembly is this? And what need has befallen?
- τίπτε δέ σε χρεώ is the same idiom Book 2 renders with “fallen” at 2.28; the
  two now agree.

**1.227** ὥς τέ μοι ὑβρίζοντες ὑπερφιάλως δοκέουσι — no deictic
- OLD: How unrestrained and exceedingly arrogant these men appear here,
- NEW: How unrestrained and exceedingly arrogant these men are seeming,
- δοκέουσι (“they seem [to me]”) recovered as the verb rather than as “appear.”

**1.405** ἀλλʼ ἐθέλω σε, φέριστε, περὶ ξείνοιο ἐρέσθαι — no deictic
- OLD: Still, my good man, I desire to inquire of the stranger who came here:
- NEW: Still, my good man, I desire to inquire of you about the stranger:
- Restores σε: Eurymachus asks *Telemachus* about the stranger, not the stranger.

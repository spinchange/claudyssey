**23.128** ὅση δύναμίς γε πάρεστιν — no deictic; πάρεστιν is "is at hand," not "here"
- OLD: we shall be lacking in courage, so far as our power extends here.”
- NEW: we shall be lacking in courage, so far as our power is present.”
- "is present" renders πάρεστιν directly; the filler *here* was doing the metrical work the verb should do.

**23.148** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε δόμων ἔκτοσθεν ἀκούων· — iterative εἴπεσκε
- OLD: Thus would someone say, hearing the sound from outside of the dwelling:
- NEW: So would a man say, hearing the sound from outside of the dwelling:
- Standardized to the poem's dominant iterative shape ("So would a man …", as at 2.324, 2.331, 10.37, 17.482, 18.72, 18.400, 20.375).

**23.152** ὣς ἄρα τις εἴπεσκε, τὰ δʼ οὐκ ἴσαν ὡς ἐτέτυκτο.
- OLD: Thus a man would say, for they did not know how matters had happened.
- NEW: So would a man say, but they did not know how matters had happened.
- Pairs verbatim with 148, as the Greek pairs εἴπεσκε with εἴπεσκε. δʼ restored as "but" (adversative), replacing the causal "for," which the Greek does not carry.

**23.181** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη πόσιος πειρωμένη — ὣς ἄρ᾿ ἔφη is in the finite ὣς φάτο family
- OLD: So then she spoke, while testing her husband; and great Odysseus,
- NEW: So she spoke, while testing her husband; and great Odysseus,
- Dropping "then" restores the ruled opening and rebalances feet 1–2 without padding.

**23.231** ὣς φάτο, τῷ δʼ ἔτι μᾶλλον ὑφʼ ἵμερον ὦρσε γόοιο·
- OLD: So then she spoke, and in him she roused still stronger desire for weeping;
- NEW: So she spoke, and in him she roused still stronger longing for weeping;
- "desire"→"longing" supplies the trochaic close the shortened opening now needs; ἵμερος is equally "longing." No words added.

**23.258** ἐπεὶ ἄρ σε θεοὶ ποίησαν ἱκέσθαι — no deictic
- OLD: wishes it, since indeed the gods have made you return here
- NEW: wishes it, since the gods have truly accomplished your homecoming
- ἱκέσθαι is the homecoming itself; naming it closes the line on a natural trochee and lets *here* go.

**23.288** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον·
- OLD: So then they spoke such things in speech to one another.
- NEW: So they were speaking such things in turn to one another.
- Fixed rendering for this recurring formula, now identical at 23.288 and 24.98 / 24.203 / 24.383. "in speech" was padding beside "spoke."

**23.354** νῦν δʼ ἐπεὶ ἀμφοτέρω πολυήρατον ἱκόμεθʼ εὐνήν, — no deictic
- OLD: But now, since at last we both have attained our long-desired bed here,
- NEW: But now, since we both at last have attained the much-longed-for chamber,
- πολυήρατον ("much-longed-for") moves to the epithet slot where it belongs; the line closes on the noun rather than on filler.

## Kept

**23.228** Ἀκτορίς, ἥν μοι δῶκε πατὴρ ἔτι δεῦρο κιούσῃ — δεῦρο is a true deictic ("hither, here"), in the Greek at that position. *here* stands.

**23.202** οὕτω τοι τόδε σῆμα πιφαύσκομαι — οὕτω genuinely means "in this way." *Thus* earns its place and is kept.

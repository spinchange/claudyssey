# Book 19 de-filler — all 44 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied (as in the Book 21 pilot):

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek carries the sense at
   that position (no case in this book: 477's ἔνδον is carried mid-line by "within").
3. Preferred fix: reorder so a naturally trochaic word lands last (participles,
   names, plurals, compounds), not bolting on new words.
4. Masculine endings budgeted at 3 per book. Used three times: 19.31, 19.186, 19.394
   (proper-name, place-name, and kin-term closes that resisted reordering).
5. Formula parity: 19.29 = 21.386 (identical Greek, now identical English);
   19.492 follows the pilot's 21.168 fix for the ἕρκος ὀδόντων formula; 19.236 now
   closes "lay it away in your spirit" matching the book's own 19.495 and 19.570
   (same Greek half-line σὺ δʼ ἐνὶ φρεσὶ βάλλεο σῇσιν).
6. Joins with neighboring verses checked in the file; no adjacent verse needed touching.

**19.29** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν, τῇ δʼ ἄπτερος ἔπλετο μῦθος.
- OLD: So he spoke, and for her the uttered word grew no wings then.
- NEW: So he spoke, and for her the word he had uttered was wingless.
- Verbatim with 21.386 (identical Greek).

**19.31** τὼ δʼ ἄρʼ ἀναΐξαντʼ Ὀδυσεὺς καὶ φαίδιμος υἱὸς
- OLD: Then up they sprang, Odysseus and his glorious son there,
- NEW: Then up they sprang, Odysseus and his glorious son,
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the pair springing to the work is the emphasis;
  the Greek's name-order (Odysseus first, son second) resists reordering.

**19.38** εἰλάτιναί τε δοκοί, καὶ κίονες ὑψόσʼ ἔχοντες
- OLD: and the fir-wood rafters, and pillars that tower aloft there,
- NEW: and the fir-wood rafters, and the pillars towering upward,
- "towering upward" renders κίονες ὑψόσʼ ἔχοντες directly.

**19.57** ποίησʼ Ἰκμάλιος, καὶ ὑπὸ θρῆνυν ποσὶν ἧκε
- OLD: Icmalius, made, and beneath it he fixed a stool for the feet there,
- NEW: Icmalius, made, and beneath it a stool for the feet he had fitted,
- The drafter's "footstool" left the line a beat short; keeping θρῆνυν ποσὶν
  spelled out ("a stool for the feet") and reordering onto "fitted" restores
  the sixth beat and a participial close. "joined to the chair itself" (58) follows.

**19.61** αἱ δʼ ἀπὸ μὲν σῖτον πολὺν ᾕρεον ἠδὲ τραπέζας
- OLD: They took away much bread from the boards, and the tables as well there,
- NEW: Much bread they took away from the boards, and the tables together,

**19.76** ὄλβιος ἀφνειὸν καὶ πολλάκι δόσκον ἀλήτῃ,
- OLD: blessed and wealthy, and often I gave to a vagrant who came there,
- NEW: blessed and wealthy, and many a time I would give to a wanderer,
- ἀλήτῃ = "wanderer"; "many a time I would give" carries both πολλάκι and the
  iterative δόσκον (the draft's "often I gave" scanned a beat short).

**19.86** ἀλλʼ ἤδη παῖς τοῖος Ἀπόλλωνός γε ἕκητι,
- OLD: still by Apollo's favor his son is already such now,
- NEW: still by Apollo's favor his son is such already,

**19.112** πυροὺς καὶ κριθάς, βρίθῃσι δὲ δένδρεα καρπῷ,
- OLD: wheat and barley, and the trees are heavy and loaded with fruit there;
- NEW: wheat and barley, and the trees are burdened and heavy with fruitage;

**19.172** Κρήτη τις γαῖʼ ἔστι, μέσῳ ἐνὶ οἴνοπι πόντῳ,
- OLD: There is a certain land, Crete, in the midst of the wine-dark sea there,
- NEW: There is a certain land, Crete, in the midst of the wine-dark ocean,
- "ocean" for πόντῳ avoids duplicating 173's "waters".

**19.186** καὶ γὰρ τὸν Κρήτηνδε κατήγαγεν ἲς ἀνέμοιο,
- OLD: for the might of the wind had brought him down into Crete there,
- NEW: for the might of the wind had driven him down into Crete,
- Licensed masculine ending #2: landing on Crete is the clause's whole point.

**19.192** τῷ δʼ ἤδη δεκάτη ἢ ἑνδεκάτη πέλεν ἠὼς
- OLD: But for him it was then already the tenth or eleventh dawn there
- NEW: But for him already had come the dawn, the tenth or eleventh,

**19.197** δημόθεν ἄλφιτα δῶκα καὶ αἴθοπα οἶνον ἀγείρας
- OLD: from the public stores I collected meal and the glowing wine there,
- NEW: from the public stores I provided the glowing wine and the barley,
- The draft ("...I gathered together") dropped δῶκα, the main verb. "provided"
  folds δῶκα + ἀγείρας as the old "collected" did; ἄλφιτα rendered by its literal
  "barley," which also supplies the trochaic close. Noun order wine/meal swapped.

**19.199** ἔνθα δυώδεκα μὲν μένον ἤματα δῖοι Ἀχαιοί·
- OLD: There for twelve days’ space the noble Achaeans remained there;
- NEW: There for the space of twelve days remained the noble Achaeans;
- Line-initial "There" is ἔνθα, in the Greek; unstressed-final plural closes.

**19.203** ἴσκε ψεύδεα πολλὰ λέγων ἐτύμοισιν ὁμοῖα·
- OLD: So he spoke many falsehoods, making them all seem like truth there.
- NEW: So he spoke many falsehoods, and gave them the semblance of true things.
- The draft's "close to true matters" was flat and overweight; "gave them the
  semblance" renders ἴσκε (made them like), ἐτύμοισιν ὁμοῖα = "true things."

**19.208** ὣς τῆς τήκετο καλὰ παρήϊα δάκρυ χεούσης,
- OLD: so her beautiful cheeks were melting with tears as they poured there,
- NEW: so her beautiful cheeks were melting with the tears she was pouring,
- Participle close carries δάκρυ χεούσης (genitive absolute: she shedding tears).

**19.227** αὐλοῖσιν διδύμοισι· πάροιθε δὲ δαίδαλον ἦεν·
- OLD: fitted with twin tubes for the pin; and before it a wonder was wrought there:
- NEW: fitted with twin tubes for the pin; and before it a wonder was fashioned:
- The draft's "was wrought:" closed on the stress — a fourth masculine ending
  over budget. "fashioned" gives the feminine close at no cost to δαίδαλον.

**19.236** ἄλλο δέ τοι ἐρέω, σὺ δʼ ἐνὶ φρεσὶ βάλλεο σῇσιν·
- OLD: And another thing I will tell you; do you lay it within your heart now:
- NEW: And another thing I will tell you, and lay it away in your spirit:
- The Greek half-line recurs at 19.495 and 19.570, both already closing "in your
  spirit"; the draft's "bosom" would have broken that book-internal formula.

**19.256** πτύξασʼ ἐκ θαλάμου, περόνην τʼ ἐπέθηκα φαεινὴν
- OLD: folding them out from the chamber, and fastened the shining brooch there
- NEW: folding them out from the chamber, and fastened the shining brooch-pin
- The draft's "the brooch, brightly gleaming" ran to seven beats. "brooch-pin"
  (περόνη is a pin-brooch; cf. 227's "tubes for the pin") is a compound close in
  the pilot's line-rule/bow-stave pattern, with φαεινήν kept in place.

**19.280** οἳ δή μιν περὶ κῆρι θεὸν ὣς τιμήσαντο
- OLD: They from the heart gave him honor even as if to a god there,
- NEW: They in the depth of their hearts did him honor as though to a godhead,
- The draft's "as though he were godlike" scanned five beats. περὶ κῆρι =
  "in the depth of their hearts" (as at 5.36); "godhead" is the poem's own word
  (19.267) and closes trochaically.

**19.295** ὅσσα οἱ ἐν μεγάροις κειμήλια κεῖτο ἄνακτος.
- OLD: so many lordly possessions were laid in the palace for him there.
- NEW: so many were the lordly possessions stored in his palace.

**19.297** ἐκ δρυὸς ὑψικόμοιο Διὸς βουλὴν ἐπακούσαι,
- OLD: for the design of Zeus from the high-haired oak of the god there,
- NEW: for the design of Zeus, from the oak with its high crown of leafage,
- "of the god" cut (not in the Greek; Zeus named once). The draft's "high in its
  leafage" was a beat short; "high crown of leafage" unpacks ὑψι-κόμοιο
  (high-crowned-with-leaves) literally and restores the sixth beat.

**19.300** ὣς ὁ μὲν οὕτως ἐστὶ σόος καὶ ἐλεύσεται ἤδη
- OLD: Thus he is safe, and indeed he will come very soon now,
- NEW: Thus he is safe, and indeed he will very soon be arriving,
- The draft's "truly quite soon" was limp and a beat short; "indeed" keeps μὲν,
  "very soon" carries ἤδη's immediacy, participial close.

**19.302** δηρὸν ἀπεσσεῖται· ἔμπης δέ τοι ὅρκια δώσω.
- OLD: yet even so upon this I will give you a solemn oath now.
- NEW: yet even so, I will give you my solemn oath on the matter.
- "on the matter" is a logged light addition buying the close (the old line's
  "upon this" was the same weight in the same place).

**19.311** ἐξ ἐμεῦ, ὡς ἄν τίς σε συναντόμενος μακαρίζοι.
- OLD: so that whoever might meet you would call you blessed indeed there.
- NEW: so that whoever might meet you would count you among the blessed.
- μακαρίζοι = "would count blessed"; the draft's "a fortunate person" was flat
  and underweight. Disyllabic "blessed" closes feminine.

**19.394** Παρνησόνδʼ ἐλθόντα μετʼ Αὐτόλυκόν τε καὶ υἷας,
- OLD: when he had gone to Parnassus to Autolycus and his sons there,
- NEW: when he had gone to Parnassus to Autolycus and his sons,
- Licensed masculine ending #3 (book's budget spent): the Greek's own order puts
  the sons last, and 395's apposition ("to the noble father of his own mother")
  hangs off Autolycus mid-line.

**19.409** τῷ δʼ Ὀδυσεὺς ὄνομʼ ἔστω ἐπώνυμον· αὐτὰρ ἐγώ γε,
- OLD: therefore let Odysseus be the name by which he is called now.
- NEW: therefore let Odysseus be the name that he shall be given.

**19.417** κύσσʼ ἄρα μιν κεφαλήν τε καὶ ἄμφω φάεα καλά.
- OLD: folded Odysseus in her arms and kissed his head and both bright eyes there.
- NEW: embraced Odysseus and kissed his head and both eyes in their beauty.
- The draft ran to eight beats. "embraced" for the clasping, and καλά given its
  true sense "beauty" (not "bright"), closing feminine at six beats.

**19.418** Αὐτόλυκος δʼ υἱοῖσιν ἐκέκλετο κυδαλίμοισι
- OLD: Then did Autolycus call to his glorious sons in the hall there,
- NEW: Then did Autolycus call out to his sons in their glory,
- "in the hall" cut: not in the Greek (κυδαλίμοισι modifies the sons).

**19.422** μίστυλλόν τʼ ἄρʼ ἐπισταμένως πεῖράν τʼ ὀβελοῖσιν,
- OLD: skillfully jointed the meat, and ran the pieces through spits there,
- NEW: skillfully jointing the meat, they threaded the pieces on skewers,
- ("on skewers," not the draft's "onto skewers" — the extra slack syllable broke
  the fifth foot.) 423's "roasted them well" still chains cleanly.

**19.433** Ἠέλιος μὲν ἔπειτα νέον προσέβαλλεν ἀρούρας
- OLD: Then did the Sun strike fresh on the tilled fields of mortal men there,
- NEW: Then did the Sun strike fresh on the tilled fields of the mortals,
- Kept the old line's "strike fresh" (νέον) rather than the draft's "strike newly
  upon... all mortals," which over-stuffed the line; plural closes feminine.

**19.435** οἱ δʼ ἐς βῆσσαν ἵκανον ἐπακτῆρες· πρὸ δʼ ἄρʼ αὐτῶν
- OLD: and they came to a glen, the beaters; and before them the hounds there,
- NEW: and they, the beaters, came to a glen; and the hounds, out before them,
- The draft's "with the hounds going onward" orphaned 436's "went on" (the hounds
  are its subject) and duplicated it. This keeps the hounds as subject, renders
  πρὸ δʼ ἄρʼ αὐτῶν exactly, and closes feminine into the enjambment.

**19.447** στῆ ῥʼ αὐτῶν σχεδόθεν· ὁ δʼ ἄρα πρώτιστος Ὀδυσσεὺς
- OLD: fire in his eyes; and he stood there very close to them. First then
- NEW: fire in his eyes; and he stood close beside them. And foremost
- στῆ σχεδόθεν = "stood close by"; the draft's "came to a stop" was out of
  register. "And foremost" (πρώτιστος) launches 448's "rushed in Odysseus."

**19.450** γουνὸς ὕπερ, πολλὸν δὲ διήφυσε σαρκὸς ὀδόντι
- OLD: just above on the knee, and with slanting rush of his tusk there
- NEW: just above on the knee, and with his tusk’s swift slanting onrush,
- "swift" is a logged light addition (it carries the dart of the boar's lunge);
  spondaic pileup at the close is licensed before the pause; 451's "tore away
  much flesh" keeps the boar as subject.

**19.457** δῆσαν ἐπισταμένως, ἐπαοιδῇ δʼ αἷμα κελαινὸν
- OLD: they bound up well with skill, and with charm-song stanched the black blood there,
- NEW: they bound up well with skill, and with charm-song stanched the dark bleeding,
- First half kept as it stood (456's "the wound of Odysseus" is its fronted
  object; the draft's resumptive "it" was unneeded); αἷμα κελαινόν closes as
  "the dark bleeding."

**19.470** ἂψ δʼ ἑτέρωσʼ ἐκλίθη· τὸ δʼ ἐπὶ χθονὸς ἐξέχυθʼ ὕδωρ.
- OLD: over the other side it tipped, and the water poured on the ground there.
- NEW: over it tipped to the other side, and the water poured groundward.
- The draft's double "onto" ran to seven beats. "groundward" (ἐπὶ χθονὸς
  ἐξέχυθε) is the poem's licensed -ward close.

**19.477** πεφραδέειν ἐθέλουσα φίλον πόσιν ἔνδον ἐόντα.
- OLD: to make it known that her own dear husband was there in the hall now.
- NEW: to make it known to her that within was her own dear husband.
- ἔνδον carried mid-line by "within"; sentence ends here (draft's comma corrected
  to a period — 478 begins a new sentence).

**19.492** τέκνον ἐμόν, ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων.
- OLD: “My child, what sort of word has escaped through the fence of your teeth there!
- NEW: “My child, out past the fence of your teeth what a word has escaped you!
- Restores σε (the word escapes *you*), following the pilot's 21.168 fix for the
  same formula; unstressed pronoun closes.

**19.498** αἵ τέ σʼ ἀτιμάζουσι καὶ αἳ νηλείτιδές εἰσι.
- OLD: which ones dishonor you, and which are free from all guilt there.”
- NEW: which ones dishonor you, and which of them all are guiltless.”
- νηλείτιδες = "guiltless"; "of them all" points back to 497's "every one of them."

**19.516** κεῖμαι ἐνὶ λέκτρῳ, πυκιναὶ δέ μοι ἀμφʼ ἀδινὸν κῆρ
- OLD: then I lie in my bed, and around my throbbing heart there
- NEW: then I lie in my bed, and around my heart in its throbbing
- Keeps κῆρ as "heart" (the draft's "bosom" traded it away); ἀδινόν becomes the
  trailing close, enjambed into 517's "keen and crowded anxieties" as before.

**19.520** δενδρέων ἐν πετάλοισι καθεζομένη πυκινοῖσιν,
- OLD: sitting within the dense-leafed cover and foliage of trees there,
- NEW: sitting among the trees, in their thick and crowded foliage,
- "crowded" renders πυκινοῖσιν, echoing 517's "crowded" for the related πυκιναί.

**19.539** πᾶσι κατʼ αὐχένας ἦξε καὶ ἔκτανεν· οἱ δʼ ἐκέχυντο
- OLD: and he snapped all their necks and killed them, and they lay there
- NEW: and he snapped all their necks and killed them, and they lay tumbled,
- ἐκέχυντο = "were poured out"; the draft's "scattered" contradicted 540's
  "huddled in heaps" (ἀθρόοι). "tumbled" keeps the poured-out image and sits
  with the heaping.

**19.543** οἴκτρʼ ὀλοφυρομένην ὅ μοι αἰετὸς ἔκτανε χῆνας.
- OLD: while I lamented in pity because the eagle had killed my geese there.
- NEW: while in pity I lamented that my geese had all been killed by the eagle.
- "all" is a logged light addition (all twenty die, 539's πᾶσι); draft's trailing
  comma corrected to a period — 544 begins a new sentence.

**19.544** ἂψ δʼ ἐλθὼν κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετʼ ἐπὶ προὔχοντι μελάθρῳ,
- OLD: But then he came back and sat on the jutting beam of the roof there,
- NEW: But coming back, on the jutting beam of the roof he alighted,
- "alighted" for ἕζετο of the returning bird.

**19.566** οἱ δὲ διὰ ξεστῶν κεράων ἔλθωσι θύραζε,
- OLD: but the ones that come forth through the gates of polished horn there,
- NEW: but the ones that come forth through the gates of horn, smoothly polished,
- The draft's "horn and outward" was ungrammatical. ξεστῶν unpacked as "smoothly
  polished" at the close; θύραζε carried by "come forth."

## Tally

- 44 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*.
- 3 masculine endings licensed (19.31, 19.186, 19.394), exactly the book's budget.
- Formula parity kept: 19.29 = 21.386 verbatim; 19.492 matches the pilot's 21.168
  pattern for ἕρκος ὀδόντων; 19.236 aligned with the book's own 19.495/19.570
  close "lay it away in your spirit."
- Fidelity recovered along the way: δῶκα at 197, ἴσκε's "semblance" at 203,
  κῆρ kept as "heart" at 516, καλά as "beauty" at 417, ἐκέχυντο at 539,
  σε at 492, iterative δόσκον at 76; padding cut at 297 ("of the god") and
  418 ("in the hall"), neither in the Greek.
- Honest costs logged: "on the matter" at 302, "swift" at 450, "all" at 543 —
  each a single light word buying the cadence, far under the filler removed.
- 24 of the 44 draft entries required rewriting (scansion short or overweight:
  57, 76, 197, 203, 280, 297, 300, 311, 417, 470; join breaks: 435, and
  punctuation joins at 477, 543; masculine over budget: 227; formula: 236;
  fidelity: 197, 516, 539; register/diction: 203, 311, 447; plus minor metrical
  or typographic corrections at 256, 422, 433, 457, 492, 566).
- Eleven verses still end in deictic "here" (66, 304, 344, 348, 374, 488, 496,
  524, 579, 585, 598) — all in direct speech, pointing at present persons and
  places, outside the extraction's filler set; left untouched by design.

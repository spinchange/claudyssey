# Book 10 — "Then"-opening audit

31 verses opened with *Then*. **7 kept** (the Greek carries a real temporal
adverb), **24 rewritten** (the Greek carries only a light connective).

**Warrant rule.** A verse keeps its opening *Then* only where the Greek line has
τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα / αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα, or an ἦμος-clause.
Where the line opens on bare δέ, αὐτάρ / ἀτάρ, ὣς + verb, ἦ ῥα, καί, γάρ, τε, or
asyndeton, the *Then* is the translator's tic, not Homer's adverb, and it goes.
Replacements follow the actual connective: αὐτάρ → *But* / *Yet*; δέ → a light
*And*, or (preferred) no connective at all with the subject, object, or verb
carried to the front, as the Greek itself does. *Straightway* is used once only,
where the Greek has αἶψα. Every replacement line still scans as six falling beats.

- OLD: Then we came to Aeaea’s island, where beautiful-haired and

**10.161** τὸν δʼ ἐγώ — δέ, object-initial
- OLD: Then, as he stepped from the woodland, I struck him full in the backbone;
- NEW: As he stepped forth from the woodland, I struck him full in the backbone;
- "stepped forth" is closer to ἐκβαίνοντα than the old bare "stepped."

**10.166** αὐτὰρ ἐγώ

- NEW: But I pulled upward the brushwood and gathered the flexible willow,

**10.181** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ τάρπησαν
- OLD: Then, when their hearts had taken full pleasure in gazing upon it,
- NEW: After their hearts had taken full pleasure in gazing upon it,

**10.203** αὐτὰρ ἐγώ
- OLD: Then I divided the whole of my strong-greaved men who attended
- NEW: Now I divided the whole of my strong-greaved men who attended
- *But* was available but would have made a sixth *But*-opening in the book's
  rewrites; the sense of αὐτάρ here is resumptive, not adversative.

**10.224** τοῖσι δὲ μύθων ἦρχε — δέ, with τοῖσι ("to them") fronted
- OLD: Then Polites started to speak to the men who were standing around him,
- NEW: Speaking among them, Polites began to address his companions,
- τοῖσι is now the line's first element, as in the Greek. The old line's "who
  were standing around him" was an expansion of that same dative and is not lost,
  only compressed back into it.

**10.244** Εὐρύλοχος δʼ αἶψʼ ἦλθε — δέ, but the line does carry αἶψα
- OLD: Then Eurylochus came running again to the swift black vessel,
- NEW: Straightway Eurylochus came running back to the swift black vessel,
- The one licensed *Straightway* in this pass: αἶψα is in the Greek and the old
  line had dropped it. "again" also went, since πάλιν is not there; "back" carries
  the return without asserting repetition.

**10.316** τεῦχε δέ μοι — δέ, verb-initial
- OLD: Then in a golden cup she prepared me a potion for drinking,
- NEW: She made ready a potion for me in a cup made of gold-work,
- Subject-and-verb first. "gold-work" matches the book's own rendering of the
  golden vessels at 10.357. Cost noted below.

**10.318** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δῶκέν τε καὶ ἔκπιον
- OLD: Then, when she gave it, I drank, and the potion in no way bewitched me;
- NEW: When she had given it, I drank, and the potion in no way bewitched me;

**10.346** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῥʼ ὄμοσεν — the τότε belongs to 347 (καὶ τότʼ ἐγώ), not here
- OLD: Then, when she finished the oath and had sworn every word to completion,
- NEW: But when she finished the oath and had sworn every word to completion,

**10.360** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δή — δή emphatic, not τότε
- OLD: Then, when the water had boiled in the brightly shining bronze cauldron,
- NEW: After the water had boiled in the brightly shining bronze cauldron,

**10.364** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ λοῦσέν τε
- OLD: Then, when she bathed me and richly anointed my body with olive oil,
- NEW: Once she had bathed me and richly anointed my body with olive oil,

**10.368** χέρνιβα δʼ ἀμφίπολος — δέ, object-initial
- OLD: Then an attendant brought water for washing my hands in a pitcher,
- NEW: Water for washing my hands an attendant brought in a pitcher,
- χέρνιβα leads the Greek line and now leads the English one.

**10.371** σῖτον δʼ αἰδοίη ταμίη — δέ, object-initial
- OLD: Then the respected housekeeper carried the bread and set it
- NEW: Bread the respected housekeeper carried in with her and set it
- σῖτον fronted, as in the Greek; "carried in with her" answers φέρουσα.

**10.375** Κίρκη δʼ ὡς ἐνόησεν — δέ, subject-initial
- OLD: Then, when Circe observed me sitting and not putting forward
- NEW: Circe, when once she perceived me sitting and not putting forward
- ἐνόησεν is "perceived / took note of," a shade sharper than "observed."

**10.393** τῶν δʼ ἐκ μὲν μελέων — δέ
- OLD: Then all the bristles the ruinous drug had made grow on their bodies,
- NEW: All of the bristles the ruinous drug had made grow on their bodies,

**10.542** ἀμφὶ δέ με … εἵματα ἕσσεν — δέ, with ἀμφί fronted
- OLD: Then she arrayed me in garments, a cloak with a tunic beneath it;
- NEW: Round me she cast for my clothing a cloak with a tunic beneath it;
- ἀμφί ("round") was unrendered in the old line and now opens the verse, exactly
  where the Greek puts it.

## Kept as warranted

**10.31** ἔνθʼ (temporal) · **10.100** δὴ τότʼ · **10.151** ἔπειτα ·
**10.185** ἦμος δʼ · **10.237** αὐτίκʼ ἔπειτα · **10.307** ἔπειτʼ ·
**10.529** ἔνθα δέ

No three of these fall within ten lines of one another, so no warranted *Then*
had to be varied for crowding.

## Opening distribution across the 24 rewrites

*But* 4 (25, 118, 166, 346) · *After* 2 (181, 360) · *Now* 2 (58, 203) ·
*When* 2 (112, 318) · *And* 1 · *Yet* 1 · *Once* 1 · *As* 1 · *Unto* 1 ·
*Straightway* 1 · subject-first 3 (224, 316, 375) · object-first 3 (368, 371, 393) ·
adverbial-phrase-first 1 (542).

No opening exceeds 4 of 24 (17%). *And* is used exactly once and *So* not at all,
so neither becomes the new metronome. Ten of the twenty-four now open on the
subject, object, verb, or the Greek's own fronted element, with no connective —
the most invisible option, and the one the Greek itself most often takes.

## Costs and flags

- **10.316** ὄφρα πίοιμι ("so that I might drink") is folded into the sense rather
  than spelled out; the old line's "for drinking" is gone. The cup's material and
  the making of the κυκεών both survive. Worth a second look.
- **10.224** loses the old "who were standing around him," which was an expansion
  of τοῖσι rather than a translation of anything separate. The compression is
  deliberate but is the largest content shift in the pass.
- **10.185** keeps its *Then* under the ἦμος rule, but the Greek pattern is
  ἦμος (185) … δὴ τότε (186): the true apodosis-"then" belongs to 186, which
  currently opens "there we lay down to sleep." A later pass may want to move the
  emphasis one line down and open 185 with a plain "When."

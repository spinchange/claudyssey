# Book 17 "Then"-opening revision

Examined: 50 verse-initial "Then"s. Kept 6 (Greek warrants a real temporal
adverb); rewrote 44 whose Greek carries only a light connective.

Warrant rule: a verse-initial "Then" stands only where the Greek line has a
genuine temporal adverb — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα,
temporal ἔνθα, ἦμος-clauses. Lines opened by bare δέ, δ᾿ αὖ/αὖτε, αὐτάρ,
ἀτάρ, ὣς + verb, καί, ἄρα, or asyndeton lose the "Then" and take an opening
that reflects the actual connective: αὐτάρ → "But"; δέ → light "And",
subject-first, verb-first, or object-first (mirroring the Greek's own order);
reply formulas (τὸν δ᾿ ἀπαμειβόμενος, τὸν/τὴν δ᾿ αὖτε προσέειπε) → answer-
formulas without "Then"; "Straightway"/"At dawn" only where the Greek has
αἶψα/ἠῶθεν. No replacement opening exceeds a fifth of the rewrites
("And" is the most used at 7 of 44). Every rewritten line still scans as six
falling beats; no edited line ends in filler; verse count unchanged (606).

## Kept (warranted) — "Then" stands

17.33 (ἔπειτ᾿), 17.61 (ἔπειτα), 17.123 (καὶ τότε δή), 17.280 (ἠμείβετ᾿
ἔπειτα), 17.301 (δὴ τότε γ᾿), 17.428 (ἔνθ᾿ ἦ τοι, temporal ἔνθα). No
crowding remains: no three "Then"-openings fall within ten lines after the
rewrites (closest pairs are 33/61 and 280/301, each ~21–28 lines apart).

Notes for review: 17.280 is the reply formula τὸν δ᾿ ἠμείβετ᾿ ἔπειτα — kept
on the strength of its explicit ἔπειτα, though the formula is arguably
fossilized. 17.61 now reads "Then … afterward," a double rendering of one
ἔπειτα; trimming it is a separate (warranted-line) polish. 17.369's epithet
was normalized to "goatherd and master of she-goats" to match the Greek
(αἰπόλος αἰγῶν) and the identical formula at 17.247.

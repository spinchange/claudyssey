# Book 21 de-filler pilot — all 57 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek itself carries the
   locative/temporal sense at that position (one case: 21.90, αὐτόθι "right here").
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, names, plurals, compounds), not adding new words.
4. A masculine ending (line closing on the stress) is a licensed pressure valve,
   budgeted at 2–3 per book. Used twice: 21.31, 21.225. (21.423's speech-intro
   "Then he said:" close is inherited from the old text and kept.)
5. Where the Greek repeats a formula verbatim, the English now does too
   (21.44 = 21.121 στάθμην ἴθυνεν; 21.178 = 21.183; 21.360 ≈ 21.367 ὁμόκλεον ἐν
   μεγάροισιν). 21.358's γλαυκῶπις is normalized to "gray-eyed" (the old line had
   "bright-eyed" against "gray-eyed" everywhere else, including 21.1).

Adjacent lines are shown (indented) only where a fix forced a consequential edit.

**21.18** μῆλα γὰρ ἐξ Ἰθάκης Μεσσήνιοι ἄνδρες ἄειραν
- OLD: for Messenian men had lifted from Ithaca sheep there,
- NEW: for the Messenian men had lifted from Ithaca sheep-flocks,

**21.29** τὴν ἥν οἱ παρέθηκεν· ἔπειτα δὲ πέφνε καὶ αὐτόν
- OLD: that which he set before him, but after that slew him as well there,
- NEW: that which he set before him; and afterward murdered him also,
- ἔπειτα is honest ("afterward"), just not dangling at the close.

**21.30** ἵππους δʼ αὐτὸς ἔχε κρατερώνυχας ἐν μεγάροισι
- OLD: and himself kept the strong-hoofed horses within his own hall there.
- NEW: and he himself in his halls kept hold of the strong-hoofed horses.

**21.31** τὰς ἐρέων Ὀδυσῆϊ συνήντετο, δῶκε δὲ τόξον
- OLD: Asking of these, he encountered Odysseus and gave him the bow there,
- NEW: Asking of these, he encountered Odysseus and gave him the bow,
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the bow is the object of the whole digression;
  closing the line on it is emphasis, not accident.

**21.44** ξέσσεν ἐπισταμένως καὶ ἐπὶ στάθμην ἴθυνεν
- OLD: planing it well with his skill, had straightened true to the line there,
- NEW: planing it well with his skill, had straightened it true to the line-rule,
- Formula: καὶ ἐπὶ στάθμην ἴθυνεν recurs at 21.121 and 23.197; "true to the
  line-rule" now recurs with it.

**21.47** ἐν δὲ κληῗδʼ ἧκε, θυρέων δʼ ἀνέκοπτεν ὀχῆας
- OLD: thrust in the key, and she pushed back the bolts of the doors there,
- NEW: thrust in the key, and she hammered back the bolts of the doorway,
- ἀνέκοπτεν is "struck back" — "hammered" is closer than "pushed" anyway.

**21.53** ἔνθεν ὀρεξαμένη ἀπὸ πασσάλου αἴνυτο τόξον
- OLD: Reaching up from that place, from a peg she took down the bow there,
- NEW: Thence she reached up and took the bow down from the peg it was hung on,

**21.65** ἄντα παρειάων σχομένη λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα
- OLD: drawing the shining veil before her face and her cheeks there;
- NEW: holding before her cheeks the shining folds of her head-veil;
- κρήδεμνα is literally the head-veil; "her face and" was itself padding.

**21.75** ὃς δέ κε ῥηΐτατʼ ἐντανύσῃ βιὸν ἐν παλάμῃσι
- OLD: Whoever most easily strings this bow in the grip of his hands there,
- NEW: Whoever among you most easily strings the bow in his fingers,

**21.82** δακρύσας δʼ Εὔμαιος ἐδέξατο καὶ κατέθηκε
- OLD: Weeping, Eumaeus received them, and laid them carefully down there;
- NEW: Weeping, Eumaeus received them and laid them down on the floor-boards;
- "carefully" was also filler; "floor-boards" (the poem's own word at 21.51)
  concretizes κατέθηκε's "down."

**21.83** κλαῖε δὲ βουκόλος ἄλλοθʼ, ἐπεὶ ἴδε τόξον ἄνακτος
- OLD: and on the other side the cowherd wept, seeing his master's bow there.
- NEW: elsewhere the cowherd wept when he looked on the bow of his master.
- ἄλλοθι = "elsewhere," restored to its true position.

**21.89–90** ἀλλʼ ἀκέων δαίνυσθε καθήμενοι, ἠὲ θύραζε / κλαίετον ἐξελθόντε, κατʼ αὐτόθι τόξα λιπόντε
- OLD: No, sit quiet and feast where you are, or else go outside there
       / and do your weeping, once you have left the bow here where it lies now,
- NEW: No, sit down and feast in silence, or take yourselves outdoors
       / both of you, weep out there, but leave the bow where it lies here,
- 89's close renders θύραζε ("out the door") itself. 90's "here" is the one
  retained locative in this book: αὐτόθι is in the Greek.

**21.102** ὢ πόποι, ἦ μάλα με Ζεὺς ἄφρονα θῆκε Κρονίων
- OLD: "Father Zeus, son of Cronos, has made me witless indeed now.
- NEW: "Oh for shame! Zeus the son of Cronos has made me witless.
- ὢ πόποι regained its exclamation (the poem renders it "Oh for shame" at 22.249).

**21.111** ἀλλʼ ἄγε μὴ μύνῃσι παρέλκετε μηδʼ ἔτι τόξου
- OLD: Come then, do not keep drawing this out with evasions and delays now,
- NEW: Come then, drag this out no more with evasions and pleadings,
- ("no longer" avoided: 21.112 already has "any longer.")

**21.113** καὶ δέ κεν αὐτὸς ἐγὼ τοῦ τόξου πειρησαίμην
- OLD: I myself also would make trial and test of the bow now:
- NEW: Yes, and I too would myself make trial and test of the weapon:

**21.120** πρῶτον μὲν πελέκεας στῆσεν, διὰ τάφρον ὀρύξας
- OLD: First he set up the axes, after he dug out a trench there,
- NEW: First he set up the axes, after digging a trench out,

**21.121** πᾶσι μίαν μακρήν, καὶ ἐπὶ στάθμην ἴθυνεν
- OLD: one long trench for them all, and he brought them true to the line there,
- NEW: one long trench for them all, and he straightened them true to the line-rule,
- Now echoes 21.44 verbatim where the Greek half-lines are identical.

**21.123–124** ὡς εὐκόσμως στῆσε· πάρος δʼ οὐ πώ ποτʼ ὀπώπει / στῆ δʼ ἄρʼ ἐπʼ οὐδὸν ἰὼν καὶ τόξου πειρήτιζε
- OLD: who looked on, seeing how straight he had set them, though never before then
       / had he seen it. Then he went to the threshold and tested the bow there.
- NEW: who looked on, seeing how straight he had set them, though he had never
       / seen them before. Going up to the threshold, he tested the bow-stave.

**21.129** ἀλλʼ Ὀδυσεὺς ἀνένευε καὶ ἔσχεθεν ἱέμενόν περ
- OLD: but Odysseus signaled no, and restrained him despite his desire there.
- NEW: but Odysseus signaled him no, and for all his desire restrained him.

**21.134** ἀλλʼ ἄγεθʼ, οἵ περ ἐμεῖο βίῃ προφερέστεροί ἐστε
- OLD: Come then, you who are stronger in strength by far than I am now,
- NEW: Come then, you who are mightier men in force than I am,

**21.136** ὣς εἰπὼν τόξον μὲν ἀπὸ ἕο θῆκε χαμᾶζε
- OLD: So he spoke, and laid the bow away from himself on the ground there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and away from himself he laid the bow earthward,
- "-ward" compounds are already the poem's idiom (homeward, earthward at 22.20).

**21.152** ὦ φίλοι, οὐ μὲν ἐγὼ τανύω, λαβέτω δὲ καὶ ἄλλος
- OLD: "Friends, I cannot string it. Let some other one take it and try now.
- NEW: "Friends, I cannot string it. Let somebody else come and take it.

**21.159** αὐτὰρ ἐπὴν τόξου πειρήσεται ἠδὲ ἴδηται
- OLD: But when he has tested this bow and seen what it truly is like then,
- NEW: But when he has made his trial of the bow and has seen it clearly,

**21.168** Λειῶδες, ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων
- OLD: "Leodes, what sort of a word has fled past the fence of your teeth there,
- NEW: "Leodes, out past the fence of your teeth what a word has escaped you,
- σε ... φύγεν: the word escapes *him* — the fix restores the pronoun the old
  line dropped, and the famous formula keeps its place.

**21.178** ἐκ δὲ στέατος ἔνεικε μέγαν τροχὸν ἔνδον ἐόντος
- OLD: and from the stores bring out a great round of lard from within there,
- NEW: and from the stores bring out a great round of the lard kept within them,

**21.179** ὄφρα νέοι θάλποντες, ἐπιχρίοντες ἀλοιφῇ
- OLD: so the young men may warm it and smear it well with the grease then,
- NEW: so that the young men may warm it and smear it all over with ointment,
- ἀλοιφή (the rendered grease) gets its own word, distinct from στέαρ (lard).

**21.181** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ αἶψʼ ἀνέκαιε Μελάνθιος ἀκάματον πῦρ
- OLD: So he spoke, and at once Melanthius kindled the tireless fire there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and at once Melanthius kindled the tireless fire.
- No new words needed: verse-final "fire" scans as its old disyllable (fi-re),
  giving the shortened final foot on its own.

**21.183** ἐκ δὲ στέατος ἔνεικε μέγαν τροχὸν ἔνδον ἐόντος
- OLD: and from the stores he brought a great round of lard from within there.
- NEW: and from the stores he brought a great round of the lard kept within them.
- Verbatim echo of 21.178, as in the Greek.

**21.203** ὣς δʼ αὔτως Εὔμαιος ἐπεύχετο πᾶσι θεοῖσι
- OLD: And in the same way Eumaeus prayed to all of the gods there
- NEW: And in the same way Eumaeus was praying to all the immortals

**21.225** ὣς δʼ αὔτως Ὀδυσεὺς κεφαλὰς καὶ χεῖρας ἔκυσσε
- OLD: and in the same way Odysseus kissed their heads and their hands there.
- NEW: and in the same way Odysseus kissed their heads and their hands.
- Licensed masculine ending #2: the scene's emotional close; the full stop after
  the stressed monosyllable is the point.

**21.234** ἀλλὰ σύ, δῖʼ Εὔμαιε, φέρων ἀνὰ δώματα τόξον
- OLD: But you, noble Eumaeus, must carry the bow through the hall there
- NEW: But you, noble Eumaeus, must carry the bow through the dwelling

**21.247** ἐντανύσαι δύνατο, μέγα δʼ ἔστενε κυδάλιμον κῆρ
- OLD: yet not even so could he string it, and loudly his proud heart groaned there.
- NEW: yet not even so could he string it, and greatly his proud heart was groaning.

**21.261** ἑστάμεν· οὐ μὲν γάρ τινʼ ἀναιρήσεσθαι ὀΐω
- OLD: For I do not think that anyone ever will carry them off now,
- NEW: For I do not think that anyone ever intends to remove them,

**21.286** δείσαντες μὴ τόξον ἐΰξοον ἐντανύσειεν
- OLD: fearing lest he might truly string the well-polished bow there.
- NEW: fearing that he might string it — the bow with its beautiful polish.
- "truly" was a second filler in the same line; both gone.

**21.296** ἄασʼ ἐνὶ μεγάρῳ μεγαθύμου Πειριθόοιο
- OLD: in the hall of Peirithous, that great-hearted lord among men there,
- NEW: in the high hall of the great-hearted lord Peirithoüs,
- The hardest line in the book: the name's stress contour (pei-RI-tho-us) fights
  the cadence. "lord among men" was pure padding; "high" is the residual cost.

**21.304** οἷ δʼ αὐτῷ πρώτῳ κακὸν εὕρετο οἰνοβαρείων
- OLD: but he, being wine-weighed down, found evil first for himself there.
- NEW: but he himself, being burdened with wine, was first to find evil.

**21.316** οἴκαδέ μʼ ἄξεσθαι καὶ ἑὴν θήσεσθαι ἄκοιτιν
- OLD: he will lead me homeward with him and make me his own wife there?
- NEW: he will conduct me home to his house and make me his bedmate?
- ἄκοιτις = "bedmate" is the poem's own rendering at 1.39.

**21.333** ἀνδρὸς ἀριστῆος· τί δʼ ἐλέγχεα ταῦτα τίθεσθε;
- OLD: of a man who is better than they are. Why make this a disgrace, then?
- NEW: of a nobler man than themselves. Why make these things your reproaches?
- ἐλέγχεα is plural "reproaches"; the plural also supplies the feminine close.

**21.350** ἀλλʼ εἰς οἶκον ἰοῦσα τὰ σʼ αὐτῆς ἔργα κόμιζε
- OLD: But go to the house and attend to the works that are yours there,
- NEW: But go into the house and see to your own proper labors,

**21.354** ἡ μὲν θαμβήσασα πάλιν οἶκόνδε βεβήκει
- OLD: She in amazement turned and went back again to the house there,
- NEW: She in her wonder turned and went back again to her chamber,

**21.358** ἡδὺν ἐπὶ βλεφάροισι βάλε γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη
- OLD: till on her eyelids bright-eyed Athena cast sweet sleep there.
- NEW: till on her eyelids gray-eyed Athena cast a sweet slumber.
- γλαυκῶπις normalized to "gray-eyed" (as at 21.1 and throughout).

**21.360** μνηστῆρες δʼ ἄρα πάντες ὁμόκλεον ἐν μεγάροισιν
- OLD: and all the suitors at once raised a clamor through the hall there.
- NEW: and all through the halls the suitors together were shouting against him.
- ὁμόκλεον is "shouted (threateningly) together" — at Eumaeus.

**21.367** δείσας, οὕνεκα πολλοὶ ὁμόκλεον ἐν μεγάροισιν
- OLD: frightened, because many men were shouting together in hall there.
- NEW: frightened, because many men in the halls were shouting against him.
- Same Greek half-line as 21.360, now the same English.

**21.370** μή σε καὶ ὁπλότερός περ ἐὼν ἀγρόνδε δίωμαι
- OLD: Otherwise, though younger than you, I will drive you to the fields there,
- NEW: Otherwise, though I am younger than you, I will drive you out fieldward,

**21.379** ἐν χείρεσσʼ Ὀδυσῆϊ δαΐφρονι θῆκε παραστάς
- OLD: and stood by Odysseus, master of craft, and put it into his hands there.
- NEW: and stood beside him and put it into the hands of Odysseus.
- Known cost: δαΐφρονι ("battle-minded") is dropped; keeping both the epithet and
  παραστάς ("standing beside") without filler defeated me in one line. Flagged for
  a second pass.

**21.386** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν, τῇ δʼ ἄπτερος ἔπλετο μῦθος
- OLD: So he spoke, and for her the word was wingless and stayed there;
- NEW: So he spoke, and for her the word he had uttered was wingless;

**21.391** βύβλινον, ᾧ ῥʼ ἐπέδησε θύρας, ἐς δʼ ἤϊεν αὐτός
- OLD: twisted of byblus, with which he bound fast the gates, then returned there,
- NEW: twisted of byblus, with which he bound fast the gates; then he entered,
- ἐς δʼ ἤϊεν = "went in"; the old "returned there / and going back" doubled it
  with 392.

**21.393** εἰσορόων Ὀδυσῆα. ὁ δʼ ἤδη τόξον ἐνώμα
- OLD: watching Odysseus. And he already was handling the bow there,
- NEW: watching Odysseus. And he already was handling the bow-stave,

**21.399** ἢ ὅ γʼ ἐφορμᾶται ποιησέμεν, ὡς ἐνὶ χερσὶ
- OLD: or he is eager to make one, so he turns it in his hands there,
- NEW: or he is eager to make one, so in his hands he revolves it
- "revolves" is the poem's own verb for the drill-turn at 9.387.

**21.408** ἅψας ἀμφοτέρωθεν ἐϋστρεφὲς ἔντερον οἰός
- OLD: fixing the well-twisted gut at the two ends with ease there,
- NEW: when he has fastened at either end the well-twisted sheep-gut,
- Restores οἰός ("of sheep"), which the old line dropped; cuts "with ease,"
  which doubled 407's ῥηϊδίως.

**21.414** γήθησέν τʼ ἄρʼ ἔπειτα πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς
- OLD: and then much-enduring noble Odysseus rejoiced there,
- NEW: and thereupon much-enduring noble Odysseus was gladdened,

**21.417** γυμνός· τοὶ δʼ ἄλλοι κοίλης ἔντοσθε φαρέτρης
- OLD: all of the others within the hollow quiver were stored there,
- NEW: all of the others were lying inside of the hollow quiver,

**21.422–423** πρώτης στειλειῆς, διὰ δʼ ἀμπερὲς ἦλθε θύραζε
- OLD: starting with the first socket, and clean through all of them passed then
       / the bronze-heavy arrow and went on through to the outside. Then he said:
- NEW: starting from the first socket, and clean through the whole of them passing,
       / out went the bronze-heavy arrow, through to the outside. Then he said:

**21.431** ἦ καὶ ἐπʼ ὀφρύσι νεῦσεν· ὁ δʼ ἀμφέθετο ξίφος ὀξὺ
- OLD: So he spoke and nodded the signal with brows to his son there;
- NEW: So he spoke, and he nodded the signal to him with his eyebrows;

**21.434** πὰρ θρόνον ἑστήκει κεκορυθμένος αἴθοπι χαλκῷ
- OLD: and stood by his father's throne in glittering armor of bronze there.
- NEW: and stood beside the throne, full-armed in the glittering war-bronze.
- The book's final line now closes on the image instead of on a particle.

## Tally

- 57 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*
  (21.90 keeps "here" for αὐτόθι, which is in the Greek).
- 2 masculine endings licensed (21.31, 21.225), within the 2–3/book budget.
- 3 formula pairs restored to verbatim echo where the Greek repeats
  (44/121, 178/183, 360/367); 1 epithet normalized (358 "gray-eyed").
- Fidelity recovered along the way: οἰός at 408, ἄλλοθι at 83, the exclamation
  at 102, the plural at 333; secondary padding cut at 82, 286, 408.
- Honest costs incurred: δαΐφρονι dropped at 379 (flagged); "high" added at 296;
  "floor-boards" concretized at 82. Total added-word residue across 57 lines is
  far below what the old lines carried in filler.

# Book 6 "Then"-opening pass — 20 examined, 4 kept, 16 rewritten

Warrant rule: a verse may open with "Then" only where the Greek carries a real
temporal adverb at that position — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα,
temporal ἔνθα, or an ἦμος-clause. Verses whose Greek offers only a light
connective (bare δέ, αὐτάρ, ἀτάρ, ὣς + verb, ἦ ῥα / ἦ τοι, καί, τε, asyndeton)
lose the "Then" and open instead from their actual connective and word order.
All replacement lines hold the project meter: six falling beats, dactyls
predominating, spondaic substitution allowed, final foot preferably disyllabic.
No replacement opening exceeds ~1/5 of the rewrites ("But" ×3, "And" ×2, all
others once each); no rewritten line ends in filler *there/now/then/here*.

---

**06.74** bare δέ (κούρη δʼ ἐκ θαλάμοιο)
- OLD: Then from her chamber the maiden carried the beautiful garments,
- NEW: Out of her chamber the maiden carried the beautiful garments,

**06.81** bare δέ (ἡ δʼ ἔλαβεν μάστιγα)
- OLD: Then she took hold of the whip and the reins that were glossy and shining,
- NEW: Grasping the whip, she took up the reins that were glossy and shining,

**06.85** bare δέ + ὅτε-clause (αἱ δʼ ὅτε δὴ ... ἵκοντο)
- OLD: Then, when they came to the beautiful stream of the flowing river,
- NEW: And when they came to the beautiful stream of the flowing river,

**06.93** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί
- OLD: Then, when they thoroughly washed every garment and cleansed it of all dirt,
- NEW: But when they thoroughly washed every garment and cleansed it of all dirt,

**06.96** bare δέ + aorist participles (αἱ δὲ λοεσσάμεναι καὶ χρισάμεναι)
- OLD: Then, when they bathed and had richly anointed their bodies with olive oil,
- NEW: After they bathed and had richly anointed their bodies with olive oil,
- The ἔπειτα of the sentence sits in 97 (δεῖπνον ἔπειθʼ εἵλοντο) and is carried
  by the "After ... they ate" sequence, not by a fronted "Then".

**06.99** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί
- OLD: Then, when desire for food and for eating was put from their bodies,
- NEW: Yet when desire for food and for eating was put from their bodies,
- Varied against 93's "But when" — αὐτάρ tolerates either; three αὐτάρ/δέ
  when-clauses within fifteen lines (85, 93, 99) now open three different ways.

**06.130** bare δέ, verb-first (βῆ δʼ ἴμεν ὥς τε λέων)
- OLD: Then he advanced like a mountain-bred lion assured of his power,
- NEW: Forth he advanced like a mountain-bred lion assured of his power,

**06.145** ὣς ἄρα (ὣς ἄρα οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο)
- OLD: Then, as he pondered, this seemed in his heart to be better and wiser:
- NEW: So, as he pondered, this seemed in his heart to be better and wiser:
- ὥς is literally "so"; the poem's ὣς-formula opening restored.

**06.186** reply formula (τὸν δʼ αὖ ... ἀντίον ηὔδα)
- OLD: Then in her turn the white-armed Nausicaa answered Odysseus:
- NEW: And in her turn the white-armed Nausicaa answered Odysseus:
- αὖ ("in turn") already lives in the line; the "Then" was doubling it.

**06.224** αὐτάρ (αὐτὰρ ὁ ἐκ ποταμοῦ)
- OLD: Then in the river illustrious lordly Odysseus bathed him,
- NEW: But in the river illustrious lordly Odysseus bathed him,
- αὐτάρ marks the turn of subject (the women withdraw; but he bathed).

**06.227** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δή
- OLD: Then, when he washed himself thoroughly, rubbing with oil all his body,
- NEW: Once he had washed himself thoroughly, rubbing with oil all his body,
- ἐπεὶ δή + aorist = "once he had"; also breaks the 224/227/229 "Then" cluster.

**06.229** τὸν μέν (asyndetic apodosis)
- OLD: then did Athena, the daughter of Zeus, make him taller in stature,
- NEW: him did Athena, the daughter of Zeus, make taller in stature,
- The fronted "him" is the Greek's own τὸν μέν, restored to its true position;
  no temporal adverb stands in the line.

**06.249** ἦ τοι (ἦ τοι ὁ πῖνε καὶ ἦσθε)
- OLD: Then great-hearted and much-enduring lordly Odysseus
- NEW: Truly great-hearted and much-enduring lordly Odysseus
- ἦ τοι ("verily") was dropped by the old line; "Truly" renders it and fills
  the vacated first beat exactly.

**06.254** bare δέ, verb-first (ὤτρυνεν δʼ Ὀδυσῆα)
- OLD: Then she encouraged Odysseus and spoke, addressing him plainly:
- NEW: Urging Odysseus onward, she spoke, addressing him plainly:

**06.303** ἀλλά (ἀλλʼ ὁπότʼ ἄν σε δόμοι κεκύθωσι)
- OLD: Then, when the palace and courtyard have covered you there from all eyesight,
- NEW: But when the palace and courtyard have covered you there from all eyesight,
- ἀλλʼ ὁπότε is exactly "But when."

**06.321** τε (δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος)
- OLD: Then the sun entered the west, and they came to the glorious grove-land,
- NEW: Down went the sun in the west, and they came to the glorious grove-land,
- δύσετο ("sank/set") now opens the line verb-first, as in the Greek.

## Kept — warranted "Then"-openings

- **06.115** σφαῖραν ἔπειτʼ ἔρριψε — ἔπειτα
- **06.217** δή ῥα τότʼ ... μετηύδα — δὴ τότε
- **06.298** καὶ τότε Φαιήκων ἴμεν — καὶ τότε

No crowding among the keeps (112/115 are only a pair; 217 and 298 stand alone).
112 and 298 are lowercase mid-sentence apodoses; 115 and 217 are the two
remaining capital "Then" openings in the book.

# Book 9 — "Then"-opening audit

37 verses in Book 9 opened with *Then* / *then* (one line in ten). **16 kept, 21 rewritten.**

**Warrant rule.** A *Then* stays only where the Greek carries a real temporal adverb —
τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα, or the apodosis of an
ἦμος-clause. Where the Greek has only a light connective — bare δέ, αὐτάρ, ἀτάρ, ἀλλά,
καί, ὣς + verb, or a speech-reply formula — the *Then* is the translator’s tic, not
Homer’s, and it goes. Replacements reflect the actual connective: αὐτάρ/ἀλλά → "But"/"Yet";
δέ → light "And", or subject-first / verb-first / participle-first with no connective at all;
speech-reply formulas → answer-formulas without *Then*; adverbs only where the Greek has one
(ἄφαρ at 328).

Where the Greek repeats a formula verbatim (250 = 310 = 343 αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δὴ σπεῦσε…;
168 = 558 ἦμος δʼ ἠέλιος κατέδυ…), the English repeats verbatim too.

---

**09.1** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος — speech-reply formula, no temporal
- OLD: Then resourceful Odysseus spoke and addressed him in answer:
- NEW: And in answer resourceful Odysseus spoke and addressed him:

**09.79** καί νύ κεν — καί + νυ, "and now I would have…"
- OLD: Then I had come without harm to the country I call my own country,
- NEW: Now I had come without harm to the country I call my own country,
- νυ is the actual particle; "Now" renders it, and the unreal past stays intact.

**09.87** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί — αὐτάρ, light adversative
- OLD: Then, when we tasted our measure of food and the drink we were given,
- NEW: But when we tasted our measure of food and the drink we were given,

**09.149** κελσάσῃσι δὲ νηυσὶ — bare δέ
- OLD: Then, when the ships had been beached, we took all of the sails from above them,
- NEW: Once that the ships had been beached, we took all of the sails from above them,

**09.168** ἦμος δʼ ἠέλιος κατέδυ — this is the ἦμος **protasis**, not its apodosis
- OLD: Then the sun entered the west and the darkness descended around us;
- NEW: When the sun entered the west and the darkness descended around us,
- The old line stole the apodosis marker for the subordinate clause. 169 ("there we lay
  down to sleep…") is the apodosis and now reads as one. Semicolon → comma accordingly.
  Formula shared with 558.

**09.250** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δή — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then, when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,
- NEW: But when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,
- Formula; identical at 310 and 343.

**09.296** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then, when the Cyclops had filled his enormous belly completely,
- NEW: Now when the Cyclops had filled his enormous belly completely,

**09.299** τὸν μὲν ἐγὼ βούλευσα — μέν, no connective force; subject pronoun fronted
- OLD: Then in the depth of my own great-hearted spirit I pondered,
- NEW: I in the depth of my own great-hearted spirit was pondering,
- Subject-first, which is what the Greek does (ἐγώ is emphatic here).

**09.310** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δή — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then, when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,
- NEW: But when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,

**09.312** δειπνήσας δʼ — bare δέ with aorist participle
- OLD: Then, when he fed, he drove all of the fat-lying flocks from the cavern,
- NEW: Having fed, he drove all of the fat-lying flocks from the cavern,
- Participle-first, matching δειπνήσας exactly.

**09.328** ἄφαρ δὲ λαβών — ἄφαρ, "forthwith"
- OLD: then in the fire’s blaze I hardened the point of the timber.
- NEW: straightway in the fire’s blaze I hardened the point of the timber.
- The one adverbial replacement in the book: the Greek genuinely has the adverb, and it
  was being spent on a temporal *then* it does not mean.

**09.331** αὐτὰρ τοὺς ἄλλους — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then I commanded the rest of the men to determine by lot-cast
- NEW: But I commanded the rest of the men to determine by lot-cast

**09.343** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δή — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then, when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,
- NEW: But when he rapidly finished the whole of the work he was doing,

**09.362** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then, when the wine had encircled the Cyclops’ thoughts and his reason,
- NEW: Once that the wine had encircled the Cyclops’ thoughts and his reason,

**09.378** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή — ἀλλά, adversative
- OLD: Then, when the olive stake in the fire, although it was green wood,
- NEW: Yet when the olive stake in the fire, although it was green wood,

**09.395** σμερδαλέον δὲ μέγʼ ᾤμωξεν — bare δέ; σμερδαλέον is the fronted adverb
- OLD: Then he cried out in a terrible giant voice, and the cavern
- NEW: Terribly loud he cried out in a giant voice, and the cavern
- σμερδαλέον now stands where the Greek puts it, at the head of the line.

**09.409** οἱ δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενοι — speech-reply formula
- OLD: Then in their turn they answered, and these words took wing as they uttered:
- NEW: They in their turn made answer, and these words took wing as they uttered:
- Subject-first: οἱ δέ is simply "and they."

**09.462** ἐλθόντες δʼ ἠβαιόν — bare δέ
- OLD: Then, when we came a small distance away from the cave and the courtyard,
- NEW: Once we had come a small distance off from the cave and the courtyard,

**09.537** αὐτὰρ ὅ γʼ ἐξαῦτις — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then once again the great Cyclops took up an even greater
- NEW: But once again the great Cyclops took up an even greater

**09.543** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή — ἀλλά
- OLD: Then, when we came to the island where all of the other well-benched ships
- NEW: Yet when we came to the island where all of the other well-benched ships

**09.558** ἦμος δʼ ἠέλιος κατέδυ — ἦμος protasis (see 168)
- OLD: Then the sun entered the west and the darkness descended around us;
- NEW: When the sun entered the west and the darkness descended around us,

**Kept as warranted (16):** 43 (ἔνθα, temporal), 59 (καὶ τότε δή), 153 (ἦμος-apodosis, 152),
171 (καὶ τότʼ), 193 (δὴ τότε), 204 (αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, enjambed from 203), 240 (αὐτὰρ ἔπειτʼ),
308 (καὶ τότε), 340 (αὐτὰρ ἔπειτʼ), 345 (καὶ τότʼ), 375 (καὶ τότʼ), 438 (καὶ τότʼ ἔπειτα),
458 (τῷ κε, conditional apodosis), 474 (καὶ τότʼ), 492 (καὶ τότε δή), 561 (δὴ τότʼ).

**Crowding.** The two clusters that ran three *Then*s inside ten lines resolved themselves:
308 / 310 / 312 became one keep (308, καὶ τότε) plus two rewrites, and 340 / 343 / 345
became two keeps (both real ἔπειτα / καὶ τότʼ) separated by a rewrite. No forced
de-warranting was needed.

**Distribution of new openings** (21 rewrites): "But when" ×3 (one repeated Greek formula),
"But" ×3, "Yet when" ×2, "Once that" ×2, "When" ×2 (one repeated formula), "Once we" ×1,
"Now" ×1, "Now when" ×1, "And in answer" ×1, "I" ×1, "They" ×1, "Having fed" ×1,
"Terribly loud" ×1, "straightway" ×1. Five are subject-, participle- or adverb-first with no
connective at all. No opening exceeds three instances.

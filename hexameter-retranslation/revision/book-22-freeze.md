**22.79** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας εἰρύσσατο φάσγανον ὀξὺ

- OLD: So he spoke, and drew forth his sharp sword from its scabbard,
- NEW: So having spoken, he drew forth his sharp sword from its scabbard,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας — participial; was carrying the finite formula's wording. Normalized to “So having spoken,”.

**22.101** ὦ πάτερ, ἤδη τοι σάκος οἴσω καὶ δύο δοῦρε

- OLD: “Father, now I will bring you a shield and a pair of spears here,
- NEW: “Father, now I will bring you a shield and a pair of spear-shafts,
- *JOB 2.* ἤδη τοι σάκος οἴσω καὶ δύο δοῦρε — no deictic; δοῦρε is the dual “two spears.” “spear-shafts” (the poem's own compound) closes.

**22.141** τεύχεα κατθέσθην Ὀδυσεὺς καὶ φαίδιμος υἱός.

- OLD: arms been stored by Odysseus and by his illustrious son here.”
- NEW: arms been stored by Odysseus and by his son the illustrious.”
- *JOB 2.* τεύχεα κατθέσθην Ὀδυσεὺς καὶ φαίδιμος υἱός — no deictic. Reordered so φαίδιμος (“illustrious”) closes the verse.

**22.142** ὣς εἰπὼν ἀνέβαινε Μελάνθιος, αἰπόλος αἰγῶν,

- OLD: So saying, Melanthius, goatherd of goats, went climbing upward,
- NEW: So having spoken, Melanthius, goatherd of goats, went climbing,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς εἰπὼν — participial; “So saying,” was a third variant. Normalized; “upward” dropped as ἀνέβαινε is already “went climbing.”

**22.216** ὁππότε κεν τούτους κτέωμεν, πατέρʼ ἠδὲ καὶ υἱόν,

- OLD: when we have killed these two, both the father and son, in the hall here,
- NEW: at the time when we kill these two, both the father and also the son,
- *JOB 2.* ὁππότε κεν τούτους κτέωμεν, πατέρʼ ἠδὲ καὶ υἱόν — no deictic and no “hall” at all in the Greek; both were imported. Recast on the Greek's own “father and son.”

**22.219** αὐτὰρ ἐπὴν ὑμέων γε βίας ἀφελώμεθα χαλκῷ,

- OLD: But when by bronze we have stripped the strength from the limbs of you all here,
- NEW: But when by the bronze we have stripped your strength from your bodies,
- *JOB 2.* ἐπὴν ὑμέων γε βίας ἀφελώμεθα χαλκῷ — no deictic; “of you all” is ὑμέων, kept. “bodies” closes.

**22.253** δώῃ Ὀδυσσῆα βλῆσθαι καὶ κῦδος ἀρέσθαι.

- OLD: that Odysseus be struck and that glory be won for ourselves here.
- NEW: that Odysseus be struck and that glory be won for our winning.
- *JOB 2.* δώῃ Ὀδυσσῆα βλῆσθαι καὶ κῦδος ἀρέσθαι — no deictic; ἀρέσθαι is “to win for oneself.” “our winning” renders the middle voice and closes.

**22.326** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας ξίφος εἵλετο χειρὶ παχείῃ

- OLD: So then, having spoken, he seized with his massive hand a sword
- NEW: So having spoken, he seized with his massive hand a sword
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας — participial; “So then, having spoken,” normalized to the fixed form (the “then” had no Greek warrant).

**22.360** ἠὲ σοὶ ἀντεβόλησεν ὀρινομένῳ κατὰ δῶμα.

- OLD: the swineherd, or he met your assault as you surged through the hall here.”
- NEW: the swineherd, or he met your assault as you surged through the great hall.”
- *JOB 2.* κατὰ δῶμα = “through the hall”, already rendered; “here” was redundant on top of it. “great hall” closes.

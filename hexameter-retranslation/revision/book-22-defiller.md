# Book 22 de-filler — all 48 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied, as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, spondees
   admitted toward pauses, final foot ending unstressed).
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, *then*, or *here* — no Greek in this book's 48
   targets warranted keeping one (ἔραζε at 280 and χαμᾶζε at 340 carry direction,
   rendered "earthward" / "down on the flooring", not a dangling particle).
3. The preferred fix was reordering so a trochaic word lands last; participles,
   names (Odysseus, Telemachus, Evenor), and the poem's own "-ward" and compound
   idiom (earthward, spear-shaft, doorpost, walling, flooring) carry most closes.
4. One masculine ending licensed (22.326), within the 3/book budget.
5. Formulas repeated verbatim in the Greek now repeat verbatim in English:
   131 = 247 (ἔπος πάντεσσι πιφαύσκων); 257 = 274 and 259 = 276 (the spear-volley
   pair); and 120 shares σταθμὸν ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο with 257/274, so all three
   now share "the stout-built great hall's doorpost." The speech-intro tail
   ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα is normalized to "spoke these winged words, saying:"
   at 100, 410, 436 (22.343/366, untouched, keep their λισσόμενος variant).
6. Voice rule checked: Athena's lines (226) use no lexical contractions.

A junior draft supplied all 48; 26 entries were rewritten in verification —
mostly for short or long counts (45, 238, 294, 336, 404 scanned five; 337, 473
scanned seven), broken joins with neighboring verses (95, 120, 329, 356, 387,
467, 497), or small unwarranted additions.

**22.37** δμῳῇσιν δὲ γυναιξὶ παρευνάζεσθε βιαίως,
- OLD: with my serving women you forced yourselves down into bed there,
- NEW: you forced yourselves down into bed with my serving women,

**22.45** εἰ μὲν δὴ Ὀδυσεὺς Ἰθακήσιος εἰλήλουθας,
- OLD: “If indeed you are truly Odysseus, the Ithacan, home now,
- NEW: “If in very truth you have come home, Odysseus the Ithacan,
- "in very truth" renders μὲν δή and is already the poem's idiom; the name and
  ethnic land last, where the Greek puts the weight.

**22.70** ὦ φίλοι, οὐ γὰρ σχήσει ἀνὴρ ὅδε χεῖρας ἀάπτους,
- OLD: “Friends, this man will not hold back those invincible hands now,
- NEW: “Friends, this man will not hold back his hands invincible,
- Postposed epithet keeps χεῖρας ἀάπτους intact and gives the dactylic close.

**22.79** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας εἰρύσσατο φάσγανον ὀξὺ
- OLD: So he spoke, and he drew his sharp sword out of the sheath there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and drew forth his sharp sword from its scabbard,
- εἰρύσσατο is "drew [from the sheath]"; "scabbard" concretizes the verb no
  further than the old line's "sheath" did.

**22.90** ἀντίος ἀΐξας, εἴρυτο δὲ φάσγανον ὀξύ,
- OLD: charging headlong against him, and drew his keen-edged sword there,
- NEW: charging headlong against him, his keen-edged sword unsheathing,
- ὀξύ carried once ("keen-edged"); εἴρυτο landed as the closing participle.

**22.95** Τηλέμαχος δʼ ἀπόρουσε, λιπὼν δολιχόσκιον ἔγχος
- OLD: Then Telemachus sprang back, leaving the long-shadowing lance there
- NEW: Then Telemachus sprang back, leaving the long-shadowing spear-shaft
- The spear must stay at line-end: 96 "fixed in Amphinomus" hangs from it.
  "spear-shaft" for ἔγχος follows the pilot's "bow-stave" for τόξον.

**22.100** ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱστάμενος ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
- OLD: and standing close beside him spoke out winged words in address there:
- NEW: and standing close beside him he spoke these winged words, saying:
- Formula tail normalized with 410 and 436.

**22.113** αὐτὸς δὲ πρώτιστα περὶ χροῒ δύσετο χαλκόν·
- OLD: First of all he clad his own body about with the bronze there;
- NEW: He himself first of all put on the bronze about his body;
- αὐτός restored as "He himself."

**22.120** τόξον μὲν πρὸς σταθμὸν ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο
- OLD: then he leaned the bow by a pillar of the well-builded hall there,
- NEW: then he leaned the bow on the stout-built great hall's doorpost,
- σταθμός here is the doorpost, not a pillar; the half-line repeats the Greek of
  257/274, so the English phrase now repeats with it.

**22.131** τοῖς δʼ Ἀγέλεως μετέειπεν, ἔπος πάντεσσι πιφαύσκων·
- OLD: Then Agelaus spoke among them, declaring his thought to them all there:
- NEW: Then Agelaus spoke out among them, his word to them all declaring:
- ἔπος is "word," not "thought"; identical Greek at 247, now identical English.

**22.142** ὣς εἰπὼν ἀνέβαινε Μελάνθιος, αἰπόλος αἰγῶν,
- OLD: So saying, Melanthius the goatherd climbed up through the hall there,
- NEW: So saying, Melanthius, goatherd of goats, went climbing upward,
- ἀνέβαινε carries the direction ("upward"); αἰπόλος αἰγῶν regains its Homeric
  pleonasm "goatherd of goats." The Greek names no hall in this line — the route
  arrives with 143's smoke-vents.

**22.224** ὣς φάτʼ, Ἀθηναίη δὲ χολώσατο κηρόθι μᾶλλον,
- OLD: So he spoke, and Athena in the deep heart grew angrier still then,
- NEW: So he spoke, and Athena in her deep heart was angered further,

**22.226** οὐκέτι σοί γʼ, Ὀδυσεῦ, μένος ἔμπεδον οὐδέ τις ἀλκή
- OLD: “Odysseus, no longer in you does steadfast fury remain now,
- NEW: “Odysseus, no longer does steadfast fury within you linger,
- Athena speaking; no contractions.

**22.238** ἠμὲν Ὀδυσσῆος ἠδʼ υἱοῦ κυδαλίμοιο.
- OLD: both of Odysseus himself and the glorious son at his side there.
- NEW: both of Odysseus himself, and also his son the illustrious.
- "at his side" was unwarranted and is gone. "and also" renders ἠδέ in the
  ἠμέν … ἠδέ correlative; "illustrious" is the poem's own word for κυδάλιμος
  in this book (141).

**22.247** τοῖς δʼ Ἀγέλεως μετέειπεν, ἔπος πάντεσσι πιφαύσκων·
- OLD: Then Agelaus addressed them, declaring his word to them all there:
- NEW: Then Agelaus spoke out among them, his word to them all declaring:
- Verbatim echo of 131, as in the Greek.

**22.257** τῶν ἄλλος μὲν σταθμὸν ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο
- OLD: One man's spear struck fast in the post of the stout-built great hall there,
- NEW: One man's spear struck fast in the stout-built great hall's doorpost,
- σταθμός = doorpost, beside 258's door as in the Greek.

**22.259** ἄλλου δʼ ἐν τοίχῳ μελίη πέσε χαλκοβάρεια.
- OLD: another man's bronze-heavy ash shaft fell in against the wall there.
- NEW: another man's bronze-heavy ash shaft fell in against the walling.
- "walling" for τοῖχος is the poem's own idiom (22.24).

**22.260** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ δὴ δούρατʼ ἀλεύαντο μνηστήρων,
- OLD: But when they had escaped from the suitors' shower of spears then,
- NEW: But when at last they had dodged aside from the spears of the suitors,
- "shower of" was not in the Greek and is gone; ἀλεύαντο is "dodged/avoided,"
  δή carried as "at last." "aside" is the one small residue, logged here.

**22.269** οἱ μὲν ἔπειθʼ ἅμα πάντες ὀδὰξ ἕλον ἄσπετον οὖδας,
- OLD: Then all of them together bit down on the monstrous floor there;
- NEW: Then all of them together sank their teeth in the monstrous flooring;

**22.274** τῶν ἄλλος μὲν σταθμὸν ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο
- OLD: One man's spear struck fast in the post of the stout-built great hall there,
- NEW: One man's spear struck fast in the stout-built great hall's doorpost,
- Verbatim echo of 257, as in the Greek.

**22.276** ἄλλου δʼ ἐν τοίχῳ μελίη πέσε χαλκοβάρεια.
- OLD: another man's bronze-heavy ash shaft fell in against the wall there.
- NEW: another man's bronze-heavy ash shaft fell in against the walling.
- Verbatim echo of 259, as in the Greek.

**22.280** ὦμον ἐπέγραψεν· τὸ δʼ ὑπέρπτατο, πῖπτε δʼ ἔραζε.
- OLD: Eumaeus' shoulder, but it flew past and fell on the earth there.
- NEW: Eumaeus' shoulder, but it flew on past and fell earthward.
- ἔραζε is directional; "earthward" is the poem's "-ward" idiom (22.20).

**22.292** ἦ ῥα βοῶν ἑλίκων ἐπιβουκόλος· αὐτὰρ Ὀδυσσεὺς
- OLD: So spoke the herdsman in charge of the oxen with curving horns there;
- NEW: So then spoke the herdsman, the keeper of curved-horned cattle;
- αὐτὰρ Ὀδυσσεύς is carried, as before, by 293's "but Odysseus."

**22.294** Τηλέμαχος δʼ Εὐηνορίδην Λειώκριτον οὖτα
- OLD: And Telemachus stabbed Leocritus, Evenor's son there,
- NEW: And Telemachus then stabbed Leocritus, the son of Evenor,
- δέ carried as "then" to fill the count honestly; patronymic unpacked.

**22.303** ἐξ ὀρέων ἐλθόντες ἐπʼ ὀρνίθεσσι θόρωσι·
- OLD: coming down from the mountains, swoop upon flocks of small birds there,
- NEW: down from the mountains they come, upon the small birds swooping,
- "flocks of" was padding and is gone; θόρωσι lands as the closing participle.

**22.308** τύπτον ἐπιστροφάδην· τῶν δὲ στόνος ὤρνυτʼ ἀεικὴς
- OLD: struck them to this side and that; and terrible groaning arose there
- NEW: struck them to this side and that; and terrible groaning was rising
- ὤρνυτο is imperfect; the line stays open into 309 "from skulls being battered."

**22.310** Λειώδης δʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἐπεσσύμενος λάβε γούνων,
- OLD: Then Leodes rushed up and clasped Odysseus fast by the knees there,
- NEW: Then Leodes rushed up and clasped fast at the knees of Odysseus,

**22.326** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας ξίφος εἵλετο χειρὶ παχείῃ
- OLD: So then having spoken, he seized with his massive hand a sword there
- NEW: So then, having spoken, he seized with his massive hand a sword
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the line closes on the weapon Odysseus is about
  to kill with, the pilot's precedent (21.31, 21.225) for closing on the
  freighted noun of an action about to unfold. It runs on into 327 "lying, the
  one that Agelaus had flung."

**22.328** κτεινόμενος· τῷ τόν γε κατʼ αὐχένα μέσσον ἔλασσε.
- OLD: as he was killed; and he drove it straight through the midst of his neck there.
- NEW: as he was killed; and clean through the middle of his neck he thrust it.
- "clean through" is the poem's own phrase for this stroke (22.16, 22.295);
  the unstressed "it" gives the falling close.

**22.329** φθεγγομένου δʼ ἄρα τοῦ γε κάρη κονίῃσιν ἐμίχθη.
- OLD: While he was still speaking, his head was mingled up with the dust then.
- NEW: Even while he was speaking, his head with the dust was mingled.
- "still" is avoided because 330 opens "Yet still the singer escaped."

**22.332** ἔστη δʼ ἐν χείρεσσίν ἔχων φόρμιγγα λίγειαν
- OLD: He stood there holding within both his hands the clear-toned lyre there
- NEW: Holding within both his hands the lyre of clear tone, he was standing,
- The old line had a doubled "there"; both are gone, and ἔστη lands last.

**22.334** ἢ ἐκδὺς μεγάροιο Διὸς μεγάλου ποτὶ βωμὸν
- OLD: whether, slipping out of the hall, to go to the great altar of Zeus there,
- NEW: whether, slipping out of the hall, to approach great Zeus’s altar,

**22.336** Λαέρτης Ὀδυσεύς τε βοῶν ἐπὶ μηρίʼ ἔκηαν,
- OLD: where Laertes and great Odysseus had often burned there
- NEW: where Laertes and great Odysseus had burned upon it the thighbones
- Rebalanced with 337: ἐπί ("upon it," the altar) is finally translated, and the
  old line's "often," which has no Greek behind it, is gone. "great" (also not
  in the Greek) is kept for the count and flagged here.

**22.337** ἦ γούνων λίσσοιτο προσαΐξας Ὀδυσῆα.
- OLD: thighbones of oxen, or else rush forward and clasp at Odysseus’ knees there.
- NEW: of oxen, or else rush forward and clasp at the knees of Odysseus.
- "thighbones / of oxen" now enjambs as βοῶν … μηρία does; the close on
  Odysseus's name echoes 342 as the Greek's γούνων formula does.

**22.340** ἦ τοι ὁ φόρμιγγα γλαφυρὴν κατέθηκε χαμᾶζε
- OLD: Then indeed he set down the hollow lyre on the ground there
- NEW: Then indeed he set the hollow lyre down on the flooring
- κατέθηκε χαμᾶζε = "put down to the ground"; the line runs on into 341
  "midway between the mixing-bowl and the chair."

**22.342** αὐτὸς δʼ αὖτʼ Ὀδυσῆα προσαΐξας λάβε γούνων,
- OLD: and he himself rushed up to Odysseus and clasped at his knees then,
- NEW: and he himself rushed forward and clasped at the knees of Odysseus,
- αὖτε ("in turn") stays untranslated, as it was in the old line.

**22.356** ἴσχεο μηδέ τι τοῦτον ἀναίτιον οὔταε χαλκῷ·
- OLD: “Stop, and do not in any way stab this guiltless man with the bronze now.
- NEW: “Stop, and in no way stab with the bronze this man, who is guiltless.
- μηδέ τι kept as "in no way"; the full stop is kept because 357 begins a new
  sentence.

**22.365** Τηλέμαχον δʼ ἄρʼ ἔπειτα προσαΐξας λάβε γούνων,
- OLD: then he rushed up to Telemachus and clasped hard at his knees there,
- NEW: then he rushed forward and clasped hard at the knees of Telemachus,
- ἔπειτα is honest at the head of the line ("then"), not dangling at the close.

**22.381** πάπτηνεν δʼ Ὀδυσεὺς καθʼ ἑὸν δόμον, εἴ τις ἔτʼ ἀνδρῶν
- OLD: Then Odysseus looked round through his house, lest any man still there
- NEW: Then Odysseus looked all through his house, to see whether any warrior
- ἔτι is carried by the untouched 382 "might yet have escaped him"; "warrior"
  for ἀνδρῶν takes its epic license from the battle scene and is flagged here.

**22.386** δικτύῳ ἐξέρυσαν πολυωπῷ· οἱ δέ τε πάντες
- OLD: out from the surf of the salt sea; and all of them lie on the sand there
- NEW: out from the surf of the salt sea; and all of them, yearning
- Rebalanced with 387: οἱ δέ τε πάντες only introduces; the verb (κέχυνται)
  belongs to 387, where it now lives.

**22.387** κύμαθʼ ἁλὸς ποθέοντες ἐπὶ ψαμάθοισι κέχυνται·
- OLD: yearning back for the sea-waves, as there on the beaches they sprawl there,
- NEW: back for the waves of the sea, lie strewn out over the beaches,
- The old pair said "sand" and "beaches" both for one ψαμάθοισι; once is enough.
  κέχυνται = "lie strewn." The comma is kept because 388 continues the sentence.

**22.404** πᾶν δʼ ἄρα οἱ στῆθός τε παρήϊά τʼ ἀμφοτέρωθεν
- OLD: all of his chest and his cheeks on both sides there
- NEW: wholly, his chest and his cheeks alike, upon both sides of him,
- πᾶν fronted as "wholly," τε … τε rendered "alike"; the line stays open into
  405 "are blood-smeared."

**22.410** καί μιν φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
- OLD: and calling to her he spoke with winged words in address there:
- NEW: and calling out to her, he spoke these winged words, saying:
- Formula tail normalized with 100 and 436.

**22.436** εἰς ἓ καλεσσάμενος ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα·
- OLD: and the swineherd, and spoke to them winged words in address there:
- NEW: and the swineherd, calling them to him, spoke these winged words, saying:
- εἰς ἓ καλεσσάμενος ("calling them to himself"), dropped by the old line, is
  restored.

**22.467** ὑψόσʼ ἐπεντανύσας, μή τις ποσὶν οὖδας ἵκοιτο.
- OLD: stretching it taut up high, so none with the feet could touch earth there.
- NEW: stretching it taut up high, so that no one’s feet might reach earthward.
- οὖδας ἵκοιτο ("might reach the ground") in the poem's "-ward" idiom; full
  stop kept, since 468 opens the simile.

**22.473** ἤσπαιρον δὲ πόδεσσι μίνυνθά περ οὔ τι μάλα δήν.
- OLD: They fluttered with their feet a little while, but not very long there.
- NEW: A little, and not very long, with their feet they kept fluttering.
- μίνυνθά περ οὔ τι μάλα δήν keeps its Homeric doubling; ἤσπαιρον is imperfect,
  landed as "kept fluttering."

**22.477** χεῖράς τʼ ἠδὲ πόδας κόπτον κεκοτηότι θυμῷ.
- OLD: and in the fury of heart they chopped off his hands and his feet there.
- NEW: and, raging in their hearts, they cut away his hands and his feet from him.
- The unstressed "from him" gives the falling close; logged as the line's one
  added pronoun.

**22.497** αἱ δʼ ἴσαν ἐκ μεγάροιο δάος μετὰ χερσὶν ἔχουσαι.
- OLD: Out of the hall then they came with torches held in their hands there.
- NEW: Out from the hall they came, in their hands the torches holding.
- ἔχουσαι lands as the closing participle; full stop kept for 498.

## Tally

- 48 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*
  (and none in *here*). No warranted keeper was needed in this book.
- 1 masculine ending licensed (22.326), within the 3/book budget.
- Formula pairs restored to verbatim echo where the Greek repeats: 131 = 247;
  257 = 274; 259 = 276; and 120 joined to 257/274 on the shared
  σταθμὸν ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο half-line ("the stout-built great hall's
  doorpost"). Speech-intro tail normalized at 100/410/436.
- Fidelity recovered along the way: αὐτός at 113, ἔπος at 131/247, αἰπόλος
  αἰγῶν and ἀνέβαινε's direction at 142, σταθμός as doorpost at 120/257/274,
  μηδέ τι at 356, ἐπί at 336, εἰς ἓ καλεσσάμενος at 436, the imperfects at
  308 and 473. Padding cut beyond the fillers themselves: "shower of" (260),
  "flocks of" (303), "at his side" (238), "often" (336), the doubled "there"
  at 332 and 387.
- Honest costs incurred and logged: "scabbard" concretized at 79; "aside" at
  260; "warrior" at 381; "from him" at 477; "great" retained at 336; αὖτε
  still untranslated at 342.
- Untouched and deliberate: the deictic *here* closes inside speeches at 101,
  141, 216, 219, 253, 360, 431 (speakers pointing at the place or people at
  hand); these were not in the extraction and are not metrical filler.

# Book 14 "Then"-opening revision

Examined: 27 verse lines opening with "Then". Kept 4 (Greek warrants a real temporal
adverb), rewrote 23 (Greek carries only a light connective).

Warrant rule: a "Then"-opening stands only where the Greek line has a genuine
temporal adverb — τότε / δὴ τότε / καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα / αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα,
or an ἦμος-clause apodosis. Bare δέ, αὐτάρ/ἀτάρ, καί, μὲν ἄρα, ὣς + verb, the
speech-reply formulas (τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος…, τὸν δʼ ἠμείβετο…), and asyndeton do
not warrant it. Replacement openings are varied so no one opening becomes the new
metronome; every edit still scans as six falling beats.

Formula note: the five occurrences of the identical Greek line
τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφης, Εὔμαιε συβῶτα now share one English formula,
"Answering him in turn you spoke, Eumaeus the swineherd:" (14.55, 165, 442, 507);
14.360, whose old back half differed ("swineherd and good man"), takes the sibling
form "Making an answer you spoke…" to hold the meter.

- NEW: Once again to him spoke resourceful divine Odysseus:

**14.278** αὐτὰρ ἐγὼ (αὐτάρ)
- OLD: Then I went to the king where he stood by the front of his chariot,

**14.353** ἔνθʼ ἀναβάς (ἔνθα locative — "there, where," resumed by ὅθι τε; not temporal)
- OLD: Then I climbed where there stood the dense growth of a many-flowered thicket,
- NEW: Up I climbed where there stood the dense growth of a many-flowered thicket,
- "Up" carries ἀναβάς; the locative sense lives in the "where" clause.

**14.360** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφης (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in answer you spoke, Eumaeus, swineherd and good man:
- NEW: Making an answer you spoke, Eumaeus, swineherd and good man:

**14.375** ἀλλʼ οἱ μὲν (ἀλλά)
- OLD: Then they all sit around and ask the man questions on all things,
- NEW: But they all sit around and ask the man questions on all things,

**14.390** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in answer to him spoke Odysseus, resourceful and steadfast:
- NEW: Making his answer to him spoke Odysseus, resourceful and steadfast:

**14.401** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσεφώνεε (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in reply the noble swineherd spoke and addressed him:
- NEW: In his turn the noble swineherd spoke and addressed him:

**14.411** τὰς μὲν ἄρα ἔρξαν (μὲν ἄρα, light)
- OLD: Then indeed they penned up the sows in their places for sleeping,
- NEW: So it was that they penned up the sows in their places for sleeping,
- "So it was that" renders ἄρα's "as it turned out" and replaces the two-beat
  "Then indeed" opening; also breaks the old 401–413 Then-cluster.

**14.413** αὐτὰρ ὁ (αὐτάρ)
- OLD: Then to his own companions the noble swineherd called out:
- NEW: But to his own companions the noble swineherd called out:

**14.419** οἱ δʼ ὗν εἰσῆγον (bare δέ)
- OLD: Then they led in a five-year-old hog, exceedingly fattened.
- NEW: In they led a five-year-old hog, exceedingly fattened.

**14.425** κόψε δʼ ἀνασχόμενος (bare δέ; participle "raising up")
- OLD: Then, lifting a billet of oak that was left from the chopping,
- NEW: Raising aloft a billet of oak that was left from the chopping,
- "aloft" carries the ἀνα- of ἀνασχόμενος that "lifting" alone had blurred.

**14.434** καὶ τὰ μὲν ἕπταχα (καί)
- OLD: Then he cut and allotted the whole into seven portions.
- NEW: And he cut and allotted the whole into seven portions.

**14.439** καί μιν φωνήσας προσέφη (καί)
- OLD: Then resourceful Odysseus spoke and addressed him directly:
- NEW: And resourceful Odysseus spoke and addressed him directly:

**14.442** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφης (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in reply you answered him, Eumaeus the swineherd:
- NEW: Answering him in turn you spoke, Eumaeus the swineherd:

**14.453** οἱ δʼ ἐπʼ ὀνείαθʼ … χεῖρας ἴαλλον (bare δέ)
- OLD: Then they reached out their hands to the good things laid ready before them.
- NEW: Out they stretched their hands to the good things laid ready before them.

**14.457** νὺξ δʼ ἄρʼ ἐπῆλθε (bare δέ)
- OLD: Then there came on a moonless and evil night, and Zeus sent rain down
- NEW: On there came a moonless and evil night, and Zeus sent rain down
- ἐπῆλθε "came on" — the inversion keeps the verb's own particle as the opening beat.

**14.459** τοῖς δʼ Ὀδυσεὺς μετέειπε (bare δέ)

**14.532** βῆ δʼ ἴμεναι (bare δέ, verb-first)
- OLD: Then he went to lie down where the white-tusked swine were accustomed,

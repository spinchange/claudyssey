# Book 18 de-filler — all 39 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling
line; no line ends in *there/now/then/here* unless the Greek warrants it (one
case: 18.266, ἐνθάδε "here"); the preferred fix is reordering so a naturally
trochaic word lands last; masculine endings budgeted at 2–3 per book (one used:
18.97); Greek-verbatim repetitions made verbatim in English (18.290 = 18.50).
One adjacent verse (18.426) was edited as a consequence of the 425 fix and is
shown as its own entry.

---

**18.22** αἵματος· ἡσυχίη δʼ ἂν ἐμοὶ καὶ μᾶλλον ἔτʼ εἴη
- OLD: all with blood; and for me there would even be greater peace then
- NEW: all with blood; and quiet for me would be all the greater
- ἡσυχίη is "quiet," restored from the old "peace"; καὶ μᾶλλον ἔτι carried by
  "all the greater," flowing into 23's "on the morrow."

**18.28** κόπτων ἀμφοτέρῃσι, χαμαὶ δέ κε πάντας ὀδόντας
- OLD: striking with both of my hands, and send all his teeth to the ground there,
- NEW: striking with both of my hands, and send all his teeth flying earthward,
- χαμαί as "earthward," the poem's "-ward" idiom (10.440, 22.20).

**18.67** ζώσατο μὲν ῥάκεσιν περὶ μήδεα, φαῖνε δὲ μηροὺς
- OLD: girded his rags about his loins, and his thighs were revealed there,
- NEW: girded his rags at the loins, his two thighs revealing,
- Lowercased and kept continuous with 66's "then Odysseus," which carries the
  subject; "two" is the one light metrical filler (thighs are two).

**18.68** καλούς τε μεγάλους τε, φάνεν δέ οἱ εὐρέες ὦμοι
- OLD: shapely and great, and his broad shoulders showed to their sight then,
- NEW: shapely and great, and plain to be seen were his broad shoulders,
- φάνεν = "plain to be seen"; "to their sight" was itself padding.

**18.86** ὅς κʼ ἀπὸ ῥῖνα τάμῃσι καὶ οὔατα νηλέϊ χαλκῷ,
- OLD: who with pitiless bronze will cut away your nose and your ears there,
- NEW: who will shear off your nose and your ears with the pitiless war-bronze,
- The junior draft's "earlobes" narrowed οὔατα; "ears" restored, with the
  poem's own "war-bronze" (21.434) closing the line.

**18.97** ἔθλασεν· αὐτίκα δʼ ἦλθε κατὰ στόμα φοίνιον αἷμα,
- OLD: he crushed inward; and instantly crimson blood came into his mouth there,
- NEW: he crushed inward; and instantly crimson blood came into his mouth,
- Licensed masculine ending #1 (the book's only one): the crunch of the blow
  and the blood at the mouth close on the stress deliberately.

**18.101** ἕλκε διὲκ προθύροιο λαβὼν ποδός, ὄφρʼ ἵκετʼ αὐλήν,
- OLD: dragged him out through the forecourt doorway, clutching his foot there,
- NEW: dragged him, clutching his foot, out through the forecourt doorway,

**18.102** αἰθούσης τε θύρας· καί μιν ποτὶ ἑρκίον αὐλῆς
- OLD: till he came to the yard and the gates of the roofed colonnade there;
- NEW: until he had come to the courtyard and the roofed colonnade's gateways;
- "gateways" keeps θύρας plural and gives the trochaic close.

**18.151** ὣς φάτο, καὶ σπείσας ἔπιεν μελιηδέα οἶνον,
- OLD: So he spoke, and after a libation drank the honey-sweet wine there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and drank of the honey-sweet wine after pouring libation,
- The junior draft's "the sweet wine of honey" broke the book's formula;
  "honey-sweet wine" restored (as at 18.426), σπείσας ἔπιεν kept in order.

**18.157** ἂψ δʼ αὖτις κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετʼ ἐπὶ θρόνου ἔνθεν ἀνέστη.
- OLD: Back again then he went and sat on the chair he had left there.
- NEW: Back again he went and sat on the chair whence he had risen.
- ἔνθεν ἀνέστη is literally "whence he had risen" — the Greek supplies the
  feminine close itself; the junior draft's masculine ending was not needed.

**18.162** μᾶλλον πρὸς πόσιός τε καὶ υἱέος ἢ πάρος ἦεν.
- OLD: more in the eyes of her husband and son than ever before then.
- NEW: more in the eyes of her husband and son than she was aforetime.
- "aforetime" for πάρος is already the poem's word (13.113, 16.143).

**18.164** Εὐρυνόμη, θυμός μοι ἐέλδεται, οὔ τι πάρος γε,
- OLD: “Eurynome, my heart is longing, as never it longed before now,
- NEW: “Eurynome, never before did my heart feel such a longing,

**18.171** ἀλλʼ ἴθι καὶ σῷ παιδὶ ἔπος φάο μηδʼ ἐπίκευθε,
- OLD: Go now, and speak to your son, and do not conceal it from him there,
- NEW: Go now, and speak a word to your son, and let nothing be hidden,
- ἔπος ("a word"), dropped by the junior draft, restored.

**18.173** μηδʼ οὕτω δακρύοισι πεφυρμένη ἀμφὶ πρόσωπα
- OLD: Do not go with your countenance thus all befouled round about there
- NEW: Do not go with your countenance thus befouled all over
- ἀμφί = "all over"; enjambs into 174's "with tears" as before. The junior
  draft's "fouled and disfigured" doubled πεφυρμένη into invention.

**18.193** ἀμβροσίῳ, οἵῳ περ ἐϋστέφανος Κυθέρεια
- OLD: such as well-garlanded Cythereia herself there
- NEW: of just such a kind as well-garlanded Cythereia
- The old "herself" (no αὐτή in the Greek) is gone with the filler.

**18.197** ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς ἔρξασʼ ἀπεβήσετο δῖα θεάων,
- OLD: So having wrought all this, the bright among goddesses left there.
- NEW: So having wrought all this, the bright among goddesses departed.

**18.236** οὕτω νῦν μνηστῆρες ἐν ἡμετέροισι δόμοισι
- OLD: so in our halls these suitors might droop their heads in defeat now,
- NEW: so now in our halls may these suitors droop their heads, defeated,
- νῦν kept mid-line where it belongs; the junior draft dropped "their heads"
  (κεφαλάς, carried into this verse by the English lineation) — restored.

**18.239** ὡς νῦν Ἶρος κεῖνος ἐπʼ αὐλείῃσι θύρῃσιν
- OLD: sitting there at the court-yard gate he nods with his head there
- NEW: sitting there at the court-yard gate, with his head he keeps nodding
- "keeps nodding" renders the iterative νευστάζων; the junior draft restated
  "as now that Irus," duplicating 238's line-end, and lost the finite verb
  that 240's "and is like a drunken man" hangs on.

**18.242** οἴκαδʼ, ὅπη οἱ νόστος, ἐπεὶ φίλα γυῖα λέλυνται.
- OLD: home by whatever road his returning lies, since his limbs have gone slack now.”
- NEW: home by whatever road his returning lies, since his own limbs are slackened.”
- φίλα γυῖα = "his own limbs."

**18.260** ἐκ Τροίης εὖ πάντας ἀπήμονας ἀπονέεσθαι·
- OLD: all will return from Troy to their homes unscathed and unharmed there.
- NEW: all will return from Troy to their homes unscathed and in safety.
- εὖ ... ἀπήμονας = "unscathed and in safety"; the junior draft's "unscathed
  and safely" mixed adjective and adverb.

**18.266** αὐτοῦ ἐνὶ Τροίῃ· σοὶ δʼ ἐνθάδε πάντα μελόντων.
- OLD: or if there within Troy I shall fall; but let all here be your care now.
- NEW: or if I shall fall there within Troy; but let all be your care here.
- The one retained locative close in this book: ἐνθάδε ("here") is in the
  Greek; mid-line "there" renders αὐτοῦ ("on that very spot").

**18.281** ὣς φάτο, γήθησεν δὲ πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς,
- OLD: So she spoke, and much-enduring noble Odysseus rejoiced there,
- NEW: So she spoke, and rejoicing was much-enduring noble Odysseus,
- The name itself gives the trochaic close.

**18.290** ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος, τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιήνδανε μῦθος·
- OLD: Thus spoke Antinous, and his saying was pleasing to all of them there.
- NEW: So spoke Antinous, and his saying was pleasing to all of them.
- The Greek line is verbatim 18.50; the English is now verbatim 18.50 too
  ("So spoke Antinous, and his saying was pleasing to all of them.") — the
  junior draft's inversion would have broken the formula pair.

**18.291** δῶρα δʼ ἄρʼ οἰσέμεναι πρόεσαν κήρυκα ἕκαστος.
- OLD: Then each man sent out his herald to carry and fetch him a gift there.
- NEW: Then to carry and fetch him a gift, each man sent out his herald,

**18.292** Ἀντινόῳ μὲν ἔνεικε μέγαν περικαλλέα πέπλον,
- OLD: For Antinous his man brought in a great and beautiful robe there,
- NEW: For Antinous, his man brought in a robe, great and beautiful,
- The postponed adjectives flow into 293's "richly embroidered."

**18.296** χρύσεον, ἠλέκτροισιν ἐερμένον ἠέλιον ὥς.
- OLD: golden, threaded with amber beads that shone like the sun there.
- NEW: golden, threaded with amber beads, like the sun in its shining.
- ἠέλιον ὥς closes the line as the simile itself.

**18.299** ἐκ δʼ ἄρα Πεισάνδροιο Πολυκτορίδαο ἄνακτος
- OLD: Then out of the house of lord Peisander, Polyctor’s son there,
- NEW: Then out of the house of lord Peisander, the son of Polyctor,
- The junior draft's "lord and son of Polyctor" misattached ἄνακτος.

**18.301** ἄλλο δʼ ἄρʼ ἄλλος δῶρον Ἀχαιῶν καλὸν ἔνεικεν.
- OLD: And then one after another Achaeans brought some fair gift there.
- NEW: And then the Achaeans brought fair gifts, one man after another.
- The junior draft dropped καλόν ("fair") — restored; ἄλλο δʼ ἄλλος is the
  "one man after another" distributive, which also closes the line.

**18.303** τῇ δʼ ἄρʼ ἅμʼ ἀμφίπολοι ἔφερον περικαλλέα δῶρα
- OLD: and with her the serving-women bore the beautiful gifts there.
- NEW: and with her, bearing the beautiful gifts, the handmaids followed.
- ἀμφίπολοι as "handmaids" (the book's word at 198, 207, 211); the junior
  draft's close on "serving-women" duplicated 302's line-end "among women."

**18.310** καὶ δαΐδας μετέμισγον· ἀμοιβηδὶς δʼ ἀνέφαινον
- OLD: and among it they mingled pine torches; and turn upon turn then
- NEW: and among it they mingled pine torches; and in changing succession
- ἀμοιβηδίς = "in changing succession," flowing into 311's slave-women who
  kept the light; the junior draft's "in constant rotation" was machinery.

**18.335** ὅς τίς σʼ ἀμφὶ κάρη κεκοπὼς χερσὶ στιβαρῇσι
- OLD: better than Irus, quickly arise, who with powerful fists there
- NEW: better than Irus, quickly arise, who with hands that are sturdy
- χερσὶ στιβαρῇσι = "sturdy hands," more literal than "powerful fists." The
  relative "who" is kept: 336's "striking about your head may drive you"
  needs it (the junior draft's "one with hands" broke the syntax).

**18.358** ἀγροῦ ἐπʼ ἐσχατιῆς—μισθὸς δέ τοι ἄρκιος ἔσται—
- OLD: out to the edge of my fields? Your wages will surely suffice there.
- NEW: out to the edge of my fields? Your wage will surely be ample.
- ἄρκιος = "ample/sufficient"; the junior draft's "fitting" changed the sense.

**18.359** αἱμασιάς τε λέγων καὶ δένδρεα μακρὰ φυτεύων;
- OLD: You could gather stones for the fences and plant the tall trees there.
- NEW: You could plant the tall trees, and gather the stones for the fences.
- The two labors swap places so the plural closes the line.

**18.366** Εὐρύμαχʼ, εἰ γὰρ νῶϊν ἔρις ἔργοιο γένοιτο
- OLD: “Eurymachus, if only a contest in labor might come now
- NEW: “Eurymachus, if only there might come a contest of labor
- γένοιτο = "might come (to be)"; "labor" closes the line and hands straight
  into 367's "between us two" (νῶϊν). The junior draft's masculine "might
  rise" scanned five beats and spent budget for no emphasis.

**18.372** αἴθωνες, μεγάλοι, ἄμφω κεκορηότε ποίης,
- OLD: tawny and large, both full-fed on grass and both in their prime there,
- NEW: tawny and large, the two of them full-fed on the grasses,
- κεκορηότε is the perfect "full-fed" (the junior draft's "fully feeding"
  turned the sated oxen into grazing ones); the old "in their prime" was
  padding with no Greek behind it, now gone.

**18.397** δεξιτερήν· πρόχοος δὲ χαμαὶ βόμβησε πεσοῦσα,
- OLD: and the wine-jug clattered to earth with a booming sound as it fell there.
- NEW: and down to the earth with a booming clatter the wine-jug went falling,

**18.398** αὐτὰρ ὅ γʼ οἰμώξας πέσεν ὕπτιος ἐν κονίῃσι.
- OLD: Groaning aloud, the man pitched on his back in the dust of the floor there.
- NEW: Groaning aloud, the man went down in the dust, pitching backward.
- ὕπτιος = "backward"; "of the floor" was padding. The junior draft's "went
  pitching" would have echoed 397's "went falling" close for close.

**18.425** νώμησεν δʼ ἄρα πᾶσιν ἐπισταδόν· οἱ δὲ θεοῖσι
- OLD: Then he served it to all men in order; and they to the gods there
- NEW: Then he served it to all men in order; and they to the immortals
- θεοῖσι as "immortals" is the poem's own practice (18.112); this forced the
  one consequential edit, at 426.

  **18.426** (adjacent, consequential)
  - OLD: poured to the blessed immortals, then drank the honey-sweet wine up.
  - NEW: poured to the blessed ones, and then drank the honey-sweet wine up.
  - μακάρεσσι is literally "the blessed ones"; the change avoids
    "immortals / immortals" across the line break.

**18.427** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ σπεῖσάν τʼ ἔπιόν θʼ ὅσον ἤθελε θυμός,
- OLD: But when they had poured and had drunk as much as the heart had desired there,
- NEW: But when they had poured out libation and drunk to their heart's desiring,
- ὅσον ἤθελε θυμός = "to their heart's desiring"; the junior draft's "full"
  was an extra word and is cut.

## Tally

- 39 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*.
- 1 warranted keeper: 18.266 closes on "here" for ἐνθάδε, which is in the Greek.
- 1 masculine ending licensed (18.97), well inside the 2–3/book budget; the
  junior draft's other two masculine closes (157, 366) were resolved feminine
  (ἔνθεν supplied "whence he had risen"; γένοιτο supplied "might come").
- 1 formula restored to verbatim echo where the Greek repeats: 18.290 = 18.50.
- 1 adjacent verse edited as a consequence: 18.426 ("the blessed ones," more
  literal for μακάρεσσι anyway).
- Fidelity recovered along the way: οὔατα "ears" (not "earlobes") at 86; ἔπος
  at 171; κεφαλάς at 236; καλόν at 301; the perfect κεκορηότε at 372; ἄρκιος
  "ample" at 358; padding with no Greek behind it removed at 193 ("herself"),
  372 ("in their prime"), 398 ("of the floor").
- Honest costs incurred: "two" added at 67 (thighs are two); "in changing
  succession" slightly expands ἀμοιβηδίς at 310. Total added-word residue is
  far below what the old lines carried in filler.
- 27 of the 39 junior-draft entries were rewritten before applying (scansion
  shortfalls, broken joins with neighbors, dropped Greek content, a broken
  formula, register slips); 12 were applied as drafted.

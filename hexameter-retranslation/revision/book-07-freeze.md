**07.23** keeps its “here”: τοῖσδε μετʼ ἀνθρώποισι — a true deictic (“among these men here”).

Speech-formula renderings now used in this book, each fixed and distinct:

- finite ὣς φάτο / ὣς ἔφατ(ο) / ὣς φάθ᾿ / ὣς ἄρ᾿ ἔφη / ὣς φάν → **“So he spoke,” / “So she spoke,” / “So they spoke,”**
- participial ὣς εἰπών / ὣς εἰποῦσ᾿ / ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας → **“So having spoken,”**

---

**07.1** ὧς ὁ μὲν ἔνθʼ ἠρᾶτο — resumptive ὥς + ἔνθα; not the φάτο family. Opening recast off “Thus”; ἔνθα kept mid-line, where it is warranted.
- OLD: Thus he prayed there, the much-enduring lordly Odysseus,
- NEW: So he was praying there, the much-enduring lordly Odysseus,

**07.25** τηλόθεν ἐξ ἀπίης γαίης· τῷ οὔ τινα οἶδα — no deictic; “here” was filler. (Line-initial “here” renders τηλόθεν’s clause and is kept.)
- OLD: here from a faraway country; and none of the mortals I know here,
- NEW: here from a faraway country; I know not a soul of the mortals,

**07.37** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασʼ — participial; standardized to “So having spoken,”
- OLD: Thus having spoken, the goddess Athena led swiftly before him,
- NEW: So having spoken, the goddess Athena led swiftly before him,

**07.78** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασʼ — participial; standardized to “So having spoken,”
- OLD: Thus having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed,
- NEW: So having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed,

**07.153** ὣς εἰπὼν — participial; standardized to “So having spoken,”
- OLD: Thus having spoken, he sat in the ash by the fire on the hearthstone;
- NEW: So having spoken, he sat in the ash by the fire on the hearthstone;

**07.179** Ποντόνοε, κρητῆρα κερασσάμενος μέθυ νεῖμον — no deictic; “now … here” were both filler. μέθυ recovered as “vintage.”
- OLD: “Lord Pontonous, now mix us a bowl and distribute the wine here
- NEW: “Lord Pontonous, mix us a bowl and distribute the vintage

**07.182** ὣς φάτο
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and the heartening wine Pontonous was mixing,
- NEW: So he spoke, and the heartening wine Pontonous was mixing,

**07.187** ὄφρʼ εἴπω τά με θυμὸς ἐνὶ στήθεσσι κελεύει — no deictic; εἴπω closes the line on the speaking itself.
- OLD: what in the heart in my breast has commanded me now to declare here.
- NEW: what in the heart in my breast has commanded me now to be speaking.

**07.214** ὅσσα γε δὴ ξύμπαντα θεῶν ἰότητι μόγησα — no deictic; θεῶν closes on “the deathless.”
- OLD: all the afflictions I labored through by the will of the gods here.
- NEW: all the afflictions I labored through by the will of the deathless.

**07.226** ὣς ἔφαθʼ
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and they all gave approval and urged that the stranger
- NEW: So he spoke, and they all gave approval and urged that the stranger

**07.243** τοῦτο δέ τοι ἐρέω ὅ μʼ ἀνείρεαι ἠδὲ μεταλλᾷς — no deictic; μεταλλᾷς (“inquire after”) absorbs the close as “learn of.”
- OLD: Yet I shall answer the thing you have asked and are seeking to learn here.
- NEW: Yet I shall answer the thing you have asked and are seeking to learn of.

**07.307** δύσζηλοι γάρ τʼ εἰμὲν ἐπὶ χθονὶ φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων — no deictic; ἐπὶ χθονί restored as “of earth-land.”
- OLD: we are a jealousy-minded race, all the mortals who live here.”
- NEW: we are a jealousy-minded race, all the mortals of earth-land.”

**07.329** ὣς φάτο, γήθησεν δὲ πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς — γήθησεν pulled onto its own Greek line (cf. 13.250), freeing 330 to render its own Greek.
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and the much-enduring lordly Odysseus
- NEW: So he spoke, and rejoiced much-enduring, noble Odysseus,

**07.330** εὐχόμενος δʼ ἄρα εἶπεν, ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν — consequential edit from 329: the old “felt great delight” duplicated γήθησεν, which belongs to 329.
- OLD: felt great delight; and he spoke in a prayer and addressed the immortals:
- NEW: praying, and uttered a word, and called on the name of the immortals:

**07.343** ὣς φάν (plural) → “So they spoke,”
- OLD: Thus did they speak, and the thought of his sleep was a welcome one to him.
- NEW: So they spoke, and the thought of his sleep was a welcome one to him.

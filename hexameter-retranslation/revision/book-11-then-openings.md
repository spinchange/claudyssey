# Book 11 — "Then"-opening de-metronoming

36 verses in Book 11 opened with *Then* (one line in ten). **4 kept, 32 rewritten.**

**Warrant rule.** A *Then* stands only where the Greek carries a real temporal
adverb at the head of the line — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα,
temporal ἔνθα, an ἦμος-clause. Where the Greek has only a light connective —
bare δέ, αὐτάρ, ἀτάρ, καί, γάρ, τε, ὣς + verb, or asyndeton — the *Then* is the
translator's habit, not Homer's, and it goes. Speech-reply formulas
(τὸν δʼ ἀπαμείβετο, τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος) are δέ-formulas and were the worst
offenders: five of the thirty-two.

**Variety.** No replacement opening exceeds five of the thirty-two rewrites
(light *And* is the largest class at five, 16%). Subject-first and verb-first
openings carry the bulk, as the most Homeric-invisible choices in English.
Two pairs are deliberately identical because the Greek half-lines are identical:
347 = 362 (τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἀλκίνοος ἀπαμείβετο) and 572 = 601 (τὸν δὲ μετʼ …
εἰσενόησα). 354 = 377 likewise (τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς).

**11.4** ἐν δὲ … λαβόντες — δέ
- OLD: then we took hold of the sheep and conveyed them aboard her, and we too
- NEW: sheep we took hold of and carried aboard her, and we ourselves also

**11.9** ἡμεῖς δʼ — δέ (the pronoun is the emphasis, not a time-word)
- OLD: Then, when we carefully tended the tackle throughout the black vessel,
- NEW: We, when we carefully tended the tackle throughout the black vessel,

**11.12** δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος — τε
- OLD: then went the sun from the sky, and the roads of the world became darkened.
- NEW: down went the sun from the sky, and the roads of the world became darkened.
- Verb-first; "down" renders δύσετο's own directional force, adds nothing.

**11.21** αὐτοὶ δʼ αὖτε — δέ + αὖτε (contrastive "we for our part," not temporal)
- OLD: then by the stream of Ocean we made our way on our journey,
- NEW: we ourselves by the stream of Ocean went on our journey,
- αὐτοὶ ("we ourselves") is in the Greek and was previously unrendered.

**11.32** Τειρεσίῃ δʼ ἀπάνευθεν — δέ
- OLD: then for Tiresias alone I would slaughter a black-fleeced
- NEW: and for Tiresias apart I would slaughter a black-fleeced
- ἀπάνευθεν = "apart, separately"; the old "alone" doubled οἴῳ at line-end.

**11.68** Τηλεμάχου θʼ — τε
- OLD: then by Telemachus, left as your only child in the palace.
- NEW: and by Telemachus, left as your only child in the palace.

**11.84** ἦλθε δʼ ἐπὶ ψυχή — δέ
- OLD: Then came the spirit of my mother, already departed,
- NEW: There came the spirit of my dead mother, already departed,
- κατατεθνηυίης ("having died") recovered as "dead," which the old line let drop.

**11.90** ἦλθε δʼ ἐπὶ ψυχή — δέ
- OLD: Then came the spirit of Theban Tiresias, prophet and seer-man,
- NEW: Up came the spirit of Theban Tiresias, prophet and seer-man,
- Same Greek formula as 84 and 467; varied here on purpose so three identical
  English openings do not themselves become a new metronome. ἐπί supports "up."

**11.132** οἴκαδʼ ἀποστείχειν — asyndeton, infinitive continuing 129's command
- OLD: Then turn away toward your home and present to the gods their appointed
- NEW: Homeward again to depart and present to the gods their appointed
- The real temporal marker of this passage is καὶ τότε δή at 129, which keeps its
  *Then*; 132 was borrowing it.

**11.193** πάντῃ οἱ κατὰ γουνόν — asyndeton (apodosis of 192's αὐτὰρ ἐπήν)
- OLD: then on the rising ground of his vineyard, wherever he chooses,
- NEW: all on the rising ground of his vineyard, wherever he chooses,
- πάντῃ ("everywhere") is in the Greek and now opens the line instead of a
  time-word the Greek never had.

**11.222** ψυχὴ δʼ ἠύτʼ ὄνειρος — δέ
- OLD: then like a dream the spirit flies free and is hovering onward.
- NEW: and like a dream the spirit flies free and is hovering onward.

**11.226** ἤλυθον, ὤτρυνεν γάρ — asyndeton + γάρ
- OLD: Then came the women—for queen Persephone sent them before me—
- NEW: Women came onward—for queen Persephone sent them before me—
- Subject-first. The Greek opens on the bare verb ἤλυθον with the subject held
  from 225; English needs the noun, and putting it first costs nothing.

**11.241–242** τῷ δʼ ἄρα εἰσάμενος — δέ + ἄρα
- OLD: Then lord Poseidon, the earth-holding shaker, assuming the river’s
       / likeness, lay down there beside her at mouths of the eddying water.
- NEW: Taking the river’s likeness, the earth-holding shaker Poseidon
       / lay down close beside her at mouths of the eddying water.
- Participle-first, which is the Greek's own order (εἰσάμενος leads). The recast
  also cleared a filler "there" from 242 — consequential edit, content unchanged.

**11.271** μητέρα τʼ Οἰδιπόδαο ἴδον — τε
- OLD: Then I beheld Epicaste, the beautiful mother of Oedipus,
- NEW: Also the mother of Oedipus, beautiful Epicaste,
- τε is additive, not temporal; the Greek leads with "the mother of Oedipus" and
  holds the name to the close, which the new line restores.

**11.281** καὶ Χλῶριν εἶδον — καί
- OLD: Then I saw Chloris, a woman of beauty beyond every telling,
- NEW: Chloris as well I beheld, a beauty beyond every telling,
- καί = "also"; "as well" is its honest weight.

**11.298** καὶ Λήδην εἶδον — καί
- OLD: Then I beheld queen Leda, the wife of the lord Tyndareus,
- NEW: Leda as well I beheld, the wife of the lord Tyndareus,
- Verbatim echo of 281's opening, as the Greek repeats καὶ … εἶδον verbatim.

**11.335** τοῖσιν δʼ … ἤρχετο μύθων — δέ
- OLD: Then white-armed Arete arose and began to address them:
- NEW: White-armed Arete arose among them and led in the speaking:
- τοῖσιν ("among them") recovered; ἤρχετο μύθων is "began/led the speaking."

**11.342** τοῖσι δὲ καὶ μετέειπε — δέ + καί
- OLD: Then the old hero Echeneus arose and addressed the assembly,
- NEW: Also among them spoke the old hero Echeneus,
- Fidelity gain: μετέειπε is simply "spoke among"; the old line's "arose" and
  "the assembly" were both additions. καί ("also") is now rendered.

**11.347** τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἀλκίνοος ἀπαμείβετο — δέ + αὖτε ("in turn")
- OLD: Then Alcinous made his reply to the elder, responding:
- NEW: Answering him in his turn, Alcinous spoke to the elder:
- αὖτε is "in turn," a sequence word but not a temporal adverb; rendering it
  properly removes any need for *Then*. Also cuts the old line's reply/responding
  tautology.

**11.354** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς — δέ
- OLD: Then resourceful Odysseus made his response to him, replying:
- NEW: And in answer resourceful Odysseus spoke to him, saying:

**11.357** πομπὴν δʼ ὀτρύνοιτε — δέ
- OLD: then made ready my sending and gave me illustrious treasures,
- NEW: urging my sending onward and giving illustrious treasures,
- ὀτρύνοιτε is "urge on, speed," not "make ready" — restored.

**11.360** καί κʼ … εἴην — καί + potential κε
- OLD: Then I should certainly find myself more honored and cherished
- NEW: So I should certainly find myself more honored and cherished
- The one *So*-opening among the rewrites; the line is a consequence-clause, which
  is what καί κε marks.

**11.362** τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἀλκίνοος ἀπαμείβετο — δέ + αὖτε
- OLD: Then Alcinous made his reply to the hero, responding:
- NEW: Answering him in his turn, Alcinous spoke to the hero:
- Verbatim echo of 347, matching the identical Greek.

**11.377** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς — δέ
- OLD: Then resourceful Odysseus made his response to him, replying:
- NEW: And in answer resourceful Odysseus spoke to him, saying:
- Verbatim echo of 354, matching the identical Greek.

**11.467** ἦλθε δʼ ἐπὶ ψυχή — δέ
- OLD: Then came the spirit of lord Achilles, the son born of Peleus,
- NEW: There came the spirit of lord Achilles, the son born of Peleus,

**11.471** ἔγνω δὲ ψυχή με — δέ
- OLD: Then the soul of Aeacus’ swift-footed grandson knew me;
- NEW: Aeacus’ swift-footed grandson knew me, the soul of him;
- Subject-first. The Greek is verb-first (ἔγνω) with the subject postponed; the
  English keeps the postponement by trailing "the soul of him."

**11.534** μοῖραν καὶ γέρας ἐσθλὸν ἔχων … ἔβαινεν — asyndeton (participial)
- OLD: then he embarked with his share and a beautiful prize of distinction,
- NEW: he went aboard with his share and a beautiful prize of distinction,
- The temporal force belongs to 533's ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ ("But when"), already rendered
  there; 534 was doubling it.

**11.572** τὸν δὲ μετʼ … εἰσενόησα — δέ + μετά ("after him")
- OLD: Then after him I distinguished the giant Orion,
- NEW: After him I distinguished Orion, the giant,
- μετά already carries the sequence; *Then* was pure duplication.

**11.599** αὐτὰρ ὅ γʼ ἂψ ὤσασκε — αὐτάρ
- OLD: Then he would struggle again and would push, while sweat from his members
- NEW: But he would thrust it back up in his straining, while sweat from his members
- αὐτάρ → *But*, and ἄψ ("back again") + τιταινόμενος ("straining") now both land;
  the old "struggle again and would push" blurred them.

**11.601** τὸν δὲ μετʼ εἰσενόησα — δέ + μετά
- OLD: Then after him I distinguished the powerful Heracles—only
- NEW: After him I distinguished the powerful Heracles—only
- Verbatim echo of 572, matching the identical Greek.

**11.630** καί νύ κʼ ἔτι προτέρους ἴδον — καί + νυ + potential κε
- OLD: Then I would surely have seen the men of the old age I longed for,
- NEW: Yes, and I would have beheld the men of the old age I longed for,
- The unrealized-potential idiom; καί νύ κε is "and now indeed I would have," which
  is modal, not temporal.

**11.639** τὴν δὲ … φέρε κῦμα ῥόοιο — δέ
- OLD: then did the current’s billow convey her along Ocean’s river,
- NEW: onward the current’s billow conveyed her along Ocean’s river,
- Note: the preceding line's "Swiftly" (638) legitimately renders αἶψα; no second
  speed-adverb was introduced here.

## Kept — warranted

**11.44** δὴ τότʼ ἔπειθʼ — the strongest warrant in the book (δή + τότε + ἔπειτα).
**11.121** δὴ ἔπειτα — ἔπειτα, in Tiresias's sequence of instructions.
**11.129** καὶ τότε δή — the apodosis Tiresias has been building toward.
**11.526** ἔνθʼ — temporal ἔνθα, the moment inside the wooden horse.

No crowding adjustment was needed: 121 and 129 are the only warranted pair inside
a ten-line window, and both carry genuine adverbs (ἔπειτα, καὶ τότε δή) marking
successive stages of one prophecy — a real sequence, not a tic. The habitual *Then*
at 132, which sat between them, is gone, so the window now reads as two marked
steps rather than three.

# Book 12 de-filler — all 24 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then*; no retained filler-class close was
   needed (the two τότʼ warrants at 12.54/164 and the τότε at 12.366 surface as
   line-*initial* "Then/then," which was never at issue).
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, plurals, compounds), not adding new words.
4. The masculine-ending budget (up to 3) went unused: 12.157, the one candidate,
   found a feminine reorder ("...turning aside from the death and destruction").
5. Where the Greek repeats a formula, the English now does too: 12.54 and 12.164
   (πλεόνεσσι τότʼ ἐν δεσμοῖσι) share the verbatim tail "in a greater number of
   fetters." Note for the Book 11 pass: 12.141 = 11.114 (ὀψὲ κακῶς νεῖαι, ὀλέσας
   ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους); 11.114 still reads "with all comrades lost there," and
   should be brought to match "late will you come and in evil condition, having
   lost all companions."
6. Voice rule holds: no lexical contractions anywhere in Circe's speech.

---

**12.2** νηῦς, ἀπὸ δʼ ἵκετο κῦμα θαλάσσης εὐρυπόροιο
- OLD: entering out on the swell of the broad-roaded face of the sea there,
- NEW: out on the swell of the broad-roaded face of the sea we were entering,
- The participle the old line already had now closes the line.

**12.14** τύμβον χεύαντες καὶ ἐπὶ στήλην ἐρύσαντες
- OLD: we built the mound and dragged onto it a great pillar of stone there;
- NEW: we built up the mound, and dragged onto it a stone pillar;
- "great" was not in the Greek (στήλην is just "pillar") and goes out with the filler.

**12.52** ὄφρα κε τερπόμενος ὄπʼ ἀκούσῃς Σειρήνοιιν.
- OLD: so you may hear the voices of both Sirens and take your delight there.
- NEW: so that you may hear the voices of both of the Sirens, delighting.
- τερπόμενος is a participle; it now closes the line as one.

**12.54** οἱ δέ σʼ ἔτι πλεόνεσσι τότʼ ἐν δεσμοῖσι διδέντων.
- OLD: then let the men bind you down with a greater number of bonds there.
- NEW: then let the men bind you down in a greater number of fetters.
- Formula pair with 12.164; the tails now match verbatim. Line-initial "then" = τότʼ,
  which is in the Greek.

**12.61** Πλαγκτὰς δή τοι τάς γε θεοὶ μάκαρες καλέουσι.
- OLD: all of the blessed immortals call these the Wandering Rocks there.
- NEW: these are the rocks the blessed immortal gods call the Wanderers.
- Πλαγκταί = "the Wanderers"; the name itself now closes the line.

**12.67** ἀλλά θʼ ὁμοῦ πίνακάς τε νεῶν καὶ σώματα φωτῶν
- OLD: pieces of ships and the bodies of men are together conveyed there,
- NEW: pieces of ships and the bodies of men are conveyed all together,
- ὁμοῦ ("together") takes the close; "conveyed" kept so as not to duplicate
  12.68's "carried away."

**12.91** σμερδαλέη κεφαλή, ἐν δὲ τρίστοιχοι ὀδόντες
- OLD: sits one hideous head, and the teeth stand in three crowded rows there,
- NEW: sits one hideous head, and the teeth in three crowded rows are standing,
- Comma (not the draft's semicolon) kept at the close: 12.92 "crowded and thick..."
  is appositive to the rows and must stay attached.

**12.111** ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτὰρ ἐγώ μιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον·
- OLD: Thus did she speak, and I answered the goddess and said in response then:
- NEW: Thus did she speak, and in words of response I answered the goddess:

**12.123** τόσσῃσιν κεφαλῇσι, τόσους δʼ ἐκ φῶτας ἕληται.
- OLD: seize with all six heads and carry six more of your men there.
- NEW: seize with all six of her heads, and carry off six more of your comrades.
- "more" restored (τόσους ... ἕληται after ἐξαῦτις: as many *again*); φῶτας as
  "comrades" for the trochaic close. Full stop kept — 12.124 begins a new sentence.

**12.141** ὀψὲ κακῶς νεῖαι, ὀλέσας ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους.
- OLD: late will you come and in evil condition, with all comrades lost there.”
- NEW: late will you come and in evil condition, having lost all companions.”
- ὀλέσας is a participle; "having lost" restores it. See header note on 11.114.

**12.157** ἤ κεν ἀλευάμενοι θάνατον καὶ κῆρα φύγοιμεν.
- OLD: or turn aside from the death and destruction and make our escape there.
- NEW: or make our escape, turning aside from the death and destruction.
- The draft spent a masculine ending here ("...and so escape"); unnecessary —
  ἀλευάμενοι is itself a participle, and fronting the main verb lets the Greek's
  own "death and destruction" close the line feminine. Budget unspent.

**12.164** ὑμεῖς δὲ πλεόνεσσι τότʼ ἐν δεσμοῖσι πιέζειν.
- OLD: then you must bind me down with a greater number of bonds there.”
- NEW: then you must bind me down in a greater number of fetters.”
- Verbatim tail with 12.54, as in the Greek.

**12.210** εἴλει ἐνὶ σπῆι γλαφυρῷ κρατερῆφι βίηφιν·
- OLD: Cyclops confined us by terrible force, and yet from the cave there,
- NEW: Cyclops confined us by terrible force, and yet from its hollow,
- The draft's "in the hollow grotto" doubled the cave already named in 12.209
  ("in his powerful cavern") and dropped ἀλλὰ καὶ ἔνθεν ("but even from there"),
  leaving 12.211's "aided by my great prowess..." to read as if Cyclops were the
  one aided. Kept the old line's bridge, with γλαφυρῷ supplying "hollow" as the
  noun ("its hollow" = the cavern's) and ἔνθεν the "from."

**12.237** ἦ τοι ὅτʼ ἐξεμέσειε, λέβης ὣς ἐν πυρὶ πολλῷ
- OLD: When she vomited upward, resembling over a powerful fire there
- NEW: When she vomited up, over a powerful fire resembling
- No comma at the close (the draft had one): "resembling / some great cauldron"
  must enjamb straight into 12.238, exactly as the old line did.

**12.338** οἱ δʼ ἄρα μοι γλυκὺν ὕπνον ἐπὶ βλεφάροισιν ἔχευαν.
- OLD: but they poured down a sweetness of sleep on the lids of my eyes there.
- NEW: but down on the lids of my eyes they poured a sweetness of slumber.
- "slumber" is the book's own word for ὕπνος (12.32, 144, 169, 372).

**12.356** τὰς δὲ περίστησάν τε καὶ εὐχετόωντο θεοῖσιν,
- OLD: Standing around the victims, they prayed to the gods, and they plucked there
- NEW: Standing around the victims, they prayed to the gods, and were plucking
- The draft's "then plucking" left 12.357's "tender young leaves" with no finite
  verb through 12.358 (a fragment) and mis-sequenced δρεψάμενοι, the leaves being
  plucked *for* the rite. Finite "were plucking" keeps the grammar and the close.

**12.366** καὶ τότε μοι βλεφάρων ἐξέσσυτο νήδυμος ὕπνος,
- OLD: Then did the sleep of great depth fly off from the lids of my eyes there;
- NEW: Then from the lids of my eyes did the sleep of great depth go flying;
- Line-initial "Then" = τότε, in the Greek.

**12.391** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῥʼ ἐπὶ νῆα κατήλυθον ἠδὲ θάλασσαν,
- OLD: When I had come once again to the ship and the shore of the sea there,
- NEW: When I had come once again down to the ship and the sea-brine,
- The draft's "sea-waters" is not the poem's word; "sea-brine" is (12.401, 431,
  444), and "down" restores κατήλυθον. Compound close in the poem's idiom.

**12.394** τοῖσιν δʼ αὐτίκʼ ἔπειτα θεοὶ τέραα προύφαινον·
- OLD: Soon the immortals began to display strange signs for the men there:
- NEW: Soon thereafter the immortals revealed to the men strange portents:
- The draft's "revealed to us" misread τοῖσιν — the portents were shown to the
  offending companions, as the old line had it. "thereafter" = ἔπειτα.

**12.398** δαίνυντʼ Ἠελίοιο βοῶν ἐλάσαντες ἀρίστας·
- OLD: feasting on Helios’ cattle they stole, the best in the herd there.
- NEW: feasting, since they had driven away the best of Helios’ cattle.
- ἐλάσαντες is "having driven off," not "stole" — the draft's correction, kept.

**12.403** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ τὴν νῆσον ἐλείπομεν, οὐδέ τις ἄλλη
- OLD: After we left the island behind, and no other land there
- NEW: After we left the island behind us, and no land other
- The Greek line itself ends on ἄλλη ("other") with γαιάων enjambed; the
  postpositive order mirrors it, and 12.404's "anywhere showed" completes it.

**12.410** ἀμφοτέρους· ἱστὸς δʼ ὀπίσω πέσεν, ὅπλα τε πάντα
- OLD: backward the mast fell, casting its tackle all into the bilge there.
- NEW: backward the mast fell, and into the bilge went all of its tackle.

**12.419** κύμασιν ἐμφορέοντο, θεὸς δʼ ἀποαίνυτο νόστον.
- OLD: borne on the waves, and the god denied their homeward return there.
- NEW: borne on the waves, and the god denied them their homeward returning.
- The draft's "kept them from" collided with 12.420's "Yet I kept moving";
  the old line's own verb "denied" stays.

**12.452** σοί τε καὶ ἰφθίμῃ ἀλόχῳ· ἐχθρὸν δέ μοί ἐστιν
- OLD: both to yourself and your powerful wife; and it is hateful to me now,
- NEW: both to yourself and your powerful wife; and to me it is hateful,
- The draft's "...it is hateful to me," scans only five beats; "to me it is
  hateful" restores the sixth and closes on a true trochee, and 12.453 "telling
  a story again..." follows as before.

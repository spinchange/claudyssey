# Book 02 "Then"-opening pass — 32 examined, 6 kept, 26 rewritten

Warrant rule: a verse may open with "Then" only where the Greek carries a real
temporal adverb — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα,
or the apodosis of an ἦμος-clause. Light connectives (bare δέ, αὐτάρ, ἀτάρ,
ὣς + verb, καί, γάρ, asyndeton) do not warrant it; those openings were rewritten
to reflect the actual connective. Every replacement still scans as six falling
beats; no rewritten line ends in filler *there/now/then/here*; no new "Then"-
openings introduced.

Variety across the 26 rewrites: And/and 5 (four of them one verbatim reply-
formula, see note at 02.129), When 4, But/but 4, So 2, while 2, Once more 2
(verbatim pair), and one each of Thus, yet, Old (subject-first), You (subject-
first), Pour (verb-first), Next, up (adverb-of-motion, reordered from the old
line's own "hauled up"). No opening exceeds a fifth of the rewrites.

**02.9** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῥʼ (light αὐτάρ + when-clause)
- OLD: Then, when the men had assembled and all were collected together,
- NEW: When the men had assembled and all were collected together,

**02.101** μή … νεμεσήσῃ (negative purpose clause; no temporal adverb)
- OLD: Then no Achaean woman around us can censure or shame me
- NEW: Thus no Achaean woman around us can censure or shame me
- "Thus" = by this device (the shroud), the actual force of the μή-clause.

**02.105** νύκτας δʼ (bare δέ, contrastive with 104's μέν)
- OLD: then in the nighttime, under the torches, again she unraveled.
- NEW: yet in the nighttime, under the torches, again she unraveled.

**02.109** καὶ τήν γʼ … ἐφεύρομεν (bare καί)
- OLD: then we discovered her loosening all of the radiant fabric.
- NEW: and we discovered her loosening all of the radiant fabric.

**02.129** τὸν δʼ αὖ … ἀντίον ηὔδα (reply formula, bare δέ + αὖ)
- OLD: Then in reply spoke the thoughtful and steadfast young Telemachus:
- NEW: And in reply spoke the thoughtful and steadfast young Telemachus:
- The Greek line recurs verbatim at 208, 309, 371 (τὴν for τόν); the English
  now recurs verbatim with it, per the project's formula rule.

**02.150** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή ("but when" — ἀλλά, not τότε)
- OLD: Then, when they came to the midst of the many-voiced Ithacan council,
- NEW: But when they came to the midst of the many-voiced Ithacan council,

**02.157** τοῖσι δὲ καὶ μετέειπε (bare δέ + καί)
- OLD: Then the old hero Halitherses arose to address them,
- NEW: Old Halitherses, the hero, arose in the midst to address them,
- Subject-first; "in the midst" renders the μετά of μετέειπε.

**02.382** ἔνθʼ αὖτʼ ἄλλʼ ἐνόησε (temporal ἔνθα — warranted, but crowded)
- OLD: Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena conceived a new purpose.
- NEW: Once more the gray-eyed goddess Athena conceived a new purpose.
- CROWDING: three warranted "Then"s stood at 382/389/393 within twelve lines.
  The ἔνθʼ αὖτε pair (382 = 393, verbatim in the Greek) is varied as a pair to
  keep its echo, and "Once more" finally renders the αὖτε the old line dropped;
  389's καὶ τότε, the most emphatic, keeps its "Then".

**02.386** ἡ δʼ αὖτε (bare δέ + αὖτε "in her turn")
- OLD: Then from Noemon, the splendid son of the wise man Phronius,
- NEW: Next from Noemon, the splendid son of the wise man Phronius,
- "Next" renders αὖτε's in-turn sequence, not an invented adverb.

**02.393** ἔνθʼ αὖτʼ ἄλλʼ ἐνόησε (= 382; crowding pair)

**02.426** ἕλκον δʼ ἱστία λευκά (bare δέ)
- OLD: then by the well-twisted ox-hide ropes they hauled up the white sail.
- NEW: up by the well-twisted ox-hide ropes they hauled the white sail.
- The old line's own "up" moves to the head; nothing added, nothing lost.

**02.430** δησάμενοι δʼ ἄρα ὅπλα (participle + δέ)
- OLD: Then, when the tackle was fastened throughout the swift black-hulled vessel,
- NEW: When they had fastened the tackle throughout the swift black-hulled vessel,
- Also restores the active voice of δησάμενοι (the crew bound it themselves).

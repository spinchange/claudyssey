# Book 02 de-filler — all 14 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling
line; no line ends in unmotivated *there/now/then*; the preferred fix is
reordering onto a naturally trochaic word; masculine endings budgeted at 2–3
per book (one used: 02.361); the Greek formula οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν (02.36 =
02.296) keeps a verbatim English echo ("not any longer").

Adjacent lines appear as their own entries only where a fix forced a
consequential edit (02.297, 02.351).

---

**02.8** οἱ μὲν ἐκήρυσσον, τοὶ δʼ ἠγείροντο μάλʼ ὦκα.
- OLD: Loudly the heralds proclaimed it; the people assembled in haste there.
- NEW: Loudly the heralds proclaimed it; in haste the people assembled.
- The line-end "assembled" against 9's "had assembled" mirrors the Greek's own
  root-echo (ἠγείροντο / ἤγερθεν).

**02.14** ἕζετο δʼ ἐν πατρὸς θώκῳ, εἶξαν δὲ γέροντες.
- OLD: down in his father's seat he sat, and the elders made way there.
- NEW: down he sat in his father's seat, while the elders yielded.
- εἶξαν = "yielded," now the verb itself supplies the feminine close.

**02.36** οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν ἧστο, μενοίνησεν δʼ ἀγορεύειν,
- OLD: No longer seated, he rose, for desire now impelled him to speak there.
- NEW: Not any longer he sat, for desire to speak had impelled him.
- "Not any longer" is the formula for οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν, echoed verbatim at
  296–297 where the Greek repeats it.

**02.121** τάων οὔ τις ὁμοῖα νοήματα Πηνελοπείῃ
- OLD: none of those women possessed an intelligence equal to hers now.
- NEW: none of those women possessed an intelligence equal to Penelope's.
- Restores the dative referent Πηνελοπείῃ the old line blurred to "hers"; the
  name closes the line exactly as it closes the Greek (dactylic final foot,
  as the pilot allowed for Peirithoüs at 21.296).

**02.132** ζώει ὅ γʼ ἦ τέθνηκε· κακὸν δέ με πόλλʼ ἀποτίνειν
- OLD: whether alive or now dead? Heavy payment would fall upon me then,
- NEW: whether he's living or dead? And a heavy repayment awaits me,
- "awaits" carries the prospective sense of the infinitive ἀποτίνειν into
  133's "owed to Icarius, should I..."; the mid-line "now" (translating
  nothing) is gone as well. Telemachus is a mortal speaker: "he's" is licit.

**02.174** φῆν κακὰ πολλὰ παθόντʼ, ὀλέσαντʼ ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους,
- OLD: I said, after sufferings many, with all his companions destroyed there,
- NEW: I said, after many afflictions, with all his companions perished,
- πάντας ("all") kept; the absolute construction with "perished" gives the
  trochaic close the old line bought with filler.

**02.258** οἱ μὲν ἄρʼ ἐσκίδναντο ἑὰ πρὸς δώμαθʼ ἕκαστος,
- OLD: Each man scattered away and returned to the house where he lived then;
- NEW: Then they scattered away, and each man went home to his household;
- ἑὰ πρὸς δώμαθʼ ἕκαστος; "home to his household" carries a slight doubling
  (ἑά "his own" underwrites both halves) — logged as residue.

**02.285** σοὶ δʼ ὁδὸς οὐκέτι δηρὸν ἀπέσσεται ἣν σὺ μενοινᾷς·
- OLD: Not for much longer shall this expedition of yours be postponed now.
- NEW: Not for much longer shall this, the journey you yearn for, be distant.
- ἀπέσσεται ("shall be distant") and ἣν σὺ μενοινᾷς ("which you yearn for")
  both restored; Athena speaks, so no contractions.

**02.296** ὣς φάτʼ Ἀθηναίη κούρη Διός· οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν
- OLD: Thus spoke Athena, the daughter of Zeus, and no longer did he then,
- NEW: Thus spoke Athena, the daughter of Zeus; and Telemachus lingered
- The name (the old translation's own supplement — the Greek subject is
  implicit) moves up from 297 so the line can close on the verb.

**02.297** μίμνεν, ἐπεὶ θεοῦ ἔκλυεν αὐδήν.
- OLD: young Telemachus, linger after the goddess's voice came upon him.
- NEW: not any longer, once he had heard the voice of the goddess.
- Forced by the 296 fix. ἔκλυεν = "he heard," restored (the old "came upon
  him" had inverted it); "not any longer" echoes 36, as the Greek does.

**02.302** ἔν τʼ ἄρα οἱ φῦ χειρί, ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε·
- OLD: took the young man by the hand and addressed him aloud by his name there:
- NEW: took the young man by the hand, and spoke a word, and addressed him:
- The double formula ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε rendered as its two limbs
  ("spoke a word, and addressed him"), the τε...τε kept as "and...and."

**02.325** ἦ μάλα Τηλέμαχος φόνον ἡμῖν μερμηρίζει.
- OLD: “Clearly young Telemachus plans the destruction and death of us all now!
- NEW: “Clearly young Telemachus plans the destruction and death of all of us!
- Pure word-order swap; nothing added, filler dropped.

**02.350** ἡδύν, ὅτις μετὰ τὸν λαρώτατος ὃν σὺ φυλάσσεις
- OLD: sweet and the finest in flavor, excepting the portion reserved there,
- NEW: sweet and the finest in flavor, except for the portion you're guarding,
- ὃν σὺ φυλάσσεις lands on the participle; mortal speaker, contraction licit.

**02.351** κείνου ὀιομένη τὸν κάμμορον, εἴ ποθεν ἔλθοι
- OLD: held while you think of that ill-fated man, if Odysseus somehow,
- NEW: thinking of that ill-fated man, if Odysseus somehow,
- Forced by the 350 fix: "held" restated φυλάσσεις, now covered by "guarding";
  ὀιομένη is carried by "thinking" alone.

**02.361** ὣς φάτο, κώκυσεν δὲ φίλη τροφὸς Εὐρύκλεια,
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and Eurycleia, his nurse from his infancy, wailed then,
- NEW: Thus he spoke; Eurycleia, his nurse from his infancy, wailed,
- Licensed masculine ending #1: κώκυσεν is the shriek itself, and the line
  breaks off on the stressed monosyllable as the cry breaks out; 362's
  "grieving aloud..." follows without a seam. (The junior's draft appended
  "wailed aloud," duplicating 362's "aloud," and ran to seven beats.)

**02.364** ἔπλετο; πῇ δʼ ἐθέλεις ἰέναι πολλὴν ἐπὶ γαῖαν
- OLD: Where can you travel across the immeasurable breadth of the earth now,
- NEW: Where do you wish to travel across the earth's measureless spaces,
- ἐθέλεις ἰέναι = "do you wish to travel," restored (the old "can" flattened
  it). "measureless spaces" inherits the old line's inflation of πολλήν
  ("wide") — logged as residue; the plural supplies the feminine close.

---

## Tally

- 14 filler-final lines reworked (16 verse lines touched: 297 and 351 were
  forced adjacent edits); 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*, and none
  needed a warranted keeper.
- 1 masculine ending licensed (02.361), within the 2–3/book budget.
- Formula restored: οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν at 36 and 296–297 now shares the verbatim
  English "not any longer."
- Fidelity recovered along the way: Πηνελοπείῃ at 121, ἣν σὺ μενοινᾷς and
  ἀπέσσεται at 285, ἔκλυεν at 297, ἐθέλεις at 364; the untranslated mid-line
  "now" at 132 also removed.
- Honest costs incurred: "home to his household" slightly doubles δώμαθʼ at
  258; "measureless spaces" at 364 keeps the old line's inflation of πολλήν.
- Of the junior's 16 draft entries, 8 were rewritten in review (36, 132, 174,
  285, 297, 302, 361, 364) — for beat-count failures (132, 174, 361, 364), a
  three-syllable final foot (297), long-/long- jingles (36, 285), and a baggy
  triple verb at 302.

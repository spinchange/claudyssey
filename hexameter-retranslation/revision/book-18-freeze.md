# Book 18 freeze — speech-formula standardization + here-final sweep

Verses changed: **20** (10 by the speech-formula ruling, 10 by the here-final sweep).
Scansion lines updated in `book-18-performance.md`: **20**.
Verse count unchanged against the Greek (428 lines).

Formula inventory for this book, after the pass:

- Finite ὣς φάτο family (40, 50, 58, 66, 75, 88, 117, 151, 185, 281, 290, 320, 387, 410, 422) — all now open "So he spoke," / "So she spoke," / "So they spoke," / "So spoke <named subject>," per the ruling. 185, 387, 410 and 422 needed changing; the rest were already conformant. 75 and 117 (ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφαν, plural) already read "So they spoke,"; 50 and 290 (ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος) keep the named-subject shape and are verbatim identical to each other, as the Greek is.
- Participial ὣς εἰπών (340) and ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας (394) — standardized to **"So having spoken,"**, the poem's dominant participial rendering. Both had drifted (340 into "Thus he spoke", 394 into the finite "So he spoke"), flattening distinct Greek formulas into the finite one; restored.
- ὣς φαμένη (206) — also participial; brought into the same "So having spoken," shape.
- ὣς ἄρʼ ὣς ἔρξασʼ (197) — a *different* participle (ἔρξασα, "having done"), correctly rendered "So having wrought all this"; left alone so it stays distinct.
- Iterative ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον (72, 400) — identical Greek verses, now identical English in the ruled "and so would a man speak…" shape.
- ἦ ῥα (108), ἦ ῥʼ (356) — untouched; neither opens with "Thus".

No line-initial *Thus* remains. **residual_thus for this book: 0.**

**18.271** κεῖνος τὼς ἀγόρευε keeps its "So he spoke in that way" opening: τὼς genuinely means "in that way" here (Penelope quoting Odysseus' parting instruction), it is not the ὣς φάτο family, and the line is not line-initial *Thus*. Only its trailing "here" was removed.

Here-final: 11 found, 10 unwarranted and reworked. **18.266 keeps its "here": ἐνθάδε is in the Greek.**

## Job 1 — speech formulas

**18.72** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον·
- OLD: and thus would a man look over and say to another beside him:
- NEW: and so would a man speak, glancing across at another beside him:
- Iterative formula, kept distinct from ὣς φάτο; line-initial-clause "thus" gone. ἰδών is the participle ("glancing"), which the old line had promoted to a finite verb.

**18.185** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη, γρηῢς δὲ διὲκ μεγάροιο βεβήκει
- OLD: So then she spoke, and the old woman went through the hall of the manor,
- NEW: So she spoke, and the old woman went through the hall of the manor,
- ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη is in the ruled family; "So then" collapsed to the ruled opening.

**18.206** ὣς φαμένη κατέβαινʼ ὑπερώϊα σιγαλόεντα,
- OLD: So saying, she came down from her upper chamber so silent,
- NEW: So having spoken, she came down from her glimmering chamber,
- Participial, standardized to the dominant participial rendering. σιγαλόεντα is "gleaming/glossy", not "silent" — the old line had read it as if from σιγή; "glimmering" corrects the sense and takes the close, and the "so" propping it up goes with it.

**18.243** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον·
- OLD: So in this way they spoke such words there one to another together.
- NEW: So the two of them spoke such things to each other together.
- Same Greek verse as 17.166 and 17.290; all three now read identically. "in this way" and "there" were both padding on the same line.

**18.340** ὣς εἰπὼν ἐπέεσσι διεπτοίησε γυναῖκας.
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and with words he scattered the women in terror.
- NEW: So having spoken, with words he scattered the women in terror.
- Participial formula restored from the finite shape, and the line-initial "Thus" goes with it.

**18.387** ὣς ἔφατʼ, Εὐρύμαχος δʼ ἐχολώσατο κηρόθι μᾶλλον,
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and Eurymachus felt even greater anger within him.
- NEW: So he spoke, and Eurymachus felt even greater anger within him.
- Straight ὣς ἔφατʼ. The Greek half-line is identical to 17.458, which already read "So he spoke, and Antinous grew still more angry in spirit"; the openings now match.

**18.394** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας σφέλας ἔλλαβεν· αὐτὰρ Ὀδυσσεὺς
- OLD: So he spoke, and seized up a stool; but noble Odysseus
- NEW: So having spoken, he seized up a stool; but noble Odysseus
- Participial formula restored; the finite rendering had flattened it into ὣς φάτο.

**18.400** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον·
- OLD: and thus would one man speak, glancing across at another beside him:
- NEW: and so would a man speak, glancing across at another beside him:
- Verbatim echo of 18.72, as in the Greek.

**18.410** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, οἱ δʼ ἄρα πάντες ὀδὰξ ἐν χείλεσι φύντες
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and they all bit hard on their lips in amazement,
- NEW: So he spoke, and they all bit hard on their lips in amazement,
- Straight ὣς ἔφαθʼ; now matches 18.40 / 18.58 / 18.66, which share the half-line.

**18.422** ὣς φάτο, τοῖσι δὲ πᾶσιν ἑαδότα μῦθον ἔειπε.
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and the speech that he uttered was pleasing to all them.
- NEW: So he spoke, and the speech that he uttered was pleasing to all of them.
- Straight ὣς φάτο. The old line's "to all them" was ungrammatical filler-compression forced by the longer opening; the ruled opening frees the syllable back.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**18.23** αὔριον· οὐ μὲν γάρ τί σʼ ὑποστρέψεσθαι ὀΐω
- OLD: on the morrow, for I do not think you would come back here
- NEW: on the morrow, for I do not think you would ever return
- No deictic; ὑποστρέψεσθαι is simply "return", which the new close renders directly.

**18.55** ἀλλʼ ἄγε νῦν μοι πάντες ὀμόσσατε καρτερὸν ὅρκον,
- OLD: Come then, now swear to me all of you a binding oath here,
- NEW: Come then, all of you swear me a powerful oath I may trust in,
- Same Greek verse as 12.298, and now the same English, as the Greek repeats. The oath's binding force replaces the filler; the doubled "then … now" also goes.

**18.105** ἐνταυθοῖ νῦν ἧσο σύας τε κύνας τʼ ἀπερύκων,
- OLD: “Sit there now, and keep away the pigs and the dogs from here,
- NEW: “Sit there now, and keep the pigs and the dogs from the doorway,
- ἐνταυθοῖ is a real locative, but it is already discharged by "Sit there" at the head of the line; the line-final "here" translated nothing. ἀπερύκων's implied threshold supplies the close.

**18.136** τοῖος γὰρ νόος ἐστὶν ἐπιχθονίων ἀνθρώπων
- OLD: For such is the mind of men who dwell upon the face of the earth here,
- NEW: For such is the mind of the men who dwell on the face of the earth,
- ἐπιχθονίων is "on the earth" and is already fully rendered; "here" was pure metrical ballast.

**18.182** ἀλλά μοι Αὐτονόην τε καὶ Ἱπποδάμειαν ἄνωχθι
- OLD: But bid Autonoe and Hippodameia to come here
- NEW: But bid Autonoe and Hippodameia to join me
- No deictic in 182; and 183 already carries "here in the chamber" for ἐν μεγάροισιν, so the old close was a duplicate as well as filler. μοι is restored as "me".

**18.232** οἵδε κακὰ φρονέοντες, ἐμοὶ δʼ οὐκ εἰσὶν ἀρωγοί.
- OLD: plotting out evil; and there are no helpers who stand at my side here.
- NEW: plotting out evil; and there are no helpers to stand beside me.
- No deictic; ἐμοί is a plain dative of interest. "beside me" absorbs it and closes trochaically.

**18.254** εἰ κεῖνός γʼ ἐλθὼν τὸν ἐμὸν βίον ἀμφιπολεύοι,
- OLD: If he came back again and took thought for the life that is mine here,
- NEW: If he came back again and took thought for the life I am living,
- τὸν ἐμὸν βίον is "my life", no deictic; the relative recast carries ἐμόν and gives the feminine close.

**18.266** αὐτοῦ ἐνὶ Τροίῃ· σοὶ δʼ **ἐνθάδε** πάντα μελόντων.
- KEPT: or if I shall fall there within Troy; but let all be your care here.
- **Warranted.** ἐνθάδε is in the Greek and is the whole point of the contrast with αὐτοῦ ἐνὶ Τροίῃ ("there in Troy" against "here at home"). Both deictics are load-bearing; the line is untouched.

**18.271** κεῖνος τὼς ἀγόρευε· τὰ δὴ νῦν πάντα τελεῖται.
- OLD: So he spoke in that way, and now all these things are fulfilled here.
- NEW: So he spoke in that way; and now all of these things are fulfilled.
- No deictic: τὰ δὴ νῦν πάντα τελεῖται is "all this is now being fulfilled". The opening is left as it stands — τὼς genuinely means "in that way" and is not the ὣς φάτο family.

**18.409** ὁππότε θυμὸς ἄνωγε· διώκω δʼ οὔ τινʼ ἐγώ γε.
- OLD: as for me, I am driving no man away from the house here.”
- NEW: as for me, I am driving no man at all from this household.”
- The Greek has no house at all in this clause, let alone a deictic: οὔ τινʼ ἐγώ γε is "not anyone, I at least". "no man at all" restores the emphatic τινα/γε that the filler had displaced.

**18.421** Τηλεμάχῳ μελέμεν· τοῦ γὰρ φίλον ἵκετο δῶμα.
- OLD: for Telemachus to care for, since to his dear house he has come here.”
- NEW: for Telemachus to care for, since to his dear house he came.”
- No deictic; the aorist ἵκετο is a simple "he came", and the old perfect plus "here" was two syllables of padding on one verb.

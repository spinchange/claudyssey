# Book 23 de-filler — all 38 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied, as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened to a feminine close unless licensed otherwise).
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek warrants it; none of
   the 38 required a keeper.
3. The preferred fix is reordering so a naturally trochaic word lands last;
   unavoidable added words are logged per verse.
4. Masculine endings budgeted at 3 per book. Used once: 23.74, where the
   cross-book boar-scar formula (21.219, 24.332) already closes on "white tusk."
5. Formulas made verbatim where the Greek repeats: 23.70 now matches the
   tooth-fence formula of 19.492 / 21.168; 23.291 restores "close-jointed
   bedstead" to echo 23.177 (πυκινὸν λέχος both times); 23.50 adopts Book 22's
   own sulfur diction (22.481–493) for θεειοῦται.
6. Voice rule (gods uncontracted in dialogue) — no god speaks in these 38 lines;
   no conflicts.

---

**23.2** δεσποίνῃ ἐρέουσα φίλον πόσιν ἔνδον ἐόντα·
- OLD: going to tell her mistress her own dear husband was inside there.
- NEW: going to tell her mistress: inside was her own dear husband.
- The junior's "that inside was" left four unstressed syllables in the third
  foot; the colon carries the reported speech without the connective.

**23.4** στῆ δʼ ἄρʼ ὑπὲρ κεφαλῆς καί μιν πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπεν·
- OLD: Then she stood at her head and addressed her, speaking a word there:
- NEW: Then she stood at her head, and spoke a word, and addressed her:
- Recast to the poem's own speech-formula idiom ("spoke a word, and addressed
  him/her" — 2.302, 15.124, 19.402, al.); closes on "addressed her."

**23.6** ὀφθαλμοῖσι τεοῖσι τά τʼ ἔλδεαι ἤματα πάντα.
- OLD: with your own eyes the thing you have longed for all your days to see now.
- NEW: with your own eyes the thing you have longed for all of your lifetime.
- ἤματα πάντα = "all your days" ≈ "all of your lifetime"; the junior's
  line-final "beholding" duplicated 5's "witness," which already renders ἴδηαι.

**23.9** κήδεσκον καὶ κτήματʼ ἔδον βιόωντό τε παῖδα.
- OLD: wrought him affliction and ate his wealth and oppressed his son there.”
- NEW: wrought him affliction, and ate his possessions, oppressing his offspring.”
- The three actions in Greek order; βιόωντο kept as "oppressing" (the old
  line's own verb), παῖδα as trochaic "offspring."

**23.20** ἀλλʼ ἄγε νῦν κατάβηθι καὶ ἂψ ἔρχευ μέγαρόνδε.
- OLD: Come now, go down again and return to the hall of the house there.
- NEW: Come now, go down again, and make your return to the hallway.
- νῦν kept mid-line ("now"); ἂψ ἔρχευ = "make your return."

**23.23** τῷ κε τάχα στυγερῶς μιν ἐγὼν ἀπέπεμψα νέεσθαι
- OLD: I would have sent her quickly away with harshness, back to the hall there,
- NEW: I would have sent her quickly away to return in sorry fashion,
- στυγερῶς = "in sorry fashion" (Murray's "in sorry plight"); νέεσθαι = "to
  return." "Back to the hall" was the old line's own filler — not in this verse's
  Greek — and would have collided with 20's close.

**23.26** οὔ τί σε λωβεύω, τέκνον φίλον, ἀλλʼ ἔτυμόν τοι
- OLD: “Child, I am not mocking you, dear one; in very truth now
- NEW: “Child, I am not mocking you, dear one, but very truly
- The junior's "but I tell you truly" duplicated 27's line-end "as I tell you";
  the bare adverb runs on into 27 exactly as ἔτυμόν τοι runs into ἦλθ᾿ Ὀδυσεύς.

**23.38** μοῦνος ἐών, οἱ δʼ αἰὲν ἀολλέες ἔνδον ἔμιμνον.
- OLD: being alone by himself, while they were forever assembled within there?”
- NEW: being alone, while always they stayed within all together?”
- The junior's nine-beat line rebuilt: μοῦνος ἐών once ("alone by himself" was
  a double rendering), αἰέν = "always," ἀολλέες = "all together," ἔνδον ἔμιμνον
  = "stayed within."

**23.43** πρίν γʼ ὅτε δή με σὸς υἱὸς ἀπὸ μεγάροιο κάλεσσε
- OLD: Not until then did your son call me out from the hall of the house there,
- NEW: Not until then did your son summon me out of the hallway,
- "Summon" also thins the echo with 44's line-end "to call me."

**23.50** ἀθρόοι, αὐτὰρ ὁ δῶμα θεειοῦται περικαλλές,
- OLD: while he is purifying the beautiful house all around there,
- NEW: but he meanwhile purges the beautiful house with sulfur,
- θεειοῦται is literally "fumigates with sulfur"; the sulfur, dropped by the
  old line, is restored in Book 22's own diction ("Bring sulfur… so I may
  sulfur the hall," 22.481–482). ἀθρόοι is already rendered in 48
  ("all in a mass").

**23.57** μνηστῆρες, τοὺς πάντας ἐτίσατο ᾧ ἐνὶ οἴκῳ.
- OLD: and the suitors who worked him harm he punished, all of them, in his house there.”
- NEW: and he has punished them all in his own house — the suitors who wronged him.”
- The junior's close on "household" duplicated 56's line-end "household;". The
  dash apposition mirrors the Greek's pendent μνηστῆρες.

**23.70** τέκνον ἐμόν, ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων,
- OLD: “My child, what sort of word has escaped through the fence of your teeth there,
- NEW: “My child, out past the fence of your teeth what a word has escaped you,
- Junior's draft kept: now verbatim with the formula at 19.492 and 21.168.

**23.74** οὐλήν, τήν ποτέ μιν σῦς ἤλασε λευκῷ ὀδόντι.
- OLD: the scar that long ago a boar drove in him with his white tusk there.
- NEW: the scar, which once a boar struck into him with his white tusk.
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the formula already closes on "white tusk" at
  21.219 ("this scar, which once a boar struck into me with his white tusk")
  and 24.332; this line now matches 21.219 word for word apart from the person.
  The tusk is the recognition-token — the emphasis is earned.

**23.76** εἰπέμεν· ἀλλά με κεῖνος ἑλὼν ἐπὶ μάστακα χερσὶν
- OLD: you yourself; but he took hold and laid his hands on my mouth there,
- NEW: you yourself; but he seized me, and over my mouth clasped his fingers,
- Junior's draft kept. Logged: χερσίν "hands" rendered "fingers" to secure the
  feminine close.

**23.81** μαῖα φίλη, χαλεπόν σε θεῶν αἰειγενετάων
- OLD: “Dear nurse, it is hard for you to discern the designs of the gods there,
- NEW: “Dear nurse, the deathless immortals’ designs are hard for your discerning,
- θεῶν αἰειγενετάων = "the deathless immortals" (the poem's phrase at 23.163);
  σε carried by "your discerning," which 82's "to draw them forth" completes as
  before.

**23.88** ἡ δʼ ἐπεὶ εἰσῆλθεν καὶ ὑπέρβη λάϊνον οὐδόν,
- OLD: But when she had entered and passed over the threshold of stone there,
- NEW: But when she had entered, and over the threshold of stone had come stepping,
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.90** τοίχου τοῦ ἑτέρου· ὁ δʼ ἄρα πρὸς κίονα μακρὴν
- OLD: close by the opposite wall; and he by the tall pillar's side there
- NEW: close by the opposite wall; and he over against the tall pillar
- "Pillar" is itself trochaic and is the poem's word (23.191); no need for the
  junior's "column." πρός = "over against"; runs on into 91 "sat…" as the
  Greek runs into ἧστο.

**23.107** οὐδʼ εἰς ὦπα ἰδέσθαι ἐναντίον. εἰ δʼ ἐτεὸν δὴ
- OLD: nor can I look in his eyes and face him. But if in truth now
- NEW: nor can I look in his eyes and face him. But if in earnest
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.111** ὣς φάτο, μείδησεν δὲ πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς,
- OLD: So she spoke, and the great-souled, long-enduring Odysseus smiled then,
- NEW: So she spoke, and he smiled, the great-souled, long-enduring Odysseus,
- Junior's draft kept: the name closes the line as Ὀδυσσεύς does.

**23.117** ἡμεῖς δὲ φραζώμεθʼ ὅπως ὄχʼ ἄριστα γένηται.
- OLD: But let us consider ourselves how this matter may turn out best now.
- NEW: But let us ourselves consider how this may best be accomplished.
- The junior's "the two of us… be resolving" added content and forced a false
  progressive; "ourselves" keeps the ἡμεῖς emphasis.

**23.133** αὐτὰρ θεῖος ἀοιδὸς ἔχων φόρμιγγα λίγειαν
- OLD: Then let the godlike singer, with clear-toned lyre in his hands there,
- NEW: Then let the godlike singer, holding the clear-toned lyre in his fingers,
- Junior's draft kept: ἔχων = "holding." Logged: "in his fingers" concretizes
  the bare participle to close the line.

**23.144** φόρμιγγα γλαφυρήν, ἐν δέ σφισιν ἵμερον ὦρσε
- OLD: into his hands the carved lyre, and stirred in them all the desire there
- NEW: into his hands the carved lyre, and stirred in them longing
- ἵμερον ὦρσε exactly, with nothing bolted on (the junior's "a great" was
  padding); runs into 145 "for sweet song…" as the Greek runs into μολπῆς.

**23.156** αὐτὰρ κὰκ κεφαλῆς κάλλος πολὺ χεῦεν Ἀθήνη
- OLD: Then Athena poured beauty abundantly down from his head there,
- NEW: Then down from his head abundant beauty poured Athena,
- Junior's draft kept; the "down from his head" echo with 157 is the Greek's
  own repetition (κὰκ κεφαλῆς … κὰδ … κάρητος).

**23.159** ὡς δʼ ὅτε τις χρυσὸν περιχεύεται ἀργύρῳ ἀνὴρ
- OLD: As when a man overlays silver with gold, one well skilled there,
- NEW: As when a man pours gold in a layer over the silver,
- περιχεύεται rendered literally ("pours … over"). The old line's "one well
  skilled" anticipated 160's ἴδρις, which 160–161 already render ("taught in
  all craftsman's art"); the junior's "being skilled in the making" kept that
  redundancy.

**23.167** κῆρ ἀτέραμνον ἔθηκαν Ὀλύμπια δώματʼ ἔχοντες·
- OLD: they who possess Olympian halls have given an iron heart there.
- NEW: they who possess the halls of Olympus have given a heart unyielding.
- ἀτέραμνον = "unyielding," not "iron"; this also stops 167 from flattening
  into 172's genuinely iron heart (σιδήρεον), so Odysseus no longer says
  "iron" twice where Homer varies the word.

**23.171** ἀλλʼ ἄγε μοι, μαῖα, στόρεσον λέχος, ὄφρα καὶ αὐτὸς
- OLD: Come then, dear nurse, make ready my bed, so that I as well now
- NEW: Come then, dear nurse, make ready my bed for me, so that I also
- The Greek has no νῦν — the old "now" (and the junior's) was pure filler.
  ὄφρα καὶ αὐτός = "so that I also," running over into 172's "may lie down by
  myself" as αὐτός runs into λέξομαι; μοι = "for me."

**23.196** κορμὸν δʼ ἐκ ῥίζης προταμὼν ἀμφέξεσα χαλκῷ
- OLD: and from the root upward I trimmed and planed the trunk with the bronze there
- NEW: and from the root upward, trimming the trunk, with the bronze I planed it
- προταμών kept participial ("trimming"), ἀμφέξεσα the finite verb; "planed
  it / well and with careful skill" (197) restores the Greek's run-on.

**23.206** σήματʼ ἀναγνούσῃ τά οἱ ἔμπεδα πέφραδʼ Ὀδυσσεύς·
- OLD: as she knew the sure signs that Odysseus had told and made clear there;
- NEW: as she knew once again the sure signs that Odysseus had shown her;
- ἀναγνούσῃ = "knew once again" (recognition); "shown her" for πέφραδε closes
  the line on the pattern the pilot prefers. The junior's passive recast did
  not scan.

**23.218** οὐδέ κεν Ἀργείη Ἑλένη, Διὸς ἐκγεγαυῖα,
- OLD: Not even Argive Helen, the daughter begotten by Zeus there,
- NEW: Not even Argive Helen, of Zeus the daughter begotten,
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.226** εὐνῆς ἡμετέρης, ἣν οὐ βροτὸς ἄλλος ὀπώπει,
- OLD: of our bed, which no other mortal had ever beheld there,
- NEW: of our own bed, which no other mortal had ever looked on,
- Keeps the Greek's relative clause and pluperfect (the junior's "unseen by"
  recast lost both); "marriage" (junior's addition) dropped.

**23.230** πείθεις δή μευ θυμόν, ἀπηνέα περ μάλʼ ἐόντα.
- OLD: now you persuade my heart, though stubborn and hard it has been there.”
- NEW: now you persuade my heart, for all its exceeding stubbornness.”
- Junior's draft kept: περ μάλα = "for all its exceeding"; dactylic final word
  licensed by the pause at speech-end.

**23.234** ὧν τε Ποσειδάων εὐεργέα νῆʼ ἐνὶ πόντῳ
- OLD: those whose stout-built ship Poseidon has shattered at sea there,
- NEW: those men whose stout-built ship Poseidon has shattered on the waters,
- Kept active with Poseidon as subject: the junior's passive left 235's
  "driving it on…" without an agent to hang from. "Men" completes the meter
  (antecedent of ὧν).

**23.237** νηχόμενοι, πολλὴ δὲ περὶ χροῒ τέτροφεν ἅλμη,
- OLD: and much salt scurf is caked on their skin from the wash of the brine there;
- NEW: and round about their skin the brine has crusted in plenty;
- περὶ χροΐ = "round about their skin," τέτροφεν = "has crusted," πολλή = "in
  plenty." νηχόμενοι is already rendered by 236's "swim" — the junior's
  restored "swimming" would have duplicated it.

**23.278** ἀρνειὸν ταῦρόν τε συῶν τʼ ἐπιβήτορα κάπρον,
- OLD: ram and bull, and a boar that mounts on the backs of the sows then,
- NEW: a ram and a bull, and a boar that over the sows goes mounting,
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.291** αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ στόρεσαν πυκινὸν λέχος ἐγκονέουσαι,
- OLD: But when they had spread the firm bedstead, working in haste there,
- NEW: But when in their eager haste they had spread the close-jointed bedstead,
- πυκινὸν λέχος = "close-jointed bedstead," the poem's own formula at 23.177
  where the Greek phrase recurs; ἐγκονέουσαι = "in their eager haste."

**23.301** τερπέσθην μύθοισι, πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἐνέποντε,
- OLD: then they delighted in speech, and spoke to each other in turn there:
- NEW: then they took their delight in talk, telling stories to each other:
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.339** οἳ δή μιν περὶ κῆρι θεὸν ὣς τιμήσαντο
- OLD: who truly honored him from the heart as if he were a god there,
- NEW: who honored him truly, deep in their hearts, as one who was godlike,
- Junior's draft kept.

**23.341** χαλκόν τε χρυσόν τε ἅλις ἐσθῆτά τε δόντες.
- OLD: giving bronze and gold in abundance, and garments as well there.
- NEW: giving bronze and gold in abundance, together with raiment.
- ἐσθῆτα = "raiment" (the poem's word at 1.165, 19.327, 24.59); ἅλις = "in
  abundance" kept in place. The junior's "in addition" was prose.

## Tally

- Reworked and applied: **38 of 38** filler-final lines.
- Junior drafts accepted unchanged: 12 (70, 76, 88, 107, 111, 133, 156, 218,
  230, 278, 301, 339). Rewritten by the senior pass: 26.
- Masculine endings used: **1 of 3** (23.74, formula-matched to 21.219/24.332).
- Warranted filler-class keepers among the 38: none needed.
- Lines elsewhere in the book still matching the surface pattern (all outside
  the extraction's 38, left untouched): 23.128 "extends here" (πάρεστι),
  23.228 "when first I came here" (δεῦρο κιούσῃ — true locative), 23.258
  "made you return here" (ἱκέσθαι, goal of motion in 259), 23.354 "long-desired
  bed here" (ἱκόμεθα, arrival). 23.228 and 23.258 are solidly warranted;
  23.128 and 23.354 are thinner and could be revisited in a later pass.
- Logged additions/licenses: "fingers" for χερσίν (76) and line-final "in his
  fingers" (133); "men" completing ὧν's antecedent (234); dactylic final foot
  at 230 ("stubbornness") and 6 ("lifetime" for ἤματα πάντα).

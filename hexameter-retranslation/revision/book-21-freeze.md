**21.69** ἐχράετʼ ἐσθιέμεν καὶ πινέμεν ἐμμενὲς αἰεὶ

- OLD: feed and drink your fill continually, day after day here,
- NEW: feed and drink your fill continually, all of your days long,
- *JOB 2.* ἐμμενὲς αἰεὶ = “continually, always” — purely durative, no locative in the verse. “here” was metrical filler; the Greek's own “always” supplies the trochaic close.

**21.136** ὣς εἰπὼν τόξον μὲν ἀπὸ ἕο θῆκε χαμᾶζε,

- OLD: So he spoke, and away from himself he laid the bow earthward,
- NEW: So having spoken, away from himself he laid the bow earthward,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς εἰπὼν — participial; standardized to the poem's dominant participial rendering (“So having spoken,”, ~50 occurrences) and thereby un-collided from the finite ὣς φάτο family.

**21.143** ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος, τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιήνδανε μῦθος.

- OLD: So spoke Antinous, and his word was pleasing to all of them.
- NEW: So he spoke, Antinous, and his word was pleasing to all of them.
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος — finite φάτο family with named subject; ruled opening restored. Greek is verbatim identical to 21.269, so the English now is too (cf. 16.406, 20.247, 20.275 for the named-subject shape).

**21.163** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν καὶ ἀπὸ ἕο τόξον ἔθηκε,

- OLD: So then he spoke, and he set his bow away from his own hands,
- NEW: So he spoke, and he set his bow away from his own hands,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν — finite; “So then he spoke” normalized to the poem's own rendering of this formula (2.257, 10.229, 17.57, 19.29, 21.386, 22.398 all “So he spoke,”).

**21.221** ὣς εἰπὼν ῥάκεα μεγάλης ἀποέργαθεν οὐλῆς.

- OLD: So he spoke, and he drew back the rags from the great scar before them.
- NEW: So having spoken, he drew the rags back from the great scar,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς εἰπὼν — participial; was rendered with the finite formula. Re-metered after the swap; “before them” (unwarranted) dropped.

**21.232** ἄλλοι μὲν γὰρ πάντες, ὅσοι μνηστῆρες ἀγαυοί,

- OLD: all the others, all these splendid and lordly suitors here,
- NEW: for all of the others, all of these splendid and lordly suitors,
- *JOB 2.* ἄλλοι μὲν γὰρ πάντες, ὅσοι μνηστῆρες ἀγαυοί — no deictic. Reordered so “suitors” falls last.

**21.269** ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος, τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιήνδανε μῦθος.

- OLD: So spoke Antinous, and his speech gave pleasure to all of them.
- NEW: So he spoke, Antinous, and his word was pleasing to all of them.
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀντίνοος — identical Greek verse to 21.143; English now repeats verbatim as the Greek does.

**21.275** κέκλυτέ μευ, μνηστῆρες ἀγακλειτῆς βασιλείης·

- OLD: “Hear me, you suitors who court our queen of the great renown here,
- NEW: “Hear me, you suitors who court the queen of the great renown,
- *JOB 2.* κέκλυτέ μευ, μνηστῆρες ἀγακλειτῆς βασιλείης — no deictic; ἀγακλειτῆς attaches to the queen. “renown” now closes the verse.

**21.292** ἡμετέρων μύθων ξεῖνος καὶ πτωχὸς ἀκούει.

- OLD: gets to hear the speeches and words that belong among us here.
- NEW: gets to hear the speeches and words that belong among us.
- *JOB 2.* ἡμετέρων μύθων … ἀκούει — “our words”; ἡμετέρων is possessive, not locative. “among us” already carries it.

**21.314** ἔλπεαι, αἴ χʼ ὁ ξεῖνος Ὀδυσσῆος μέγα τόξον

- OLD: Do you suppose that if this stranger should string great Odysseus’ bow here,
- NEW: Do you suppose that this stranger should string the bow of Odysseus,
- *JOB 2.* αἴ χʼ ὁ ξεῖνος Ὀδυσσῆος μέγα τόξον — no deictic. Recast so the name closes the line; “great” absorbed into the Greek's own genitive phrasing.

**21.325** ἦ πολὺ χείρονες ἄνδρες ἀμύμονος ἀνδρὸς ἄκοιτιν

- OLD: “Men far inferior woo the wife of a blameless man here,
- NEW: “Men far inferior woo the wife of a man without blemish,
- *JOB 2.* ἦ πολὺ χείρονες ἄνδρες ἀμύμονος ἀνδρὸς ἄκοιτιν — no deictic. ἀμύμονος (“blameless”) recast as “without blemish” to land a trochee last.

**21.361** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων·

- OLD: Thus would a man among those overbearing young men be saying:
- NEW: So would a man among those overbearing young men be speaking:
- *JOB 1.* ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων — iterative; standardized to the poem's fixed shape at 2.324 / 17.482 (“So would a man among those … be speaking:”). Removes a line-initial “Thus.”

**21.372** αἲ γὰρ πάντων τόσσον, ὅσοι κατὰ δώματʼ ἔασι,

- OLD: If only against all these men, as many as are in the house here,
- NEW: If only against all these men, as many as hold to our household,
- *JOB 2.* ὅσοι κατὰ δώματʼ ἔασι — κατὰ δώματα is “in the house”, already rendered; “here” was a second, redundant locative. “household” closes.

**21.375** ἡμετέρου ἐξ οἴκου, ἐπεὶ κακὰ μηχανόωνται.

- OLD: out of our house, since it is evil they scheme and devise here.”
- NEW: out of our house, since it is evil they scheme and imagine.”
- *JOB 2.* ἐπεὶ κακὰ μηχανόωνται — no deictic; μηχανάομαι is “contrive/devise”. “imagine” gives the feminine close without padding.

**21.396** ὧδε τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον·

- OLD: Thus a man would say, glancing over toward one who was near him:
- NEW: So would a man speak, glancing across at another beside him:
- *JOB 1.* ὧδε τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον — iterative, the “glancing at his neighbour” variant; standardized to the shape used at 18.72 and 18.400. Removes a line-initial “Thus.”

**21.401** ἄλλος δʼ αὖ εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων·

- OLD: Another in turn would say of the young men swollen with arrogance:
- NEW: So would another among those young men swollen with arrogance:
- *JOB 1.* ἄλλος δʼ αὖ εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων — the paired iterative; brought into the same fixed shape as 361, matching 2.331.

**21.404** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφαν μνηστῆρες· ἀτὰρ πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς,

- OLD: So said the suitors; but then resourceful Odysseus,
- NEW: So they spoke, these suitors; but then resourceful Odysseus,
- *JOB 1.* ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφαν μνηστῆρες — finite φάτο family, PLURAL subject; “So said the suitors” → the ruled plural opening “So they spoke,”.

**21.425** ἥμενος, οὐδέ τι τοῦ σκοποῦ ἤμβροτον οὐδέ τι τόξον

- OLD: I did not miss this mark, nor was I long in stringing the bow here.
- NEW: I did not miss this mark, nor was I long at the stringing.
- *JOB 2.* οὐδέ τι τοῦ σκοποῦ ἤμβροτον οὐδέ τι τόξον — no deictic. Reordered onto the verbal noun “stringing.”

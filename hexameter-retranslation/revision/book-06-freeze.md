## Job 1 — speech formulas

**06.66** ὣς ἔφατʼ· αἴδετο γὰρ θαλερὸν γάμον ἐξονομῆναι
- OLD: Thus did she speak, for she shrank from naming her marriage in fullness
- NEW: So she spoke, for she shrank from naming her marriage in fullness

**06.71** ὣς εἰπὼν δμώεσσιν ἐκέκλετο, τοὶ δʼ ἐπίθοντο.
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and he called to the slaves, who at once gave obedience.
- NEW: Thus having spoken, he called to the slaves, who at once gave obedience.
- The Greek is participial, not finite: the old line had it inside the ὣς φάτο
  family by mistake. Now matches 6.41 / 6.127 / 6.316.

**06.198** ἦ ῥα καὶ ἀμφιπόλοισιν ἐυπλοκάμοισι κέλευσε·
- OLD: Thus did she speak and call out to the beautiful-haired serving women:
- NEW: She spoke, and called out to the beautiful-haired serving women:
- ἦ ῥα καί, kept distinct from both fixed formulas; same treatment as 5.28.

**06.211** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, αἱ δʼ ἔσταν τε καὶ ἀλλήλῃσι κέλευσαν,
- OLD: Thus did she speak, and they halted and called to each other in answer,
- NEW: So she spoke, and they halted and called to each other in answer,

**06.223** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, αἱ δʼ ἀπάνευθεν ἴσαν, εἶπον δʼ ἄρα κούρῃ.
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and they moved off and told the young maiden his answer.
- NEW: So he spoke, and they moved off and told the young maiden his answer.

**06.247** ὣς ἔφαθʼ, αἱ δʼ ἄρα τῆς μάλα μὲν κλύον ἠδʼ ἐπίθοντο,
- OLD: Thus did she speak, and they listened to her and readily answered,
- NEW: So she spoke, and they listened to her and readily answered,
- 211 / 223 / 247 all render ὣς ἔφαθʼ, αἱ δʼ …; the opening now repeats verbatim
  exactly where the Greek does.

**06.328** ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, τοῦ δʼ ἔκλυε Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη.
- OLD: Thus did he speak in his prayer, and Pallas Athena had heard him.
- NEW: So he spoke in his prayer, and Pallas Athena had heard him.
- εὐχόμενος ("praying") is retained: the Greek marks this one, so the English may.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**06.153** εἰ δέ τίς ἐσσι βροτῶν, τοὶ ἐπὶ χθονὶ ναιετάουσιν
- OLD: But if you are one of the mortals who live on the earth here,
- NEW: But if you are one of the mortals who make the earth their dwelling,
- No deictic in the Greek; ναιετάουσιν is "dwell," and the verb now supplies the
  trochaic close instead of the filler.

**06.177** ἐς πρώτην ἱκόμην, τῶν δʼ ἄλλων οὔ τινα οἶδα
- OLD: after my terrible labors, and none of the others I know here,
- NEW: after my terrible labors, and none of the others I know of,
- No deictic; "know of" is also closer to οἶδα with the partitive τῶν ἄλλων.

**06.194** ἄστυ δέ τοι δείξω, ἐρέω δέ τοι οὔνομα λαῶν.
- OLD: also shall tell you the name by which all the people are called here.
- NEW: also shall tell you the name by which all of the people are called.
- No deictic. Licensed masculine ending: every feminine-close rewrite cost either
  a padded noun ("nation," "country") or an unidiomatic verb, and the naming is
  the point of the line. One masculine ending in the book, within budget.

**06.203** δηιοτῆτα φέρων· μάλα γὰρ φίλοι ἀθανάτοισιν
- OLD: very dear to the deathless gods are the people who live here.
- NEW: since to the deathless gods this people is greatly beloved.
- No deictic; "who live" was not in the Greek either. γάρ recovered as "since,"
  and the predicate adjective closes the verse trochaically.

**06.244** αἲ γὰρ ἐμοὶ τοιόσδε πόσις κεκλημένος εἴη
- OLD: If such a man might only be called my husband and live here,
- NEW: If it were only that such a man might be called my husband,
- The deictic belongs to 245 (ἐνθάδε ναιετάων), where the English already renders
  it as "remain in our country." 244's "here" was the doubled copy; removing it
  lets κεκλημένος πόσις end the verse, which is where the Greek puts it.

## Not changed, with warrant

**06.145** ὣς ἄρα οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι,
- KEPT: So, as he pondered, this seemed in his heart to be better and wiser:
- Not a speech formula and no "Thus" opening, so out of scope. Flagged: its
  verbatim Greek twin at 5.474 reads "Yet as he weighed it, this seemed in his
  mind to be greatly the better." See the editor's summary.

**06.235** ὣς ἄρα τῷ κατέχευε χάριν κεφαλῇ τε καὶ ὤμοις.
- KEPT: over his head and his shoulders. He moved off apart from the women,
- Correlative ὣς closing a simile, not a speech formula; the English already
  carries it across the 234/235 boundary without a "Thus."

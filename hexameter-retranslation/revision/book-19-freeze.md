## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**19.361** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη, γρηῢς δὲ κατέσχετο χερσὶ πρόσωπα
- OLD: So then she spoke, and the old woman covered her face with her two hands,
- NEW: So she spoke, and the old woman covered her face with both of her two hands,
- "So then" was a stray variant of the ruled opening; "both of" rebalances the
  interior feet against the two syllables lost at the head.

**19.386** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔφη, γρηῢς δὲ λέβηθʼ ἕλε παμφανόωντα
- OLD: So then he spoke, and the old woman took up a beautiful basin,
- NEW: So he spoke, and the old woman took up a beautiful shining basin,
- Same fix. παμφανόωντα ("all-shining") was in the Greek and unrendered in the
  old line; restoring it pays for the syllables the opening gave up, so this is
  recovered fidelity rather than padding.

**19.600** ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ἀνέβαινʼ ὑπερώϊα σιγαλόεντα
- OLD: So she spoke, and she went up to her silent upper chamber,
- NEW: So having spoken, she went up to her silent upper chamber,
- Participial ὣς εἰποῦσʼ must NOT read as the finite ὣς φάτο. Moved to the
  poem's dominant participial rendering ("So having spoken," 37 occurrences).

**19.300** ὣς ὁ μὲν οὕτως ἐστὶ σόος καὶ ἐλεύσεται ἤδη
- OLD: Thus he is safe, and indeed he will very soon be arriving,
- NEW: So then he is safe, and indeed he will very soon be arriving,
- Line-initial "Thus" not covered by the ruling. The Greek connective is ὣς...
  οὕτως resuming the report, so the opening is recast without a new "Thus" and
  without a line-initial "Then."

### Residual *Thus* kept

**19.151** ὣς τρίετες μὲν ἔληθον ἐγὼ καὶ ἔπειθον Ἀχαιούς
- KEPT: Thus for three years I escaped their notice and kept them persuaded;
- ὣς here genuinely means "in this way" — it summarizes the weaving trick just
  described. "Thus" earns its place.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

### Kept (Greek-warranted deictic)

**19.66** ξεῖνʼ, ἔτι καὶ νῦν ἐνθάδʼ ἀνιήσεις διὰ νύκτα
- KEPT: "Stranger, will you still even now annoy us all through the night here,
- ἐνθάδε is in the Greek.

**19.585** πρὶν γάρ τοι πολύμητις ἐλεύσεται ἐνθάδʼ Ὀδυσσεύς
- KEPT: for before they can do it, resourceful Odysseus will come here,
- ἐνθάδε is in the Greek.

### Reworked (unwarranted filler)

**19.304** ἱστίη τʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἀμύμονος, ἣν ἀφικάνω
- OLD: and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have now come here:
- NEW: and the hearth of the blameless Odysseus, to which I am coming:
- No deictic in the Greek. ἀφικάνω is present tense; "I am coming" is both
  closer and self-sufficient. ἀμύμων = "blameless" (the poem's usual word).

**19.344** γίγνεται· οὐδὲ γυνὴ ποδὸς ἅψεται ἡμετέροιο
- OLD: neither shall any woman lay hold on this foot that is mine here,
- NEW: neither shall any woman lay hold of a foot of my body,
- ἡμετέροιο is a plain possessive, not a deictic; "this ... here" was inventing
  a gesture the Greek does not make.

**19.348** τῇ δʼ οὐκ ἂν φθονέοιμι ποδῶν ἅψασθαι ἐμεῖο
- OLD: such a woman I would not begrudge if she handled my feet here.
- NEW: such a woman I would not begrudge to lay hold of my two feet.
- ἅψασθαι is the same verb as 344's ἅψεται; the English now echoes it too, and
  ποδῶν is dual/plural ("two feet"), which supplies the trochaic close.

**19.374** οὐκ ἐάας νίζειν· ἐμὲ δʼ οὐκ ἀέκουσαν ἄνωγε
- OLD: you did not let them wash you; but me, not unwilling at all here,
- NEW: you did not let them wash you; but me, and I am not unwilling,
- "at all here" was doubled filler; ἀέκουσαν alone carries it.

**19.488** εἴ χʼ ὑπʼ ἐμοί γε θεὸς δαμάσῃ μνηστῆρας ἀγαυούς
- OLD: if a god should subdue the proud suitors beneath my own hands here,
- NEW: if beneath my own hands a god should subdue the proud suitors,
- Pure reordering, no new words: ὑπʼ ἐμοί fronted (as in the Greek), and the
  plural "suitors" gives the falling close.

**19.496** εἴ χʼ ὑπό σοι γε θεὸς δαμάσῃ μνηστῆρας ἀγαυούς
- OLD: if a god should subdue the proud suitors beneath your own hands here,
- NEW: if beneath your own hands a god should subdue the proud suitors,
- The Greek repeats 488 verbatim but for ἐμοί/σοι; the English now repeats too.

**19.524** ὣς καὶ ἐμοὶ δίχα θυμὸς ὀρώρεται ἔνθα καὶ ἔνθα
- OLD: so also my own heart is divided and driven two ways here,
- NEW: so also my own heart is divided and driven both ways over,
- ἔνθα καὶ ἔνθα is already fully rendered by "two/both ways"; the trailing
  "here" was a second, empty translation of the same phrase.

**19.579** τῷ κεν ἅμʼ ἑσποίμην, νοσφισσαμένη τόδε δῶμα
- OLD: with him then I shall go, leaving behind this house here,
- NEW: with him then I shall go, and forsake this house I am leaving,
- τόδε is already carried by "this house"; νοσφίζομαι is stronger than "leave
  behind" — "forsake" restores it. Kept clear of 580's "wedded home."

**19.598** ἔνθα κε λεξαίμην· σὺ δὲ λέξεο τῷδʼ ἐνὶ οἴκῳ
- OLD: There I shall lie; and you must lie down somewhere in this house here,
- NEW: There I shall lie; and you must lie down somewhere in this dwelling,
- τῷδε is the deictic and is already spent on "this"; the "here" was the filler.

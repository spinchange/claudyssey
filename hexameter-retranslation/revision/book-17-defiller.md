# Book 17 de-filler — 43 filler-final lines reworked (42 changed, 1 warranted keeper)

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened to a feminine close).
2. No reworked line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek carries the
   sense at that position. One keeper: 17.10 ends "there" for ἐκεῖθι, which stands
   line-final in the Greek. 17.594 keeps mid-line "here" for ἐνθάδε.
3. The preferred fix throughout is reordering so a naturally trochaic word lands
   last (participles at 365/569, "onward"/"earthward" compounds at 64/237, plurals
   at 208/231/604, the name-close at 96, formula closes at 92/427).
4. Masculine endings used: 2 of the budgeted 3 — 17.295 (the quarry catalogue ends
   on its last item, as the Greek ends on λαγωούς) and 17.542 (Penelope's rare
   laugh takes the stress: γέλασσε δὲ Πηνελόπεια).
5. Formulas: 17.86 = 17.179 (χλαίνας μὲν κατέθεντο κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε) now
   render identically apart from the inherited first/then opener; 17.346 = 17.351
   (αἰτίζειν μάλα πάντας ἐποιχόμενον μνηστῆρας) now share the clause verbatim;
   17.427 now matches 14.258 (same Greek line) word for word; 17.92 now matches
   15.136 (same Greek formula); 17.215 joins the poem-wide "spoke … and addressed
   them" rendering of ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν; 17.550 keeps 17.557's
   "in cloak and tunic."
6. Voice rule: every reworked speech line is mortal speech (Telemachus, Odysseus,
   Eumaeus, Melanthius, Penelope, Menelaus quoted); the one contraction (17.517
   "he's") is Eumaeus.

No adjacent verse needed its own edit; joins were solved inside the target lines
(258 avoids doubling 259's "beside him"; 604 keeps the hall-word adjacent to 605's
"full of the banqueters").

**17.4** εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος, ὅ οἱ παλάμηφιν ἀρήρει,
- OLD: and he took up the stout spear, well fitted into his palm there,
- NEW: and he took up the stout spear, that into his palm was well fitted,

**17.9** πρίν γʼ αὐτόν με ἴδηται· ἀτὰρ σοί γʼ ὧδʼ ἐπιτέλλω.
- OLD: till she has seen me myself. But to you I give this charge now.
- NEW: till she has seen me myself. But to you I give this instruction.

**17.10** τὸν ξεῖνον δύστηνον ἄγʼ ἐς πόλιν, ὄφρʼ ἂν ἐκεῖθι
- OLD: Lead this unhappy stranger to town, so that once he is there
- NEW: (unchanged) Lead this unhappy stranger to town, so that once he is there
- ἐκεῖθι ("there") stands line-final in the Greek; the ending is warranted and kept.

**17.23** αὐτίκʼ ἐπεί κε πυρὸς θερέω ἀλέη τε γένηται.
- OLD: will lead me, once I have warmed by the fire and daylight has come there.
- NEW: will lead me, once I have warmed by the fire, when the day’s warmth has risen.
- ἀλέη is the sun's warmth; "day's warmth" recovers it ("daylight" was drift).

**17.30** αὐτὸς δʼ εἴσω ἴεν καὶ ὑπέρβη λάϊνον οὐδόν.
- OLD: and he himself went within and crossed the threshold of stone there.
- NEW: and he himself went inside and crossed over the stone threshold.

**17.32** κώεα καστορνῦσα θρόνοις ἔνι δαιδαλέοισι,
- OLD: as she was spreading soft fleeces upon the elaborate chairs there.
- NEW: as over the elaborate chairs she was spreading soft fleeces.

**17.39** κύσσε δέ μιν κεφαλήν τε καὶ ἄμφω φάεα καλά,
- OLD: and she kissed his head and both the bright beautiful eyes there,
- NEW: and she kissed his head and both of his eyes, bright and lovely,

**17.56** ἐνδυκέως φιλέειν καὶ τιέμεν, εἰς ὅ κεν ἔλθω.
- OLD: and to treat him with kindness and honor until I have come there.”
- NEW: and to treat him with kindness and honor until my own coming.”
- "own" is added (one syllable) to secure the sixth beat; it renders the emphatic
  first person of εἰς ὅ κεν ἔλθω — logged as the honest cost of this line.

**17.64** τὸν δʼ ἄρα πάντες λαοὶ ἐπερχόμενον θηεῦντο.
- OLD: and all of the people gazed upon him in wonder as he came there.
- NEW: and all of the people gazed on him in wonder as he came onward.
- ἐπερχόμενον = "coming on"; "onward" is the participle's own sense, not padding.

**17.72** ξεῖνον ἄγων ἀγορήνδε διὰ πτόλιν· οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν
- OLD: leading the guest through the town to the place of assembly; nor long then
- NEW: leading the guest through the town to the place of assembly; no longer
- οὐδʼ ἔτι δήν merged into "no longer"; the enjambment into 73 is unchanged.

**17.86** χλαίνας μὲν κατέθεντο κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε,
- OLD: first they laid down their cloaks on the benches and carved chairs there,
- NEW: first they laid down their cloaks upon the chairs and the benches,
- "carved" was an embellishment not in the Greek, used only here (17.179, same
  Greek, lacked it); cut so the identical Greek line renders identically.

**17.92** καλῇ χρυσείῃ, ὑπὲρ ἀργυρέοιο λέβητος,
- OLD: over a silver basin, from a beautiful vessel of gold there,
- NEW: fair and golden, holding it over a silver basin,
- Now verbatim with 15.136, where the same Greek formula already stands.

**17.95** εἴδατα πόλλʼ ἐπιθεῖσα, χαριζομένη παρεόντων.
- OLD: placing many good things beside it, giving freely from what was there.
- NEW: placing many good things beside it, sharing what lay ready.
- χαριζομένη παρεόντων "giving graciously of what was at hand" → "sharing what
  lay ready"; "sharing" carries the grace-note of χαρίζομαι.

**17.96** μήτηρ δʼ ἀντίον ἷζε παρὰ σταθμὸν μεγάροιο
- OLD: Over against them his mother sat down by the pillar of the hall there,
- NEW: Over against them, by the pillar of the hall, sat his mother,

**17.112** ἐλθόντα χρόνιον νέον ἄλλοθεν· ὣς ἐμὲ κεῖνος
- OLD: come after long absence from elsewhere back home in his youth there;
- NEW: newly come home in his youth from elsewhere, after long absence;
- ἄλλοθεν kept as "elsewhere"; χρόνιον closes the line as "after long absence."

**17.127** νεβροὺς κοιμήσασα νεηγενέας γαλαθηνοὺς
- OLD: lays down her tender young fawns, newborn and still needing milk there,
- NEW: lays down her tender young fawns, newborn and still milk-nursing,
- γαλαθηνούς "suckling" → the compound "milk-nursing."

**17.135** κὰδ δʼ ἔβαλε κρατερῶς, κεχάροντο δὲ πάντες Ἀχαιοί,
- OLD: and threw him down with great force, and all of the Achaeans rejoiced there,
- NEW: and threw him down with great force, while all the Achaeans were rejoicing,

**17.179** χλαίνας μὲν κατέθεντο κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε,
- OLD: then they laid down their cloaks on the benches and chairs in the hall there,
- NEW: then they laid down their cloaks upon the chairs and the benches,
- Same Greek line as 17.86; the English now matches apart from the inherited
  first/then opener. "in the hall" was padding, cut.

**17.200** τὼ βήτην, σταθμὸν δὲ κύνες καὶ βώτορες ἄνδρες
- OLD: So the two of them went; and the dogs and the herdsmen remained there
- NEW: So the two of them went, and the dogs with the herdsmen lingered,

**17.208** ἀμφὶ δʼ ἄρʼ αἰγείρων ὑδατοτρεφέων ἦν ἄλσος,
- OLD: and around it there stood a grove of water-fed poplar trees there,
- NEW: and all around it there stood a grove of the water-fed poplars,
- The mid-line "there stood" (ἦν) stays; the dangling second "there" goes and
  the line closes on the poplars themselves.

**17.215** τοὺς δὲ ἰδὼν νείκεσσεν ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν,
- OLD: Seeing the two, he reviled them and spoke and called each by name there,
- NEW: Seeing the two, he reviled them and spoke out a word and addressed them,
- Joins the poem's standard rendering of ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν ("spoke
  out a word and addressed her," 19.90, and the poem-wide "spoke and addressed").

**17.225** καί κεν ὀρὸν πίνων μεγάλην ἐπιγουνίδα θεῖτο.
- OLD: then perhaps by drinking whey he might put some flesh on his thigh there.
- NEW: then perhaps by drinking whey he would thicken his thigh with muscle.
- μεγάλην ἐπιγουνίδα θεῖτο "put on a great thigh-muscle": "thicken" carries
  μεγάλην; ἐπιγουνίς (the muscle above the knee) is restored — "flesh" was loose.

**17.231** πολλά οἱ ἀμφὶ κάρη σφέλα ἀνδρῶν ἐκ παλαμάων
- OLD: many footstools around his head from the hands of men in the hall there
- NEW: many footstools, hurled at his head from the hands of the suitors,
- "hurled" makes explicit what ἐκ παλαμάων + 232's ῥιπταζόμενος describe; ἀνδρῶν
  rendered contextually as "the suitors" — both flagged as interpretive.
  "in the hall" was padding, cut. 232's "will scrape all his ribs" still attaches.

**17.237** ἦ πρὸς γῆν ἐλάσειε κάρη ἀμφουδὶς ἀείρας.
- OLD: or lifting him high by the middle should smash his head on the earth there.
- NEW: or, lifting him high and clear of the ground, might dash his head earthward.
- ἀμφουδίς read with οὖδας: "clear of the ground"; πρὸς γῆν closes as "earthward."

**17.241** ὔμμʼ ἐπὶ μηρίʼ ἔκηε, καλύψας πίονι δημῷ,
- OLD: burned for you thigh-pieces, swathed over thickly with glistening fat there,
- NEW: burned for you thigh-pieces, swathed over thickly with glistening fatness,

**17.258** τῷ πάρα μὲν κρειῶν μοῖραν θέσαν οἳ πονέοντο,
- OLD: Those who were serving set down by his side his portion of meat there,
- NEW: A portion of meat was set at his side by those who were serving,
- Flipped passive so the line does not close on "beside him," which 259 already
  carries at its own close; all the Greek's content survives the flip.

**17.295** αἶγας ἐπʼ ἀγροτέρας ἠδὲ πρόκας ἠδὲ λαγωούς·
- OLD: used to lead him to hunt wild goats and deer and the hares there.
- NEW: used to take him to hunt the wild goats and the deer and the hares.
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the quarry catalogue ends on its last item, as
  the Greek line ends on λαγωούς; a trochaic close here would cost an added word.

**17.346** αἰτίζειν μάλα πάντας ἐποιχόμενον μνηστῆρας·
- OLD: he should go among all of the suitors and beg from each one there;
- NEW: he should go begging from man to man among all of the suitors;
- ἐποιχόμενον "going up to each in turn" → "from man to man"; formula with 17.351.

**17.351** αἰτίζειν μάλα πάντας ἐποιχόμενον μνηστῆρας·
- OLD: go among all of the suitors and beg from each one there;
- NEW: go begging from man to man among all of the suitors;
- Same Greek line as 17.346; the shared clause is now verbatim.

**17.365** βῆ δʼ ἴμεν αἰτήσων ἐνδέξια φῶτα ἕκαστον,
- OLD: Then he set out to beg, going rightward from man unto man there,
- NEW: Then he set out to beg of each of the men, going rightward,
- φῶτα ἕκαστον "each man" restored; ἐνδέξια closes the line as the participial
  "going rightward."

**17.368** ἀλλήλους τʼ εἴροντο τίς εἴη καὶ πόθεν ἔλθοι.
- OLD: asking each other who he might be and from where he had come there.
- NEW: asking among themselves who he was, and from where he was coming.

**17.411** οἱ δʼ ἄλλοι πάντες δίδοσαν, πλῆσαν δʼ ἄρα πήρην
- OLD: But all the rest gave too, and they filled up the beggar’s pouch there
- NEW: But all of the rest gave also, and they filled up the beggar’s wallet
- "wallet" for πήρη matches the poem's own use at 17.357 and 17.503.

**17.427** στῆσα δʼ ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ ποταμῷ νέας ἀμφιελίσσας.
- OLD: There in the stream of Egypt I halted my curving ships there.
- NEW: And there in the river of Egypt I moored my ships, double-curving.
- Verbatim with 14.258 (identical Greek line in the same Cretan tale), capitalized
  for the sentence start; the doubled "There … there" is gone.

**17.495** τὴν δʼ αὖτʼ Εὐρυνόμη ταμίη πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπεν·
- OLD: Then the housekeeper Eurynome answered and spoke in response there:
- NEW: Then in her turn the housekeeper Eurynome answered her, saying:
- αὖτε = "in her turn"; the close joins the poem's "answered her, saying:" formula.

**17.517** ἀλλʼ οὔ πω κακότητα διήνυσεν ἣν ἀγορεύων.
- OLD: Yet still he has not brought to an end all the woes he recounts there.
- NEW: Yet still he has not brought to an end the misery he’s telling.
- κακότητα is singular; "the misery" recovers it. The contraction is Eumaeus's
  (mortal speech).

**17.533** σῖτος καὶ μέθυ ἡδύ· τὰ μὲν οἰκῆες ἔδουσιν,
- OLD: grain and sweet wine; their own house-servants consume these things there,
- NEW: grain and sweet wine; and these things their household servants are eating,

**17.542** σμερδαλέον κονάβησε· γέλασσε δὲ Πηνελόπεια,
- OLD: terribly all the house rang out; and Penelope laughed then,
- NEW: terribly all the house rang out, and Penelope laughed;
- Licensed masculine ending #2: Penelope laughs almost nowhere in the poem; the
  stress lands on the laugh, as the Greek clause lands on γέλασσε.

**17.550** ἕσσω μιν χλαῖνάν τε χιτῶνά τε, εἵματα καλά.
- OLD: I will clothe him in cloak and tunic, fine garments to wear there.”
- NEW: I will clothe him in cloak and tunic, beautiful garments.”
- "to wear" was an inherited invention, cut; "in cloak and tunic" stays aligned
  with 17.557 (near-identical Greek half-line).

**17.569** τῷ νῦν Πηνελόπειαν ἐνὶ μεγάροισιν ἄνωχθι
- OLD: Therefore bid Penelope stay within the halls and wait there,
- NEW: Therefore bid Penelope now to stay within the halls, waiting,
- τῷ νῦν = "Therefore … now"; μεῖναι (570) closes the line as "waiting," which
  hands cleanly into 570's "though she is eager."

**17.589** ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς ἀγόρευεν, ὁ δʼ ᾤχετο δῖος ὑφορβὸς
- OLD: So she spoke thus; and the noble swineherd then went on his way there
- NEW: So she spoke thus, and away then went the noble swineherd,
- "then" stays mid-line as a narrative connective; the line closes on ὑφορβός.

**17.594** σὸν καὶ ἐμὸν βίοτον· σοὶ δʼ ἐνθάδε πάντα μελόντων.
- OLD: there, your livelihood and my own; let all here be your concern now.
- NEW: there, your livelihood and my own; let all of it here be your business.
- Mid-line "here" is ἐνθάδε, in the Greek at this position; only the unwarranted
  final "now" is gone. Sentence-final period kept (595 opens a new sentence).

**17.602** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ αὖτις ἄρʼ ἕζετʼ ἐϋξέστου ἐπὶ δίφρου,
- OLD: So he spoke, and the other sat down again on the polished seat there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and the other again on the well-polished stool was seated,
- ὁ δʼ "the other" kept (the subject switches from Telemachus to Eumaeus);
  δίφρου = "stool," ἐϋξέστου = "well-polished."

**17.604** βῆ ῥʼ ἴμεναι μεθʼ ὕας, λίπε δʼ ἕρκεά τε μέγαρόν τε,
- OLD: he went on his way to the swine, and left the enclosure and hall there,
- NEW: he went on his way to the swine, and left the enclosures and palace,
- ἕρκεα is plural ("enclosures"); μέγαρον as "palace" keeps the hall-word at the
  line's close so 605's "full of the banqueters" still attaches to it.

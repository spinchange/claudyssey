# Book 13 de-filler — all 33 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied (per the Book 21 pilot):

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No reworked line ends in *there*, *now*, *then*, or *here*; the one filler-class
   ending left in the book is 13.12 "bestowed here.", where ἐνθάδε is in the Greek.
3. The preferred fix is reordering so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, names, plurals, compounds), not adding new words.
4. No masculine endings were needed in this book (budget 3, used 0).
5. Formula care: "the light of the sun" kept at 33 to echo 35 (φάος ἠελίοιο twice);
   "finely woven" at 136 matches the book's own rendering of ὑφαντός at 218;
   "seaway" at 88 matches 85; "Naiads" at 350 matches 348.
6. Voice rule held: Poseidon (133, 147) and Athena (292, 362–5, 397–414) speak
   without lexical contractions in every reworked line.

Adjacent lines were checked at every join; none required edits.

**13.24** τοῖσι δὲ βοῦν ἱέρευσʼ ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο
- OLD: For them the holy strength of Alcinous slaughtered an ox there
- NEW: For them an ox was slaughtered by the holy strength of Alcinous,
- Passive turn keeps the formula "the holy strength of Alcinous" (as at 20, 49, 64)
  intact and closes on the name, as the Greek line does.

**13.33** ἀσπασίως δʼ ἄρα τῷ κατέδυ φάος ἠελίοιο
- OLD: welcome indeed to him is the light of the sun when it sets there,
- NEW: welcome indeed to him is the light of the sun in its sinking,
- "in its sinking" renders κατέδυ (went down) as its own gerund; "the light of the
  sun" is kept intact so 33 still echoes 35 (φάος ἠελίοιο in both).

**13.42** ὄλβια ποιήσειαν· ἀμύμονα δʼ οἴκοι ἄκοιτιν
- OLD: make them bring blessing; and may I find my blameless wife in my home there,
- NEW: make them bring blessing; and may I find at home my blameless consort,
- "consort" for ἄκοιτιν is the book's own word at 334 ("children and consort").

**13.44** ὑμεῖς δʼ αὖθι μένοντες ἐϋφραίνοιτε γυναῖκας
- OLD: And may you, staying here, give joy to your wedded wives there
- NEW: And may you, staying here, give joy to the wives you have wedded
- αὖθι already lives in "staying here"; κουριδίας (45) unfolded as "you have
  wedded" so γυναῖκας keeps its own word "wives" and the close is a participle.

**13.50** Ποντόνοε, κρητῆρα κερασσάμενος μέθυ νεῖμον
- OLD: “Pontonous, mix in the bowl and serve out the wine through the hall now
- NEW: “Pontonous, mix in the bowl, and serve out the wine through the palace
- "through the palace" = ἀνὰ μέγαρον (51).

**13.56** αὐτόθεν ἐξ ἑδρέων. ἀνὰ δʼ ἵστατο δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς,
- OLD: there from their seats. But lordly Odysseus arose then,
- NEW: there from their seats. And upward rose lordly Odysseus,
- Line-initial "there" stays: αὐτόθεν is in the Greek. "upward" renders the
  ἀνά of ἀνίστατο; the line now closes on the name, as the Greek does.

**13.62** παισί τε καὶ λαοῖσι καὶ Ἀλκινόῳ βασιλῆϊ.
- OLD: in your children and people, and in Alcinous the king there.”
- NEW: in your children and people, and in Alcinous the ruler.”

**13.65** ἡγεῖσθαι ἐπὶ νῆα θοὴν καὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης·
- OLD: to guide him on to the swift ship and down to the shore of the sea there.
- NEW: to guide him along to the swift ship and down to the seashore.
- θῖνα θαλάσσης compressed to the compound "seashore" (the poem's compound idiom).

**13.69** ἡ δʼ ἄλλη σῖτόν τʼ ἔφερεν καὶ οἶνον ἐρυθρόν.
- OLD: and the third one carried provisions, both bread and ruddy wine there.
- NEW: and the third one carried provisions, both bread and wine that was ruddy.
- Adjective expanded to a relative for the close (as the pilot did at 21.286).

**13.72** δεξάμενοι κατέθεντο, πόσιν καὶ βρῶσιν ἅπασαν·
- OLD: and set them away in the hollow ship, all the food and the drink there.
- NEW: and set them away in the hold, the food and the drink altogether.
- The Greek line carries no ship-word (ἐν νηῒ γλαφυρῇ sits in 71, whose English
  already says "in the vessel" — the drafter's "hollow vessel" would have doubled
  it in consecutive lines). "in the hold" concretizes the κατά of κατέθεντο;
  "altogether" renders ἅπασαν.

**13.77** κόσμῳ, πεῖσμα δʼ ἔλυσαν ἀπὸ τρητοῖο λίθοιο.
- OLD: and loosed the cable away from the pierced anchoring stone there.
- NEW: and away from the pierced anchoring stone they loosed the cable.
- Pure reorder: πεῖσμα stays a singular "cable" and nothing is added.

**13.86** ἡ δὲ μάλʼ ἀσφαλέως θέεν ἔμπεδον· οὐδέ κεν ἴρηξ
- OLD: Yet she ran very steadily, firm and secure; not even a hawk there,
- NEW: Yet she ran very steadily, firm and secure; not even a harrier,
- ἴρηξ κίρκος (86–87) is one bird in double name; "harrier" (the Circus hawk) and
  87's "falcon" split the pair as the old text already did with "hawk"/"falcon".

**13.88** ὣς ἡ ῥίμφα θέουσα θαλάσσης κύματʼ ἔταμνεν,
- OLD: So she, running swiftly, kept cleaving the swells of the sea there,
- NEW: So she, running swiftly, kept cleaving the swells of the seaway,
- "seaway" as at 85, the same waterscape.

**13.99** αἵ τʼ ἀνέμων σκεπόωσι δυσαήων μέγα κῦμα
- OLD: these keep outside the great wave down, which the stormy winds raise there,
- NEW: these keep off the great wave that the ill-blowing winds are raising,
- δυσαήων finally gets its literal "ill-blowing"; ἀνέμων keeps its plain "winds".

**13.133** πάγχυ, ἐπεὶ σὺ πρῶτον ὑπέσχεο καὶ κατένευσας.
- OLD: since you first had promised and nodded your head in assent there.
- NEW: since you first had promised and nodded your head in assenting.
- Poseidon speaking: contraction-free, as before.

**13.136** χαλκόν τε χρυσόν τε ἅλις ἐσθῆτά θʼ ὑφαντήν,
- OLD: bronze and gold in abundance, and woven garments as well there,
- NEW: bronze and gold in abundance, and garments finely woven,
- "finely woven" is the book's own rendering of ὑφαντός for these same gifts
  at 218 ("the finely woven fair garments").

**13.147** αἶψά κʼ ἐγὼν ἔρξαιμι, κελαινεφές, ὡς ἀγορεύεις·
- OLD: “I would act on it straightway, dark-cloud lord, exactly as you say there;
- NEW: “I would act on it straightway, dark-cloud lord, just as you have spoken;
- Poseidon speaking: contraction-free.

**13.214** ἀνθρώπους ἐφορᾷ καὶ τίνυται ὅς τις ἁμάρτῃ.
- OLD: and exacts retribution from any man whenever he sins there.
- NEW: and exacts retribution from any man who is sinning.

**13.250** ὣς φάτο, γήθησεν δὲ πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς,
- OLD: So she spoke, and the much-enduring brilliant Odysseus rejoiced then,
- NEW: So she spoke, and rejoiced much-enduring, brilliant Odysseus,
- Verb–subject inversion mirrors the Greek order (γήθησεν δὲ ... Ὀδυσσεύς)
  and lands the name at the close.

**13.258** χρήμασι σὺν τοίσδεσσι· λιπὼν δʼ ἔτι παισὶ τοσαῦτα
- OLD: bringing these treasures with me, and I left as many behind there
- NEW: bringing these treasures with me, and leaving as many behind me
- λιπών kept participial, per the preferred fix; "for my children" (παισί)
  follows in 259 as before.

**13.279** σπουδῇ δʼ ἐς λιμένα προερέσσαμεν, οὐδέ τις ἡμῖν
- OLD: Eagerly into the harbor we rowed, and none among us then
- NEW: Eagerly into the harbor we rowed, and none among us
- "then" simply dropped; "among us" closes unstressed on its own.

**13.281** ἀλλʼ αὔτως ἀποβάντες ἐκείμεθα νηὸς ἅπαντες.
- OLD: but simply disembarked from the ship and all of us lay there.
- NEW: but just as we were, we stepped from the ship, and all of us settled.
- αὔτως = "just as we were"; "stepped" for ἀποβάντες is the book's own
  disembarking verb at 116; ἅπαντες restored as "all of us"; "settled"
  renders ἐκείμεθα (lay down to rest).

**13.292** ἐν πάντεσσι δόλοισι, καὶ εἰ θεὸς ἀντιάσειε.
- OLD: passed you in all your deceits, even if some god met you face to face there.
- NEW: passed you in all of your deceits, even should a god come to meet you.
- "face to face" cut: ἀντιάσειε is simply "should meet." Athena speaking:
  contraction-free.

**13.350** ἔρδεσκες νύμφῃσι τεληέσσας ἑκατόμβας·
- OLD: you used to perform for the nymphs, full-finished hecatombs there;
- NEW: you used to perform for the Naiads, hecatombs full-finished;
- The old word order (with the apposition to 349's "many and many a great gift")
  is kept; only the tail is reordered so the participle closes. "Naiads" names
  the same nymphs as at 348.

**13.354** χαίρων ᾗ γαίῃ, κύσε δὲ ζείδωρον ἄρουραν.
- OLD: rejoicing in his own land, and he kissed the grain-giving earth there.
- NEW: rejoicing in his own land, and he kissed the grain-giving cropland.
- "cropland" for ἄρουραν (plow-land) keeps the γαίη/ἄρουρα distinction.

**13.364** θείμεν αὐτίκα νῦν, ἵνα περ τάδε τοι σόα μίμνῃ·
- OLD: cave put away these possessions, so all may remain safe for you there;
- NEW: cave put away these possessions, so they all remain safe for you;
- τοι is a dative of interest: "safe for you" kept exactly.

**13.368** ἆσσον πάντʼ ἐφόρει, χρυσὸν καὶ ἀτειρέα χαλκὸν
- OLD: carried all inward close by, the gold and the tireless bronze there,
- NEW: carried all close within, the gold and the bronze unwearying,
- ἀτειρέα postposed as "unwearying" — the adjective alone, nothing bolted on.

**13.372** τὼ δὲ καθεζομένω ἱερῆς παρὰ πυθμένʼ ἐλαίης
- OLD: Then the two of them sat by the foot of the sacred olive tree there
- NEW: Then the two of them sat by the foot of the sacred olive
- "olive" alone matches the poem's own usage at 102; no punctuation added,
  as the sentence runs on into 373.

**13.380** πάντας μέν ῥʼ ἔλπει καὶ ὑπίσχεται ἀνδρὶ ἑκάστῳ,
- OLD: gives hope to every man and promises each one in turn there,
- NEW: gives hope to every man, and to each one keeps making promises,
- The iterative "keeps making" carries Penelope's stringing-along; plural
  "promises" supplies the close.

**13.403** σῇ τʼ ἀλόχῳ καὶ παιδί, τὸν ἐν μεγάροισιν ἔλειπες.
- OLD: and to your wife and the son whom you left behind in your hall there.
- NEW: and to your wife and the son whom you left in your halls behind you.
- "halls" now plural, matching μεγάροισιν exactly.

**13.404** αὐτὸς δὲ πρώτιστα συβώτην εἰσαφικέσθαι,
- OLD: You yourself first of all must go to the keeper of swine there,
- NEW: You yourself first of all must make your way to the swineherd,
- "make your way to" = εἰσαφικέσθαι (betake yourself to); "swineherd"
  (the compound συβώτης) lands the trochaic close.

**13.436** ἀμφὶ δέ μιν μέγα δέρμα ταχείης ἕσσʼ ἐλάφοιο,
- OLD: Over him too she put the great hide of a swift-running deer there,
- NEW: Over him too she put the great hide of a deer that was swift-running,
- Adjective expanded to a relative for the close (as at 69).

**13.440** ἐς Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν ἔβη μετὰ παῖδʼ Ὀδυσῆος.
- OLD: then she went to glorious Lacedaemon after his son there.
- NEW: then she went to glorious Lacedaemon after the son of Odysseus.
- παῖδʼ Ὀδυσῆος is literally "the son of Odysseus": the book's final line
  now closes on the hero's name, as the Greek does.

## Tally

- 33 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then/here*.
- The book's one remaining filler-class ending is 13.12 "bestowed here.",
  warranted by ἐνθάδʼ in the Greek (outside this batch, left untouched).
- 0 masculine endings used (budget 3).
- Formula care: 33 keeps "the light of the sun" against 35 (φάος ἠελίοιο twice);
  88 reuses 85's "seaway"; 136 matches 218's "finely woven"; 350 matches 348's
  "Naiads"; 24 keeps "the holy strength of Alcinous" whole (as at 20, 49, 64).
- Fidelity recovered along the way: δυσαήων ("ill-blowing") at 99, αὔτως and
  ἅπαντες at 281, plural μεγάροισιν at 403, singular πεῖσμα kept at 77,
  ἄρουρα distinguished from γαίη at 354; padding cut at 292 ("face to face")
  and 72 (the imported ship-word the Greek line does not carry).
- Honest costs incurred: "hold" concretized at 72 (from κατά + context);
  "finely" at 136 (endorsed by 218); adjective-to-relative expansions at 69
  and 436; "harrier" at 86 sharpens ἴρηξ by one species. Total added-word
  residue across 33 lines is far below what the old lines carried in filler.
- For a later pass, not this one: 250 "much-enduring brilliant Odysseus" vs
  353 "great-enduring noble Odysseus" — the Greek repeats πολύτλας δῖος
  Ὀδυσσεύς, the English epithets still vary (both inherited).

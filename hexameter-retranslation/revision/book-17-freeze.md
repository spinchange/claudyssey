Verses changed: **12** (8 by the speech-formula ruling, 4 by the here-final sweep).
Scansion lines updated in `book-17-performance.md`: **12**.
Verse count unchanged against the Greek (606 lines).

Formula inventory for this book, after the pass:

- Finite ὣς φάτο family (26, 147, 150, 177, 233, 348, 374, 409, 458, 462, 481, 488, 541, 551, 574, 602) — all now open "So he spoke," / "So she spoke," / "So spoke <named subject>," per the ruling. Only 551 needed changing; the rest were already conformant.
- Participial ὣς εἰπών (84, 254, 324) — standardized to **"So having spoken,"**, the poem's dominant participial rendering (35 occurrences against 3 of "So saying"). All three had drifted into the finite "So he spoke," shape, flattening a distinct Greek formula; restored.
- ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν (57) — finite, so the ruled formula applies; the line is now verbatim identical to the pilot's 21.386, which renders the same Greek verse.
- Iterative ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε (482) — given the "So would a man … be speaking:" shape, matching 2.324 / 2.331.
- ἦ ῥα (197, 356, 396) — untouched; none opens with "Thus".

No line-initial *Thus* remains. **residual_thus for this book: 0.**

Here-final: 4 unwarranted, all reworked. **17.10 keeps its final "there"** — ἐκεῖθι is a genuine locative in the Greek and earns the position (it is a *there*, not a *here*, and is listed for the record rather than as a here-warrant).

## Job 1 — speech formulas

**17.57** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν, τῇ δʼ ἄπτερος ἔπλετο μῦθος.
- OLD: So then he spoke, and for her his word became wingless and silent.
- NEW: So he spoke, and for her the word he had uttered was wingless.
- "So then" collapsed to the ruled opening; "and silent" was padding for the lost syllables. Now verbatim with 21.386, the same Greek line.

**17.84** ὣς εἰπὼν ξεῖνον ταλαπείριον ἦγεν ἐς οἶκον.
- OLD: So he spoke, and led the much-enduring stranger on to the household.
- NEW: So having spoken, he led the much-enduring stranger homeward.
- Participial formula restored. ἐς οἶκον rendered "homeward" (the poem's own -ward idiom) rather than the flabbier "on to the household".

**17.166** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: Thus they were speaking such things in turn to one another.
- NEW: So the two of them spoke such things to each other together,
- Not the φάτο family: line-initial "Thus" recast. The Greek verse is identical to 17.290, which already read this way; the two now match, as the Greek does. οἱ μέν is the dual pair Odysseus and Penelope (via Eumaeus), so "the two of them" is also the more faithful subject.

**17.254** ὣς εἰπὼν τοὺς μὲν λίπεν αὐτοῦ ἦκα κιόντας,
- OLD: So he spoke, and he left them there as they slowly kept coming;
- NEW: So having spoken, he left them there as they slowly kept coming;
- Participial formula restored; "there" renders αὐτοῦ and is not line-final.

**17.324** ὣς εἰπὼν εἰσῆλθε δόμους εὖ ναιετάοντας,
- OLD: So he spoke, and entered the house where people were dwelling in comfort,
- NEW: So having spoken, he entered the house men dwelt in with comfort,
- Participial formula restored; the relative clause tightened to absorb the added syllables without padding.

**17.482** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων·
- OLD: Thus would a man among those overweening young suitors say:
- NEW: So would a man among those overweening young suitors be speaking:
- Iterative formula: distinct from ὣς φάτο and kept distinct, in the "So would a man … be speaking:" shape of 2.324 / 2.331. The old masculine close on "say:" also goes.

**17.505** ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς ἀγόρευε μετὰ δμῳῇσι γυναιξίν,
- OLD: Thus she spoke among all her serving women there in the chamber,
- NEW: She in this way was speaking among her serving-women,
- Not the φάτο family (ἀγόρευε, imperfect, with ὣς mid-line): line-initial "Thus" recast, and ὣς given its true "in this way" sense in its actual position. "there in the chamber" was padding — 506 already supplies ἐν θαλάμῳ ("seated within").

**17.551** ὣς φάτο, βῆ δὲ συφορβός, ἐπεὶ τὸν μῦθον ἄκουσεν·
- OLD: Thus she spoke, and the swineherd departed when he heard her commandment,
- NEW: So she spoke, and the swineherd departed when he heard her commandment,
- Straight ὣς φάτο; subject is Penelope. Now matches its sisters 17.348 and 17.574, which render the same Greek verse.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**17.105** πρὶν ἐλθεῖν μνηστῆρας ἀγήνορας ἐς τόδε δῶμα,
- OLD: before the overbearing suitors came back into this house here,
- NEW: before the overbearing suitors came into this dwelling of ours,
- No deictic to warrant it: τόδε is already carried by "this". "back" was a second filler (the Greek has only ἐλθεῖν); both gone.

**17.268** δικλίδες· οὐκ ἄν τίς μιν ἀνὴρ ὑπεροπλίσσαιτο.
- OLD: no man alive could lightly break through and plunder the place here.
- NEW: no man alive could lightly break through and plunder the household.
- Nothing locative in the Greek. "the household" gives the trochaic close on its own.

**17.460** νῦν δή σʼ οὐκέτι καλὰ διὲκ μεγάροιό γʼ ὀΐω
- OLD: “Now I no longer suppose you will go from the hall here
- NEW: “Now I no longer suppose you will go from this hall of ours
- διὲκ μεγάροιο is motion through the hall, not a deictic. γε's emphasis is picked up by "of ours" instead of by filler.

**17.501** ξεῖνός τις δύστηνος ἀλητεύει κατὰ δῶμα
- OLD: Some poor unhappy stranger goes wandering through the great house here,
- NEW: Some poor unhappy stranger goes wandering through the great palace,
- κατὰ δῶμα is "through the house", with no deictic. "palace" supplies the feminine ending without adding a word.

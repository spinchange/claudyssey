# Book 24 de-filler — all 29 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied (as in the Book 21 pilot):

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then*; no Greek warrant was needed in this
   book — none of the 29 lines carries αὐτόθι/ἔνθα/τότε/ἔπειτα at the close.
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, names, plurals, compounds), not adding new words.
4. Masculine endings used twice, within the 2–3/book budget: 24.31 (θάνατον καὶ
   πότμον both demand stressed nouns; "doom" closes the death-wish), 24.155
   (πρόσθʼ ἡγεμόνευε; "leading the way" resisted every feminine reorder without
   loss).
5. Formula kept: πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς stays "godlike Odysseus, long-suffering"
   at 504, matching 490 and 537 (a junior draft had broken it to "noble").
6. Voice rule checked: Athena's imperative at 531 and her line-intros use no
   lexical contractions; mortal speeches (Amphimedon, Odysseus, Laertes, Medon)
   unchanged in register.

**24.13** ἤϊσαν· αἶψα δʼ ἵκοντο κατʼ ἀσφοδελὸν λειμῶνα,
- OLD: then in haste they arrived at the meadow of pale asphodel there,
- NEW: then in haste they arrived at the pale asphodel meadow,
- Reordered to the adjectival "asphodel meadow" (κατʼ ἀσφοδελὸν λειμῶνα is
  adjective + noun in the Greek), landing "meadow" last; bare "asphodel" as a
  close left the sixth beat on a secondary stress.

**24.31** δήμῳ ἔνι Τρώων θάνατον καὶ πότμον ἐπισπεῖν·
- OLD: there in the land of the Trojans you had met death and your doom then;
- NEW: there in the land of the Trojans you had met with your death and your doom;
- Licensed masculine ending #1. The line-initial "there" renders δήμῳ ἔνι, not
  the cut filler.

**24.70** πεζοί θʼ ἱππῆές τε· πολὺς δʼ ὀρυμαγδὸς ὀρώρει
- OLD: footmen and horsemen alike; and a great uproar arose there.
- NEW: footmen and horsemen alike; and there arose a great uproar.
- Expletive "there arose" (structural English, no Greek locative behind it);
  the line now closes on "uproar."

**24.91** οἷʼ ἐπὶ σοὶ κατέθηκε θεὰ περικαλλέʼ ἄεθλα,
- OLD: what surpassingly splendid prizes the goddess set out there
- NEW: what prizes surpassingly splendid were set out by the goddess
- Passive recast; ἐπὶ σοὶ is already carried by 92's "in your honor,
  silver-footed Thetis," which stays appositive to "the goddess."

**24.112** βοῦς περιταμνομένους ἠδʼ οἰῶν πώεα καλά,
- OLD: while you were driving off cattle, or beautiful flocks of sheep there,
- NEW: while you were driving off beautiful flocks of sheep, or of cattle,
- Simple reorder of the two objects; καλά stays with the sheep-flocks (πώεα
  καλά), as in the Greek.

**24.155** ὕστερος, αὐτὰρ Τηλέμαχος πρόσθʼ ἡγεμόνευε.
- OLD: while Telemachus went first before him leading the way there.
- NEW: while Telemachus went first before him, leading the way.
- Licensed masculine ending #2.

**24.159** οὐδέ τις ἡμείων δύνατο γνῶναι τὸν ἐόντα
- OLD: None of us could discern him for the man that he truly was there,
- NEW: None of us could discern him for the man that he was truly,
- Postposed "truly" — poetic hyperbaton, but the six beats hold (NONE, COULD,
  -CERN, MAN, WAS, TRU-).

**24.166** ἐς θάλαμον κατέθηκε καὶ ἐκλήϊσεν ὀχῆας,
- OLD: carried it into the chamber, and fastened the doors with the bolts there.
- NEW: carried it into the chamber, and fastened the bolts on the doorway.

**24.167** αὐτὰρ ὁ ἣν ἄλοχον πολυκερδείῃσιν ἄνωγε
- OLD: Then by the craft in his heart he instructed his own dear wife there
- NEW: Then by the craft in his heart he gave his wife his instructions,
- ἄνωγε as the noun "instructions"; "own dear" (not in ἣν ἄλοχον) dropped —
  a fidelity gain. 168's infinitive "to set…" follows the noun cleanly.

**24.172** ἀλλʼ ὅτε χεῖρας ἵκανεν Ὀδυσσῆος μέγα τόξον,
- OLD: But when at last to the hands of Odysseus the great bow had come there,
- NEW: But when at last the great bow had come to the hands of Odysseus,

**24.184** κτεῖνον ἐπιστροφάδην, τῶν δὲ στόνος ὤρνυτʼ ἀεικὴς
- OLD: killing to right and to left, and hideous groaning arose there
- NEW: killing to right and to left, while hideous groaning was rising
- ὤρνυτο is imperfect; "was rising" is the more literal aspect. No comma at the
  close — 185's "as heads were battered" continues the clause, as before.

**24.209** ἐν τῷ σιτέσκοντο καὶ ἵζανον ἠδὲ ἴαυον
- OLD: where the serving men ate and sat and also took sleep there,
- NEW: where the serving men ate their meals and sat and took slumber,
- "their meals" makes σιτέσκοντο's object explicit (logged addition).

**24.210** δμῶες ἀναγκαῖοι, τοί οἱ φίλα ἐργάζοντο.
- OLD: thralls of necessity, who worked at the things that he loved there.
- NEW: thralls of necessity, men who worked at the tasks that pleased him.
- "men" is in δμῶες and supplies the sixth beat. Full stop kept: 211 opens a
  new sentence ("In the house there was also…").

**24.215** δεῖπνον δʼ αἶψα συῶν ἱερεύσατε ὅς τις ἄριστος·
- OLD: and for our supper at once sacrifice the best of the swine there.
- NEW: and quickly, for our supper, sacrifice whichever swine is finest.
- ὅς τις ἄριστος moved to the close; full stop kept (216 begins "I for my
  part…").

**24.221** ἆσσον ἴεν πολυκάρπου ἀλωῆς πειρητίζων.
- OLD: moved more near to the orchard rich in fruit, with testing in mind there.
- NEW: moved more near to the orchard rich in fruit, still testing,
- πειρητίζων closes the line as the participle itself; "still" is a small
  logged connective. The verb-first opening is kept because 220 ends with the
  subject ("…but noble Odysseus").

**24.236** κύσσαι καὶ περιφῦναι ἑὸν πατέρʼ, ἠδὲ ἕκαστα
- OLD: whether to kiss his father and clasp him close in his arms there,
- NEW: whether to kiss his father and throw his arms around him,
- περιφῦναι as "throw his arms around him"; the weak pronoun closes unstressed.

**24.316** ἀμφοτέρῃσι δὲ χερσὶν ἑλὼν κόνιν αἰθαλόεσσαν
- OLD: Then with both of his hands he took up the dark sooty dust there
- NEW: Then he took up in both of his hands the dust, dark and sooty,

**24.347** ἀμφὶ δὲ παιδὶ φίλῳ βάλε πήχεε· τὸν δὲ ποτὶ οἷ
- OLD: Round his beloved son he flung both arms in embrace there;
- NEW: Round his own dear son he flung both arms, embracing;
- Semicolon kept before 348's change of subject ("and great-enduring noble
  Odysseus caught him…").

**24.371** ὡς ἴδεν ἀθανάτοισι θεοῖς ἐναλίγκιον ἄντην·
- OLD: face to face how like to the immortal gods he had become there;
- NEW: face to face how like he had grown to the gods, the immortals;
- ἀθανάτοισι θεοῖς kept as noun + apposition ("the gods, the immortals") —
  a draft's "deathless immortals" was tautological.

**24.382** πολλῶν ἐν μεγάροισι, σὺ δὲ φρένας ἔνδον ἐγήθεις.
- OLD: loosened the knees in the house, and your own heart would have rejoiced there.”
- NEW: loosened the knees in the house, and your own heart would have been joyful.”
- The past counterfactual is kept ("would have been"), continuing 379–381's
  "such a man I had been… I would have / loosened…"; ἐγήθεις rendered as the
  adjective for the feminine close.

**24.385** ἑξείης ἕζοντο κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε·
- OLD: then they sat down in order on benches and chairs in a row there.
- NEW: then in order upon the benches and chairs they settled.
- The old line tripled ἑξείης ("in order" + "in a row" + "there"); it is now
  translated once. Full stop kept (386 begins "And just as…").

**24.408** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ αὖτις ἄρʼ ἕζετʼ ἐϋξέστου ἐπὶ δίφρου.
- OLD: So he spoke, and again he sat on the well-polished stool there.
- NEW: So he spoke, and again on the well-polished stool he was seated.

**24.449** θῦνε κατὰ μέγαρον· τοὶ δʼ ἀγχιστῖνοι ἔπιπτον.
- OLD: rushing among them; and close-packed they tumbled and fell there.”
- NEW: rushing among them; and close on each other they kept falling.”
- ἀγχιστῖνοι ἔπιπτον = "they fell one close upon another" (imperfect); "close
  on each other… kept falling" carries both words without padding.

**24.467** αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῥʼ ἕσσαντο περὶ χροῒ νώροπα χαλκόν,
- OLD: But when they had put upon their flesh the glittering bronze there,
- NEW: But when they had put on their bodies the bronze, brightly gleaming,
- νώροπα postposed as the closing participle; "brightly" is a small logged
  addition carrying the flash in νώροπα.

**24.502** τοῖσι δʼ ἐπʼ ἀγχίμολον θυγάτηρ Διὸς ἦλθεν Ἀθήνη
- OLD: Then there came close by them Athena, the daughter of Zeus, there,
- NEW: Then close beside them came the daughter of Zeus, Athena,
- The old line had "there" twice; both gone, and the line closes on the name,
  in the Greek's own order (θυγάτηρ Διὸς… Ἀθήνη).

**24.504** τὴν μὲν ἰδὼν γήθησε πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς·
- OLD: Seeing her, then godlike Odysseus, long-suffering, rejoiced there,
- NEW: Seeing her, then rejoiced godlike Odysseus, long-suffering,
- Verb-subject inversion keeps the book's formula "godlike Odysseus,
  long-suffering" verbatim (= 490, 537) and closes feminine on "-suffering."

**24.531** ἴσχεσθε πτολέμου, Ἰθακήσιοι, ἀργαλέοιο,
- OLD: “Hold from the painful war, men of Ithaca, hold now,
- NEW: “Hold yourselves back from the painful war, you men of Ithaca,
- ἴσχεσθε appears once in the Greek; the doubled "hold… hold now" is gone, and
  the vocative Ἰθακήσιοι closes the line. Athena speaking: no contractions.

**24.533** ὣς φάτʼ Ἀθηναίη, τοὺς δὲ χλωρὸν δέος εἷλεν·
- OLD: So spoke Athena, and upon them all pale fear seized hold then;
- NEW: So spoke Athena; and pale fear seized hold upon all of them;
- Semicolon kept at the close — 534 continues the sentence ("from their
  hands…"). "pale fear" matches 450's rendering of χλωρὸν δέος.

**24.545** ὣς φάτʼ Ἀθηναίη, ὁ δʼ ἐπείθετο, χαῖρε δὲ θυμῷ.
- OLD: So spoke Athena, and he obeyed, and his heart was made glad there.
- NEW: So spoke Athena, and he obeyed her, his heart rejoicing.
- χαῖρε δὲ θυμῷ as the absolute "his heart rejoicing" — active, as the Greek
  is, replacing the passive "was made glad."

## Tally

- 29 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*,
  and none needed a Greek warrant to keep one.
- 2 masculine endings licensed (24.31, 24.155), within the 2–3/book budget.
- Formula preserved at 504 ("godlike Odysseus, long-suffering" = 490, 537);
  χλωρὸν δέος stays "pale fear" (450, 533).
- Fidelity recovered along the way: ἑξείης translated once at 385; "own dear"
  cut at 167 (not in ἣν ἄλοχον); imperfects restored at 184 (ὤρνυτο) and 449
  (ἔπιπτον); the doubled imperative cut at 531; the double "there" cut at 502.
- Honest costs incurred (logged): "their meals" at 209, "men" at 210, "still"
  at 221, "brightly" at 467.
- For a second pass: English 500 ("…clothed all their flesh in the gleaming
  bronze armor") may sit on the same Greek formula as 467 (νώροπα χαλκόν);
  if so, the pair could be made verbatim. Also 24.32's "Panchaeans" looks like
  a slip for "Panachaeans" — outside this pass's scope.

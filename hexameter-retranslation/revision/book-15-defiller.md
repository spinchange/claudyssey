# Book 15 de-filler — all 39 filler-final lines resolved

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot: every replacement is a six-beat falling line;
no line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek carries the sense (no such
case survives in this book — 15.455's αὖθι and 15.391/15.520's νῦν are kept mid-line,
in their true positions); the preferred fix is reordering so a naturally trochaic word
lands last; masculine endings budgeted at 3, used once (15.120); formulas kept where
the book repeats them (βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος → "Menelaus, good at the war-cry" verbatim
with 15.14, 15.57, 15.67; αἴθοπα οἶνον stays "flaming wine" with 14.447 and 16.14 —
the draft's silent switch to "fire-bright" was reverted, as in the Book 3 pass).
One adjacent verse (15.262) was touched, shown with its own entry, to restore the
Greek's αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα to 15.261.

---

**15.11**  κτήματά τε προλιπὼν ἄνδρας τʼ ἐν σοῖσι δόμοισιν
- OLD: leaving your treasures behind and men in your halls there,
- NEW: leaving behind you your treasures, and in your own halls the men-folk,
- The draft ended on "treasures," severing 15.12's appositive "men so arrogant"
  (Greek runs ἄνδρας ... / οὕτω ὑπερφιάλους across the line-break). "men-folk"
  restores the men to the close and keeps the feminine cadence; "-folk" is the
  one light lexical expansion (logged).

**15.84**  ἠέ τινα τριπόδων εὐχάλκων ἠὲ λεβήτων,
- OLD: for us to carry away, some tripod of well-burnished bronze there,
- NEW: for us to carry away, a bronze tripod handsomely burnished,

**15.92**  αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ τό γʼ ἄκουσε βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος,
- OLD: But when war-shouting Menelaus had heard what he said there,
- NEW: But when this had been heard by Menelaus, good at the war-cry,
- Joins the book's own formula for βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος (15.14, 15.57, 15.67);
  the old line's "war-shouting" was the odd one out.

**15.96**  ἀνστὰς ἐξ εὐνῆς, ἐπεὶ οὐ πολὺ ναῖεν ἀπʼ αὐτοῦ·
- OLD: rising up from his bed, since he lived not far from the house there;
- NEW: rising up from his bed, for not far off from him was his dwelling;
- ἀπʼ αὐτοῦ is "from him" (Menelaus), not "from the house" — fidelity gained.

**15.120**  ὣς εἰπὼν ἐν χειρὶ τίθει δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον
- OLD: So he spoke, and the hero son of Atreus placed in his hands there
- NEW: So he spoke, and the hero son of Atreus set into his hand
- Licensed masculine ending #1 (the only one used in this book): χειρί is dative
  singular, so the plural "hands" was itself an error, and the cup enjambs into
  121; the close on "hand" is the Greek's own emphasis.

**15.133**  τοὺς δʼ ἦγε πρὸς δῶμα κάρη ξανθὸς Μενέλαος.
- OLD: Then fair-haired Menelaus led them both on into the house there,
- NEW: Then into the house he led them both, fair-haired Menelaus,

**15.141**  οἰνοχόει δʼ υἱὸς Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο.
- OLD: and the son of glorious Menelaus poured out the wine there.
- NEW: and wine was poured out by the son of glorious Menelaus.

**15.153**  ἧος ἐνὶ Τροίῃ πολεμίζομεν υἷες Ἀχαιῶν.
- OLD: all through the time in Troy when we sons of Achaea made war there.”
- NEW: all the time we were warring at Troy, we sons of Achaea.”

**15.165**  γήθησαν, καὶ πᾶσιν ἐνὶ φρεσὶ θυμὸς ἰάνθη.
- OLD: Seeing the sign, they rejoiced, and the heart in the breast of each warmed there.
- NEW: Seeing the sign, they rejoiced, and warm grew the heart in each bosom.
- The draft's "within them the heart was warming" dropped πᾶσιν ἐνὶ φρεσὶ; "in
  each bosom" restores the distributive and the breast (the poem's own word,
  15.486).

**15.168**  ἢ νῶϊν τόδʼ ἔφηνε θεὸς τέρας ἦε σοὶ αὐτῷ.
- OLD: whether the god has shown this portent to us two, or to you there.”
- NEW: whether to us two, or to you yourself, the god has shown this portent.”
- The draft's close "or to yourself" was an unflagged masculine ending; fronting
  the alternatives lets the line close on "portent" (τέρας) instead.

**15.224**  τηλεδαπός, φεύγων ἐξ Ἄργεος ἄνδρα κατακτάς,
- OLD: one from a distant land, fleeing from Argos for killing a man there,
- NEW: one from a distant land, a man-slayer fleeing from Argos,
- The draft ("having killed a man, fled from Argos") ran to seven beats and made
  the participle read as finite; ἄνδρα κατακτάς compressed to "man-slayer,"
  with φεύγων kept as the participle and the proper name at the close.

**15.230**  ὅς οἱ χρήματα πολλὰ τελεσφόρον εἰς ἐνιαυτὸν
- OLD: who by force was retaining his wealth for a full turning year then.
- NEW: who was holding his plentiful wealth by force for a full twelvemonth.
- The draft's "was his wealth for a full year retaining" was tortured English;
  χρήματα πολλὰ regains "plentiful" (dropped since the first pass), εἶχε βίῃ
  (run over from 231) stays as "holding ... by force," τελεσφόρον εἰς ἐνιαυτὸν
  is "a full twelvemonth."

**15.258**  σπένδοντʼ εὐχόμενόν τε θοῇ παρὰ νηῒ μελαίνῃ,
- OLD: He found him pouring libation and making prayer by his swift black ship there,
- NEW: He found him beside his swift black ship, pouring libation and praying,

**15.261**  λίσσομʼ ὑπὲρ θυέων καὶ δαίμονος, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα
- OLD: I beseech you by these offerings here, and then by the god there,
- NEW: I beseech you by these your offerings, by the god too, and thereafter
- The old line misplaced ἔπειτα mid-line; the draft dropped it entirely (and ran
  to five beats). In the Greek αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα stands at the line-end, pivoting to
  262's second oath-object — "and thereafter" restores it in place. This forced
  the one adjacent-line touch:

**15.262**  σῆς τʼ αὐτοῦ κεφαλῆς καὶ ἑταίρων οἵ τοι ἕπονται
- OLD: and by your own head too, and the heads of companions who follow:
- NEW: by your own head as well, and the heads of companions who follow:
- The leading "and" (now supplied by 261's "and thereafter") is dropped; "too"
  becomes "as well" to avoid doubling 261's "too."

**15.282**  ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας οἱ ἐδέξατο χάλκεον ἔγχος,
- OLD: So he spoke, and accepted from him the bronze-shod spear there,
- NEW: So he spoke, and accepted from him the bronze-shod spear-shaft,
- The draft's "his spear that was bronze-shod" closed on the stress unflagged;
  "spear-shaft" (the poem's compound, cf. 13.267 "bronze-shod spear-point")
  gives the trochaic close and keeps the inherited epithet.

**15.284**  ἂν δὲ καὶ αὐτὸς νηὸς ἐβήσετο ποντοπόροιο.
- OLD: Then he himself as well went aboard the seafaring ship there.
- NEW: Then he himself as well climbed aboard the seafaring vessel.

**15.297**  ἡ δὲ Φεὰς ἐπέβαλλεν ἐπειγομένη Διὸς οὔρῳ
- OLD: Then the ship made for Pheae, driven hard by the wind of Zeus there,
- NEW: Then the ship, driven hard by the wind of Zeus, made for Pheae,

**15.302**  δορπείτην· παρὰ δέ σφιν ἐδόρπεον ἀνέρες ἄλλοι.
- OLD: taking supper; and beside them the other men supped as well there.
- NEW: taking their supper; and other men beside them were supping.

**15.305**  ἤ μιν ἔτʼ ἐνδυκέως φιλέοι μεῖναί τε κελεύοι
- OLD: whether he still would kindly invite him to stay and remain there
- NEW: whether kindly he still would befriend him, and bid him be staying

**15.308**  ἠῶθεν προτὶ ἄστυ λιλαίομαι ἀπονέεσθαι
- OLD: At dawn I desire to go toward the city and beg there,
- NEW: At dawn I am eager to go to the city and do my begging,
- The draft dropped "beg" as unwarranted, but πτωχεύσων opens 15.309 in the Greek
  and is rendered nowhere else in the English — the old line's "and beg" was
  carrying it. Restored; λιλαίομαι regains "I am eager."

**15.321**  δρηστοσύνῃ οὐκ ἄν μοι ἐρίσσειε βροτὸς ἄλλος,
- OLD: No other mortal in service with me could contend or dispute there,
- NEW: No other mortal could rival or contend with me in service,

**15.353**  Λαέρτης μὲν ἔτι ζώει, Διὶ δʼ εὔχεται αἰεὶ
- OLD: Laertes still is alive, but always he prays unto Zeus there
- NEW: Laertes indeed is still living, but always to Zeus he is praying

**15.358**  ἡ δʼ ἄχεϊ οὗ παιδὸς ἀπέφθιτο κυδαλίμοιο,
- OLD: She indeed perished in sorrowing after her glorious son there,
- NEW: She indeed perished, in grief for her son so glorious,
- "so" is a light metrical intensifier on κυδαλίμοιο (logged).

**15.369**  καλὰ μάλʼ ἀμφιέσασα, ποσὶν δʼ ὑποδήματα δοῦσα
- OLD: beautiful garments to wear, and gave me sandals under my feet there,
- NEW: lovely garments to wear, and gave me sandals for my footsteps,
- "for my footsteps" slightly loosens ποσὶν ("for my feet") to gain the close.

**15.374**  ἐκ δʼ ἄρα δεσποίνης οὐ μείλιχον ἔστιν ἀκοῦσαι
- OLD: But from my mistress now there is no kindly thing to be heard there,
- NEW: But from my mistress nothing kindly comes to my hearing,
- The mid-line "now" (no Greek warrant) goes as well; "my" hearing is the one
  supplied word, standing for the infinitive ἀκοῦσαι. The contrastive "But from
  my mistress" answers 373's "From that I have eaten..."

**15.391**  σιγῇ νῦν ξυνίει καὶ τέρπεο, πῖνέ τε οἶνον
- OLD: now in silence attend and take your delight, and drink of the wine there
- NEW: now give heed in silence, and drink of the wine, and take your pleasure,
- νῦν is in the Greek and keeps its place at the head of the line; τέρπεο and
  πῖνε are reordered so the line does not run into 392's "sitting at ease."

**15.396**  εὑδέτω ἐξελθών· ἅμα δʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι
- OLD: let him go out and sleep; and together with dawn as it appears there,
- NEW: let him go out and sleep; and as soon as dawn is appearing,

**15.409**  ἀλλʼ ὅτε γηράσκωσι πόλιν κάτα φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων,
- OLD: But whenever the tribes of men throughout the city grow old there,
- NEW: But whenever the tribes of men, throughout the city, are aging,

**15.419**  τὴν δʼ ἄρα Φοίνικες πολυπαίπαλοι ἠπερόπευον.
- OLD: Her the Phoenicians, masters of many deceits, led astray there.
- NEW: Her the Phoenicians led astray, being masters of manifold cunning.
- The draft ended with a comma against 420's sentence-opening "While"; the
  Greek's full stop is restored.

**15.423**  εἰρώτα δὴ ἔπειτα τίς εἴη καὶ πόθεν ἔλθοι·
- OLD: Then after this he asked her who she was and whence she had come there.
- NEW: Then after this he questioned her, who she was and whence she was coming.
- Draft's "from whence" (pleonasm) trimmed and its comma raised to a full stop
  before 424's "She at once pointed out..."

**15.448**  οἴσω γὰρ καὶ χρυσόν, ὅτις χʼ ὑποχείριος ἔλθῃ·
- OLD: For I will bring gold also, whatever may come to my hands then.
- NEW: For I will bring gold also, whatever falls into my keeping.
- The draft's "gold as well" duplicated 449's line-ending "as well" (old "also"
  kept), and its comma is raised to a full stop before 449's "And I could..."

**15.455**  οἱ δʼ ἐνιαυτὸν ἅπαντα παρʼ ἡμῖν αὖθι μένοντες
- OLD: They, remaining there among us for the full course of a year then,
- NEW: They, among us remaining there for the whole of a year’s turning,
- αὖθι ("right there, on the spot") is in the Greek: the mid-line "there" stays;
  only the unwarranted final "then" is removed.

**15.500**  δεῖπνόν τʼ ἐντύνοντο κερῶντό τε αἴθοπα οἶνον.
- OLD: and they made ready a meal, and mixed the flaming wine in the bowl there.
- NEW: and they made ready a meal, and the flaming wine they were mixing.
- "in the bowl" was itself padding; the draft's "fire-bright" is reverted to
  the neighboring books' formula "flaming wine" (14.447, 16.14).

**15.507**  δαῖτʼ ἀγαθὴν κρειῶν τε καὶ οἴνου ἡδυπότοιο.
- OLD: a noble feast of meats and of sweet delightful wine there.”
- NEW: a noble feast of meats and of wine sweet for the drinking.”
- ἡδυπότοιο is literally "sweet to drink"; the draft's "sweetly delightful"
  was vaguer than the Greek.

**15.509**  πῆ γὰρ ἐγώ, φίλε τέκνον, ἴω; τεῦ δώμαθʼ ἵκωμαι
- OLD: “But where shall I go, dear child? To whose house shall I come now,
- NEW: “Where then shall I go, dear child? To whose house shall I be coming,
- The "then" is the idiomatic English deliberative for γάρ in a question, held
  mid-line — not the banned line-final filler.

**15.517**  φαίνεται, ἀλλʼ ἀπὸ τῶν ὑπερωΐῳ ἱστὸν ὑφαίνει.
- OLD: rather apart from them in the upper room she weaves at her loom there.
- NEW: rather she weaves, apart from them, at her loom in the upper chamber.
- The draft's "she's weaving" both dropped ἱστόν (the loom) and slipped a
  contraction into Telemachus' otherwise uncontracted register.

**15.520**  τὸν νῦν ἶσα θεῷ Ἰθακήσιοι εἰσορόωσι·
- OLD: whom now the men of Ithaca look upon as if on a god there.
- NEW: on whom now, as though on a god, the men of Ithaca are gazing.
- The draft's "whom ... are gazing" lacked its preposition; νῦν stays mid-line
  where the Greek has it.

**15.528**  μεσσηγὺς νηός τε καὶ αὐτοῦ Τηλεμάχοιο.
- OLD: down to the ground between the ship and Telemachus himself there.
- NEW: down to the ground, midway between the ship and Telemachus.
- μεσσηγύς is fully rendered by "midway between"; the emphatic αὐτοῦ is absorbed
  into the definite reference (keeping "himself" forces a stressed close). The
  draft's comma is raised to the sentence-ending full stop before 529.

**15.550**  Τηλέμαχος δʼ ὑπὸ ποσσὶν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα,
- OLD: But Telemachus bound his beautiful sandals under his feet there,
- NEW: But under his feet Telemachus bound his beautiful sandals,

# Book 23 "Then"-opening revision — 25 rewritten, 7 kept

Warrant rule: a line-initial "Then" survives only where the Greek carries a real
temporal adverb at the joint — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα (free-standing),
αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal ἔνθα, ἦμος-clauses. Lines whose Greek opens on a light
connective — bare δέ, αὐτάρ, ὣς + verb, καί, τε, γάρ, or asyndeton — lose the
"Then" and take an opening that reflects the actual connective. The two
τὴν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα reply-formula lines (69, 80) are treated as habitual per
the project's speech-formula rule: the ἔπειτα there is formula-internal filler,
not a narrative time-step. Free-standing ἔπειτα (45, 89, 207, 287) keeps its
"then".

Variety audit of the 25 replacement openings: And ×5 (10, 34, 58, 279, 295);
But ×3 (96, 133, 156); To her in turn ×2 (25, 39); Thereupon ×2 (69, 80);
In turn to him ×2 (256, 285); Answering ×2 (129, 263); all others single
(Up, Standing, To him, In answer, while, back, Him, across, they). Largest
family is 5/25 — at the one-fifth ceiling, no new metronome. Every rewrite
still scans as six falling beats; no edited line ends in there/now/then/here;
formula pairs identical in the Greek stay identical in English (25=39, 69≈80,
256=285, 10=58).

**23.1** bare δέ (γρηῢς δʼ)
- OLD: Then the old woman ran to the upper room with laughter about her,
- NEW: Up to the upper room ran the old woman with laughter about her,

**23.4** δʼ ἄρα (στῆ δʼ ἄρʼ ὑπὲρ κεφαλῆς)
- OLD: Then she stood at her head, and spoke a word, and addressed her:
- NEW: Standing above her head, she spoke a word, and addressed her:
- ὑπέρ = "above," recovered in passing.

**23.129** τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in turn the much-counseling Odysseus answered and told him:
- NEW: Answering him, the much-counseling Odysseus told him:
- Participle-first mirrors ἀπαμειβόμενος itself.

**23.133** αὐτάρ (αὐτὰρ θεῖος ἀοιδὸς)
- OLD: Then let the godlike singer, holding the clear-toned lyre in his fingers,
- NEW: But let the godlike singer, holding the clear-toned lyre in his fingers,

**23.143** μέν ... δέ (ὅπλισθεν δὲ γυναῖκες)
- OLD: then the women dressed; and the godlike singer took up
- NEW: while the women made themselves ready; and the godlike singer took up
- "made themselves ready" is closer to middle-voice ὅπλισθεν than "dressed."

**23.156** αὐτάρ (αὐτὰρ κὰκ κεφαλῆς)
- OLD: Then down from his head abundant beauty poured Athena,
- NEW: But down from his head abundant beauty poured Athena,
- αὐτάρ marks the change of agent: the housekeeper bathed him, but Athena
  poured the beauty.

**23.164** ἂψ δʼ αὖτις (bare δέ)
- OLD: then once again he sat on the chair from which he had risen,
- NEW: back once again he sat on the chair from which he had risen,
- ἄψ = "back," recovered.

**23.173** τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπε (reply formula)
- OLD: Then resourceful Penelope answered him, prudent in spirit:
- NEW: Him resourceful Penelope answered, prudent in spirit:

**23.201** bare δέ (ἐκ δʼ ἐτάνυσσα)
- OLD: then I stretched out upon it a strap of oxhide, bright crimson.
- NEW: across it I stretched out a strap of oxhide, bright crimson.
- ἐκ ... ἐτάνυσσα = stretched across; "over it" avoided because 23.200 ends
  "decorating it over."

**23.256** τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπε (reply formula)
- OLD: Then in return to him prudent Penelope answered:
- NEW: In turn to him the prudent Penelope made her answer:

**23.263** τὴν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη (reply formula, = 23.129 Greek shape)
- OLD: Then in return to her spoke resourceful Odysseus:
- NEW: Answering her in return spoke resourceful Odysseus:

**23.279** τε (οἴκαδʼ ἀποστείχειν, ἔρδειν θʼ)
- OLD: then to go homeward again, and offer sacred hecatombs
- NEW: and to go homeward again, and offer sacred hecatombs
- The time-step in this passage belongs to 23.276 (καὶ τότε), which keeps its
  "then"; 279 is just the next infinitive in the list.

**23.285** τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπε (= 23.256)
- OLD: Then in return to him prudent Penelope answered:
- NEW: In turn to him the prudent Penelope made her answer:

**23.295** bare δέ + participle (ἐς θάλαμον δʼ ἀγαγοῦσα)
- OLD: Then when she had led them into the chamber she turned back from them.
- NEW: And when she had led them into the chamber she turned back from them.
- The ἔπειτα in Greek 295 belongs to the NEXT clause (οἱ μὲν ἔπειτα), already
  rendered by 23.296 "They then gladly approached."

**23.301** asyndeton, verb-first (τερπέσθην μύθοισι)
- OLD: then they took their delight in talk, telling stories to each other:
- NEW: they took their delight in talk, telling stories to each other:
- Asyndetic apodosis after 23.300 "But when...," exactly as in the Greek.

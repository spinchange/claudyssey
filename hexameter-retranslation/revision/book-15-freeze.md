## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**15.120** ὣς εἰπὼν ἐν χειρὶ τίθει δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον
- OLD: So he spoke, and the hero son of Atreus set into his hand
- NEW: So having spoken, the hero Atreus’ son set into his hand
- Participial ὣς εἰπών was rendered with the finite formula. "son of Atreus" →
  "Atreus’ son" pays back the two syllables the participial opening costs.

**15.130** ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ἐν χερσὶ τίθει, ὁ δʼ ἐδέξατο χαίρων.
- OLD: So she spoke and placed it into his hands, and he received it gladly.
- NEW: So having spoken, she placed it in his hands; he received it gladly.
- Feminine participle ὣς εἰποῦσ᾿ takes the same participial rendering as the
  masculine; the English participle is uninflected, so the subject pronoun carries it.

**15.215** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας ἔλασεν καλλίτριχας ἵππους
- OLD: So he spoke, and drove back the fair-maned horses in haste to
- NEW: So having spoken, he drove the fair-maned horses in haste to
- "back" dropped: ἂψ belongs to 216 (where "back … to the city" already stands),
  and it was doing double duty across the enjambment.

**15.282** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας οἱ ἐδέξατο χάλκεον ἔγχος,
- OLD: So he spoke, and accepted from him the bronze-shod spear-shaft,
- NEW: So having spoken, he took from him the bronze-shod spear-shaft,
- Same Greek verse as 16.40; the two now read alike in English apart from the
  spear-epithet each book had already settled on.

**15.454** ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ἀπέβη πρὸς δώματα καλά,
- OLD: So she spoke, and after speaking went away to the beautiful dwelling.
- NEW: So having spoken, she went away to the beautiful dwelling.
- The old line said the speaking twice ("So she spoke … after speaking"). The
  participial formula absorbs both, and the line loses three syllables of padding.

**15.493** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- OLD: Thus they in this fashion addressed one another with speeches,
- NEW: So in such words they spoke and addressed one another together,
- Not a speech-close formula; recast off line-initial *Thus*. Now verbatim-identical
  to 16.321, where the Greek verse is identical (only the final stop differs, as
  the syntax there closes the sentence).

**15.525** ὣς ἄρα οἱ εἰπόντι ἐπέπτατο δεξιὸς ὄρνις,
- OLD: So he spoke, and there flew past on the right hand a bird of omen,
- NEW: Even as he was speaking, there flew by a bird on the right hand,
- Same Greek verse as 15.160, which already read "Even as he was speaking…".
  ὣς ἄρα οἱ εἰπόντι is a dative participle ("to him thus speaking"), not the finite
  ὣς φάτο, so it must not take the ruled formula — and the book's two instances
  must match each other. Matched to 160.

**15.539** ἦ καὶ Πείραιον προσεφώνεε, πιστὸν ἑταῖρον·
- OLD: So he spoke, and addressed Piraeus, his faithful companion:
- NEW: He spoke, and he called to Piraeus, his faithful companion:
- ἦ ῥα / ἦ καί is not ὣς φάτο and had wrongly taken the formula's opening. The bare
  "He spoke" keeps ἦ's plain assertive force and vacates "So he spoke," for the
  formula proper.

**15.547** ὣς εἰπὼν ἐπὶ νηὸς ἔβη, ἐκέλευσε δʼ ἑταίρους
- OLD: So he spoke, and went aboard the ship, and ordered his comrades
- NEW: So having spoken, he went aboard, and he ordered his comrades
- "the ship" cut as recoverable (ἐπὶ νηός is the ship he has been standing beside
  for twenty lines); that pays for the participial opening without padding.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**15.113** δώρων δʼ, ὅσσʼ ἐν ἐμῷ οἴκῳ κειμήλια κεῖται,
- OLD: And of the gifts, whatever lie stored as treasures within my house here,
- NEW: And of the gifts, whatever within my house lies stored as a treasure,
- No deictic in the Greek: ἐν ἐμῷ οἴκῳ is "in my house," already carried by "within
  my house." The final "here" was pure metrical filler. Reordered so κειμήλιον
  ("treasure") lands last as a trochee — no words added.

**15.492** — *kept, Greek-warranted*
πολλὰ βροτῶν ἐπὶ ἄστεʼ ἀλώμενος ἐνθάδʼ ἱκάνω.
- "after much roaming through cities of mortal men, have come here.”"
- ἐνθάδε is a true deictic and is the point of the sentence: Odysseus has come
  *to this place*. Retained; line unchanged.

## Job 3 — residual *Thus*

**15.484** οὕτω τήνδε τε γαῖαν ἐγὼν ἴδον ὀφθαλμοῖσι.
- OLD: Thus it was I myself first saw this country here with my own eyes.”
- NEW: Thus it was I myself with my own eyes first looked on this country.”
- *Thus* **kept and counted as justified**: οὕτω genuinely means "in this way" —
  it closes Eumaeus' account of how he came to Ithaca. The unwarranted "here" is
  gone (τήνδε γαῖαν = "this country," which the demonstrative already carries);
  the line now closes on "country" instead of on filler.

**15.341** — *not line-initial, left as is*: "Would that thus, Eumaeus, …" renders
ὣς in a wish-clause, mid-line; outside the scope of the ruling.

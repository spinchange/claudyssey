## Changed verses

**3.15** no deictic in τοὔνεκα γὰρ καὶ πόντον ἐπέπλως — unwarranted verse-final *here*
- OLD: not in the least; for precisely because of this purpose you sailed here,
- NEW: not in the least; for precisely because of this purpose you voyaged,

**3.29** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασʼ (participial)
- OLD: Thus having spoken, Athena proceeded ahead of him swiftly;
- NEW: So having spoken, Athena proceeded ahead of him swiftly;

**3.50** no deictic in τοὔνεκα σοὶ προτέρῳ — unwarranted verse-final *here*
- OLD: therefore to you do I first give the beautiful cup made of gold here.”
- NEW: therefore to you do I first give the beautiful goblet of gold.”

**3.51** ὣς εἰπὼν (participial; had been flattened to a finite formula)
- OLD: Thus he spoke and set in her hand the cup of the honey-sweet vintage.
- NEW: So having spoken, he set the cup of sweet wine in her fingers.

**3.62** ὣς ἄρʼ ἔπειτʼ ἠρᾶτο — not the ὣς φάτο family; line-initial *Thus* recast
- OLD: Thus did she pray, and herself was fulfilling the prayer she had offered.
- NEW: So then she prayed, and was herself fulfilling the whole of the prayer.

**3.329** ὣς ἔφατʼ
- OLD: Thus he spoke, while the sun sank down and the twilight descended.
- NEW: So he spoke, while the sun sank down and the twilight descended.

**3.337** ἦ ῥα — NOT ὣς φάτο; it opened with *Thus*, so recast to a shape distinct from the ruled formula
- OLD: Thus spoke the daughter of Zeus, and the people attended her summons.
- NEW: She had spoken, the daughter of Zeus, and the people gave heed to her.

**3.351** πάρα ἐμοί is possessive (“I have”), not a place-deictic — unwarranted verse-final *here*
- OLD: I have an ample abundance of beautiful blankets and cloaks here.
- NEW: I have an ample abundance of beautiful cloaks and of blankets.

**3.371** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασʼ (participial)
- OLD: Thus having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed,
- NEW: So having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed,

**3.385** ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος — ruled formula kept whole, the participle follows it
- OLD: Thus did he pray, and the goddess Athena attended his prayer.
- NEW: So he spoke, praying, and Pallas Athena gave ear to his prayer.

**3.430** ὣς ἔφαθʼ
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and at once every person was busily working.
- NEW: So he spoke, and at once every person was busily working.

**3.477** ὣς ἔφαθʼ
- OLD: Thus he spoke, and the sons gave ear and at once they obeyed him.
- NEW: So he spoke, and the sons gave ear and at once they obeyed him.

## Retained line-initial *Thus* (the Greek genuinely means “in this way”)

**3.184** `ὣς ἦλθον, φίλε τέκνον, ἀπευθής, οὐδέ τι οἶδα`
- Thus I came home, dear child, knowing nothing and bearing no tidings,
- ὣς ἦλθον — “in this way I came (home)”, summing up the voyage just narrated.

**3.284** `ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθα κατέσχετʼ, ἐπειγόμενός περ ὁδοῖο,`
- Thus Menelaus was held in that place, though he hastened his journey,
- ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθα — the resumptive ὣς ὁ μέν, “and so he, there …”.

**3.301** `ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθα πολὺν βίοτον καὶ χρυσὸν ἀγείρων`
- Thus Menelaus, collecting abundant possessions and gold-work,
- ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθα — same resumptive construction as 3.284.

## Retained verse-final *here* (Greek deictic present)

**3.61** `οὕνεκα δεῦρʼ ἱκόμεσθα θοῇ σὺν νηὶ μελαίνῃ.`
- fully accomplished for which in our swift black ship we have come here.”
- δεῦρʼ (“hither / to this place”) stands in the Greek — a true deictic, so verse-final *here* is warranted.

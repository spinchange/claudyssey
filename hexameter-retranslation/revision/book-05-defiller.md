# Book 05 de-filler — all 22 filler-final lines resolved

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then* unless the Greek itself carries the
   locative/temporal sense at that position (one case: 5.68, αὐτοῦ "right there" —
   the old line is kept unchanged, with its warrant logged).
3. The preferred fix is reordering the line so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, formula-nouns, compounds already in the book's idiom: sea-way,
   keel-beam, billow, mortal humanity), not adding new words.
4. No masculine endings were needed in this book (budget 3, used 0).
5. Where the Greek repeats within the book, the English repetitions stay verbatim:
   μέγα κῦμα at 296/366 both close on "mountainous billow" (451's ἔσχε δὲ κῦμα
   joins them); πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς at 486 keeps the book's fixed
   "the steadfast and noble Odysseus" (as at 171, 354); 196 closes on
   "mortal humanity," the book's own verse-final formula at 2 and 32.
6. Voice rule holds: Calypso (165) and Ino (346) speak without contractions.

**05.36** οἵ κέν μιν περὶ κῆρι θεὸν ὣς τιμήσουσιν
- OLD: They in the depth of their hearts shall honor the man like a god there,
- NEW: They in the depth of their hearts, like a god, shall render him honor,
- "render him honor" is a standard phrasal equivalent of τιμήσουσιν; the
  object-noun supplies the feminine close.

**05.55** ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ τὴν νῆσον ἀφίκετο τηλόθʼ ἐοῦσαν
- OLD: Yet when he reached that faraway island and came to its shore there,
- NEW: Yet when at last he arrived at the island lying far distant,
- "and came to its shore" was itself padding (not in the Greek) and half-doubled
  56's "stepped onto dry land"; τηλόθʼ ἐοῦσαν is now rendered where it sits,
  as a participial phrase ("lying far distant"). δή = "at last."

**05.66** σκῶπές τʼ ἴρηκές τε τανύγλωσσοί τε κορῶναι
- OLD: little horned owls and hawks and the crows of the sea with long tongues there,
- NEW: little horned owls and hawks and the long-tongued crows of the sea-way,
- "of the sea" (67's εἰνάλιαι, already carried up into this line) becomes
  "of the sea-way," the book's own compound (17, 50, 52); τανύγλωσσοι regains
  its compound shape as "long-tongued."

**05.68** ἡ δʼ αὐτοῦ τετάνυστο περὶ σπείους γλαφυροῖο
- OLD: Over the hollow cave a flourishing vine had been trained there,
- NEW: (unchanged)
- The one warranted keeper in this book: αὐτοῦ ("in that very place") is in the
  Greek, so the final "there" is honest, not filler — as with αὐτόθι at 21.90.
  "trained" is also the right verb for τετάνυστο of a vine and stays.

**05.69** ἡμερὶς ἡβώωσα, τεθήλει δὲ σταφυλῇσι.
- OLD: grown to its prime and abundantly heavy with clusters of grapes now.
- NEW: grown to its prime, its clusters of grapes abundantly thriving.
- τεθήλει ("was flourishing") now gets its own closing participle; "thriving"
  rather than "flourishing" because 68 already carries "flourishing." The
  invented "heavy" is gone.

**05.130** τὸν μὲν ἐγὼν ἐσάωσα περὶ τρόπιος βεβαῶτα
- OLD: I was the one who delivered him, riding astride of the keel there,
- NEW: I was the one who delivered him, mounted astride of the keel-beam,
- βεβαῶτα = "having bestridden"; "mounted astride of" is the poem's own idiom
  for exactly this posture at 371 ("mounted astride of a single beam") — the
  same event Calypso is recounting here. "-beam" is the compound's residue.

**05.165** αὐτὰρ ἐγὼ σῖτον καὶ ὕδωρ καὶ οἶνον ἐρυθρὸν
- OLD: Bread I shall set in the vessel and water and red-colored wine there,
- NEW: Bread I shall set in the vessel, and water, and wine that is ruddy,
- Calypso speaking: no contractions. "ruddy" for ἐρυθρόν supplies the
  feminine close.

**05.181** χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν·
- OLD: Stroking him there with her hand, she addressed him aloud by his name there:
- NEW: Then with her hand she stroked him, and spoke, and by name she addressed him:
- The old line carried the filler twice. The three Greek verbs now come in
  order (κατέρεξεν / ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ / ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν), and the unstressed-pronoun
  close ("addressed him") keeps the speech-tag feminine — no masculine license
  spent on a formula line.

**05.196** Ἑρμείας, νύμφη δʼ ἐτίθει πάρα πᾶσαν ἐδωδήν
- OLD: while every kind of the food that mortal humanity eats there
- NEW: while every kind of the food that is eaten by mortal humanity
- οἷα βροτοὶ ἄνδρες ἔδουσιν is gnomic; the passive keeps it so, and the line
  now closes on "mortal humanity," the book's own verse-final formula (2, 32).

**05.232** καλὴν χρυσείην, κεφαλῇ δʼ ἐφύπερθε καλύπτρην.
- OLD: girdle of gold, then covered the crown of her head with a veil there.
- NEW: girdle of gold, then covered the crown of her head with a veiling.
- καλύπτρη as "a veiling" (the act-noun for the head-covering); "head-veil"
  was rejected because "head" already stands mid-line.

**05.255** πρὸς δʼ ἄρα πηδάλιον ποιήσατο, ὄφρʼ ἰθύνοι.
- OLD: also he fashioned a steering oar that would hold to the course there.
- NEW: also he fashioned a steering oar that would hold the course steady.
- ὄφρʼ ἰθύνοι ("so it might keep straight") as "hold the course steady";
  "steering oar" stays intact as the book's fixed rendering of πηδάλιον
  (270, 315). The raft remains "it" throughout this book, so no "her."

**05.259** ἱστία ποιήσασθαι· ὁ δʼ εὖ τεχνήσατο καὶ τά.
- OLD: which he constructed as well with the skill and the craft he possessed there,
- NEW: which he constructed as well, with the whole of his skill and his craftwork,
- εὖ τεχνήσατο καὶ τά: "the whole of" carries the εὖ-emphasis where the old
  line had "he possessed" — one intensifier for another, filler gone.

**05.279** ὀκτωκαιδεκάτῃ δʼ ἐφάνη ὄρεα σκιόεντα
- OLD: Then on the eighteenth day the shadowy mountains appeared there,
- NEW: Then on the eighteenth day the shadowy mountains were appearing,
- The tempting reorder ("...appeared the shadowy mountains,") would butt
  against 280's line-initial "mountains"; the progressive keeps the anadiplosis
  clean. Line-initial "Then" renders δʼ + the dawn-count and is not verse-final.

**05.296** καὶ Βορέης αἰθρηγενέτης, μέγα κῦμα κυλίνδων.
- OLD: also the clear-born North Wind rolling a mountainous wave there.
- NEW: also the clear-born North Wind, rolling a mountainous billow.
- κυλίνδων stays a bare participle, as in the Greek; "mountainous billow"
  for μέγα κῦμα, matching 366 verbatim where the Greek formula repeats.

**05.326** ἐν μέσσῃ δὲ καθῖζε τέλος θανάτου ἀλεείνων.
- OLD: sitting himself in the midst and evading the ending of death there.
- NEW: sitting himself in the midst, the ending of death evading.
- ἀλεείνων restored to its Greek line-final position.

**05.346** τῆ δέ, τόδε κρήδεμνον ὑπὸ στέρνοιο τανύσσαι
- OLD: Here, spread this deathless veil underneath the breadth of your chest now.
- NEW: Here, spread this deathless veil beneath the breadth of your bosom.
- Ino (a divinity) speaking: no contractions. "bosom" for στέρνοιο is the
  book's own word (82, 157, 190, 454). Line-initial "Here" is τῆ δέ itself.

**05.366** ὦρσε δʼ ἐπὶ μέγα κῦμα Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων
- OLD: earth-shaking lord Poseidon uplifted a mountainous wave there,
- NEW: earth-shaking lord Poseidon roused up a mountainous billow,
- ὦρσε = "roused up" (better than "uplifted"); "mountainous billow" = 296,
  the Greek's own μέγα κῦμα repetition kept verbatim in English.

**05.368** ὡς δʼ ἄνεμος ζαὴς ἠΐων θημῶνα τινάξῃ
- OLD: Just as a turbulent wind tosses up a dry pile of chaff there,
- NEW: Just as a turbulent wind tosses up a dry pile of stubble,
- "stubble" (cut, threshed stalks) for θημῶνα ἠΐων, taking 369's καρφαλέων
  ("dry") with it as before; 369's "wisps" still agrees.

**05.451** ὣς φάθʼ, ὁ δʼ αὐτίκα παῦσεν ἑὸν ῥόον, ἔσχε δὲ κῦμα
- OLD: Thus did he speak, and the god stayed the current and held back the wave there,
- NEW: Thus did he speak, and the god stayed the current and held back the billow,
- "billow" for κῦμα as at 296/366; "the god" stays the unbroken subject
  carrying into 452's "calming."

**05.456** ἂν στόμα τε ῥῖνάς θʼ· ὁ δʼ ἄρʼ ἄπνευστος καὶ ἄναυδος
- OLD: running in torrents from mouth and nostrils; without breath or speech there
- NEW: running in torrents from mouth and nostrils; breathless, and speechless,
- ἄπνευστος καὶ ἄναυδος rendered literally as the paired privatives the Greek
  has; they hang predicatively over 457's "down he lay," as in the Greek.

**05.462** δέξατο χερσὶ φίλῃσιν· ὁ δʼ ἐκ ποταμοῖο λιασθεὶς
- OLD: into her well-loved hands. From the river Odysseus turned then,
- NEW: into her well-loved hands; and Odysseus turned from the river,
- λιασθείς ("withdrawing from") as "turned from"; the semicolon mirrors the
  Greek colon mid-line, and the line still runs on into 463's "sank in the
  reeds" with Odysseus as subject.

**05.486** τὴν μὲν ἰδὼν γήθησε πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς
- OLD: Seeing the bed, the steadfast and noble Odysseus rejoiced there,
- NEW: Seeing the bed, the steadfast and noble Odysseus was gladdened,
- γήθησε as "was gladdened," the pilot's own solution for the identical
  formula-verb at 21.414; the book's fixed epithet-string "the steadfast and
  noble Odysseus" (171, 354) stays verbatim.

## Tally

- 22 filler-final lines resolved: 21 reworked, 1 kept with warrant
  (5.68 "there" = αὐτοῦ, which is in the Greek). 0 now end in unmotivated
  *there/now/then*.
- 0 masculine endings used (budget 3): every close is feminine, including the
  speech-tag at 181 the draft had proposed as masculine.
- Formulas held verbatim where the Greek repeats: μέγα κῦμα → "mountainous
  billow" (296 = 366, with 451's κῦμα joining); "the steadfast and noble
  Odysseus" kept intact at 486 (= 171, 354); 196 now closes on the book's
  verse-final "mortal humanity" (= 2, 32).
- Fidelity recovered along the way: τανύγλωσσοι as a compound at 66; τεθήλει
  its own participle at 69; βεβαῶτα as "mounted astride" at 130 (echoing 371);
  the three-verb order at 181; ὦρσε at 366; ἄπνευστος καὶ ἄναυδος literal at
  456; padding cut at 55 ("came to its shore") and 69 ("heavy").
- Honest costs incurred: "-beam" added at 130 (keel-beam); "the whole of"
  carries εὖ at 259; "steady" carries ἰθύνοι at 255; "sea-way" for εἰνάλιαι
  at 66; "stubble" for chaff-heap at 368. All far below the filler the old
  lines carried.

# Book 08 "Then"-opening revision

Book totals across both passes: examined 51, kept 5, rewritten 46.

**Pass 1** (capitalized "Then"-openings): examined 40, kept 4, rewrote 36.
**Pass 2** (2026-08-23, lowercase mid-sentence verse-initial "then"s): examined the
remaining 11 and re-verified pass 1's four keeps; kept 1 (486, δὴ τότε), rewrote 10.

Warrant rule applied: a "Then"-opening survives only where Murray's Greek carries a real
temporal adverb at the line head — τότε, δὴ τότε, καὶ τότε, ἔπειτα, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα, temporal
ἔνθα, or an ἦμος-clause apodosis. A light connective — bare δέ, αὐτάρ/ἀτάρ (including the
αὐτὰρ ἐπεί "but when"-clause, whose adverb is a translator's addition), τε, καί, ὣς + verb,
or asyndeton — does not warrant "Then", and the speech-reply formulas (τὸν δ᾿ ἠμείβετ᾿
ἔπειτα, τὸν δ᾿ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη, τὸν δ᾿ αὖτε προσέειπε) are recast as answer-formulas
without it. Every replacement line still scans as six falling beats; where the Greek repeats
a formula verbatim, the English now does too (4 = 421; 62 ≈ 261; 72 = 485; 140 = 400;
152 = 412 ≈ 463).

Opening-type spread across the 36 rewrites: But when ×6, And/And in answer ×6, Now ×3,
Once/Once more ×3, Near ×2, Forth ×2, Answering ×2, Lord ×2, and one each of Two-and-fifty,
Up, When, After, So, Back, Yet, Unto, Into, Down, Elsewhere — no family exceeds a sixth.

## Pass 2 — lowercase verse-initial "then"s (2026-08-23)

Same warrant standard. Opening-word spread across these 10 rewrites: and ×2,
subject-first ×2 (great Odysseus, I), when, up, round, high, so, near — with "near"
mandated by the verbatim-formula rule (471 = 62 in the Greek).

**08.52** bare δέ (ἐν δ᾿ ἱστόν τ᾿ ἐτίθεντο)
- OLD: then in the black ship duly the mast and the sails they were stepping,
- NEW: and in the black ship duly the mast and the sails they were stepping,

**08.92** asyndeton (ἂψ Ὀδυσεὺς κατὰ κρᾶτα καλυψάμενος; ἄψ already lives in "once again")
- OLD: then great Odysseus covered his head once again and lamented.
- NEW: great Odysseus covered his head once again and lamented.
- Subject-first apodosis after the "Yet when…" clause of 90–91, matching the Greek's asyndeton.

**08.173** bare δέ + circumstantial participle (ἐρχόμενον δ᾿ ἀνὰ ἄστυ)
- OLD: then, as he passes along through the city, they gaze on him, godlike.
- NEW: when he is passing along through the city, they gaze on him, godlike.
- The participle ἐρχόμενον is itself the "as/when he goes"; no temporal adverb exists.

**08.287** bare δέ (βῆ δ᾿ ἰέναι πρὸς δῶμα)
- OLD: then made his way to the home of the widely renowned Hephaestus,
- NEW: and made his way to the home of the widely renowned Hephaestus,

**08.326** δ᾿ ἄρα (ἄσβεστος δ᾿ ἄρ᾿ ἐνῶρτο γέλως)
- OLD: then in the blessed gods rose laughter that never could be quenched,
- NEW: up in the blessed gods rose laughter that never could be quenched,
- "up…rose" unpacks ἐνῶρτο ("rose up"); the old line's rhythm kept intact.

**08.366** bare δέ (ἀμφὶ δὲ εἵματα ἕσσαν; ἀμφί "round about")
- OLD: then they arrayed her in beautiful clothes, a great wonder to look on.
- NEW: round her they cast the beautiful clothes, a great wonder to look on.
- ἀμφί restored to its true position; "cast round" is the literal ἀμφι-ἕννυμι.

**08.375** bare δέ (ὁ δ᾿ ἀπὸ χθονὸς ὑψόσ᾿ ἀερθείς; ὑψόσε "on high")
- OLD: then the other would leap from the ground and would easily catch it,
- NEW: high would the other one leap from the ground and would easily catch it,
- ὑψόσε, which the old line had dropped, supplies the opening beat.

**08.467** τῷ κεν (logical "therefore", not temporal)
- OLD: then I shall pray to you even there as I pray to a goddess,
- NEW: so shall I pray to you even there as I pray to a goddess,
- Apodosis of the wish in 465–466; τῷ is inferential, rendered "so."

**08.471** bare δέ (κῆρυξ δ᾿ ἐγγύθεν ἦλθεν ἄγων ἐρίηρον ἀοιδόν = 62 verbatim)
- OLD: then the herald approached them and led the respected singer,
- NEW: near to them came the herald and led the respected singer,
- Formula rule: now matches revised 62 word for word, as the Greek does.

**08.497** asyndeton + αὐτίκα ("at once", already in the line)
- OLD: then I shall speak out at once and relate to the whole of humanity
- NEW: I shall at once speak out and relate to the whole of humanity
- αὐτίκα already lived in the line as "at once"; the prefixed "then" was pure habit.

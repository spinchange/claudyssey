# Book 08 de-filler — all 19 filler-final lines reworked

Rules applied as in the Book 21 pilot:

1. Every replacement is a six-beat falling line (dactyls predominating, final foot
   shortened), same as the project norm.
2. No line ends in *there*, *now*, or *then*; no keeper needed a Greek warrant in
   this book.
3. The preferred fix is reordering so a naturally trochaic word lands last
   (participles, names, plurals); no new words bolted on to buy the cadence.
4. One masculine ending licensed (08.261), within the 2–3/book budget.
5. Formula echoes kept: the near-anaphora ἂν δʼ ἵσταντο (110) / ἂν δʼ ἔσταν (118)
   now echoes in English ("were rising" closes both); θεὸν ὥς (173) and θεῷ ὥς
   (453) are rendered as kin ("godlike" / "as a god is"); πᾶσι θεοῖσιν at 305
   takes the pilot's own rendering from 21.203 ("all the immortals");
   φόρμιγγα λίγειαν stays "the clear-voiced lyre" verbatim (67, 255, 261).

---

**08.18**  υἱὸν Λαέρταο δαΐφρονα· τῷ δʼ ἄρʼ Ἀθήνη
- OLD: battle-wise lord Odysseus, for Pallas Athena had poured there
- NEW: battle-wise lord Odysseus, for Pallas Athena was pouring
- The verb belongs to 19's Greek; tense softened to progressive so the
  participle lands the close, enjambing into "marvelous grace…" as before.

**08.52**  ἐν δʼ ἱστόν τʼ ἐτίθεντο καὶ ἱστία νηὶ μελαίνῃ,
- OLD: then in the black ship duly they stepped both the mast and the sails there,
- NEW: then in the black ship duly the mast and the sails they were stepping,

**08.68**  αὐτοῦ ὑπὲρ κεφαλῆς καὶ ἐπέφραδε χερσὶν ἑλέσθαι
- OLD: head of the singer, and showed how his hands could take hold of the lyre there.
- NEW: head of the singer, and showed him how with his hands to take it.
- "the lyre" was just named in 67; the pronoun avoids the near-duplicate,
  and ἑλέσθαι ("to take") now lands the line.

**08.108**  Φαιήκων οἱ ἄριστοι, ἀέθλια θαυμανέοντες.
- OLD: nobles had followed, intending to marvel at all the athletic games there.
- NEW: nobles had followed, intending to marvel at all of the contests.
- "athletic" was an unneeded gloss on ἀέθλια; "contests" is the poem's own word.

**08.110**  μυρίοι· ἂν δʼ ἵσταντο νέοι πολλοί τε καὶ ἐσθλοί.
- OLD: followed behind in their thousands, and many fine young men arose there.
- NEW: followed behind in their thousands, and many fine young men were rising.
- ἂν δʼ ἵσταντο is "rose up," not merely stood; the imperfect suits the
  progressive as the catalogue of risers follows in 111.

**08.118**  ἂν δʼ ἔσταν τρεῖς παῖδες ἀμύμονος Ἀλκινόοιο,
- OLD: Three sons born to the blameless Alcinous also arose there:
- NEW: Three sons of the blameless Alcinous also were rising:
- "born to" was padding (παῖδες Ἀλκινόοιο is a plain genitive); the close
  now echoes 110, as ἂν δʼ ἔσταν echoes ἂν δʼ ἵσταντο.

**08.173**  ἐρχόμενον δʼ ἀνὰ ἄστυ θεὸν ὣς εἰσορόωσιν.
- OLD: then, when he walks through the city, they look at the man as a god there.
- NEW: then, as he passes along through the city, they gaze on him, godlike.
- "the man" was not in the Greek; θεὸν ὣς εἰσορόωσιν closes on the poem's
  recurring adjective.

**08.192**  λᾶος ὑπὸ ῥιπῆς· ὁ δʼ ὑπέρπτατο σήματα πάντων
- OLD: Past every mark it flew, lightly coursing away from his hand there.
- NEW: Lightly it flew from his hand, and it coursed past all of the markers.

**08.200**  χαὶρων, οὕνεχʼ ἑταῖρον ἐνηέα λεῦσσʼ ἐν ἀγῶνι.
- OLD: felt great joy, for a loyal companion appeared on the field there.
- NEW: felt great joy, since he saw in the contest a kindly companion.
- λεῦσσε ("saw") restored as the finite verb; ἐν ἀγῶνι is "in the contest,"
  the poem's own word, not "on the field"; ἑταῖρον ἐνηέα lands the close.

**08.255**  οἰσέτω, ἥ που κεῖται ἐν ἡμετέροισι δόμοισιν.
- OLD: all of the clear-voiced lyre, which lies in the palace somewhere there.”
- NEW: all of the clear-voiced lyre, which somewhere lies in our palace.”
- που moved earlier in the clause; ἡμετέροισι δόμοισι ("our palace") closes
  Alcinous's speech.

**08.256**  ὣς ἔφατʼ Ἀλκίνοος θεοείκελος, ὦρτο δὲ κῆρυξ
- OLD: Thus spoke godlike Alcinous; then the herald arose there,
- NEW: Thus spoke godlike Alcinous; then up rose the herald,
- ὦρτο δὲ κῆρυξ keeps its verb-subject order in the English inversion.

**08.261**  κῆρυξ δʼ ἐγγύθεν ἦλθε φέρων φόρμιγγα λίγειαν
- OLD: Then the herald approached and carried the clear-voiced lyre there
- NEW: Then the herald came near and carried the clear-voiced lyre
- Licensed masculine ending #1: the lyre is what the whole passage has been
  waiting for since 254; closing the line on it is emphasis, not accident
  (the pilot's warrant for 21.31's bow). Enjambs to "for Demodocus" as before.

**08.271**  Ἥλιος, ὅ σφʼ ἐνόησε μιγαζομένους φιλότητι.
- OLD: Helios, Sun, had seen the two gods as together they lay there.
- NEW: Helios, Sun, had seen the two of them joined in their loving.
- μιγαζομένους φιλότητι recovered ("joined in their loving"); the referents
  are established, so "the two of them" suffices.

**08.277**  βῆ ῥʼ ἴμεν ἐς θάλαμον, ὅθι οἱ φίλα δέμνιʼ ἔκειτο,
- OLD: into the chamber he went, where his own marriage bed had been placed there;
- NEW: into the chamber he went, where his own dear bed was lying;
- ἔκειτο restored ("was lying"); οἱ φίλα δέμνια is "his own dear bed" —
  "marriage" was an English addition (the marriage bed is already named
  at 269–270), and cutting it lets the line scan clean.

**08.305**  σμερδαλέον δʼ ἐβόησε, γέγωνέ τε πᾶσι θεοῖσιν·
- OLD: Crying aloud in a terrible voice, he called all the gods there:
- NEW: Crying aloud in a terrible voice, he called all the immortals:
- πᾶσι θεοῖσιν rendered as at 21.203 ("all the immortals"), which lands the
  trochaic close before the speech.

**08.324**  θηλύτεραι δὲ θεαὶ μένον αἰδοῖ οἴκοι ἑκάστη.
- OLD: Yet all the goddesses stayed in their homes from a sense of the shame there.
- NEW: Yet all of the goddesses, held back by shame, stayed each in her dwelling.
- οἴκοι ἑκάστη ("each at her home") moved to the close; αἰδοῖ kept mid-line.

**08.380**  ἑστεῶτες κατʼ ἀγῶνα, πολὺς δʼ ὑπὸ κόμπος ὀρώρει.
- OLD: young men struck out the time at the ring, and an uproar arose there.
- NEW: young men struck out the time at the ring, and a great din was rising.
- πολύς recovered ("great"); "and," not "while," since 379 already opens
  its clause with "while all the other."

**08.426**  ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ πυρὶ χαλκὸν ἰήνατε, θέρμετε δʼ ὕδωρ,
- OLD: Heat a great cauldron of bronze on the fire, so the water is warmed there,
- NEW: Heat a great cauldron of bronze on the fire, warming the water,
- θέρμετε δʼ ὕδωρ kept as a coordinate action via the participle rather
  than the old locative tag; flows into 427's "so that the stranger…"

**08.453**  τόφρα δέ οἱ κομιδή γε θεῷ ὣς ἔμπεδος ἦεν.
- OLD: while in her home he had always been tended the same as a god there.
- NEW: while all that time he was tended steadily, just as a god is.
- τόφρα ("all that time") and ἔμπεδος ("steadily") both restored; "in her
  home" was not in the Greek and duplicated 452's "the home of Calypso."

## Tally

- 19 filler-final lines reworked; 0 now end in unmotivated *there/now/then*;
  no keeper needed a Greek warrant.
- 1 masculine ending licensed (08.261 "the clear-voiced lyre"), within the
  2–3/book budget.
- Formula echoes kept: 110/118 (ἂν δʼ ἵσταντο / ἂν δʼ ἔσταν → "were rising");
  173/453 (θεὸν ὥς / θεῷ ὥς); 305 aligned with 21.203's πᾶσι θεοῖσιν;
  "the clear-voiced lyre" verbatim throughout.
- Fidelity recovered along the way: λεῦσσε and ἐν ἀγῶνι at 200, ἔκειτο at 277,
  μιγαζομένους φιλότητι at 271, πολύς at 380, τόφρα and ἔμπεδος at 453,
  θέρμετε δʼ ὕδωρ at 426; secondary padding cut at 108 ("athletic"),
  118 ("born to"), 173 ("the man"), 277 ("marriage"), 453 ("in her home").
- Honest costs incurred: 271's "the two gods" reduced to "the two of them"
  (referents established); 426's second imperative carried by a participle;
  305 says "immortals" for θεοῖσιν (pilot precedent).
- Not in scope: nine Book 08 lines end in *here* (27, 28, 98, 147, 160, 164,
  222, 312, 572); they were not in the extraction and are left for a future
  pass — several look like genuine filler.

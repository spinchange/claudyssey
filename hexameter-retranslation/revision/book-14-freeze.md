## Job 1 — speech-formula standardization

**14.48** ὣς εἰπὼν κλισίηνδʼ ἡγήσατο δῖος ὑφορβός,
- OLD: So he spoke, and the noble swineherd led him on to the shelter,
- NEW: So having spoken, the noble swineherd led him to the shelter,
- ὣς εἰπών, not ὣς φάτο: the old line flattened the participial formula into the finite
  one. "on" was the padding that the shorter opening had left room for.

**14.72** ὣς εἰπὼν ζωστῆρι θοῶς συνέεργε χιτῶνα,
- OLD: So he spoke, and swiftly he tightened his tunic close with a girdle,
- NEW: So having spoken, he swiftly tightened his tunic with a girdle,
- Same flattening fixed. "close" was filler doubling συνέεργε; cutting it pays for the
  longer participial opening exactly.

**14.418** ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας κέασε ξύλα νηλέϊ χαλκῷ,
- OLD: So then he spoke, and with pitiless bronze he was splitting the kindling.
- NEW: So having spoken, with pitiless bronze he was splitting the kindling.
- ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας is the participial family, so it takes the participial rendering, not a
  "So then he spoke" variant of the finite one.

**14.518** ὣς εἰπὼν ἀνόρουσε, τίθει δʼ ἄρα οἱ πυρὸς ἐγγὺς
- OLD: So saying, he sprang to his feet, and set him a bed by the firelight,
- NEW: So having spoken, he sprang up and set him a bed by the firelight,
- "So saying" was a third variant of the participial formula; standardized. ἀνόρουσε is
  simply "sprang up," so "to his feet" was expansion and its removal funds the opening.

## Job 2 — here-final sweep

**14.66** ὡς καὶ ἐμοὶ τόδε ἔργον ἀέξεται, ᾧ ἐπιμίμνω.
- OLD: just as increase is given to this work of mine, where I stay here.
- NEW: just as increase is given to this work of mine, which I labor at.
- No deictic in the Greek. ᾧ ἐπιμίμνω is "at which I abide/persist" — a relative of the
  *work*, not of a place, so "which I labor at" is both truer and filler-free.

**14.124** ἀλλʼ ἄλλως κομιδῆς κεχρημένοι ἄνδρες ἀλῆται
- OLD: Rather, men wanting support, mere vagabonds roaming about here,
- NEW: Rather, men wanting support, mere vagabonds ever roaming,
- No deictic. ἀλῆται is "wanderers" with no place attached; "about here" was pure
  metrical filler. "ever" carries the iterative force the participle already implies.

**14.169** μίμνησκʼ· ἦ γὰρ θυμὸς ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν ἐμοῖσιν
- OLD: do not remind me of these things. My heart in my breast here
- NEW: do not remind me of these things. For the heart in my bosom
- No deictic; "here" was filler after an already-locative "in my breast." ἦ γάρ is the
  causal connective the old line dropped, restored as "For," which pays for the cut.

**14.188** ὁπποίης τʼ ἐπὶ νηὸς ἀφίκεο· πῶς δέ σε ναῦται
- OLD: Where are your parents? On what kind of ship did you come here?
- NEW: Where are your parents? On what kind of vessel did you journey?
- No deictic. ἀφίκεο is "you arrived"; the destination is supplied by the next verse
  ("to Ithaca"), so "here" was both filler and a duplication. "vessel"/"journey" gives
  the trochaic close by reordering, not padding.

**14.407** νῦν δʼ ὥρη δόρποιο· τάχιστά μοι ἔνδον ἑταῖροι
- OLD: But now it is time for supper; and quickly my comrades within here
- NEW: But now it is time for supper; and quickly indoors my companions
- ἔνδον was already rendered by "within"; "here" was a second locative on top of it.
  Moving the adverb forward as "indoors" lets "companions" close the line trochaically.

**14.462** κέκλυθι νῦν, Εὔμαιε καὶ ἄλλοι πάντες ἑταῖροι,
- OLD: “Listen to me now, Eumaeus, and all you companions besides here;
- NEW: “Listen to me now, Eumaeus, and all you other companions;
- No deictic. "besides here" rendered ἄλλοι loosely and then padded; "other" is ἄλλοι
  exactly, and "companions" supplies the falling close.

**14.516** αὐτός τοι χλαῖνάν τε χιτῶνά τε εἵματα δώσει,
- OLD: he himself will give you a cloak and a tunic to wear here,
- NEW: he himself will give you a cloak and a tunic for clothing,
- No deictic. εἵματα ("garments") is a word the old line had dropped in favor of the
  filler; "for clothing" restores it and closes the verse unstressed.

**14.161** τοῦδʼ αὐτοῦ λυκάβαντος ἐλεύσεται ἐνθάδʼ Ὀδυσσεύς.
- KEPT: Within this very round of the year Odysseus will come here.
- ἐνθάδε is in the Greek, and it is the point of the prophecy — Odysseus will come
  *to this very place*. Warranted; not filler.

## Residual "Thus" openings (justified)

**14.137** ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθʼ ἀπόλωλε, φίλοισι δὲ κήδεʼ ὀπίσσω
- KEPT: Thus he has perished away from us there, and behind him has left grief
- Summative ὣς ὁ μέν, not a speech formula. (Its "there" is mid-line and renders ἔνθʼ,
  which is in the Greek.)

**14.409** ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον,
- KEPT: Thus they were speaking such things in turn to one another.
- Summative ὣς οἱ μέν. This verse is a repeated formula whose English is already
  verbatim-identical at 17.166, 20.172, and 20.240; changing it here alone would break
  an echo the Greek itself makes. Rendered "Thus" consistently poem-wide for this
  construction.

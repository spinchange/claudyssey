# The Odyssey in English hexameter

This folder is an experiment in translating Homer into an English analogue of
dactylic hexameter. [All 24 books](#contents) are complete: Books 1–4 form the
Telemachy, Books 5–8 form the Homecoming of Odysseus, Books 9–12 form the
Great Wanderings, and Books 13–24 bring Odysseus home to Ithaca. The original
[proem test](book-01-proem.md), lines 1–10, is retained with its translation
notes.

## Metrical approach

Homer's meter is quantitative: each of its six feet is organized by syllable
length. English does not use syllable length in the same way, so this version
uses stress as its audible principle. Each line has six falling beats. Dactylic
feet (one stressed syllable followed by two unstressed syllables) predominate;
contracted feet and spondaic weight are allowed, especially toward pauses, and
the final foot is normally shortened.

The governing pattern is therefore:

`— u u | — u u | — u u | — u u | — u u | — x`

This is an accentual counterpart to Homeric hexameter, not a claim that English
can reproduce ancient Greek quantity. The other constraints for the experiment
are one English verse for each Greek verse, no rhyme, and enough syntactic
freedom for the six-beat movement to sound spoken rather than mechanical.

## Voice rule

The gods never use lexical contractions in dialogue: *I am*, *you will*, and
*cannot*, for example, rather than *I'm*, *you'll*, or *can't*. Mortals and the
narrator may use contractions where character, register, and meter call for
them. Metrical contraction—the use of a shorter foot—is a separate matter and
remains available in every voice.

## Fable audio

The `audio-openai-fable` directory contains the OpenAI Fable narration at
0.96 speed, using a single measured epic voice and the project pronunciation
map. Individual files are under `books/`; the continuous listening volumes are
`telemachy-books-01-04.mp3`, `homecoming-books-05-08.mp3`, and
`great-wanderings-books-09-12.mp3`, and
`odysseus-on-ithaca-books-13-24.mp3`. The latter two volumes are also kept
under `books/` beside their component files.

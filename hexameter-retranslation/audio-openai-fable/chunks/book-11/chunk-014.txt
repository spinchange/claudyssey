Tityus also I saw, the child of the glorious Earth-goddess,
lying extended along the ground over nine roods in measure;
vultures, two of them, perched on the sides of his body and tore at
both sides of his liver, their beaks in the caul, while he could not repel them.
This was because he assaulted Zeus’s honored consort, the goddess
Leto, when she went through lovely-dancing Panopeus toward Pytho.

Tantalus also I saw, enduring a terrible torment,
standing in water, the pool lapping up to the point of his chin there;
suffering thirst, he could not take up even one drop for drinking.
Every time that the old man bent in his eagerness, hoping
he might drink, all the water was swallowed away and had vanished;
black earth showed at his feet, made dry by a god in the sunlight.
High-leafed trees above him were pouring their fruit by his forehead,
pears and pomegranates and apple trees shining with fruit in abundance,
figs in their sweetness and olives that flourished in fullest profusion.
Every time that the old man stretched out his hands to take hold of
these, a wind would toss them away toward the dark-shadowed cloud banks.

Sisyphus also I saw, enduring a terrible torment,
grappling a monstrous stone in his arms and attempting to raise it;
bracing his body and straining with powerful hands and with both feet,
upward he pushed the stone to the summit; but every time he
nearly was going to thrust it across, its terrible weight turned,
rolling the shameless boulder again to the plain at the hill-foot.
Then he would struggle again and would push, while sweat from his members
streamed, and the dust from the labor arose from his head in a cloud there.

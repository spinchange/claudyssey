“Therefore never be gentle yourself, even dealing with your wife;

never reveal every word that you know and disclose it before her,
but tell part of the story, and let the remainder be hidden.

Yet no murder will come to Odysseus ever from woman;

far too wise and intelligent-minded within is the daughter
born to Icarius, prudent and thoughtful Penelope, your wife.

Young as a bride was the woman we left when we went to the battle;

there was a boy at her breast, no more than an infant, who doubtless
now takes his seat among men and is counted among them, a fortunate
child!

For the father who loves him will see him on coming to homeland,
and he will lovingly clasp his father, the fitting tradition.

But my own wife never allowed me to fill both my eyes there,
looking upon my child, before she destroyed me, his father.

One other thing I will tell you;

remember and store it within you:

secretly, never in public, bring your ship into your own
cherished country, since women can never be trusted hereafter.

Come now, tell me this other, and speak with the truth as I ask you:

have you heard whether somewhere my son still dwells with the living,
either in Orchomenus or perhaps in the sandy-soiled Pylos,
or with the lord Menelaus in wide-spreading Sparta?

Surely the noble Orestes has not yet died on the earth-land.”
Thus did he speak, and I answered the spirit and said in response then:

“Atreus’ son, why question me this?

I know no report of him,
whether he lives or has died;

empty words are an evil to utter.”
So the two of us stood exchanging our sorrowful language,
grieving deeply in heart and letting the great tears fall from us.

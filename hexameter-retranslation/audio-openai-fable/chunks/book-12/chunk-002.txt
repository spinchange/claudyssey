Everything thus we attended.

Our coming from Hades did not pass
Circe’s notice;

at once she prepared herself, quickly approaching,
bringing along her maids, who carried an ample provision
both of the bread and the meat and the fire-bright wine in abundance.

Standing among us, she spoke as the radiant one among goddesses:

“Hardy men, you went living below to the house of the death-god,
dying two times, when the rest of mankind only dies a single time.

Come now, eat of your food and partake of the wine that is waiting,
here as the day travels onward.

When Dawn shows herself tomorrow,
then you shall sail, and I shall point out your road and explain each
thing, so no ruinous scheme may destroy you and make you encounter
terrible grief in the sea or upon the dry land in your journey.”
Thus did she speak, and our proud hearts consented and yielded within us.

So through the whole of the day till the light of the sun had descended,
feasting we sat upon meat beyond counting and sweet wine beside it.

But when the sun went down and the sacred darkness had gathered,
all of the men lay down by the sternward cables to slumber.

She took hold of my hand and apart from the faithful companions
made me sit down, and she lay by my side and inquired of each detail;

I in its due and proper order recounted the whole tale.

Then lady Circe arose and addressed me with words of instruction:

“All these events have been brought to completion;

now listen and hear me,
and an immortal himself will recall every word to your memory.

First you will come to the Sirens, whose music bewitches all people,
every human who travels the sea and arrives in their country.

Whoever approaches unwittingly there and gives ear to the Sirens’
voices will never again find his wife and his innocent children
standing beside him in joy when he comes to the house of his fathers.

No:

the clear-toned song of the Sirens will hold and enchant him,
sitting at ease in their meadow, and round them a great heap of bones lies,
bones of the rotting men with their skins shriveling round them.

Drive the ship past;

you must knead honey-sweet wax in your fingers,
stopping the ears of the faithful companions, lest any should hear them.

Yet you yourself may listen, if that is the thing you desire:

let them bind both your hands and your feet in the swift-moving vessel,
upright against the mast-step, with ends of the rope round the mast-tree,
so you may hear the voices of both Sirens and take your delight there.

If you entreat your companions and order the men to release you,
then let the men bind you down with a greater number of bonds there.

After your comrades have driven the vessel beyond them, I shall not
tell you completely from there which road you must choose of the two ways.

You must weigh every choice in the heart that is beating within you;

yet I will give an account of both, so that you can consider.

On the one side are the overhanging rocks, and against them
roars the tremendous swell of Amphitrite, the dark-eyed sea-queen;

all of the blessed immortals call these the Wandering Rocks there.

That way even the winged things cannot pass through, nor the timid
doves that bring ambrosia upward to father Zeus’s dwelling;

always the vertical rock takes one from the line as they pass it,
then the great Father sends one more bird, so their number remains full.

That way never has any man’s ship succeeded in passing;

pieces of ships and the bodies of men are together conveyed there,
carried away by the salt-water waves and the storms of destructive fire.

Only one sea-running vessel has ever gone safely between them,

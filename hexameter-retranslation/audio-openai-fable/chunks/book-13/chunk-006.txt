Tell me this truly as well, so that I may know it for certain:

what land is this, what people, and what men are native within it?

Is it some island clear in the sky, or else is it headland
sloping down to the sea from the rich-soiled face of the mainland?”
Then in return the gray-eyed goddess Athena addressed him:

“You are a simple man, O stranger, or else have come from far off,
if indeed you inquire about this land.

It is not at all so
nameless as that;

indeed there are very many who know it,
both all who dwell toward the dawn and the rising sun’s splendor,
and all who dwell on the farther side toward the mist-shrouded twilight.

Rugged indeed it is, and no land for the driving of horses,
yet not exceedingly poor, though in no way broad it is fashioned.

For within it grain beyond measure is grown, and within it is wine too,
and the rain is always upon it, and flourishing dewdrops.

It is good for the grazing of goats and the grazing of cattle;

and woodland
of every kind is there, and springs that run through all seasons.

Therefore, stranger, the name of Ithaca reaches even to Troyland,
though they say it lies very far from the land of Achaea.”
So she spoke, and the much-enduring brilliant Odysseus rejoiced then,
glad in the land of his fathers, as Pallas Athena had told him,
daughter of Zeus who bears the aegis, the mighty protector.

Then he addressed her and spoke out winged words in answer;

yet he did not speak what was true, but held back the story,
ever revolving within his breast his profit-devising intelligence:

“I had heard of Ithaca even in spacious Crete-land,
far over the sea;

and now I myself have come to it,
bringing these treasures with me, and I left as many behind there
for my children, and now I flee, since I killed the beloved son of
Idomeneus, Orsilochus swift of foot, who in spacious
Crete used to surpass all livelihood-winning men by his swiftness,
because he intended to rob me of all the plunder of Troyland,
plunder for which I endured many griefs in the depth of my spirit,
passing through wars among men and the grievous waves of the sea too,
because I was not willing to serve and oblige his father
there in the land of the Trojans, but commanded my own companions.

Him I struck as he came from the fields with my bronze-shod spear-point,
waiting in ambush near to the road with one of my comrades.

Very dark night possessed the sky, and no man among mortals
noticed us there, and unseen I stripped the life from within him.

But when at last I had killed him by means of the pitiless bronze blade,
straightway I went to a ship of the noble and stately Phoenicians,
and I entreated them, and I gave them heart-pleasing plunder.

I instructed them then to set me ashore at Pylos
or in holy Elis, where the Epeians hold their dominion.

But the force of the wind drove them away from that course after leaving,
greatly against their will, for they did not desire to deceive me.

Driven from there in our wandering, we came to this place in the nighttime.

Eagerly into the harbor we rowed, and none among us then
had any thought of supper, although we were longing to take it,
but simply disembarked from the ship and all of us lay there.

There upon me sweet sleep came over me, weary and spent with my labors,
while they took all my goods from the well-fashioned hollow ship’s hold
and laid them down where I myself on the sand had been sleeping.

Then they embarked and departed for Sidon, well-peopled and thriving,
while I was left behind there in sorrow, my heart full of anguish.”
So he spoke, and the goddess gray-eyed Athena smiled on him,

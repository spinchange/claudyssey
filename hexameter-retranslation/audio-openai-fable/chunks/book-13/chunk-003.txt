“Farewell, my queen, continually, until old age comes upon you
and death, the things that stand over all mortal human creatures.

But I am going home;

may you take delight in this household,
in your children and people, and in Alcinous the king there.”
Having spoken thus, noble Odysseus stepped over the threshold.

And with him the strength of Alcinous sent forth the herald
to guide him on to the swift ship and down to the shore of the sea there.

And then Arete sent attendant women to go there beside him:

one of them bearing a mantle well washed and a tunic,
and another she told to convey the close-fitted coffer with them;

and the third one carried provisions, both bread and ruddy wine there.

But when they had come to the ship and the water of ocean,
then the proud escorts quickly received all these things in the vessel,
and set them away in the hollow ship, all the food and the drink there.

Then for Odysseus they spread out a cloak and a linen cover
on the deck-planks of the hollow ship, so that there he might slumber,
still without waking, at stern;

and he too climbed aboard and lay down
silently, while they sat at the thole-pins each in his order,
and loosed the cable away from the pierced anchoring stone there.

And when they had leaned back, they kept threshing the sea with the oar-blades,
and over his eyelids sweet sleep descended and settled,
unbroken, sweetest of all, and nearest in likeness to death.

And as on a plain a team of four stallions, all males together,
all at once spring forward urged on by strokes of the whip-thong,
lifting themselves up high, and swiftly accomplish the roadway,
so then her stern kept lifting, while after her surged a great wave,
dark-purple, huge, of the loud-resounding and many-voiced seaway.

Yet she ran very steadily, firm and secure;

not even a hawk there,
no, not a falcon, would keep pace with her, lightest of flyers.

So she, running swiftly, kept cleaving the swells of the sea there,
bearing a man whose counsels were like to the counsels of immortals,
who before had suffered indeed very many griefs in his spirit,
testing the wars of men and the grievous and wearisome billows,
but at that time he slept without stir, having forgotten his sorrows.

When that star had arisen, the brightest by far among all stars,
which beyond all others comes on as herald of Dawn the early-born,
then at that hour the seafaring ship was nearing the island.

There is a harbor of Phorcys, the old man, the ancient of ocean,
in the land of Ithaca;

and in it two jutting headlands
stand there sheer, breaking away, and bend toward the mouth of the harbor;

these keep outside the great wave down, which the stormy winds raise there,
while within well-benched ships remain without any mooring,
once they have reached the measure and bounds of the anchoring station.

Now at the head of the harbor there stands a long-leafed olive,
and close beside it a lovely cave, dim and mist-filled,
sacred to nymphs whom people indeed call the Naiad maidens.

In it are mixing-bowls and jars with handles on both sides,
fashioned of stone;

and there as well the bees store honey.

In it too are long looms of stone, where the nymphs are weaving
sea-purple cloths, a wonder and marvel for any to look on.

In it are springs ever flowing;

and two doors belong to it also.

One turns toward the North Wind, and men may descend by that entry,
but the other faces the South, and is more divine;

by that pathway
men do not enter at all, for it is the road of immortals.

There then they brought her inside, for they knew the place from olden time;

and
she ran onto shore as far as the half of her whole length,
hurrying strongly;

for such was the drive from the hands of the rowers.

But from the harbor he climbed by a rough and trackless footpath,
over the wooded land by the heights, where Pallas Athena
had shown him the noble swineherd, the man who most deeply
cared for the goods and estate that noble Odysseus had gathered.

There in the forecourt he found him seated, where round him a farmyard
had been built high up, in a place with a wide view about it,
fair and spacious and broad with a running way;

and the swineherd
built it himself for the swine of his lord who had gone from the island,
with no help from the mistress nor help from old lord Laertes,
built it of quarried stones and topped it with thorn of wild pearwood;

and outside he had driven stakes all around on both sides there,
thick-set and crowded, split from the dark heartwood of the oak tree.

Inside the yard he had fashioned a dozen pens for the swine there,
close by one another, as bedding and lairs for the she-swine;

and in each pen fifty sows that lay upon the ground there
had been enclosed, breeding females;

the boars slept outside them,
many fewer in number, for these were consumed and diminished
by the godlike suitors at feasts, since ever the swineherd
sent them the best of the well-fattened hogs of the drove there;

and the boars were in number three hundred and sixty together.

Beside them four hounds, like savage beasts, always were sleeping,
dogs that the swineherd had reared, that captain and master of servants.

He himself was fitting and binding sandals under his own feet,
cutting the leather from hide of an ox, well cured and shining.

The others had gone, each man in a different way, with the gathered
swine, the three;

but the fourth he had sent to the city perforce there,
driving a hog to the arrogant suitors against his own wishing,
so that, after the slaughter, their hearts might be glutted with meat there.

Suddenly then the loud-baying dogs caught sight of Odysseus;

these with barking and yelping rushed straight upon him;

but Odysseus
sat himself down in his craft, and his staff fell loose from his hand there.

There by his own stockyard he would have endured a disgraceful
wound and affliction, but quickly the swineherd ran after them,
darting through the foredoor in haste, and the hide dropped out of his hand there.

Shouting aloud, he drove off the dogs, each one in a different
direction, pelting them hard with a thick rain of stones, and he told him:

“Old man, the dogs came close indeed to tearing and mangling
you all at once, and on me you would have poured down shame and reproach there.

Yet on me the gods have bestowed other griefs and lamentings;

for I sit here grieving and mourning a godlike master,
while for other men I tend and fatten the tusked swine for eating;

and he somewhere wanders in want of food, in his longing,
over the people and city of men who speak alien language,
if indeed he still lives and still looks on the light of the sunlight.

But come now, follow me, old sir, and let us go to the shelter,
so that you too, when your spirit has taken its fill of food and wine there,
may tell me what land is your own and what troubles you have suffered.”
So he spoke, and the noble swineherd led him on to the shelter,
brought him within and sat him down, and he heaped up thick brushwood,
and over it spread the hide of a shaggy and wild mountain goat there,
where he himself used to sleep, large and furry;

and glad was Odysseus
that he received him thus, and he spoke and addressed him by saying:

“May Zeus grant to you, stranger, and all the other immortals,
that thing which above all else you desire, since you welcomed me kindly.”

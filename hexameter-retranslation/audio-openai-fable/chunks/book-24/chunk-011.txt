“Hold from the painful war, men of Ithaca, hold now,
so that with no more bloodshed you may be parted most quickly.”
So spoke Athena, and upon them all pale fear seized hold then;

from their hands, in their terror, their weapons all flew and were scattered,
and they all fell down on the ground when the goddess had uttered her outcry;

then they turned toward the city, desiring the safety of living.

Terribly shouted aloud then godlike Odysseus, long-suffering,
and with a rush he swooped like an eagle that flies in the high air.

Then indeed the son of Cronos let fly his smoky thunderbolt,
and it fell there in front of the gray-eyed daughter of the mighty sire.

Then to Odysseus spoke again gray-eyed Athena:

“Zeus-born son of Laertes, Odysseus of many devices,
hold now, and cease from the strife of the war that makes equals of all men,
lest in some way Zeus son of Cronos, wide-thundering, be angered.”
So spoke Athena, and he obeyed, and his heart was made glad there.

Then thereafter she set a sworn peace between both of the parties,
Pallas Athena, the child of Zeus who bears the aegis,
like to Mentor in build and in voice altogether.

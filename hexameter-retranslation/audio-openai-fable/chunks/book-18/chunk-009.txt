Come then, feast well, and go lie down at home when the spirit commands you;

as for me, I am driving no man away from the house here.”
Thus he spoke, and they all bit hard on their lips in amazement,
marveling much at Telemachus, how boldly indeed he had spoken.

Then among them Amphinomus rose in assembly and answered,
glorious son of Nisus, descended from lordly Aretias:

“Friends, let no man then be angry because of a word that is righteous,
answering back with hostile speech and with bitter contention.

Do not buffet the stranger about, nor any other servant
dwelling here in the halls of godlike Odysseus our master.

Come, let the wine-pourer begin with the cups and the first-poured libation,
so that, after pouring, we may lie down and go to our houses.

As for the stranger, let us leave him here in the halls of Odysseus
for Telemachus to care for, since to his dear house he has come here.”
Thus he spoke, and the speech that he uttered was pleasing to all them.

Then the hero Moulius mixed in the bowl for the company gathered,
herald from Dulichium, and servant he was to Amphinomus.

Then he served it to all men in order;

and they to the gods there
poured to the blessed immortals, then drank the honey-sweet wine up.

But when they had poured and had drunk as much as the heart had desired there,
then they went to go lie down, each man to his own house returning.

“Well have you spoken, beloved old friend, and it now is appropriate
young Telemachus follows your word, for that course is far better.
Let him accompany you and then sleep in the halls of your household.
I shall return to the black-colored ship and attend to the comrades,
strengthening all of their spirits and giving them each their instructions.
I may claim to be there the only man who is elder;
all of the others are younger companions who follow from friendship,
each of them equal in age to the great-hearted young Telemachus.
There let me lie for the night by the hollow and black-colored vessel.
Then in the morning I go to the great-hearted Cauconian people,
where an old debt is still owed to me, neither small nor recent.
Since this young man has now come to your house, you must send him hereafter,
riding a chariot, joined by a son of your own; and provide him
horses that run with the lightest of feet and are strongest in labor.”

Thus having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed,
taking the form of a sea eagle; all who beheld it were startled.
Nestor the old man marveled at what his own eyes had discovered.
Taking Telemachus' hand, he addressed him aloud by his name there:

“Dear friend, no fear is in me you shall ever prove coward or worthless,
if as a man still so young you have gods to attend and escort you.
This was no other of all the immortals who dwell on Olympus,
no, but the daughter of Zeus, most glorious Tritogeneia,
she who among all the Argives gave honor to noble Odysseus.
Queen, be propitious, and grant excellent glory and fame now,
both upon me and my children and also my reverent bedmate.
I in return shall provide you a broad-browed heifer, a yearling,
never subdued, one no man has led underneath the constraining
yoke; I shall offer her, pouring the gold all around on her horn-tips.”

The Odyssey — Book 6

Nausicaa at the river; the naked stranger; the road to the city.

So there the steadfast and noble Odysseus rested in slumber,
conquered by sleep and exhaustion. Meanwhile the goddess Athena
went to the land and the city that held the Phaeacian people,
those who in earlier days made their dwelling in spacious Hypereia,
close to the Cyclopes, arrogant men who continually raided,
since the Cyclopes surpassed the Phaeacians greatly in power.
Godlike Nausithous uprooted and led them away from that country,
settling them in Scheria, far from the bread-eating peoples.
Round the city he drove a wall and erected the houses,
built the temples of gods, and divided the plowlands among them.
Long since, broken by destiny, he had gone to the household of Hades;
now Alcinous governed with counsels inspired by godhead.
Into his house came the gray-eyed goddess Athena,
there to devise the return of the great-hearted lord Odysseus.
Into a richly constructed chamber she entered, where one young
maiden was sleeping, resembling immortals in body and beauty,
Nausicaa, the daughter of great-hearted lord Alcinous.
Two slave women were sleeping beside her, with beauty from Graces,
each at a post of the doorway, and shining doors closed before them.
Like a breath of the wind she swept to the bed of the maiden,
stood at the head of her bed and addressed her and gave her instruction,
taking the bodily form of the daughter of Dymas, the famous
shipman, a girl of her age and exceedingly dear to her spirit.
Taking her likeness, the gray-eyed goddess Athena addressed her:
“Nausicaa, how did your mother give birth to a child so unmindful?
All your resplendent clothing is lying uncared for around you,
though your own marriage is near, when you need beautiful garments,
both for yourself and to give to the people who lead the procession.
From such possessions an excellent reputation arises
there among people, and both your father and honored mother are gladdened.
Come, let us go to the washing pools at the showing of daylight;
I shall accompany you and shall labor beside you, so sooner
you are prepared, for truly you shall not be long without marriage.
Already all of the best men throughout Phaeacian country
court you, and all of them spring from the same proud lineage as you.
Come then, urge your renowned father before early dawn to provide you
mules and a wagon, so all of the sashes and robes may be carried,
also the shining blankets and covers that need to be washed there.
This is more fitting for you as well than to travel there walking,
since from the city the washing pools lie at a very great distance.”
Thus having spoken, the gray-eyed goddess Athena departed
up to Olympus, where people declare that the seat of immortals
stands unshaken forever by winds and is never made wet by
rain; neither snow draws near, but the clear upper sky without cloud there
stretches around, and a radiant whiteness continually shines there.
There all the blessed gods take their joy every day of existence.
There went the gray-eyed goddess when she had advised the young maiden.
Straightway Dawn on her beautiful throne came upward and wakened
Nausicaa of the beautiful robe, who marveled at dreamland,
passing throughout the house to inform both her father and mother.
both her beloved father and mother she found in the household.
Mother was sitting beside the fire among serving women,
spinning the yarn of a violet sea hue; her father she encountered
there at the door, on his way to the famous rulers assembled
there in the council where all of the lordly Phaeacians called him.
Standing beside her beloved father, she spoke and addressed him:
“Dearest Papa, will you now make ready a wagon to carry,
one raised high upon excellent wheels, so the beautiful garments
lying around me in dirt I may take to the river for washing?
It is appropriate also for you, when among the first rulers
sitting in council, to have clean clothing around your body.
Five well-beloved sons have been born to you here in the palace,
two of them married, while three are flourishing men without marriage;
always they wish to have clothes newly washed and clean on their bodies
when they go out to the dance, and all these concerns fill my thinking.”
Thus did she speak, for she shrank from naming her marriage in fullness
there to her well-loved father; but he understood it and answered:
“Child, I do not begrudge you the mules or whatever you ask for.
Go, and the slaves shall make a fine wagon prepared for the journey,
one with a body raised high, good wheels, and a rack fitted over.”
Thus did he speak, and he called to the slaves, who at once gave obedience.
Out in the courtyard they made the smoothly running mule wagon,
bringing the mules underneath and securing them under the harness.
Then from her chamber the maiden carried the beautiful garments,
placing them all in the wagon’s elaborately polished body.
Mother prepared and filled up a chest with the heartening food there,
every variety, laying delicacies and pouring wine into
goatskin container; and then the young maiden climbed into the wagon.
Mother provided the soft olive oil in a vessel of gold-work,
so Nausicaa and all of her serving women might use it after bathing.
Then she took hold of the whip and the reins that were glossy and shining,
striking the mules into running; and clattering loudly their hoofbeats
sounded, and tirelessly onward they strained, bearing clothes and the maiden,
not by herself, for beside her the serving women were walking.
Then, when they came to the beautiful stream of the flowing river,
there where the washing pools never failed and abundance of clear flow
welled from beneath and ran over to cleanse even garments most dirty,
there from the wagon they loosened the mules and removed all the harness,
driving them onward beside the eddying course of the river,
there to graze on the honey-sweet water grass; then from the wagon
armful by armful the garments they carried and laid in dark water,
stamping them briskly in washing trenches and racing each other.
Then, when they thoroughly washed every garment and cleansed it of all dirt,
all they spread out in order along the beach pebbles of sea-way,
there where the waves coming onto the shore washed cleanest the shingle.
Then, when they bathed and had richly anointed their bodies with olive oil,
there by the side of the river they ate their meal and were waiting
while in the radiance sent by the sun all the garments were drying.
Then, when desire for food and for eating was put from their bodies,
they cast off the veils from their heads and played with a ball there,
while white-armed Nausicaa led all the women in singing.
Just as the arrow-showering Artemis goes through the mountains,
either along high Taygetus or on Erymanthus,
taking delight in the boars and the rapidly running red deer there;
nymphs of the wilds, all daughters of Zeus who possesses the aegis,
play there beside her, and Leto is gladdened at heart as she watches.
High over all of them Artemis carries her head and her forehead,
easily known, although every one of the nymphs is a beauty;
so did the unmarried maiden surpass all the women who served her.
Yet when at last she intended to fold all the beautiful garments,
harness the mules once again, and return to the city and household,
then the gray-eyed goddess Athena devised a new counsel,
how great Odysseus might wake and behold the beautiful maiden,
and she might lead him at last to the city that held the Phaeacians.
Then at a serving woman the princess cast the round ball there;
missing the woman, she cast it instead in a deep-whirling eddy,
making them all cry aloud; and illustrious lordly Odysseus
wakened, and sitting upright he pondered within mind and spirit:
“Ah me, now to the country of what mortal people have I come?
Are they insolent people and savage and strangers to justice,
or are they friendly to strangers, with god-fearing hearts in their bodies?
Surely the feminine cry of young maidens has sounded around me,
nymphs who inhabit the steep mountain peaks and the springs of the rivers,
also the grass-covered meadows that lie at the side of the waters.
Or may it be I am close to articulate human beings?
Come then, I shall make trial myself and discover the answer.”
Thus having spoken, from under the thicket illustrious Odysseus
crept, and he broke with a powerful hand from the densely grown woodland
one leafy branch, so he might cover his nakedness like a man should.
Then he advanced like a mountain-bred lion assured of his power,
moving beneath the assault of the rain and the wind, while his two eyes
burn in his head, and he comes against cattle or sheep in the pasture,
even pursuing the wild deer; hunger within him commands him
even to enter a closely built fold and attack all the livestock.
So did Odysseus intend to approach the beautiful-haired girls,
naked although he was, since a terrible need had befallen him.
Dreadful he seemed to the girls, disfigured by brine of the salt sea,
scattering each to a different place on the jutting shore beaches.
Only the daughter of great Alcinous stayed, for Athena
planted boldness within her heart and took fear from her body.
Standing, she faced him and held her ground; but lordly Odysseus
pondered whether to clasp at the knees of the beautiful maiden,
or, while standing apart, to address her with honey-sweet language,
begging that she show the city and also provide him with clothing.
Then, as he pondered, this seemed in his heart to be better and wiser:
standing apart to address her with honey-sweet words of entreaty,
lest the young maiden be angered at heart if he clasped at her knees there.
Straightway then he addressed her with words both honeyed and cunning:
“Queen, I am here at your knees: are you goddess, or are you a mortal?
If you are one of the gods who possess the expanses of heaven,
Artemis, daughter of Zeus, is the goddess to whom I would match you,
nearest in stature and loveliness, nearest in bodily bearing.
But if you are one of the mortals who live on the earth here,
three times blessed are both your father and reverend mother,
three times blessed are your brothers; surely the hearts in their bodies
always are warmed with delight and with pleasure because of your beauty,
watching a sapling like you step gracefully into the dancing.
Yet above all in his heart is that man most greatly exalted
who with his bridal gifts outweighs rivals and leads you homeward.
Never before have my eyes seen anyone equal in beauty,
neither a woman nor man; astonishment takes me in gazing.
Once on the island of Delos, beside the altar of Phoebus,
I saw a young palm tree rising to heaven with beautiful branches;
I had come there as well, and a numerous army attended,
following me on a journey from which many sorrows would reach me.
Just as in seeing the palm I marveled a long time in spirit,
since no such sapling had ever arisen before from the soil there,
so, young woman, I marvel at you and am filled with amazement,
greatly afraid to embrace your knees, though sorrow is on me.
Only yesterday morning, the twentieth day, I escaped from
out of the wine-dark sea; all the time until then I was carried
onward by waves and by violent storms from the island Ogygia.
Now some divinity cast me ashore, perhaps to be troubled
even in this place; I think that my evil shall not yet be ended,
since before that the gods shall fulfill many more of my troubles.
Queen, take pity upon me; to you I have first come directly,
after my terrible labors, and none of the others I know here,
none of the people whose city and country surround me. Now show me
where is the town, and provide me a rag to be thrown round my body,
if in arriving you brought any cloth that had wrapped up the linen.
May all the gods give you everything dear to your heart and your longing,
husband and household, and may they bestow good harmony with them;
nothing is stronger and finer than when, being like-minded together,
husband and wife in the household live; many pains to their haters,
joys to their friends they provide, but they know their happiness best.”
Then in her turn the white-armed Nausicaa answered Odysseus:
“Stranger, since you do not seem to be base or devoid of good judgment,
Zeus who is lord of Olympus himself gives happiness freely,
both to the noble and base, to each as his purpose determines.
He must have given you this; you must bear it, however it happens.
Now, since you come to our country and city, you shall not be lacking
either in clothing or anything else that is due to the hard-worn
suppliant stranger who happens to meet us. I shall show the city,
also shall tell you the name by which all the people are called here.
These are Phaeacians who hold this city and country around it;
I am the daughter of great-hearted lord Alcinous,
upon whom all Phaeacian power and strength are depending.”
Thus did she speak and call out to the beautiful-haired serving women:
“Stop where you are, my attendants! Why do you flee from a mortal?
Surely you do not imagine that this is some enemy warrior?
No mortal man who is living exists, nor ever shall be born,
who could arrive in the land of Phaeacian men bringing warfare;
very dear to the deathless gods are the people who live here.
Far from all others we dwell in the greatly resounding salt water,
out at the edge of the world, and no mortal people come near us.
This man instead is a miserable wanderer, straying among us,
whom we must now tend well, since strangers and beggars are all from
Zeus, and even a small gift given in welcome is precious.
Come, my attendants, provide this stranger with eating and drinking;
bathe him as well in the river wherever the wind cannot reach him.”
Thus did she speak, and they halted and called to each other in answer,
leading Odysseus away to a place with a shelter, according
to the command of Nausicaa, daughter of great Alcinous.
There they laid down a tunic and cloak to provide him with clothing;
also they gave him the smooth olive oil in the golden container,
urging the stranger to bathe in the flowing streams of the river.
Then great Odysseus spoke to the beautiful serving women:
“Stand at a distance, attendants, while I by myself wash the sea-brine
there from my shoulders, and rub olive oil all around me; for truly
oil has been far from my skin for a terribly long span of time now.
Yet in your presence I shall not bathe, since shame would possess me,
standing there naked among young women with beautiful tresses.”
Thus did he speak, and they moved off and told the young maiden his answer.
Then in the river illustrious lordly Odysseus bathed him,
washing the brine from his back and his broad strong shoulders, the sea-crust
rubbed from his head that had come from the barren expanses of ocean.
Then, when he washed himself thoroughly, rubbing with oil all his body,
also had dressed in the clothes the unmarried maiden had given,
then did Athena, the daughter of Zeus, make him taller in stature,
broader to look at as well; and she caused from the head of Odysseus
locks to flow downward in curls like flowers of purple hyacinth.
Just as a skillful man pours gold around silver, a craftsman
taught by Hephaestus and Pallas Athena in every fine artwork,
artworks with manifold grace; so she poured her grace round Odysseus,
over his head and his shoulders. He moved off apart from the women,
sitting upon the sea-shingle, radiant with beauty and favor.
There the young maiden gazed at him long, and she spoke to her fair-haired
serving attendants, addressing the women who waited around her:
“Listen to me, white-armed serving women, to what I shall tell you.
Not without all of the gods who inhabit the heights of Olympus
willing it has this man come here among the Phaeacian people.
Previously, he appeared to my eyes an unworthy companion;
now he resembles the gods who inhabit the expanses of heaven.
If such a man might only be called my husband and live here,
and if it pleased him as well to remain in our country forever!
Come, my attendants, provide this stranger with eating and drinking.”
Thus did she speak, and they listened to her and readily answered,
setting the food and the drink in front of the lord Odysseus.
Then great-hearted and much-enduring lordly Odysseus
ravenously drank and ate, since long he had gone without eating.
Meanwhile white-armed Nausicaa devised something different:
folding the clothes, in the beautiful wagon she placed them in order,
yoked the strong-hoofed mules underneath it, and mounted the wagon.
Then she encouraged Odysseus and spoke, addressing him plainly:
“Rise now, stranger, and go to the city, so I may direct you
there to the house of my prudent father, where I can assure you
you shall become acquainted with all the Phaeacians' finest.
Only do this, since I do not believe you are lacking in judgment.
While we continue among the fields and the works of the people,
follow the mules and the wagon on foot with the serving women,
walking there briskly; I shall be traveling onward before you.
Yet when we come to the city, encircled by high walls around it,
there on each side of the city a beautiful harbor is lying;
narrow the entrance is found, while the curved ships are drawn from the water
up by the road, for every man has a place for his vessel.
There is the meeting place at the beautiful shrine of Poseidon,
tightly constructed of quarried stones set firmly and deeply.
There they attend to the black ships' tackle and all of the rigging,
cables and sails, and they plane all the oars until smooth for the rowing.
No care at all for the bows and the quivers have these Phaeacians,
only for masts and the oars of the ships and the balanced vessels,
crossing in these with delight over all of the gray salt water.
I must avoid their malicious talk, so that none may revile me
later behind me, for insolent men are abundant among them.
One of the baser men, meeting us there, might speak in this manner:
‘Who is this stranger who follows Nausicaa, handsome and mighty?
Where did she find him? Surely she means him to be her husband.
Perhaps she attended a castaway man from a far-sailing vessel,
someone who came from an alien land, since no people are near us;
or a divinity, long entreated, descended to meet her,
coming from heaven in answer, and he shall possess her forever.
Better if she has gone elsewhere herself and discovered a husband,
since she dishonors the people who live in Phaeacian country,
though she has many excellent men among those who would court her.’
So they shall speak, and the gossip would make a reproach against me.
Even myself I would blame any maiden who acted in this way,
who, with her dear father and mother alive and unwilling,
mingled with men before she had come to the day of her marriage.
Stranger, give close attention to what I am telling you, so you
soon may secure from my father an escort and passage for homecoming.
Close to the roadway you shall find a beautiful grove of Athena,
poplars around it, a spring welling up, and a meadow surrounding.
There is my father's estate and his flourishing orchard around it,
lying as far from the city as voice of a shouting man carries.
Sit there and wait for a while, until we who travel before you
enter the city ourselves and arrive at my father's palace.
When you believe we have come to the palace and entered it fully,
then make your way to the city and ask the Phaeacian people
where is the palace belonging to great-hearted lord Alcinous.
Easily it may be known; even an infant could lead you
there by the hand, since none of the houses Phaeacians inhabit
bear any likeness to that noble house of the hero Alcinous.
Then, when the palace and courtyard have covered you there from all eyesight,
hurry across the great hall until you arrive at my mother.
Close to the fire she sits in the light and the warmth of the hearth-flame,
spinning a yarn of the color of sea-purple, wondrous to gaze on,
leaning against a great pillar, her serving women behind her.
Close to her there is my father's chair, reclining against hers;
there he sits drinking his wine every day like an immortal.
Pass by my father and cast both your arms round the knees of my mother,
if you desire to behold the day of your homecoming quickly,
filled with delight, even though your homeland is lying far distant.
If in her heart she determines to think with a kindness about you,
there is a hope that your friends and your family still shall be seen there,
entering into your well-built house in the land of your fathers.”
Thus having spoken, she struck with the shining whip at the mule-team;
rapidly onward they left the stream of the river behind them.
Smoothly they trotted along, and they carried their legs well beneath them;
carefully she kept driving, so all of the walkers might follow,
serving attendants and lord Odysseus, and wisely she lashed them.
Then the sun entered the west, and they came to the glorious grove-land,
sacred to Pallas Athena. There noble Odysseus sat down;
straightway then he prayed to the daughter of Zeus in the open:
“Hear me now, child of Zeus who possesses the aegis, Unwearied.
Hear me now at the last, since you did not hear me before this
when I was broken, when the lord of the earth-shaking waters broke me.
Grant me a welcome of pity and friendship among the Phaeacians.”
Thus did he speak in his prayer, and Pallas Athena had heard him.
Yet she did not appear to him face to face in the grove there;
awe of her father's own brother restrained her, who furiously raged on
against Odysseus the godlike, until he arrived in his homeland.

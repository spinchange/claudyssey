# Note corrections — Book 22 (draft for review)

## 22.1 — the "participle," and "thirteen books" (O22-001-167-A-001 and A-002, both MINOR)

One note, two errors.

**Current** (`translation/book-22.md:513`):
> [^L1]: The book opens on the shedding of the rags — γυμνώθη, "he was stripped bare": the disguise falls in one participle, and the leap to the threshold follows in the same breath. Thirteen books of costume come off in half a line; the arrows pour out at his feet like the truth arriving all at once.

**Proposed:**
> [^L1]: The book opens on the shedding of the rags — γυμνώθη, "he was stripped bare": the disguise falls in a single verb, and the leap to the threshold follows in the same breath. Nine books of costume come off in half a line; the arrows pour out at his feet like the truth arriving all at once.

**Why:** γυμνώθη is a finite aorist, not a participle; and the beggar disguise dates from Book 13 — nine books, not thirteen.

## 22.147–149 — the terror formula "applied for the only time" (O22-001-167-A-010, MODERATE — priority)

**Current** (`translation/book-22.md:545`):

**Proposed:**
> [^L147]: "The knees of Odysseus went slack, and his heart" — the formula of terror-at-death, last applied to him amid the Book 5 storm and landing (5.297, 5.406). The sea's worst fear-line comes ashore here: the plan's single unraveling, armed suitors in his own hall, everything won and suddenly at stake again. "The work now looked great to him" — μέγα ἔργον: the same flat phrase as ever, doing enormous duty.

**Why:** The identical line strikes Odysseus twice in Book 5; the uniqueness claim, and the argument built on it, were false. The land-echo of a sea-formula is the accurate (and still forceful) observation.

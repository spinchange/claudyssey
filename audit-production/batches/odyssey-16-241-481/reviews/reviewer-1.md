# Reviewer 1 Audit — Odyssey 16.241–481

Overall, the translation is strong and frequently vivid without sacrificing the Greek’s tactical and emotional logic. I found two moderate action/number distortions and three minor errors, chiefly in explanatory claims. Fixed-wording-only formula deviations are excluded.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 16.295–298 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.97 | “So we can rush and take them up” makes the action another retrieval of the weapons. The Greek has already specified taking the shields in hand; Odysseus then says they will rush upon and seize or overcome the suitors. The English omits that attack and duplicates the weapon-taking. | `δοιὰ βοάγρια χερσὶν ἑλέσθαι, / ὡς ἂν ἐπιθύσαντες ἑλοίμεθα· τοὺς δέ κ’ ἔπειτα / Παλλὰς Ἀθηναίη θέλξει` | Render the second action against the suitors: “so that, rushing upon them, we may seize/overcome them.” |
| R1-002 | 16.347 and note `[^L347]` | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.88 | “We swore it would never be done” changes a failed prediction into an oath, and the note repeats that unsupported speech act. Eurymachus says only that they said or thought Telemachus would not accomplish the voyage. | `φάμεν δέ οἱ οὐ τελέεσθαι` | Use “we said/thought he would never bring it off”; describe the line as a grudging admission, not an oath. |
| R1-003 | 16.348–350 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.99 | “Those two out there” reduces the ambush party to two men. Greek plural `κείνοις` means the men of the returning ambush ship, a substantial crew, not a dual pair. | `ἐρέτας ἁλιῆας ἀγείρομεν, οἵ κε τάχιστα / κείνοις ἀγγείλωσι` | Use “those men out there” or “the ambush party.” |
| R1-004 | 16.412 (note `[^L412]`) | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.98 | Calling this “the first defection from inside the machine” ignores Medon’s earlier disclosure of the original ambush plot to Penelope in Book 4. This is a repetition of his informative role, not its first occurrence. | Here, `κῆρυξ γάρ οἱ ἔειπε Μέδων, ὃς ἐπεύθετο βουλάς`; Medon already reports the suitors’ hidden counsel at 4.675–679. | Call this Medon’s renewed warning or second disclosure to Penelope; remove the priority claim. |
| R1-005 | 16.449–451 (note `[^L449]`) | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.86 | The note says the weeping-and-sleep refrain “closes her every appearance.” It does recur at several major Penelope night-scenes, but not every appearance: after her Book 18 audience with the suitors, for example, she returns upstairs with their gifts without this refrain. | `κλαῖεν ἔπειτ’ Ὀδυσῆα ... ὄφρα οἱ ὕπνον / ἡδὺν ... βάλε ... Ἀθήνη` is the genuine recurrence here; its distribution is not universal. | Say that the refrain closes several major appearances or recurring night-scenes, rather than every appearance. |

## What the Translation Does Especially Well

1. Lines 241–255 preserve both Telemachus’ reverence for his father and the tactical force of his objection, carrying the detailed head count cleanly into his warning about the price of vengeance.
2. Lines 364–392 render Antinous’ conspiracy with excellent control of its operational detail, political calculation, fear of public exposure, and chilling alternatives of murder or belatedly lawful courtship.
3. Lines 418–448 preserve the full moral architecture of Penelope’s rebuke and Eurymachus’ answer: inherited obligation, violated hospitality, false protection, childhood intimacy, and the narrator’s immediate exposure of his lie.

## Concerns Considered

| Lines | Confidence | Concern | Why not counted |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 16.260 (note `[^L260]`) | 0.59 | “Those two never miss a real fight” universalizes Odysseus’ narrower assurance that Athena and Zeus will not remain apart from this particular battle for long. | The wording reads as rhetorical characterization rather than a literal poem-wide rule, and the immediate claim about divine aid remains accurate. |
| 16.349 | 0.63 | “Seamen for rowers” generalizes `ἐρέτας ἁλιῆας`, which more specifically identifies fishermen recruited as rowers. | Their operative role as an improvised rowing crew is preserved, and the lost occupational specificity has little narrative consequence. |
| 16.471 (note `[^L471]`) | 0.69 | Calling Hermes’ hill a cairn and saying Eumaeus pauses and looks back adds topographical form and actions not expressly narrated. | `Ἕρμαιος λόφος` can plausibly denote a Hermes-marked hill or cairn, and its position supplies a natural harbor vantage point; the precise movement remains interpretive. |
| 16.476 (note `[^L476]`) | 0.67 | “The honorific formerly reserved for Alcinous” may overstate the identity of `ἱερὴ ἲς Τηλεμάχοιο` with Alcinous’ distinct `ἱερὸν μένος` periphrasis. | Both expressions function as “sacred strength” honorifics, so the proposed status comparison is plausible even though the Greek formulas are not identical. |

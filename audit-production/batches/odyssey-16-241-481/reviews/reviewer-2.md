# Reviewer 2 Report

## Overall assessment

This is a vigorous and generally faithful rendering of the tactical planning, the suitors’ failed ambush, Penelope’s confrontation with Antinous, and the quiet return to Eumaeus’ hut. I found four material but localized problems: the attack plan briefly redirects a verb from the suitors to the weapons, an unsupported oath replaces the suitors’ mistaken expectation, a plural ambush party becomes two men, and one note overlooks Medon’s identical earlier warning to Penelope. Fixed-wording-only formula deviations are excluded.

## Especially successful renderings

1. **16.241–269:** Telemachus’ numerical reckoning and the exchange about Athena and Zeus preserve both the impossible scale of the fight and the dry confidence of father and son.
2. **16.363–405:** Antinous’ ruthless political calculation and Amphinomus’ qualified resistance remain lucid across a long, rapidly shifting council scene.
3. **16.417–448:** Penelope’s indictment and Eurymachus’ deceitful reassurance retain their contrasting rhetorical force, culminating effectively in the narrator’s exposure of his concealed intent.

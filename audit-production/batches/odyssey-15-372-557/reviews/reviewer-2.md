# Reviewer 2 Report

## Overall assessment

The translation is consistently clear and generally faithful across Eumaeus’ autobiography and Telemachus’ arrival. It handles the embedded dialogue, changes of scene, commercial vocabulary, and omen sequence with strong control. I found three localized fidelity problems: one line reverses the Phoenicians’ commercial action, and two notes make demonstrably false cross-passage or structural claims. None disrupts the passage’s main narrative.

## Especially successful renderings

1. **15.372–401:** Eumaeus’ account of what the household slaves have lost and his invitation to exchange remembered sorrows preserve the social detail, intimacy, and reflective turn of the Greek.
2. **15.423–443:** The Phoenician woman’s account of her origin and her oath-bound escape plan remains lucid across several shifts of speaker and retains the escalating secrecy of the dialogue.
3. **15.525–538:** The hawk-and-dove omen is rendered with precise spatial staging, and Theoclymenus’ interpretation and Telemachus’ guarded response preserve their distinct rhetorical force.

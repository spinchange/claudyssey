## What the Translation Does Especially Well

1. Lines 372–379 render the household and status argument with unusual precision, preserving all four privileges Eumaeus associates with a favored slave: house, property, a desirable wife, and reciprocal loyalty.
2. Lines 398–401 preserve both sides of the emotional movement—mutual grief in the present and the later pleasure of remembering suffering—without flattening the contrast.
3. Lines 475–483 keep the voyage, the nurse’s sudden death, and Eumaeus’ sale moving with the Greek’s swift narrative sequence while retaining the concrete image of the corpse dropped overboard.

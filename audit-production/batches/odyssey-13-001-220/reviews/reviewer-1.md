# Reviewer 1 — *Odyssey* 13.1–220

## Overall assessment

This is a highly faithful and often exceptionally effective rendering. It preserves the passage's narrative mechanics, speech acts, similes, technical shipboard detail, and abrupt Scheria-to-Ithaca transition with unusual care. I find two firm, localized fidelity problems, both minor: one false narrative-priority claim in a note and one lexical collapse in Odysseus' indictment of the Phaeacian leaders. Fixed-register wording differences are left to the automated formula audit and are not counted here.

| R1-001 | 13.42 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.91 | The note says that before the Phaeacians Odysseus' wife is “named at last” here and that his reticence from 7.225 is repealed only now. But in the Apologoi just delivered to that same Phaeacian audience, Odysseus explicitly asks Anticleia about his “wedded wife” and her mind (`μνηστῆς ἀλόχου`, 11.177), and her answer continues to discuss that wife at 11.181–183. The note therefore makes the present farewell a false first disclosure and builds an unsupported protective-reticence chronology on it. | The present verse's `ἀμύμονα ... ἄκοιτιν` is not Odysseus' first explicit mention of his wife before the Phaeacian court; compare his own embedded speech at 11.177 and Anticleia's reply at 11.181–183. | Narrow the claim: this is a newly direct farewell wish in the court-frame, or a return to the wife after the Apologoi, not her first free naming before the Phaeacians. |
| R1-002 | 13.209–210 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.97 | “Not righteous through and through, then, and not just” renders both coordinated predicates as moral justice-language. This duplicates `δίκαιοι` and drops the distinct charge in `νοήμονες`: Odysseus thinks the leaders are not altogether thoughtful, sensible, or intelligent as well as not just. | `οὐκ ἄρα πάντα νοήμονες οὐδὲ δίκαιοι / ἦσαν` coordinates `νοήμονες` (“thoughtful/sensible/intelligent”) with `δίκαιοι` (“just”). | Preserve both accusations, e.g. “not altogether sensible, then, nor just.” |

## Especially successful renderings

1. **13.31–35:** The plowman simile is exact in its action and bodily sequence—daylong plowing, longing for supper, welcomed sunset, and failing knees—while “aches for his supper” gives `λιλαίεται` appropriate force.
2. **13.73–92:** The bed made on the stern, the onset of `νήγρετος` sleep, the four-horse and falcon speed images, and the final forgetting of suffering are all carried coherently across line boundaries without loss of narrative agency.
3. **13.185–202:** The mid-line cut from the Phaeacians at the altar to Odysseus waking is sharply preserved, as are Athena's mist, delayed recognition, Odysseus' physical lament, and the recurring arrival-question's ethical alternatives.

# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed two findings and Reviewer 2 proposed five. The proposals
about the false first mention of Odysseus' wife and the collapsed distinction
between sense and justice are duplicate pairs and are merged. Five distinct
findings are sustained: three affect the translated verse and two affect
explanatory notes. The verse weakens an operative direction into urging,
redirects a mortal's inferiority to Poseidon into self-subjection, and replaces
Odysseus' charge of deficient sense with a second justice term. The notes make
a false narrative-priority claim about Odysseus' wife and overstate the scope
of the three-way arrival-question recurrence. No fixed-wording-only formula
deviation is counted.

## Especially Successful Renderings

- **13.31-35:** The plowman simile preserves the daylong work, urgent
  supper-hunger, welcomed sunset, and failing knees while making Odysseus'
  impatience physically immediate.
- **13.73-92:** The stern bedding, onset and quality of the deathlike sleep,
  horse-team and falcon speed images, and final forgetting of suffering remain
  coherent across the extended voyage sequence.
- **13.185-202:** The mid-line transition from the Phaeacians at the altar to
  Odysseus waking, Athena's mist and purpose, his bodily lament, and the
  recurring ethical alternatives of the arrival-question are sharply
  preserved.

## Calibration Implications

The calibrated speech-act rule applies directly when `ἐκέλευον` follows a
separate verb of approval: “urged” does not preserve the additional operative
direction. Explicit pronouns controlling comparison or submission must survive
even when the surrounding English remains fluent. Coordinated evaluative terms
should not be collapsed when they make distinct intellectual and moral
charges. Narrative-priority and verbatim-recurrence claims in notes require
precise comparison classes and exact line ranges. Auditing a false statement
about the extent of Greek recurrence remains distinct from counting a
fixed-English-wording deviation owned by automation.

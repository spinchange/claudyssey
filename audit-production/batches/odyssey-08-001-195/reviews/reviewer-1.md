# Reviewer 1 Report

The translation is generally semantically strong and preserves the passage's movement, social dynamics, and poetic energy. Five firm issues remain: two concrete semantic distortions, one weakened speech act, one loss of moral force, and one grammatical reversal in the contrast between appearance and eloquence.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 8.37 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.96 | "Lash your oars well to the benches" replaces the ship's thole-pins or rowlocks with benches, misstating the concrete attachment point. Line 53's leather slings preserve the fastening method but not the fittings. | `δησάμενοι δʼ ἐὺ πάντες ἐπὶ κληῖσιν ἐρετμά`: `κληῖσιν` denotes thole-pins or rowlocks. | Use "lash your oars securely at the rowlocks/thole-pins." |
| R1-002 | 8.124 and note L124 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.98 | "The width of a mule-team's furrow" turns a substantial ploughing range or furrow length into the narrow physical width of one furrow. This materially collapses Clytoneus' lead, and the note repeats the error. | `ὅσσον τʼ ἐν νειῷ οὖρον πέλει ἡμιόνοιιν`: the comparison is to the distance or ploughing range represented by a mule team's furrow. | Use "the length of a mule-team's furrow" or "a mule team's ploughing range." |
| R1-003 | 8.153 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.90 | "Why do you all urge this on me" weakens Odysseus' complaint that the Phaeacians are bidding or directing him to compete while mocking him. | `τί με ταῦτα κελεύετε κερτομέοντες;`: `κελεύετε` is "bid/order/direct," while `κερτομέοντες` supplies the mocking manner. | Use "why do you all bid me do this, mocking me?" |
| R1-004 | 8.166 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.83 | "A man blinded by folly" weakens the moral and social force of `ἀτάσθαλος`, which marks Euryalus as presumptuous or reckless, not merely cognitively foolish. | `ἀτασθάλῳ ἀνδρὶ ἔοικας`: `ἀτάσθαλος` denotes reckless, presumptuous, culpably outrageous conduct. | Use "you are like a reckless/presumptuous man." |
| R1-005 | 8.169-170 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.99 | "The god crowns his shape with words" reverses the relationship between `μορφήν` and `ἔπεσι`. The physically unimpressive man is compensated because the god adorns his words with beauty; the English instead has words adorn his physical shape. | `θεὸς μορφὴν ἔπεσι στέφει`: `μορφήν` is placed around `ἔπεσι`, "words," dative. | Use "the god crowns his words with beauty/grace." |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **8.62-70:** Demodocus' entrance preserves the balanced good-and-evil structure and stages his chair, lyre, food, and wine clearly.
2. **8.83-95:** Odysseus' repeated concealment, tears, libation, and renewed mourning reproduce the iterative sequence.
3. **8.186-193:** The discus throw retains the heavier implement, spinning cast, sound, cowering crowd, and flight beyond every mark.

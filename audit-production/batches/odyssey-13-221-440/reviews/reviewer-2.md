# Reviewer 2 — Fidelity Review of *Odyssey* 13.221–440

This is a notably faithful and agile passage: the disguised encounter, Cretan tale, recognition dialogue, Ithacan briefing, and physical transformation all preserve their Greek actions and relationships with unusual consistency. I find three material issues, all minor inaccuracies in explanatory notes rather than errors in the translated verse. Fixed-register wording differences have not been counted.

| R2-001 | 13.336–338 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.98 | The note says that Athena reuses Anticleia's 11.181–183 report “verbatim” and that mother and goddess file it “in the same words.” Only the final line is verbatim. ## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **13.254–286:** The first Cretan tale keeps its rapid causal chain intact—from the withheld truth through Orsilochus' attempted dispossession, the night ambush, the bargain with the Phoenicians, the adverse wind, and the abandonment on the beach—while sustaining Odysseus' improvisatory voice.
2. **13.291–310:** Athena's delighted recognition speech preserves the dense characterization of both partners: `κερδαλέος`, `ἐπίκλοπος`, `ποικιλομῆτα`, the shared `κέρδεα`, and the contrast between Odysseus' mortal excellence and Athena's divine fame all remain rhetorically distinct.
3. **13.397–440:** The promised disguise and its execution correspond closely clause by clause, and the intervening directions concerning Eumaeus, Telemachus, and the ambush preserve agency, sequence, and the dialogue's shift from household strategy to divine action.

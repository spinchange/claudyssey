# Reviewer 2

Overall, the translation is highly faithful across the passage, preserving agency, speech-act force, emotional pressure, and technical detail with unusually close control. I found one firm, localized explanatory-note error: the translation collapses two distinct Greek seabird terms and the note then builds a false exact recurrence from that choice. No formula-register-only deviations are counted.

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **5.13–17:** The captivity and blocked-homecoming passage retains Calypso’s coercion, Odysseus’ incapacity, and the full logistical sequence of ships, oars, companions, and sea passage without flattening the syntax.
2. **5.118–144:** Calypso’s protest preserves both the operative charge of divine jealousy and the contrast between her justified bitterness and her eventual submission to Zeus’ command. The repeated Zeus-couplet at 103–104 and 137–138 remains semantically intact in its changed rhetorical setting.
3. **5.214–224:** Odysseus’ answer keeps the decisive concessive structure: he acknowledges Penelope’s mortal inferiority, then pivots without qualification to his enduring desire for home and willingness to suffer. “Let this be added to the rest” is especially effective compensation for the compact Greek close.

# Reviewer 2

## Overall assessment

This is a strong and generally exact passage. The English preserves the
gift-exchange, Nausicaa's farewell, Demodocus' song, the captive-woman simile,
and Alcinous' closing questions with unusual semantic and rhetorical care. I
find no firm verse-level fidelity error. The two firm findings are confined to
explanatory notes: one gives an impossible line-distance estimate, and another
calls a recurrence verbatim despite a meaningful lexical change in its opening
category. Several narrower verse and note issues were considered but remain
below the materiality threshold.

## Especially successful renderings

1. **8.408-415:** Euryalus' apology and Odysseus' answer retain the social
   delicacy of `ἀρεσσάσθω`, the storm-wind dismissal of the insult, and the
   pointed wish that Euryalus never feel the sword's lack.
2. **8.492-498:** “Change your path” aptly carries the shift in Demodocus'
   song while echoing the Muse-taught `οἴμας` at 481; the horse's making,
   deceptive function, and Odysseus' promised proclamation all remain clear.
3. **8.523-531:** The captive-woman simile is rendered with exceptional
   precision, preserving the husband's defensive death, the woman's clinging
   lament, the captors' blows, enslavement, and the return to Odysseus' tears.

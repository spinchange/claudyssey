# Reviewer 1

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. Lines 344–353 preserve Phemius’ plea with excellent rhetorical progression: artistic vocation, divine inspiration, personal suitability, and coerced service.
2. Lines 381–389 render the netted-fish simile with clarity and restraint, retaining the creatures’ longing for the sea and the sun’s lethal action.
3. Lines 401–418 handle the bloodied-lion comparison, Eurycleia’s attempted triumph cry, and Odysseus’ prohibition with unusually precise shifts of tone and moral register.

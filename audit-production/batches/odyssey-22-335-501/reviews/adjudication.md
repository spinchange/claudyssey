# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 22.335–501

## Overall Assessment

The translation is exceptionally strong through the supplications, moral reckoning, forced cleanup, executions, purification, and recognition. The eight sustained findings concern explanatory notes rather than the verse translation: three unsupported additions and five inaccurate descriptions of recurrence, chronology, narrative priority, or earlier scenes.

Fourteen firm reviewer IDs resolve to nine distinct proposal keys. Five true duplicate pairs were merged. Eight distinct proposals are sustained and one is rejected. The claim that divine and human agency is divided “evenly” is normalized to ADDITION because the note preserves both Greek causes while supplying an unsupported proportion. The mutilation-catalogue finding is set at MODERATE because the inaccurate cross-book synthesis drives the note’s claim that the revenge closes an exact moral ledger.

R1-006 is rejected. “Eurycleia’s first thought after Armageddon” can coherently refer to her first response after the entire violent sequence has ended with the women’s hanging and Melanthius’ mutilation. Her earlier inventory and proposal to wake Penelope occur while the aftermath and further executions remain in progress.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal remains unresolved. The wingless-word ordinal is a false explanatory count, not a fixed-wording-only FORMULA finding.

## Rejected and Unresolved Proposals

R1-006 is rejected. Eurycleia inventories the women and proposes waking Penelope after the suitors’ deaths but before the executions of the twelve women and the mutilation of Melanthius. When she proposes clothing at 22.485–489, the book’s violent sequence has ended. “First thought after Armageddon” can therefore function as a bounded literary description of her first newly expressed concern after the complete bloodshed, rather than a claim that she has said nothing since the suitors fell.

No proposal remains unresolved.

The following concerns remain below threshold:

| Lines | Concern | Reason not counted |
|---|---|---|
| 22.354–360 note | Saying Telemachus “chose the maids’ deaths” may overstate his agency because Odysseus orders their execution. | Telemachus independently replaces the ordered sword execution with hanging, giving him substantial responsibility for the deaths as ultimately carried out. |
| 22.407–410 | “Hot as she was” loosely renders Eurycleia’s eagerness or intention to cry out. | In context “hot” can idiomatically convey her emotional eagerness, while the desired cry is explicit immediately before it. |
| 22.421–429 note | “The mistress kept the discipline” goes beyond Penelope’s refusal to let the newly grown Telemachus command the women. | Penelope’s reservation of authority makes continued disciplinary responsibility a reasonable inference. |
| 22.455–456 | “Hoes” may be too specific for `λίστροισιν`. | Scrapers, spades, shovels, and hoe-like implements all fit the represented floor-scraping action, and the object’s exact ancient form is uncertain. |
| 22.468–473 note | “No other ancient text” makes an unbounded literary-historical superlative. | The sentence functions primarily as rhetorical evaluation and is not necessary factual support for a rendering. |
| 22.495–501 | “Went off up through the house” adds upward movement to `ἀπέβη διὰ δώματα`. | The destination among the women’s rooms makes upward movement contextually plausible, although it is not lexically explicit. |
| 22.497 note | “After one hundred and eight corpses” can sound like a total body count. | The traditional 108 clearly denotes the suitor company and can reasonably function as shorthand for the principal slaughter rather than every death in the scene. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **22.335–380:** Phemius’ deliberation and plea, Telemachus’ intervention, Medon’s emergence from the oxhide, and Odysseus’ maxim preserve both the danger and the passage’s sudden low comedy.
2. **22.381–418:** The netted-fish and bloodied-lion similes, Eurycleia’s attempted triumph cry, and Odysseus’ prohibition maintain precise shifts between pity, horror, joy, and moral restraint.
3. **22.437–501:** The forced cleanup, hanging, mutilation, sulfur fumigation, and final recognition by the loyal women retain the Greek’s concrete sequencing without euphemizing its violence.

## Calibration Implications

- Explanatory notes should distinguish plausible inferred motive from narrated intention.
- Literary-priority and recurrence ordinals must use a clearly defined and chronologically accurate comparison class.
- False formula-occurrence counts remain semantic note findings; fixed wording itself belongs to the automated lane.
- Coordination of two causes does not license a claim of equal causal weight.
- Laborious narrative sequence does not establish a measured number of hours.
- Broad claims about a character’s adult decisions must account for earlier exercises of household and tactical authority.
- Cross-book motif catalogues must be checked element by element before being described as complete or exactly repeated.
- Chronological synthesis should preserve both elapsed time and the agents of earlier actions.

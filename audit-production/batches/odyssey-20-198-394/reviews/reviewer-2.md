# Reviewer 2 — Semantic Review

The verse is highly effective overall, especially in Philoetius’ loyalist scene, Telemachus’ public assertion of authority, and Theoclymenus’ vision. I found seven firm issues, six in the notes and one technical lexical error in the translation. Most arise from repeatedly placing the contest and slaughter one day too late. All are minor; no moderate, major, or critical errors were found.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 20.230, 253 notes | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | “Redemption is tomorrow” and “tomorrow the same three hands…” place the return’s fulfillment and battle a day too late. The bow contest and slaughter occur later on the festival day already underway. | Dawn arrived at 20.91, and 20.156 identifies the current day as the festival. Books 21–22 continue into the contest and slaughter without another night intervening. | Replace “tomorrow” with “today,” “later that day,” or “within hours.” |
| R2-002 | 20.284 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note calls Athena’s goad “the third day running” and says the accumulated rage is spent “tomorrow.” The parallel goad in Book 18 occurred on the preceding narrative day; Book 19 is the intervening night, and the revenge occurs later on the present day. | The repeated passage is at 18.346–348 and 20.284–286. No additional dawn falls between them, and no dawn separates 20.284 from the contest and slaughter. | Call this the second successive day of the goad and say that the rage is spent later today. |
| R2-003 | 20.364 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.98 | The note calls Theoclymenus’ prediction “the last warning the suitors receive from anyone.” Leodes subsequently warns that the bow will strip many leading men of life, before the attack begins; Odysseus later announces that no suitor will escape. | At 21.152, Leodes says the bow `πολλοὺς ... ἀποφθίσει`—will destroy many chief men. At 22.65–67 Odysseus explicitly says he does not think one of them will escape. | Narrow the claim to the last sustained warning from an external seer before the contest. |
| R2-004 | 20.376 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.96 | The note says Odysseus strung his bow “before any of [the suitors] was born.” The poem establishes the bow’s pre-Troy history but supplies no ages proving that every suitor was unborn at the time. | Neither `νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων` here nor the bow’s history in Book 21 provides an exhaustive chronology of the suitors’ births. | Say that Odysseus mastered the bow long before these young men attempted it, without making a universal birth-date claim. |
| R2-005 | 20.382 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.99 | “Many-benched ship” replaces the ship’s explicit rowing hardware with adjacent furniture. | `νηῒ πολυκληῗδι` denotes a ship with many tholes or rowlocks, hence a many-oared ship; `κληΐς` is not a bench. | Use “many-oared,” “many-rowlocked,” or “many-tholed ship.” |
| R2-006 | 20.387 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note calls the scene “massacre-eve,” says Penelope hears everything “tonight,” and places the contest “tomorrow.” This is the daytime festival feast, followed continuously by the contest and slaughter later the same day. | The scene follows Dawn at 20.91 and the festival announcement at 20.156; Theoclymenus’ vision is correctly described elsewhere in the notes as occurring at noonday. | Replace the night/eve/tomorrow framing with “on the day of the massacre” and “later at the contest.” |
| R2-007 | 20.387–389 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.95 | The note describes Penelope as an “unseen” audience. The Greek says that she placed her chair opposite the men and listened to each one; it supplies no concealment. | `κατʼ ἄντηστιν θεμένη ... δίφρον` places her chair opposite or facing them, followed by `ἑκάστου μῦθον ἄκουεν`, “she heard each man’s speech.” | Describe her as seated opposite the company and listening, without asserting that they cannot see her. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **20.198–239:** Philoetius’ compassion, memories of Odysseus, dilemma over the cattle, conditional hope, and immediate offer of strength are rendered with clear emotional and logical progression.

2. **20.257–320:** Telemachus’ tactical seating of Odysseus, public protection of the stranger, response to the ox-hoof, and declaration that death is preferable to continued household outrage preserve both the action and his new authority.

3. **20.345–372:** Athena’s derangement of the suitors and Theoclymenus’ vision retain the Greek’s disturbing bodily sequence—alien laughter, bloody meat, tears, darkness, ghosts, vanished sun, and the seer’s controlled departure.

## Concerns Considered

| Lines | Confidence | Concern | Why not counted |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 20.213 | 0.61 | “Strangers order me” turns `ἄλλοι`, “other men,” into outsiders, although the suitors are largely local Achaeans. | “Strangers” can mark their alien status relative to Odysseus’ household rather than geographic origin, and their illegitimate commands remain clear. |
| 20.219–221 | 0.70 | “Cattle that others claim” may shift `βουσὶν ... ἀλλοτρίῃσι`, cattle belonging to another person, into cattle merely claimed by the suitors. | In context both expressions emphasize that Philoetius is not their owner, and the surrounding speech makes the true household ownership recoverable. |
| 20.242 note | 0.68 | Calling the eagle carrying a dove “the same sign” as 15.525ff may overstate the recurrence: the earlier bird is a hawk, approaches from the favorable right, and tears the dove. | Both omens use a raptor and dove to protect or validate Telemachus, so “same sign” can denote the repeated omen-pattern rather than identical species and mechanics. |
| 20.259 | 0.68 | “Battered stool” converts evaluative `ἀεικέλιον`, unseemly or wretched, into specific physical damage. | A battered appearance is a plausible concrete realization of an unseemly stool, and the intended social degradation is preserved. |
| 20.351 note | 0.66 | Saying that “every clause” of the vision is redeemed in Book 22 may overstate literal correspondence, particularly the vanished sun and visible ghosts. | The note is reasonably read as describing symbolic fulfillment through slaughter and death rather than exact replication of every supernatural image. |

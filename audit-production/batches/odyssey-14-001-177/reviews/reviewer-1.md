# Reviewer 1 — Odyssey 14.1–177

The translation is consistently accurate in action, agency, speech, and tone, and it handles Eumaeus’ social position and emotional loyalty with particular care. I found no moderate or major errors. Two localized minor issues meet the calibrated reporting threshold: a concrete animal-husbandry term is replaced by a different behavior, and an explanatory note assigns an exact age that the Greek does not supply.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |

| R1-001 | 14.15 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.85 | “Wallowing sows” changes a concrete resting or housing detail into a different porcine behavior. | `σύες χαμαιευνάδες` means sows “sleeping/couching on the ground”; `χαμαιευνάδες` does not denote wallowing. The preceding `εὐνὰς συσίν` also makes their bedding the immediate subject. | Use “ground-sleeping sows,” “low-couching sows,” or “sows bedded on the ground.” |
| R1-002 | 14.80–81 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.93 | The note’s “fatted five-year hogs” supplies an exact age absent from this contrast and presents it as a property of the suitors’ hogs generally. | The verse contrasts `χοίρεα` (young pigs) with `σιάλους ... σύας` (fattened or mature hogs); it contains no numeral or age term. | Remove “five-year” and describe the upper-class portion simply as “fattened hogs” or “mature, fattened hogs.” |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. Lines 29–38 preserve the full physical sequence of the dogs’ charge, Odysseus’ calculated sitting and dropped staff, Eumaeus’ intervention, and the threatened shame without confusing agency.
2. Lines 55–66 accurately retain the narrator’s apostrophe, the Zeus-given law of hospitality, the slaves’ fear under young masters, and the promised house–land–wife sequence.
3. Lines 121–147 render Eumaeus’ distrust of itinerant reports and his grief for Odysseus with close control of tone, culminating in the registered and contextually apt “my beloved lord.”

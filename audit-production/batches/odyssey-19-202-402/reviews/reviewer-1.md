# Reviewer 1 — Semantic Review

The translation is vivid and generally faithful, especially through Penelope’s grief, the clothing test, and Eurycleia’s approach to recognition. The firm issues are localized: one calibrated lexical weakening in the verse and four explanatory-note claims that misstate recurrence, participant scope, deception, or naming agency. Fixed-wording-only formula deviations are excluded.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 19.275–276 | LEXICAL | MODERATE | 0.99 | “Zeus and Helios were set at odds with him” preserves the Odysseus name-pun but weakens their consequential anger or hostility into mere opposition or disagreement. | `ὀδύσαντο γὰρ αὐτῷ / Ζεύς τε καὶ Ἠέλιος` says that Zeus and Helios were angry or wrathful with him because his companions killed the cattle. | Preserve both the pun and hostility, for example by making the gods “wrathfully at odds with him.” |
| R1-002 | 19.309 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.98 | The note says all three occurrences of the blessing formula answer “a true prophecy of return.” Its first occurrence answers Theoclymenus’ prediction of Telemachus’ enduring dynastic power, not Odysseus’ return. | At 15.531–534 Theoclymenus declares that no Ithacan line is more kingly than Telemachus’ and that his family’s power will endure; Telemachus answers with the formula at 15.536–538. | Describe the formula as answering favorable or true prophetic assurances; distinguish the first dynastic prophecy from the two return prophecies. |
| R1-003 | 19.357 note | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.83 | “The three watchful-minded women … are about to be two people in a room” conflates the poem-wide set with the two participants present. The third woman bearing `περίφρων`, Arete, is not in this scene. | `περίφρων` characterizes Penelope repeatedly, Arete at 11.345, and Eurycleia here; only Penelope and Eurycleia are in the room. | Write “Two of the poem’s three watchful-minded women—the queen and the nurse—are now in the room.” |
| R1-004 | 19.383 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.92 | The note claims that Odysseus “does not lie,” but his parry invents third-party testimony that the fictional Aethon and Odysseus have been seen together and judged alike. Their physical identity makes the resemblance true, but not the alleged witnesses’ report. | `φασὶν ὅσοι ἴδον ... ἡμέας ἀμφοτέρους` asserts that all who saw “the two of us” say they are alike; no separate Aethon exists. | Call the reply an equivocation that embeds a true resemblance inside fabricated testimony, rather than denying that it is a lie. |
| R1-005 | 19.401 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | “The namer of Odysseus is his recognizer” fuses Autolycus with Eurycleia. Eurycleia prompts the naming and later recognizes Odysseus, but Autolycus chooses and bestows the name. | Eurycleia places the child on Autolycus’ knees and asks him to choose a name at 19.401–404; Autolycus supplies “Odysseus” at 19.405–409. | Preserve the distinct agents: the recognizer was present at the naming, while the grandfather was the namer. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

- 19.203 crisply preserves the programmatic paradox of lies made to resemble truth.
- 19.204–212 renders the melting-snow simile and Odysseus’ horn-or-iron stillness with strong emotional and imagistic contrast.
- 19.225–248 handles the brooch, onion-skin tunic, and Eurybates portrait with unusually precise concrete detail.

## Concerns Considered

| 19.211 note | 0.70 | Calling “He wept for his wife in his heart” *literal* blurs `ἐλέαιρε`, “pitied,” with the wife’s grieving verb. | The surrounding Greek does give Odysseus concealed tears, so the note can be read as a compressed synthesis of lines 210–212 rather than a consequential reassignment. |

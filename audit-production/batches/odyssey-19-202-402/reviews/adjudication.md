# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 19.202–402

## Overall Assessment

The translation is vivid and generally faithful, particularly in Penelope’s grief, the clothing test, and the approach to Eurycleia’s recognition. Seven distinct findings are sustained from twelve firm reviewer proposals. Five true duplicate pairs were merged. Several reviewer classifications or rationales required modification, especially at 19.275–276, note L357, and note L401.

At note L357, the corpus supports three women called `περίφρων`: Penelope, Eurycleia, and Arete, who is `βασίλεια περίφρων` at 11.345. Reviewer 2’s claim that only Penelope and Eurycleia bear the epithet is therefore false. The note nevertheless remains grammatically defective because it says “the three ... queen, nurse” and then turns those three into “two people in a room” without identifying Arete or marking Penelope and Eurycleia as two members of the three.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal remains unresolved.

## Rejected and Unresolved Proposals

No firm proposal was rejected outright, and none remains unresolved. The following portions of reviewer reasoning were rejected or modified:

- The MODERATE severity proposed for 19.275–276 is too high. “At odds with” still communicates adversarial opposition, the cattle offense is explicit, and the wreck follows immediately. What is lost is the gods’ affective wrath, making the error real but local.
- Reviewer 2’s factual premise for note L357 is incorrect. Arete is the third woman: the `βασίλεια περίφρων` of 11.345 refers back to Arete, who begins speaking at 11.335. The note’s actual defect is that it fails to identify her and grammatically collapses three poem-wide bearers into the two women present.
- Reviewer 2’s GRAMMAR classification at note L401 was rejected. The sentence is grammatically intelligible; its proposition about who bestowed the name is factually false, so MISTRANSLATION is the better category.

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **19.203–212:** The paradox of lies resembling truth, Penelope’s snow-melting grief, and Odysseus’ concealed emotion retain their contrasting imagery and psychological force.
2. **19.218–250:** The brooch, onion-skin tunic, and Eurybates portrait provide precise and progressively convincing recognition tokens.
3. **19.317–348:** The hospitality sequence and Odysseus’ qualified refusal of foot-washing preserve both social courtesy and tactical caution.

## Calibration Implications

- A punning compensation can reduce severity without eliminating a lexical finding when the source’s emotional force is still weakened.
- Explanatory notes must distinguish a wreck’s keel from a raft even when both belong to the broader return narrative.
- Formula-history claims must classify the content of each recurrence rather than assuming identical surrounding prophecy.
- Corpus-wide epithet counts require checking unnamed or contextually identified bearers; at 11.345 `βασίλεια περίφρων` is Arete.
- A proposition may be true while its asserted evidentiary source is fabricated. Commentary should not call such speech wholly truthful.
- Presence at a naming scene, prompting a name, and bestowing the name are distinct forms of agency.

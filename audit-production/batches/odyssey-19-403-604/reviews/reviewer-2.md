# Reviewer 2 — Semantic Review

The translation is generally strong, especially in the physical narration of the hunt, recognition, and dream. I found six firm issues, all minor: three localized verse-level distortions and three places where commentary asserts chronology, priority, or rhetorical effects not established by the Greek. No major, moderate, or critical errors were found.

| R2-001 | 19.407 and note | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.91 | “At odds with many” preserves the name-pun but weakens the anger in Autolycus’ explanation and obscures its direction. The note’s “having incurred wrath” likewise suggests that others are angry with him, whereas Autolycus presents himself as the one angered against many. | In `πολλοῖσιν ... ὀδυσσάμενος`, Autolycus is the subject of “having become angry/hostile,” with `πολλοῖσιν` as the people against whom that feeling is directed. | Retain the pun while restoring anger and agency, for example: “Since I have come here angered against many … let Odysseus be his name.” |
| R2-002 | 19.433 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note assigns Odysseus the exact age of sixteen during the Parnassus hunt. Neither this passage nor Autolycus’ earlier promise supplies that age. | Autolycus says only `ὁππότʼ ἂν ἡβήσας`—“when he has reached youth/manhood”—and the hunt narrative gives no numeral. | Replace “at sixteen” with “in his youth” or another non-numerical description. |
| R2-003 | 19.439, 454 notes | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.94 | The notes call the boar Odysseus’ “first great adversary” and its death his “first kill.” The scene narrates a youthful hunt but never identifies either as his first. | The boar is introduced only as `μέγας σῦς`; after Odysseus strikes it, `ἀπὸ δʼ ἔπτατο θυμός`. | R2-004 | 19.474–475 and note | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.85 | After Eurycleia recognizes and directly addresses Odysseus, “not before I had handled all of him—my master” introduces an unsupported switch from second to third person. ## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **19.418–427:** The preparation, division, and consumption of the feast are conveyed clearly while preserving the type-scene’s orderly progression into sunset and sleep.

2. **19.439–466:** The boar hunt is vivid and mechanically precise: the protected lair, boar’s oblique charge, wound above the knee, spear-thrust through the shoulder, healing, and return all remain readily traceable to the Greek.

3. **19.467–507:** The recognition sequence handles its rapid physical chain exceptionally well—the tactile discovery, falling foot, ringing basin, spilled water, conflicting emotions, Athena’s intervention, throat-grip, demand for silence, and concealment of the scar.

## Concerns Considered

| 19.513 and note | 0.69 | “My one delight” adds exclusivity to `τέρπομαι`, and the note repeats that stronger formulation. | Penelope’s surrounding account depicts grief as overwhelmingly habitual, so the intensification is contextually plausible even though “one” is not expressed. |
| 19.518–523 note | 0.63 | The note says the nightingale sings for Itylus “always,” whereas `θαμά` means often or repeatedly. | The transformed nightingale’s continuing lament makes “always” understandable as mythic shorthand; its materiality is limited. |
| 19.536–537 | 0.68 | “Wheat softened in water” treats `ἐξ ὕδατος` as describing soaked grain, though the phrase may instead describe the geese coming from the water or feeding at the water trough. | The construction and physical feeding arrangement permit more than one interpretation, and the later trough supports the chosen scene. |
| 19.568 | 0.64 | “My strange dream, I fear” transfers the ominous force of `αἰνὸν` from the dream into Penelope’s attitude; the Greek calls the dream dreadful or grim. | “Strange” retains an uncanny quality, while “I fear” supplies nearby affective compensation. |
| 19.571 note | 0.64 | Calling the coming morning “the eve of the dark of the moon” states the lunar phase more exactly than this immediate passage does. | The waning-month chronology and following Apollo festival make the inference plausible, though it should ideally be marked as reconstruction. |
| 19.584 | 0.62 | “Do not put off this contest … one hour” introduces a specific time unit absent from `μηκέτι νῦν ἀνάβαλλε`. | “Not one hour” functions naturally as an idiom for “do not delay any longer,” so it need not assert a literal calculated interval. |

# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 19.403–604

## Overall Assessment

The translation is generally strong, especially in the boar hunt, the recognition sequence, and Penelope’s dream narrative. Seven distinct findings are sustained from thirteen firm reviewer proposals. Four true duplicate pairs were merged. Two unique Reviewer 1 proposals were rejected: the claimed object error at 455 and the claimed literal one-hour measurements at 584 and note L589.

At 407, `ὀδυσσάμενος` presents Autolycus as angered or hostile toward many people. “At odds with many” preserves both opposition and the name-pun but weakens the affective force and makes the relation reciprocal. The note’s “having incurred wrath” goes further by suggesting the opposite direction—that others are angry with Autolycus.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal remains unresolved.

## Rejected and Unresolved Proposals

Two firm proposals were rejected. None remains unresolved.

### R1-004 — 19.455

The preceding subject is the boar, which falls dead at 454. At 455 `τὸν μὲν` naturally resumes that masculine referent: Autolycus’ sons attend to the boar or carcass. The correlated `ὠτειλὴν δέ` at 456 then turns to Odysseus’ wound, which they bind and staunch. The English preserves that two-object sequence and should not be changed to make `τὸν` Odysseus.

### R1-007 — 19.584 and note L589

“One hour” in “do not put off this contest ... one hour” is a conventional minimizer meaning “do not delay any longer.” It need not claim that a measured sixty-minute interval is available. Likewise, the note’s “one more hour and no wand could have held the marriage apart” is counterfactual literary rhetoric about how close the scene has come to recognition, not an exact chronological calculation. This differs from note L571’s categorical placement of the scene “at midnight.”

## Calibration Implications

- Reciprocal opposition is not fully equivalent to subject-directed anger; commentary must also preserve who feels hostility toward whom.
- A narrated youthful episode does not establish an exact age or absolute biographical first.
- Masculine pronoun resolution should follow discourse structure as well as proximity; at 455 the `μέν ... δέ` sequence distinguishes the boar from Odysseus’ wound.
- A translation should not insert a pronoun shift and then use commentary to treat that shift as source-text rhetoric.
- Unsupported “always” expansions of `πάρος` remain ADDITION findings when the earlier state is preserved but made exceptionless.
- Idiomatic time minimizers should not be audited as literal measurements without evidence that the passage depends on exact duration.
- Future arrival and future presence are not interchangeable when disguise and dramatic truth make the motion semantically important.

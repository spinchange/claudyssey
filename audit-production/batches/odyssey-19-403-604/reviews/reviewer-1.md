# Reviewer 1 — Semantic Review

The passage is dynamically and often precisely rendered, particularly in the boar hunt, recognition scene, and dream narrative. The firm issues are localized: two verse-level semantic shifts, two unsupported temporal or habitual expansions, and three explanatory-note additions. Fixed-wording-only formula deviations are excluded.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 19.407–409 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.96 | “At odds with many” preserves the name-pun effectively but weakens the anger or hatred in the etymology of Odysseus’ name. | `πολλοῖσιν ... ὀδυσσάμενος` presents Autolycus as having incurred or felt wrath in relation to many people, not merely being in disagreement with them. | Preserve the pun while retaining hostility, for example through “wrathfully at odds” or an accompanying anger term. |
| R1-002 | 19.433 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note assigns Odysseus the exact age of sixteen during the hunt, although the poem supplies no numeral. | Autolycus’ earlier `ὁππότ’ ἂν ἡβήσας` says only “when he has reached youth/manhood”; the hunt narrative does not specify his age. | Say “in his youth” or “when he had come of age.” |
| R1-003 | 19.454 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.88 | Calling the boar Odysseus’ “first kill” supplies an unsupported biographical priority. The episode is his earliest narrated kill, but the poem does not say it was the first animal or adversary he ever killed. | Lines 429–454 narrate this particular hunt and killing without a first-time marker. | Call it his “earliest narrated kill” or simply his youthful boar-kill. |
| R1-004 | 19.455 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | “Busied themselves with the carcass” gives the sons of Autolycus the wrong object. They attend to wounded Odysseus, as the immediately following wound-binding confirms. | `τὸν ... ἀμφεπένοντο` refers to Odysseus; lines 456–458 continue with binding his wound, stanching his blood, and bringing him home. | Render “the sons of Autolycus tended him” or “gathered around Odysseus.” |
| R1-005 | 19.552–553 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.95 | “Just where they always were” turns prior location into an exceptionless habit. | `ἧχι πάρος περ` means “where they were before” or “as before”; it does not itself mean “always.” | Use “just where they were before” or “in their former place.” |
| R1-006 | 19.571 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.96 | The note places the announcement “at midnight,” an exact time not supplied by the narrative. | The scene is late enough that Penelope says the hour of sleep is near at 510–511, but no midnight marker appears. | Say “late at night” or omit the clock-time claim. |
| R1-007 | 19.584 and 589 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.98 | “Do not put off this contest … one hour” and the note’s “one more hour” both add an exact interval. The Greek expresses no further delay and a counterfactual continuation of conversation, without measuring either as an hour. | `μηκέτι νῦν ἀνάβαλλε` means “do not delay any longer”; at 589–590 Penelope says continued conversation would keep sleep from her eyes. | Use “do not delay this contest any longer,” and describe the scene as unable to survive much further conversation without specifying an hour. |

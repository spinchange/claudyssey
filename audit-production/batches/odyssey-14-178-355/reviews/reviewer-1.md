# Reviewer 1

The passage is generally strong: it sustains the Cretan persona, preserves the raid and shipwreck narratives with considerable precision, and handles recurring material without a distinct semantic formula failure. Five material issues qualify as firm findings. Fixed-register wording differences alone are not counted.

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **193–198:** The expansive wish for food, wine, quiet, and a full year of narration carries the syntax across the line breaks cleanly and retains both the scale of the sufferings and their dependence on the gods’ will.
2. **262–272:** The translation preserves the raid’s moral and causal sequence—arrogance, pillage, abduction, killing, alarm, dawn counterattack, panic, death, and forced enslavement—without euphemizing the violence.
3. **301–315:** The shipwreck sequence successfully retains the dark-blue cloud, sudden thunderbolt, sulfur, men carried like sea-crows, the saving mast, and the exact nine-day/tenth-night chronology.

# Reviewer 1

The translation is highly faithful across the passage, preserving the extended speeches, narrative agency, and Nausicaa’s shifts between hospitality, desire, and social caution. I found two firm, localized issues, both minor: one weakened speech act and one unsupported factual claim in a note.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 216 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.94 | “Urged him to bathe” weakens the handmaids’ operative instruction into encouragement. The distinction is localized, but it slightly changes the social action to which Odysseus responds by requesting privacy. | `ἤνωγον ... μιν λοῦσθαι` means that they bade or instructed him to bathe. | Use “bade him bathe” or “told him to bathe.” |
| R1-002 | 273 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.96 | The note calls Nausicaa “a sixteen-year-old,” asserting an exact age that the poem does not supply. Her youth and marriageability are clear, but the numeral is unsupported. | The passage calls her `κούρη` and elsewhere `παρθένος ἀδμής`; neither expression establishes an exact age. | Replace the numerical age with “young marriageable woman,” “adolescent princess,” or another explicitly non-exact description. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

- **170–180:** Odysseus’ account of the twenty-day ordeal and transition into supplication retains the temporal sequence, accumulated suffering, tact, and blessing without flattening his rhetoric.
- **229–235:** Athena’s beautification and the gold-over-silver craftsman simile are rendered with unusually close semantic and imagistic correspondence.

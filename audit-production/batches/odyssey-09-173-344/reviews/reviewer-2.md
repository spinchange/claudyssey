# Reviewer 2

## Overall assessment

The verse translation is strong throughout this section, especially in the
Cyclops' dairy routine, the supplication exchange, and the physical staging of
the escape plan. I found one firm issue in an explanatory note: it incorrectly
turns Odysseus' lie about Poseidon wrecking his ship into a prophecy of the
later loss of his fleet.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 9.283 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MODERATE` | 0.98 | The note says that the lie is prophetic because Poseidon “will smash his ships.” Poseidon does not destroy Odysseus' fleet in the subsequent wanderings: the Laestrygonians destroy eleven ships, and Zeus' thunderbolt destroys the last after the cattle of the Sun. The claim therefore misstates both the later events and divine agency. | The present verse is explicitly a false report: `νέα μέν μοι κατέαξε Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων` (“Poseidon the earth-shaker smashed my ship”). The future narrative does not fulfill it as the note claims. | Remove the fleet-prophecy claim. If desired, connect the lie more narrowly with Poseidon's already demonstrated hostility or with his later persecution of Odysseus, without claiming that he destroys Odysseus' ships. |

Counts by severity: **0 MINOR**; **1 MODERATE**; **0 MAJOR**; **0 CRITICAL**.

Especially successful renderings:

1. **9.218-223** preserves the ordered dairy inventory, including the three age
   groups, whey-filled vessels, pails, and milking bowls.
2. **9.263-278** clearly carries the whole rhetorical clash between Odysseus'
   appeal to Zeus of Strangers and the Cyclops' rejection of divine restraint.
3. **9.299-335** keeps the checked sword impulse, the immovable-stone logic,
   the ship-mast scale comparison, and the lot-drawing sequence exact and
   intelligible.

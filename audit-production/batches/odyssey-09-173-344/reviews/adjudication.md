# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed four findings and Reviewer 2 proposed one. The proposals
about the 9.283 note identify the same false shipwreck prophecy and are merged.
Three findings are sustained: the substitution of crates for cheese racks, the
note's description of the men as starving after they have eaten cheese, and
the note's conflation of Poseidon's later wrecking of a raft with the
destruction of Odysseus' ships. The proposal about “better men” is rejected
because, in the immediate boast of superiority over the gods, that idiom can
denote the stronger party without materially changing the Cyclops' claim.

## Calibration Implications

Concrete technical substitutions in agricultural equipment remain minor
findings when the larger activity survives. Explanatory contrasts must respect
the immediately narrated sequence: lack of offered hospitality does not make
characters starving after they have eaten. Claims that a lie is later
fulfilled require the same essential agent, object, and event; thematic
resemblance between Poseidon's raft-storm and other agents' destruction of
ships is not literal prophecy. At the same time, do not force a narrow lexical
gloss when an English comparative idiom preserves the contextually operative
claim of superior power.

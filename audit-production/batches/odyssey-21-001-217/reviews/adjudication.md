# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 21.1–217

## Overall Assessment

The translation is vivid and generally faithful, particularly in the bow’s history, Penelope’s announcement of the contest, and Telemachus’ near-success. Ten distinct findings are sustained from fifteen firm reviewer proposals. Five duplicate pairs were merged. The principal errors are false factual synthesis in the notes, two grammatical-role shifts in the verse, and one repeated concrete-object mistranslation.

Four proposal dispositions are modified: R2-004 and R2-005 are raised from MINOR to MODERATE; R1-007 is reclassified from MISTRANSLATION to ADDITION; and R2-008 is reduced from MODERATE to MINOR because emancipation and familial incorporation remain plausible implications even though the note presents them too categorically.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No firm proposal was rejected or left unresolved. No fixed-wording-only FORMULA issue was counted.

## Findings

| O21-001-217-A-001 | 21.48 note | ADDITION | MINOR | SUSTAINED; DUPLICATES MERGED | “Twenty years shut” supplies an unattested duration for the storeroom’s remaining unopened. The Greek narrates Penelope unlocking and opening it now but does not say when it was previously opened. The bow’s long residence in the house does not establish continuous closure of the room. |
| O21-001-217-A-002 | 21.55–56 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | SUSTAINED | The note calls the bow “the third time” an object has stood in for Odysseus, but its stated sequence is his clothes, the bow, and the bed “to come.” Within that sequence the bow is second. |
| O21-001-217-A-003 | 21.98 note; cf. 21.418 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | SUSTAINED | The note says the Greek “taste” idiom returns at 418 and places the payoff two hundred lines later. Line 98 has `γεύσεσθαι`; line 418 has `πειρήσεσθαι`, and the passages are 320 lines apart. The repeated English “taste” is a translator-created echo across different Greek verbs. |
| O21-001-217-A-004 | 21.113–117 note | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | SUSTAINED | The note reverses Telemachus’ stated logic by saying that, if he strings the bow, Penelope need not leave. In `οὔ κέ μοι ἀχνυμένῳ ... μήτηρ / λείποι`, he says that her leaving would no longer cause him grief because he would remain capable of taking up his father’s gear. The note turns readiness to endure her departure into prevention of it. |
| O21-001-217-A-005 | 21.122–123 and note | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | SUSTAINED; DUPLICATES MERGED; R2 SEVERITY MODIFIED | The English relative clause attaches “who had never seen it before” to the spectators, and the note endorses that false ambiguity. Greek plural `πάντας ἰδόντας` identifies the spectators, while singular `ὀπώπει` returns to Telemachus: everyone marvels that he arranged the axes properly although he himself had never seen the setup. This materially changes the characterization of his inherited competence. |
| O21-001-217-A-006 | 21.131–133 and note | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | SUSTAINED; DUPLICATES MERGED; R2 SEVERITY MODIFIED | “Defend a man” makes the accusative man someone Telemachus protects. Middle `ἄνδρʼ ἀπαμύνασθαι` means to ward a man off or defend oneself against him when he becomes hostile first. The translation reverses the hostile man’s semantic role, and the note repeats the misleading wording. |
| O21-001-217-A-007 | 21.138, 165 | LEXICAL | MINOR | SUSTAINED | In this repeated bow-and-arrow construction, `κορώνῃ` denotes the curved bow-tip or horn against which the arrow is leaned, not a door-hook. The door panels are separately expressed by `σανίδεσσιν`. The wrong concrete object appears twice. |
| O21-001-217-A-008 | 21.141 and 21.209 notes | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | SUSTAINED; DUPLICATES MERGED | Both notes move the slaughter to “tomorrow,” although the bow trial, Odysseus’ disclosure, and the killings belong to one continuous festival day. The additional claim that the deaths follow roughly the trial’s rotation is unsupported: Leodes tries the bow first, whereas Antinous is the first suitor killed. |
| O21-001-217-A-009 | 21.209–211 note | ADDITION | MINOR | SUSTAINED; DUPLICATES MERGED; R1 CATEGORY MODIFIED | The note expands a limited report into a “complete” census of loyalty across Odysseus’ “whole estate.” `οἴοισι δμώων` restricts the immediate comparison to slaves, and `οὔ τευ ἄκουσα` restricts the evidence to prayers Odysseus has heard. The source does not establish an exhaustive estate-wide survey. |
| O21-001-217-A-010 | 21.212–216 note | ADDITION | MINOR | SUSTAINED; R2 SEVERITY MODIFIED | The note presents the promise categorically as Homer’s sole “manumission scene” and as “adoption.” The Greek promises wives, possessions, nearby houses, and standing as Telemachus’ companions and brothers, but does not expressly enact emancipation or legal adoption. Those benefits make elevated status and even implied emancipation plausible, limiting severity to MINOR. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **21.13–41:** The bow’s complicated pedigree remains clear, including the two searches in Messene, Heracles’ violation of guest-friendship, and the weapon’s preservation as a domestic memorial rather than Trojan war gear.
2. **21.73–79:** Penelope’s contest preserves ease as the criterion, passage through all twelve axes, departure from her marital house, and the lingering dream-memory of that house.
3. **21.124–129:** Telemachus’ three failed pulls, near-success on the fourth, and Odysseus’ silent intervention retain precise pacing and agency.

## Calibration Implications

- An English relative clause must not override an unambiguous Greek number contrast.
- Middle-voice constructions with an accusative opponent require care: “ward off a man” is not “defend a man.”
- Notes must distinguish source recurrence from translator-created English echo and verify stated line distances.
- Ordered trials do not establish a corresponding death order without evidence.
- Concrete-object polysemy must be resolved from the immediate construction.
- Audible evidence from a limited group should not become an exhaustive estate-wide census.
- Social-status implications may be discussed as interpretations, but legal labels should not be presented as explicit acts when the Greek supplies only rewards and relational language.

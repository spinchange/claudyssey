# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed three findings and Reviewer 2 proposed two. The proposals
about the unsupported five-hundred-man estimate are duplicates and are merged.
Three findings are sustained: “mainland” contradicts the floating-island
setting, the 10.120 note turns resemblance and shared divine proximity into
unsupported Giant genealogy, and the 10.124 note invents an approximate crew
total. The 10.72 note proposal is rejected because the note itself identifies
Poseidon specifically; its claim that Aeolus is “not wrong” can therefore
refer to his detection of real divine hostility without endorsing his plural
claim that all the blessed gods hate Odysseus.

## Calibration Implications

Formulaic landfall language must be adapted when a literal “mainland” creates
a contextual geographic contradiction; this is a semantic issue, not merely a
fixed-wording deviation. Genealogical claims require explicit kinship evidence:
resemblance to Giants and parallel closeness to gods do not establish a common
bloodline. Approximate numerical claims in notes remain additions when the
poem supplies ships but no crew complement. Conversely, adjudicate qualifying
language across the full note: naming one hostile god can narrow a preceding
general statement rather than ratify a character's plural theology.

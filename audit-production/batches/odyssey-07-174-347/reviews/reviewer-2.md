# Reviewer 2 Report

The translation is consistently strong across this batch, preserving difficult syntax, narrative pacing, and Odysseus' rhetorical self-presentation. I find three firm issues: a weakened collective directive, a consequential temporal error concerning Odysseus' sleep during the voyage, and a false claim of verbatim recurrence in a note.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 226-227 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.95 | "Urged that the stranger be sent home" weakens the operative force of the assembled Phaeacians' response. They approve Odysseus' words and formally bid that he be conveyed home. | `οἱ δʼ ἄρα πάντες ἐπῄνεον ἠδʼ ἐκέλευον / πεμπέμεναι τὸν ξεῖνον`: `ἐκέλευον` expresses bidding or directing. | Use "approved and bade that the stranger be sent home" or "approved and called for the stranger to be sent home." |
| R2-002 | 317-320 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.98 | "Till then you will lie mastered by sleep" mislocates Odysseus' sleep before the voyage. Alcinous says the escort is tomorrow and that at that time, while Odysseus lies overcome by sleep, the crew will row him home. The simultaneity is consequential to the uncanny transport. | `πομπὴν ... αὔριον ἔς· τῆμος δὲ σὺ μὲν δεδμημένος ὕπνῳ / λέξεαι, οἱ δʼ ἐλόωσι γαλήνην`: `τῆμος` means "then/at that time," and `σὺ μὲν ... οἱ δέ` coordinates sleeping with rowing. | Use "The escort will be tomorrow. Then you will lie mastered by sleep, while they row..." |
| R2-003 | Note L346; 346-347 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.96 | The claim that the closing couplet reuses the Nestor-night ending "verbatim" is false. The passages share formulaic endings and closely echo one another, but both Greek lines alter their opening clauses. | Book 3 has `αὐτὸς δʼ αὖτε καθεῦδε ... / τῷ δʼ ἄλοχος δέσποινα ...`; Book 7 has `Ἀλκίνοος δʼ ἄρα λέκτο ... / πὰρ δὲ γυνὴ δέσποινα ...`. | Say that the couplet "closely echoes" or "reworks the formulaic ending." |

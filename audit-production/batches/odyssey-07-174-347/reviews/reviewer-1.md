# Reviewer 1 Report

The translation is consistently strong across this passage, especially in its handling of extended syntax, Odysseus' narrative voice, and the hospitality sequence. I find two localized fidelity problems: a singular-to-plural shift in the service of Odysseus, and a weakening of the Phaeacian leaders' directive.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 174-175 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.92 | "For them to wash," "beside them," and "before them" turn individualized service of Odysseus into service for plural recipients. The context requires grammatical adaptation: the meal is for the single stranger, and line 177 confirms that he alone eats and drinks. | The infinitive `νίψασθαι` and the table and bread actions continue the service of Odysseus; line 177 resumes with singular `αὐτὰρ ὁ ... Ὀδυσσεύς`. | Use "for him to wash," "beside him," and "before him." |
| R1-002 | 226-227 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.80 | "Urged that the stranger be sent home" weakens the operative force of the assembled Phaeacians' response. After approving Odysseus' words, they bid or direct that he be conveyed home. | `οἱ ... πάντες ἐπῄνεον ἠδʼ ἐκέλευον / πεμπέμεναι τὸν ξεῖνον`: all approved and `ἐκέλευον`, "bade/commanded," that the stranger be escorted. | Use "bade that the stranger be sent home" or "called for the stranger to be sent home." |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **215-221:** The belly speech preserves bodily indignity, coercive agency, and the abrupt command.
2. **267-286:** The shipwreck narrative closely tracks duration, causal sequence, Poseidon's obstruction, and physical exhaustion.
3. **311-316:** Alcinous' marriage offer retains likeness of mind and the qualification that Odysseus will not be detained unwillingly.

# Reviewer 1 Audit — *Odyssey* 18.1–214

The verse is consistently strong in action, characterization, and rhetorical sequencing, particularly through the staged fight, Odysseus’ warning to Amphinomus, and Penelope’s divinely managed entrance. I found one moderate verse error and three minor factual errors in the notes. Fixed-wording formula differences are excluded.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 15 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.99 | “Man possessed” literalizes the emphatic vocative `δαιμόνιε` into a claim of supernatural possession. Here the word expresses exasperated address, not diagnosis. | `δαιμόνιʼ, οὔτε τί σε ῥέζω κακὸν οὔτʼ ἀγορεύω`. Nothing suggests possession or enchantment. | Use an emphatic address such as “Strange man,” “You fool,” or “Good man.” |
| R1-002 | Note L44 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.91 | Calling the fight prize “the suitors’ one act of organized generosity in the poem” is a false exclusivity claim. They have collectively filled Odysseus’ pouch, and later coordinate gifts for Penelope. | At 17.411–412 the other suitors give food and fill the pouch. At 18.291–301 each sends a herald to fetch a gift. | Narrow the claim to this contest or call it their first formally organized prize. |
| R1-003 | Note L128 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note says Odysseus received `ἐπητής` “from Penelope’s own report at 13.332.” Athena, not Penelope, directly calls him civil, quick-witted, and steady-souled there. | Book 13.329 introduces Athena’s speech; at 13.332 she says `ἐπητής`, `ἀγχίνοος`, and `ἐχέφρων`. | Attribute the earlier praise to Athena. |
| R1-004 | Note L175 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.97 | The note places Odysseus “downstairs tending torches” during Eurynome’s remark. He is near the threshold and tends braziers only later, after Penelope’s audience. Its claim that he cannot “read” fulfillment is also unsupported because he has met adult Telemachus. | Eurynome speaks at 175–176. Odysseus takes his station beside the braziers only at 343–344; father and son recognized each other in Book 16. | Keep the beard-condition connection, but remove the chronology and unsupported exception. |

# Reviewer 1 Report

The translation is generally accurate, energetic, and attentive to the passage's shifts between athletic challenge, comic narrative, and divine legal negotiation. Five firm issues remain: an omitted negation, a changed civic role, an agency reversal in the surety proverb, and two misleading note claims.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | Note L215; 8.223-228 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.88 | "Heracles and Eurytus, who challenged gods and died for it" assigns Eurytus' consequence to both heroes. Both rivaled immortals in archery, but only Eurytus is said to have died for challenging Apollo. | Plural `οἵ ... ἀθανάτοισιν ἐρίζεσκον` includes both; singular `ἔθανεν μέγας Εὔρυτος` and `μιν ... ἔκτανεν` identify Eurytus alone as Apollo's victim. | Say "Heracles and Eurytus, who rivaled gods; Eurytus died for directly challenging Apollo." |
| R1-002 | 8.229 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.99 | "I hurl a spear as far as another man shoots an arrow" omits `οὐκ`, reducing Odysseus' exceptional boast to ordinary parity. He claims to throw as far as no other man can shoot. | `δουρὶ δʼ ἀκοντίζω ὅσον οὐκ ἄλλος τις ὀιστῷ`: "with a spear I cast as far as no other man does with an arrow." | Restore the negation and exclusive scope. |
| R1-003 | 8.258-260 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.96 | "Chosen stewards" changes the nine men's explicit public role as appointed judges or umpires. | R1-004 | 8.351 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.94 | "Worthless the pledges you take for worthless men" makes Poseidon the recipient of a pledge. | R1-005 | Note L364; 8.364-366 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.84 | The note says the divine bath formula differs from its mortal counterpart "only" by ending in Paphos.

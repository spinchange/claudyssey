# Adjudication Report

The closing movement of Book 14 is translated with strong control of narrative
sequence, ritual action, and the cloak-fable's rhetoric. All eleven firm
reviewer proposals are sustained, with four duplicate pairs merged into seven
distinct findings. One `MODERATE` note-level claim reverses Eumaeus' larger
relationship to Odysseus' deceptions; the other six findings are `MINOR` local
distortions in address, agency, speech force, or explanatory commentary. No
fixed-wording-only `FORMULA` deviation is counted.

## Especially Successful Renderings

- **14.393-406:** The wager and Eumaeus' ironic refusal preserve the two
  conditions, the proposed penalty, its deterrent purpose, and the moral
  absurdity of praying to Zeus after killing a protected guest.
- **14.414-438:** The sacrifice retains the ordered sequence of bristles,
  prayer, stunning and slaughter, raw first portions in fat, barley, roasting,
  sevenfold division, divine share, and the honored chine.
- **14.475-502:** The cloak-fable vividly preserves the freezing ambush, lone
  tunic, whispered appeal, invented dream, Thoas' rapid departure, and the
  teller's relief beneath the abandoned cloak.

## Calibration Implications

Vocative `δαιμόνιε` should be judged semantically as well as against the fixed
register: a supernatural paraphrase remains reportable when it asserts divine
contact. English subject continuity must be checked against explicit Greek
contrastive pronouns, and calibrated speech-act force applies when `ἄνωγον`
assigns a command role even though the result is otherwise clear. Explanatory
notes must separate real dramatic irony from false grammatical double
reference, local recognition of a tale's profit from global immunity to
deception, and an emotional culmination from the literal final line.

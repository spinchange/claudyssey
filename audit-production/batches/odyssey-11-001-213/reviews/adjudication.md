# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed three findings and Reviewer 2 proposed three. The shared
proposals about Crimea's name and the oar interval are duplicate pairs and are
merged, yielding four distinct findings. The Crimea proposal is modified:
the modern name comes through `Qırım`, whose earlier etymology is disputed, so
the note's error is presenting descent from “Cimmerian” as settled rather than
a conclusively disproved derivation. The other findings are sustained: Circe
is Helios' daughter, not granddaughter; the two oar passages begin forty-four
line numbers apart, not thirty; and `δικασπόλος` denotes a judge or dispenser
of justice, not a lawmaker. No fixed-wording-only formula deviation is counted.

## Calibration Implications

Modern etymological claims should be qualified when the immediate transmission
history is known but the deeper origin remains disputed; an uncertain
derivation cannot be presented as fact or rejected more categorically than the
evidence permits. Divine genealogies should be checked against explicit
Homeric parentage. Numerical structural claims require direct comparison of
the cited line numbers. Concrete civic titles remain auditable: administering
judgment is not interchangeable with legislating even when both can belong to
elite public authority.

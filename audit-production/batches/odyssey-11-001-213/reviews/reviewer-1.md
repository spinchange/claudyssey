# Reviewer 1

The verse translation is highly faithful through the voyage, necromantic rite,
Tiresias' prophecy, and Anticleia's report. The firm problems occur in the
notes: two factual errors are concentrated in the Cimmerian gloss, and one
structural interval is materially miscounted.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 11.14 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.97 | Saying Crimea “carries” the Cimmerians' name presents a false etymology as fact. Ancient writers associated Cimmerians with the region and named the Cimmerian Bosporus for them, but the modern name *Crimea* derives through Turkic *Qırım*, not from “Cimmerian.” | The Greek names `Κιμμερίων ἀνδρῶν` and supplies their dark setting (14–19); it makes no etymological claim about the modern regional name. | Retain the ancient geographical association while removing the parenthetical etymology. |
| R1-002 | 11.14 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.99 | The phrase “Helios' own grandchild Circe” gives Circe the wrong generation. In the poem Circe is Helios' daughter, not his granddaughter. | Circe's established Homeric parentage is directly from Helios (`Κίρκη ... θυγάτηρ Ἠελίοιο`, 10.138–139). | Change “grandchild” to “daughter.” |
| R1-003 | 11.77 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.99 | The note says the oar image returns “thirty lines later,” but Tiresias introduces the second oar at 11.121, forty-four line numbers after 11.77 (and still forty-one after Elpenor's speech ends at 80). | Elpenor requests the tomb-oar at 77–78; Tiresias' inland-oar instruction begins at 121. | Replace the numerical interval with “about forty lines later,” or simply “later in Tiresias' prophecy.” |

Counts by severity: 0 `CRITICAL`, 0 `MAJOR`, 0 `MODERATE`, 3 `MINOR` — 3 firm findings total.

Especially successful renderings:

1. Lines 23–50 reproduce the ordered necromantic protocol with its offerings,
   victims, crowding dead, commands to the crew, and guarded blood fully intact.
2. Lines 100–137 keep Tiresias' long conditional prophecy lucid, especially the
   Thrinacian alternatives and the concrete recognition-sign of the inland oar.
3. Lines 181–203 preserve the social detail and emotional progression of
   Anticleia's report, from Penelope and Telemachus through Laertes' seasonal
   beds to her own cause of death.

| 11.91 note | 0.71 | “Blood is the medium of truth-telling, not of identity” sounds universal, yet Anticleia drinks and “at once” recognizes Odysseus at 153. | In context the sentence may be limited to Tiresias, who explicitly recognizes Odysseus before drinking and asks for blood in order to speak unerring prophecy. |

# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 23.1–186

## Overall Assessment

The translation is highly effective in Penelope’s skeptical questioning, the suspended recognition scene, the false-wedding strategy, and Athena’s beautification of Odysseus. The nine sustained findings comprise two meaningful grammatical distortions and seven localized verse-or-note problems involving lexical force, physical staging, recurrence history, comparative scope, or grammatical attribution.

Thirteen firm reviewer IDs resolve to nine distinct proposal keys. Four true duplicate pairs were merged. All nine distinct proposals are sustained, though “bullied his son” is reduced to MINOR because it retains repeated coercive mistreatment while weakening the more direct force of `βιόωντο`. The relative-clause error at 177–180 remains MODERATE because it transfers Odysseus’ construction of the bridal chamber to the bed and prematurely alters the recognition test’s setup.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal was rejected or remains unresolved. The tooth-fence occurrence finding concerns a materially inaccurate explanatory history, not fixed-wording-only FORMULA conformance.

His construction of the bed is disclosed separately in his ensuing response, so the English both removes chamber authorship and prematurely changes the test’s setup. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **23.35–40:** Penelope’s demand for evidence preserves her skepticism and the contrast between Odysseus alone and the suitors assembled together.
2. **23.93–110:** The alternating recognition and nonrecognition, Penelope’s stunned silence, and her appeal to hidden mutual signs maintain the scene’s carefully suspended certainty.
3. **23.153–165:** Eurynome’s bathing of Odysseus and Athena’s beautification, including the gold-over-silver craftsman simile, retain their imagery, divine agency, and rhetorical architecture.

## Calibration Implications

- Modern verbs such as “bully” should be tested for whether they retain explicit ancient force or violence, with severity moderated when repeated coercion survives.
- An English dash or omitted verse number does not establish unfinished Greek syntax; the clause itself must be parsed.
- Preserve the calibrated culpability of the `ἀτασθαλίαι` family rather than shifting it toward cognitive blindness.
- Numerical claims about repeated words or formula histories must distinguish Greek occurrences from translator-created repetition.
- Physical gestures joined by coordination and participles must retain their correct objects.
- Comparative scope is material when it changes a recognition claim from greater certainty into superiority over third parties.
- Social classification and literal glosses in recurrence notes must be checked at every cited location.
- Relative-pronoun gender can decisively identify an architectural antecedent and should not be overridden by nearby narrative knowledge.
- No fixed-wording-only FORMULA variation should enter semantic adjudication.

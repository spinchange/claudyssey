# Reviewer 1 Report

The translation is consistently strong across this passage, especially in its handling of Athena's guidance, the palace description, and Odysseus' supplication. I found two localized semantic deviations: one obscures the palace's unfailing abundance, and the other replaces an explicitly manly characterization of Laodamas with a different moral quality.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 7.98-99 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.80 | "What they had never failed" most naturally says that the Phaeacian leaders never failed to drink and eat. The Greek instead explains that they continually possessed an abundant supply. The perpetual feasting remains inferable, but its material basis in unfailing provisions is displaced. | `πίνοντες καὶ ἔδοντες· ἐπηετανὸν γὰρ ἔχεσκον` states that they drank and ate because they had abundance continually or unfailingly. | Make the continuing supply explicit, such as "for their abundance never failed" or "for they always had plenty." |
| R1-002 | 7.170 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.97 | "Kind-hearted Laodamas" replaces an epithet denoting manliness or love of manly valor with an unrelated ethical characterization. Alcinous' special affection for his son is separately expressed in line 171 and does not compensate for the lost characterization. | `ἀγαπήνορα Λαοδάμαντα`; `ἀγαπήνωρ` means manly, valor-loving, or devoted to manly excellence. | Use "manly Laodamas," "valiant Laodamas," or another rendering that preserves the epithet's heroic force. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **7.14-17:** Athena's protective mist and its purpose are clear; "needle him with words" gives `κερτομέοι τʼ ἐπέεσσι` effective social and rhetorical force.
2. **7.84-94:** The palace's radiance, metallic architecture, and Hephaestus' deathless watchdogs retain concrete detail and accumulating wonder.
3. **7.142-154:** The translation preserves the exact dramatic sequence of the supplication, the mist dispersing, the hall's astonishment, the plea, and the seat in the ashes.

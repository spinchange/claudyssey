# Reviewer 1

The translation is consistently accurate and often handles Homeric syntax, speech, and narrative irony with considerable precision. I found one clear agency shift in the verse and one limited factual overstatement in an explanatory note; neither affects the broader coherence of the passage.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 3.84-85 | GRAMMAR | MODERATE | 0.97 | The English transfers the sacking of Troy from Odysseus to Nestor. In the Greek, Odysseus is the subject of both fighting alongside Nestor and sacking the city; "when you sacked" instead makes Nestor the grammatical agent of the second action. | `δίου Ὀδυσσῆος ... ὅν ποτέ φασι / σὺν σοὶ μαρνάμενον Τρώων πόλιν ἐξαλαπάξαι`: the accusative `ὅν` (Odysseus) governs the infinitive `ἐξαλαπάξαι`, while `σὺν σοὶ μαρνάμενον` describes him fighting with Nestor. | Keep Odysseus as agent of both actions: for example, "who once, they say, fought at your side and sacked the Trojans' city." |
| R1-002 | 3.245 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.89 | The note says Nestor "has outlasted three generations of men," which implies that all three generations have passed away before him. The verse says that he has ruled over three generations, not that he has survived all three; the traditional picture is that he lives into and rules the third. The translated verse itself is accurate. | `τρὶς γὰρ δή μίν φασιν ἀνάξασθαι γένεʼ ἀνδρῶν` means that he is said to have ruled over three generations of men. | Say that Nestor "has lived through three generations" or "is ruling his third generation," avoiding the claim that he has outlived all three. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **3.14-28:** "not a scrap of it," "close-woven speech," and the paired "your own heart will see" / "a god will prompt" preserve both Telemachus' social hesitation and the passage's balanced human-divine motivation.
2. **3.55-62:** Athena's prayer is lucid and ceremonially controlled, while "she herself was accomplishing it all" precisely retains the narrator's irony in `καὶ αὐτὴ πάντα τελεῦτα`.
3. **3.151-179:** The compressed account of the divided returns is especially strong: "lay on our anger," "aching for home," "the man of shifting counsels," and "measured the great gulf" preserve action, characterization, and nautical imagery without sacrificing clarity.

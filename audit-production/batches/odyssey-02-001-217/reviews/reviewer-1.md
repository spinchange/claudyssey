Overall, the translation is semantically close and often strong in preserving the public rhetoric of the assembly, especially the escalating speech acts around Telemachus, Antinous, Halitherses, and Eurymachus. I find one firm material issue: a local but consequential agency shift in the marriage-arrangement language at 2.53-54.

Especially successful renderings:

- Lines 35-38: "rejoiced at the omen of the word" and the scepter handoff preserve both the public assembly mechanics and the kledon force of `φήμῃ`.
- Lines 70-79: Telemachus' appeal keeps the legal, emotional, and civic logic of his complaint clear, including the contrast between recoverable public debt and the present "pains past any remedy."
- Lines 161-170: Halitherses' warning strongly preserves the omen-reader's confidence, the rolling force of `μέγα πῆμα κυλίνδεται`, and the urgent alternation between communal action and the suitors stopping themselves.

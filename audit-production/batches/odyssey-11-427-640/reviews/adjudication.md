# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed four findings and Reviewer 2 proposed three. The proposals
about Achilles' “hour” at 11.501 and Ajax's silence in the 11.563 note are
duplicate pairs and are merged. Three distinct findings are sustained: the
11.455 note miscounts the distance between Penelope's exemption and
Agamemnon's generalization; “even for an hour” gives Achilles' indefinite
brief return an unsupported precise duration and the note repeats it; and the
11.563 note falsely says that every other ghost speaks. The 11.438 and 11.577
proposals are rejected because their English remains defensible without a
secure material change. No fixed-wording-only formula deviation is counted.

## Calibration Implications

Numerical claims in notes should be checked directly even when “shortly after”
would preserve the interpretive point. Indefinite duration should not be
converted into a precise unit and then made the basis of commentary. Claims of
uniqueness must identify the relevant comparison class: Ajax uniquely refuses
a direct appeal, but many shades do not speak. Conversely, conventional
English magnitude language should not be treated as exact enumeration without
a material contextual consequence, and historical units require attention to
both their linear and area senses before a dimensional error is reported.

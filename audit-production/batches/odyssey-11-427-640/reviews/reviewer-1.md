# Reviewer 1 — Odyssey 11.427–640

## Overall assessment

This is a forceful and mostly exact rendering of the exchanges with Achilles and Ajax and of the closing underworld tableaux. Four localized issues meet the reporting threshold: two unsupported numerical sharpenings, a materially wrong scale for Tityus, and a note that makes Ajax uniquely silent despite several explicitly present silent shades. The remaining concerns either preserve their force in context or involve defensible lexical choices. Fixed-register wording alone has not been counted.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 11.438 | `ADDITION` | `MINOR` | 0.78 | “We died in our thousands” converts the Greek's unnumbered “many” into a substantially stronger numerical scale. The general point that Helen occasioned many deaths remains, but the source does not quantify them by thousands. | `Ἑλένης μὲν ἀπωλόμεθʼ εἵνεκα πολλοί` is simply “many of us perished for Helen's sake.” | Retain the indefinite quantifier: “many of us died” or equivalent. |
| R1-002 | 11.501 and note | `ADDITION` | `MINOR` | 0.82 | “Even for an hour” gives Achilles a precise unit of desired return where he asks only to come back briefly; the note then treats the invented unit as emphatic fact (“One hour, for the father”). | `μίνυνθά περ` means “even for a little while/briefly,” with no expressed hour. | Use “even briefly” or “even for a little while,” and keep the note's emphasis qualitative rather than numerical. |
| R1-003 | 11.563 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.91 | “Every other ghost talks” falsely makes Ajax the sole silent shade. Patroclus and Antilochus have just arrived with Achilles at 467–470 and never speak; the catalogue also presents numerous other shades without speeches. Ajax's refusal is exceptional because he is directly addressed and pointedly withholds an answer, not because every other ghost in the scene speaks. | `καὶ Πατροκλῆος καὶ ἀμύμονος Ἀντιλόχοιο` explicitly includes the two silent companions; `ὁ δέ μʼ οὐδὲν ἀμείβετο` marks Ajax's non-answer to Odysseus. | Narrow the claim to “every other shade whom Odysseus directly addresses answers,” or simply stress Ajax's refusal of a direct appeal. |
| R1-004 | 11.577 and note | `LEXICAL` | `MODERATE` | 0.95 | “Nine roods” materially shrinks Tityus. A Homeric `πέλεθρον` is roughly one hundred Greek feet (about thirty metres); an English linear rood is only about 16.5–24 feet (roughly five to seven metres), and “rood” more commonly denotes an area. The resulting body is only about one-fifth to one-quarter of the stated span, while the note repeats the faulty measure. | `ἐπʼ ἐννέα κεῖτο πέλεθρα` makes him stretch across nine plethra, approximately nine hundred Greek feet in total. | Retain “nine plethra,” with a gloss, or use “nine hundred feet” / “nine hundred-foot lengths” to preserve the giant scale. |

## Especially successful renderings

1. **11.481–491** preserves both Odysseus' attempt to console Achilles and Achilles' stark preference for the poorest life over sovereignty among the dead.
2. **11.508–516** neatly balances Neoptolemus' excellence in counsel with his refusal to yield to anyone in battle.
3. **11.543–564** gives Ajax's isolation, Odysseus' remorseful appeal, and the shade's unanswered departure their full dramatic force.

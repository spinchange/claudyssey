# Reviewer 2

## Overall assessment

The translation is semantically strong across the encounters with Achilles,
Ajax, the punished dead, and Heracles, and it preserves unusually dense action
without flattening the passage. I found three firm, localized issues: an
unsupported exact duration in Achilles' wish and two explanatory-note
overstatements, one about line proximity and one about the uniqueness of Ajax's
silence.

The asserted immediacy materially sharpens the note's account of the speech's self-contradiction. | Penelope is exempted at `ἀλλʼ οὐ σοί ... φόνος ... ἔκ γε γυναικός` and praised through `περίφρων Πηνελόπεια` (11.444-446); `οὐκέτι πιστὰ γυναιξίν` occurs at 11.456 after the intervening family recollection and landing advice. | Replace “one line after” with “shortly after” or identify the intervening movement of Agamemnon's thought. |

## Especially successful renderings

1. **11.510-540** preserves every stage of the deliberately father-facing
   Neoptolemus report: counsel, open battle, the horse, safe departure, and
   Achilles' joyful response.
2. **11.543-567** renders Ajax's posture, Odysseus' conciliatory appeal, the
   unanswered departure, and Odysseus' own divided impulse with exceptional
   clarity.
3. **11.582-600** keeps the mechanics of Tantalus' and Sisyphus' punishments
   precise, including the repeated temporal structure and the bodily details of
   thirst, strain, sweat, and dust.

## Concerns Considered

| 11.438 | 0.61 | “We died in our thousands” gives a more concrete scale than plural `πολλοί` (“many”). | In the Trojan-war context “thousands” is a conventional, non-exact intensification of mass loss, and its material effect is too slight for a firm addition. |
| 11.568-570 | 0.57 | “Delivering law” can suggest legislation, whereas `θεμιστεύοντα` here describes Minos dispensing judgments. | The immediately following line has the dead pleading cases before the seated lord, and the note explicitly calls Minos a sitting judge with jurisdiction; the coherent passage prevents a consequential role change. |
| 11.577 | 0.66 | “Nine roods” substitutes an English land-area measure (and, in an obsolete sense, a much shorter linear pole) for nine `πέλεθρα`, potentially changing Tityus' scale. | “Rood” has a traditional poetic use as a general land measure, and the Greek construction itself can evoke the ground-area his body covers; without a stable unit equivalence, the degree of dimensional distortion is uncertain. |

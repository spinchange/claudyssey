# Adjudication: *Odyssey* 1.156–444

## Overall Assessment

The verse translation is highly faithful in narrative sequence, speech force, characterization, and local poetic effect. The sustained findings consist of one `MODERATE` error in the explanatory note at 1.184, which reverses the direction of trade, and five `MINOR` violations of the authoritative formula and word-policy register. The proposed errors at 1.280 and 1.398 remain plausible but below the materiality threshold; the claimed agency reversal at 1.436 is rejected because Telemachus is the defensible and syntactically preferable subject.

| R1 concern, R2-002 | 1.280 | **Uncertain** | `ἐρέτῃσιν ἐείκοσιν` explicitly denotes twenty rowers, whereas “twenty oars” names their equipment. Nevertheless, the imperative “Man a ship” supplies the human crew, and a twenty-oared ship conventionally implies the corresponding rowers. The difference is real, but its materiality remains below the firm-finding threshold. |
| R1-005 | 1.398 | **Uncertain** | `ληίσσατο` explicitly presents the slaves as booty or plunder. “Won” is less direct about seizure, but in the epic phrase “slaves that … won” it can still denote acquisition by conquest. Their enslaved status and Odysseus’ acquisition remain explicit, leaving the claimed material weakening uncertain. |
| R2-005 | 1.436 | **Rejected** | The unexpressed subject of `ὤιξεν` can and most naturally does shift to Telemachus, who is already salient as object `ἑ` in 434 and remains the subject of the coordinated actions `ἕζετο`, `ἔκδυνε`, and `ἔμβαλε` in 437–438. Making Eurycleia open the doors would instead require another immediate unmarked subject change at 437. “He opened” is defensible. |

The wording is narrower without producing a material distortion. |
| R2 concern | 1.330 | **Rejected** | `κλίμακα ... ὑψηλήν` more literally describes a high or tall staircase, but “steep stairway” is spatially coherent and does not materially alter Penelope’s descent. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **1.225–229:** “For plainly no man here brought his own share” effectively unpacks `οὐκ ἔρανος`, while “burn with anger” preserves the force of `νεμεσσήσαιτο`.
2. **1.267–278:** Athena’s uncertainty formula, commands to Telemachus, and registered bride-gift refrain are preserved with strong rhetorical progression; “I charge you” is especially effective for `ἄνωγα`.
3. **1.374–380:** Telemachus’ ultimatum preserves the repeated `νήποινον` logic through “nothing paid” and “nothing paid for you,” carrying the argument from uncompensated consumption to uncompensated death.

## Calibration Implications

The low Jaccard score confirms that production review still requires independent dual review and adjudication. Formula-policy enforcement was uneven: each reviewer found a clear fixed-rendering violation that the other missed, and Reviewer 2 treated the sex-specific slave vocabulary only as a concern despite the register’s explicit rule.

Future calibration should also distinguish an actual agency reversal from Homeric unmarked subject shifts across coordinated verbs. Finally, the 1.280 and 1.398 disagreements show the need to assess compensation before promoting lexical differences: “man” restores the crew at 1.280, while the martial sense available in “won” makes the loss at 1.398 plausible but not firmly material.

# Reviewer 2 — Semantic Review

The batch is semantically strong overall, with especially effective handling of Odysseus’ self-command, Penelope’s prayer, and the mill-woman’s omen. I found four firm issues, all minor: one unsupported provenance claim, one loss of a named mythological agent, and two inaccurate chronological claims in the notes. No moderate, major, or critical errors were found.

| R2-001 | 20.2–4 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.88 | The note says every layer of Odysseus’ bedding is stolen livestock and a by-product of his devoured estate. ## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **20.14–30:** The mother-dog and roasting-paunch similes are rendered vividly while preserving their contrasting psychological functions: the heart’s protective rage and the body’s restless strategic calculation.

2. **20.61–76, 78–90:** Penelope’s prayer, the Pandareus myth, and the consoling dream retain their intricate sequence of annihilation wish, divine nurture, interrupted marriage, and imagined reunion.

3. **20.97–120:** Odysseus’ double-omen request and its fulfillment through thunder and the exhausted mill-woman’s speech remain exceptionally clear, including the labor embodied in her curse.

## Concerns Considered

| Lines | Confidence | Concern | Why not counted |

| 20.14 and note | 0.57 | “Soft-boned pups” adds a specific anatomical image to `ἀμαλῇσι`, which means tender, weak, or soft. | The wording effectively conveys the puppies’ vulnerability and does not materially change the guarding simile. |
| 20.116 note | 0.64 | “Zeus thunders for her” may imply that the thunder answers the mill-woman’s curse, although the thunder occurs before she speaks and she interprets it as an available sign. | The note can be read thematically rather than as a strict causal sequence, and the larger account correctly presents her speech and the thunder as paired omens. |
| 20.121 | 0.72 | “The guilty would pay” suppresses Odysseus as the expressed agent of punishment in `φάτο γὰρ τίσασθαι ἀλείτας`, “he said/thought that he would punish the offenders.” | In this revenge context, English “they would pay” readily implies that Odysseus will exact payment, leaving the practical agency recoverable. |
| 20.138 | 0.62 | “When he began to think of rest and sleep” treats `μιμνήσκοιτο` as private reflection, though the response to it suggests that he mentioned or called attention to sleep. | The verb permits “bethink oneself of,” and Penelope could respond to contextual signs without a formal request. |

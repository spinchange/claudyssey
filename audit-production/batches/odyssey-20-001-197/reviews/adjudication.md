# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 20.1–197

## Overall Assessment

The translation is vivid and generally faithful, especially in Odysseus’ self-command, Athena’s reassurance, Penelope’s prayer, and the double-omen sequence. Seven distinct findings are sustained from nine firm reviewer proposals. Two true duplicate pairs were merged. Several disputed issues cross the threshold because the notes state inference as fact or materially alter agency, chronology, or comparison class.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal remains unresolved.

## Rejected and Unresolved Proposals

No firm proposal was rejected or left unresolved.

The competing below-threshold arguments for two disputed findings were considered:

- At note L2, the suitors’ broader consumption of Odysseus’ livestock makes the oxhide’s connection to his depleted estate plausible. The finding is nevertheless sustained because “every layer” presents that unattested provenance as certain rather than interpretive.
- At 120–121, “the guilty would pay” conventionally suggests that Odysseus will make them pay. The practical agent is therefore recoverable, but the English still changes the grammatical proposition from his planned vengeance to their future suffering. The result is a localized MINOR finding.

Other concerns remain below threshold:

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **20.18–30:** Odysseus’ self-address preserves the canine wordplay, disciplined endurance, and grotesquely comic paunch simile.
2. **20.33–53:** Athena’s exchange with Odysseus retains both his strategic anxiety and her raid-scaled reassurance.
3. **20.97–120:** The request for two independent omens and its fulfillment through thunder and the exhausted mill-woman’s speech remain exceptionally clear.

## Calibration Implications

- Plausible estate-wide inference should be marked as inference rather than converted into exact object provenance.
- Book boundaries do not establish elapsed nights or days; chronology must follow explicit sleep and dawn markers.
- Named mythological agents should not be reduced to generic natural forces when the source deliberately changes vocabulary.
- Temporal deixis in notes should follow the continuous narrative day rather than the later book division containing an event.
- Superlatives inherit their comparison class from the local group unless the text explicitly expands it.
- Agentive control of an infinitive remains auditable even when surrounding revenge context lets readers infer the omitted agent.
- Numbered or priority patterns in commentary require a clearly defined and complete comparison class.

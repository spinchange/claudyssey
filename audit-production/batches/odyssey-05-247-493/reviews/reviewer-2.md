# Reviewer 2

The translation is consistently attentive to action, agency, imagery, and line-to-line syntax. I found three material but localized fidelity issues: one moderate distortion and two minor additions or agency shifts. No critical or major errors emerged.

| R2-001 | 5.256–257 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.88 | “For ballast” assigns the brushwood a technical purpose not stated in the Greek. The passage says only that Odysseus heaped abundant brushwood onto the structure; unlike the preceding wickerwork, its function is not specified. | `πολλὴν δʼ ἐπεχεύατο ὕλην` means “and he heaped on much brushwood.” The explicit purpose clause `κύματος εἶλαρ ἔμεν` belongs to the surrounding willow fence. | Remove “for ballast” and retain the concrete action, such as “heaped brushwood over it.” |
| R2-002 | 5.475–477 and note L477 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.93 | “Grown from a single stock,” reinforced by the note’s “sharing one root,” turns two plants growing from the same place into a single-rooted botanical organism. The note then builds its wild-and-cultivated interpretation partly on that unsupported common root. | `δοιοὺς ... θάμνους, / ἐξ ὁμόθεν πεφυῶτας` identifies “two thickets/bushes growing from the same place.” Their close interlacing is separately expressed at 480–481; no shared stock or root is stated. | Render them as “two thickets growing from the same spot,” retaining their interlacing, and remove the note’s claim that they share one root. |
| R2-003 | 5.491–493 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.96 | “When she had folded his dear eyelids shut” assigns the final covering action directly to Athena. In Greek, Sleep is the masculine agent that covers Odysseus’ eyelids and releases him from exhaustion. Athena remains the cause by pouring Sleep over his eyes, but the closing personification is lost. | After Athena pours `ὕπνον` over his eyes, masculine `ἀμφικαλύψας` makes Sleep the agent “having covered his dear eyelids.” | Let Sleep retain the final agency: Athena pours sleep over him, “and it covered his dear eyelids” and ended his exhaustion. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

- **5.272–277:** The celestial-navigation sequence clearly preserves the Pleiades, late-setting Boötes, the Bear’s watch on Orion, her exemption from Ocean, and the instruction to keep her on the left.
- **5.306–312:** Odysseus’ wish for a heroic death at Troy retains the contrast between funeral rites and transmitted fame there and his feared anonymous death at sea.
- **5.432–435:** The octopus simile precisely preserves the reversal between pebbles clinging to the animal’s suckers and Odysseus’ skin remaining on the rocks.

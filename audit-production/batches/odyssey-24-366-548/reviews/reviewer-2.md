# Reviewer 2

The translation is consistently vivid and generally accurate, particularly in the domestic reunion, Medon’s testimony, and the final battle. The principal weaknesses lie in the notes: several confident thematic claims exceed or misread the evidence, most importantly reversing which faction constitutes the majority in the final assembly. One clear grammatical typo also remains in the verse translation.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | Note L376 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.96 | The note’s claim that “three generations” of the family have spoken the wish is inaccurate. The earlier speakers it identifies are Menelaus at 4.341 and Telemachus at 18.235; Menelaus is not a generation in Odysseus’ family line, while Odysseus himself is absent from the claimed sequence. Book 17 merely reports Menelaus’ earlier words. | Laertes speaks the wish at 24.376; the cited parallels supply Menelaus and Telemachus, not grandfather, father, and son within one family. | Describe this as the formula’s final recurrence across Menelaus, Telemachus, and Laertes, without presenting them as three generations of a single family. |
| R2-002 | Note L427 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.94 | The note converts Eupeithes’ indefinite “many brave men” into exactly “six hundred men” and insists that “the arithmetic … is true.” The Odyssey supplies no crew total here, and the figure depends on importing a conventional fifty-man complement for each of Odysseus’ twelve ships. | `πολλοὺς ... ἐσθλοὺς` (24.427) means “many … brave men”; no numeral or crew size appears in this passage. | Retain “many men” or explicitly label six hundred as a later reconstruction rather than a fact stated by the poem. |
| R2-003 | Notes L451 and L463 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.99 | Both notes reverse the assembly’s division by claiming that more than half march to arms and that peace is accepted only by a minority. More than half rise after Halitherses’ warning; the “others” remain, reject his speech, follow Eupeithes, and then rush for their weapons. The vendetta faction is therefore the minority. | `ἡμίσεων πλείους` rise at 24.463–464; `τοὶ δʼ ... μίμνον` introduces the others who remain, and these are the group for whom `οὐ ... ἅδε μῦθος`, who obey Eupeithes and `ἐπὶ τεύχεα ἐσσεύοντο` (24.464–466). | State that a majority accepts Halitherses’ counsel and departs, while the remaining minority follows Eupeithes to arms. Revise the resulting political interpretation accordingly. |
| R2-004 | Note L477 | AMBIGUITY | MODERATE | 0.82 | The note resolves `ἔκλησιν θέωμεν` categorically as the gods literally erasing grief from the fathers’ minds. The phrase can denote forgetfulness, cessation of grievance, or an imposed amnesty; the civic settlement does not require a claim of supernatural memory deletion. | `ἔκλησιν θέωμεν ... φόνοιο` (24.484–485) asks that an `ἔκλησις` of the killings be established, followed by restored mutual affection, wealth, and peace. | Preserve the interpretive range: divine forgetfulness, cessation of vengeance, or political amnesty. Avoid presenting literal mind-erasure as settled fact. |
| R2-005 | 509 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.99 | “We have excelled **from of old**” contains a duplicated preposition and is ungrammatical. | `ἐξ ἀρχῆς` (24.509) means “from the beginning/from of old.” | Use either “we have excelled from of old” or “we have excelled from the beginning.” |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **366–371:** Laertes’ washing and Athena-assisted transformation are rendered with clear agency and a convincing visual progression.
2. **413–420:** Rumor’s movement through Ithaca, the communal lament, and the contrasting burial arrangements are lucidly distinguished.
3. **439–450:** Medon’s report preserves the alternating manifestations of the god beside Odysseus and within the hall.

## Calibration Implications

Broad spatial and temporal claims in notes must leave room for scenes explicitly narrated outside the claimed center. Added pronoun objects are consequential when they alter a character's knowledge or turn a general request for information into a presupposed eyewitness encounter. Namesakes and cross-book speaker lists require direct verification rather than association by name. Proleptic literary comparisons should distinguish an approaching character from one already present in the cited location. Finally, exact formulaic recurrence does not license a semantic universal absent from the repeated Greek: automation owns fixed-wording conformance, while reviewers still own unsupported “always” and “never” claims. Rhetorical shorthand and plausible contextual concretization should remain below threshold when the coherent passage prevents material misunderstanding.

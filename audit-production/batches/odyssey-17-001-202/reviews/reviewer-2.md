# Reviewer 2 Report

## Overall assessment

This is a controlled and largely faithful rendering of Telemachus’ return, his guarded report to Penelope, Theoclymenus’ prophecy, and Odysseus’ preparations to enter the city. The emotional exchanges and repeated Homeric passages are especially effective. I found five localized problems: one question falsely presupposes a sighting of Odysseus, two notes misstate narrative structure or character history, another places Odysseus at the palace before he has left the farm, and the suitors’ prior play becomes an unsupported permanent habit. Fixed-wording-only formula deviations are excluded.

## Especially successful renderings

1. **17.31–60:** Eurycleia’s welcome, Penelope’s embrace, and Telemachus’ controlled instructions preserve the rapid movement between tenderness, danger, and secrecy.
2. **17.107–149:** Telemachus’ relaying of the Pylos and Sparta material remains lucid through an extended embedded speech and retains the force of the deer-and-lion prophecy.
3. **17.151–165:** Theoclymenus’ oath and prophecy, followed by Penelope’s courteous but guarded response, sustain the dramatic irony with unusual clarity.

## Concerns Considered

| 17.23 | 0.58 | “The sun’s heat comes” specifies solar warmth where `ἀλέη` says only that warmth should arise after Odysseus has warmed himself at the fire. | Morning sunlight is a natural contextual source of the additional warmth, and the distinction does not affect the reason for delaying departure. |
| 17.116–117 | 0.68 | “A bolted chariot” supplies a particular fastening method for `ἅρμασι κολλητοῖσιν`, which means a joined, fitted, or compactly constructed chariot. | Bolting is a plausible form of joining, and the construction method has no narrative consequence, leaving the materiality uncertain. |
| Note L108 | 0.62 | Saying Telemachus gives “not one syllable of the ambush” may overlook his earlier statement at 17.47 that he has escaped sheer death. | The note can reasonably refer only to his formal travel report at 17.108–149, which gives no details of the ambush; the earlier allusion does not disclose its mechanics. |
| 17.195–199 and Note L197 | 0.66 | Rendering `σκῆπτρον` as “stick” suppresses the possible verbal link with the staffs or scepters of kings and assembly speakers. | In this immediate context the object is an ordinary walking staff, and the attached note explicitly restores the regal wordplay, making the net rhetorical loss uncertain. |

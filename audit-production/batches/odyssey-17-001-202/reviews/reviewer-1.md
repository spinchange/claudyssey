# Reviewer 1 Audit — Odyssey 17.1–202

## Overall assessment

Overall, the passage is highly faithful and handles repeated Homeric material with particular discipline. Two localized issues merit revision: one verse narrows Penelope’s question into an unsupported implication that Telemachus saw Odysseus, and one note confuses two men named Antiphus.

| R1-001 | 44 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.93 | “What sight of him did you meet?” adds an object absent from the Greek and implies that Telemachus personally saw Odysseus. | R1-002 | Note to 68 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.97 | The note muddles the living Antiphus of 17.68 with the different Antiphus whom the Cyclops killed, and then incorrectly groups Antiphus among the men who spoke for Odysseus in the Book 2 assembly. ## Especially successful renderings

1. Lines 124–146 preserve the lion-and-fawns simile and the relayed Proteus report with both semantic clarity and disciplined correspondence to their earlier appearances.
2. Lines 155–159 render Theoclymenus’ oath and prophecy precisely, retaining the guest-table, hearth, and “planting evil” imagery.
3. Lines 185–202 handle Eumaeus’ divided respect and fear, the masters’ rebukes, and Odysseus’ beggar equipment with strong lexical and social-register fidelity.

# Reviewer 1

The translation handles the reunion, the year on Aeaea, Circe's underworld
directions, and Elpenor's death with sustained clarity. The firm issues are
localized except for one explanatory reading that turns an emphatic form of
address into literal divine possession and builds a larger interpretation on
that added premise.

Especially successful renderings:

1. Lines 388–399 make the companions' physical restoration, recognition, and
   overwhelming reunion legible without flattening the sequence.
2. Lines 508–540 preserve Circe's dense geographical and ritual instructions,
   including the ordered libations, sacrifices, orientation, and control of the
   blood.
3. Lines 551–560 render Elpenor's anti-heroic death with the source's blunt
   progression from drunken isolation through the forgotten ladder to the
   broken neck.

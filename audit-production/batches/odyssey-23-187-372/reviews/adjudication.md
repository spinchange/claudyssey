# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 23.187–372

## Overall Assessment

The second half of Book 23 is rendered with considerable precision, especially in the olive-tree bed construction, Penelope’s recognition, the shipwreck simile, Teiresias’ prophecy, and the compressed catalogue of Odysseus’ wanderings. The eight sustained findings concern three consequential interpretive or grammatical changes and five localized lexical or explanatory-note errors.

Nine firm reviewer IDs resolve to eight distinct proposal keys. One true duplicate pair was merged. All eight distinct proposals are sustained. The “from the sea” finding remains AMBIGUITY because `ἐξ ἁλός` has materially different source and separation construals. The note’s claim that neither spouse’s ordeal outranks the other remains ADDITION/MODERATE: `ἀμφοτέρω` and the balanced clauses include both experiences but do not establish equal rank.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal was rejected or remains unresolved. No fixed-wording-only FORMULA deviation was counted.

| O23-187-372-A-001 | 23.209–210 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | SUSTAINED | “You who in everything understood the most of men” removes the tactful exception in `ἐπεὶ τά περ ἄλλα μάλιστα / ἀνθρώπων πέπνυσο`. ## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **23.187–204:** The olive-tree bed construction preserves the craft sequence, rooted architecture, materials, and emotional force of Odysseus’ proof.
2. **23.225–246:** Penelope’s recognition, the land-and-swimmers simile, and Athena’s suspension of dawn retain their agency, physical imagery, and carefully reversed emotional perspective.
3. **23.300–341:** The compressed account of the wanderings remains lucid and follows the Greek catalogue’s order and recurring syntax with exceptional control.

## Calibration Implications

- Concessive phrases such as `τὰ ἄλλα` must survive when they tactfully except immediate conduct from general praise.
- Exact counts of deceptive narratives require a defined comparison class.
- `ἄτη` must be parsed as entertained delusion or ruinous impulse rather than automatically as later recognition of wrongdoing.
- Consequentially disputed prepositional constructions should remain open or be acknowledged in the note.
- Bed-making verbs should not be attached literally to a rigid frame.
- Dual inclusion and balanced clauses do not by themselves establish equal weight or rank.
- Structural-parallel claims must preserve who issued the earlier command and its content.

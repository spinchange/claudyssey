# Reviewer 1

Overall, the second half of Book 23 is rendered with considerable precision, especially in the bed construction, recognition simile, prophecy recital, and compressed catalogue of Odysseus’ wanderings. I found three moderate and two minor material issues. None is a fixed-wording-only formula deviation.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 23.209–210 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.96 | “You who in everything / understood the most of men” removes Penelope’s explicit qualification “in other respects.” The Greek praises Odysseus’ general wisdom while tactfully excepting his present anger; the English turns this into an unrestricted superlative. | `ἐπεὶ τά περ ἄλλα μάλιστα / ἀνθρώπων πέπνυσο`: `τὰ ἄλλα` means “in other things/respects,” with `περ` reinforcing the concession. | Render “since in all other things you are the wisest of men,” or equivalent language retaining the exception. |
| R1-002 | 23.222–224 and note L218 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.99 | “Not until too late did she lay to heart that folly” changes Helen’s divinely induced delusion into belated recognition that her conduct was foolish. The note then builds a jurisprudence of personal deception on that altered logic, although the Greek names ignorance of the future and divine instigation, not deception by a human speaker. | `θεὸς ὤρορεν` says a god impelled Helen; `τὴν δʼ ἄτην οὐ πρόσθεν ἑῷ ἐγκάτθετο θυμῷ / λυγρήν` says she had not previously admitted or entertained that ruinous delusion in her heart. It does not say she realized her folly too late. | Render along the lines of “before that, she had never entertained the ruinous delusion”; frame the note around divine impulse and ignorance rather than belated self-judgment or deception. |
| R1-003 | 23.281–284 and note L267 | AMBIGUITY | MODERATE | 0.84 | “Death will come to me / from the sea” categorically resolves the consequential ambiguity of `ἐξ ἁλός`, and the note repeats that resolution as the prophecy’s settled content. The phrase has long supported both “out of/from the sea” and “away from the sea,” readings that produce materially different deaths and relations to the preceding inland journey. | `θάνατος δέ μοι ἐξ ἁλὸς ... ἐλεύσεται` permits competing separation/source construals. | Preserve or explicitly acknowledge the ambiguity—e.g. “from, or far from, the sea”—especially in the note, rather than presenting one disputed construction as certain. |
| R1-004 | 23.326 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.97 | “The thronging Sirens” gives `ἁδινάων` a spatial crowding sense inappropriate to this sound-centered construction, losing their loud, close, or trilling vocal quality. | `Σειρήνων ἁδινάων φθόγγον` explicitly places the epithet beside their `φθόγγος`, “voice/sound.” | Use “the loud-voiced Sirens,” “the clear-toned Sirens,” or another rendering that preserves the sonic characterization. |
| R1-005 | 23.350–353 note L350 | ADDITION | MODERATE | 0.87 | The note converts the coordination of Penelope’s and Odysseus’ trials into a categorical equality: “neither ordeal outranks the other.” `ἀμφοτέρω` establishes that both suffered, but does not assign equal weight or rank to their distinct experiences. The unsupported equality is made the note’s “final settlement” of the double plot. | `πολέων κεκορήμεθʼ ἀέθλων / ἀμφοτέρω` says that both have had their fill of many trials, followed by distinct descriptions of her weeping and his divine restraint. Neither the dual nor the coordination expresses comparative equivalence. | Say that the speech sets their sufferings beside one another or includes both in its reckoning, without claiming that the Greek ranks them equally. |

Severity counts: **CRITICAL 0; MAJOR 0; MODERATE 3; MINOR 2. Total firm findings: 5.**

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **23.187–204:** The olive-tree bed construction preserves the sequence, craft vocabulary, rooted architecture, and emotional force of Odysseus’ proof.
2. **23.225–240:** Penelope’s recognition and the land-to-shipwrecked-swimmers simile retain their agency, imagery, and carefully reversed emotional perspective.
3. **23.300–341:** The compressed account of the wanderings remains lucid and remarkably close to the Greek catalogue’s order and recurring phrasing.

## Concerns Considered

| Lines | Confidence | Concern | Why not counted |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 23.234–235 | 0.61 | “Poseidon has smashed / … driving it under wind and heavy wave” makes Poseidon the natural agent of “driving,” whereas `ἐπειγομένην ἀνέμῳ καὶ κύματι` presents the ship as being pressed by wind and wave. | Poseidon remains the explicit ultimate destroyer, and the English can be understood as describing the means and conditions of his wrecking, so the net agency change is uncertain. |
| 23.240 | 0.57 | “She could not once let her white arms go” is stronger than `οὔ πω πάμπαν ἀφίετο`, “she did not yet wholly release.” | The English works idiomatically as continuous embrace and does not materially alter the reunion’s action. |
| 23.291 | 0.66 | “Spread the packed bedstead” combines a spreading verb with a rigid frame, where `στόρεσαν πυκινὸν λέχος` means making up or spreading the well-built bed. | The preceding line already supplies soft coverings and makes the intended bed-making action recoverable; the remaining problem may be awkward diction rather than material semantic loss. |
| Note L215 | 0.58 | “Five false autobiographies in six books” may use an unclear or inaccurate count of Odysseus’ Ithacan deceptive narratives and their book-span. | Whether shorter self-presentations count as full autobiographies, and whether “six books” means intervening book-number distance, leaves the comparison class insufficiently determinate for a firm numerical finding. |

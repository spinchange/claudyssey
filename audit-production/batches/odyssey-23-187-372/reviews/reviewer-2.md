# Reviewer 2

The second half of Book 23 is rendered with strong overall fidelity, especially in the bed speech, the shipwreck simile, and the restatement of Teiresias’ prophecy. Four material issues remain: one moderate reinterpretation of Penelope’s Helen argument and three minor problems in explanatory chronology, bed-making language, and a claimed structural parallel. Fixed-register wording differences have not been counted.

| R2-001 | 215 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.84 | The claim that Odysseus told “five false autobiographies in six books” is overprecise. Five can be reached only by counting the largely truth-compatible warning to Amphinomus in Book 18 as a fabricated autobiography, while the relevant self-presentations extend from Books 13 through 19 rather than occupying a straightforward six-book span. | The clearly invented identities or life histories are given to Athena in Book 13, Eumaeus in Book 14, Antinous in Book 17, and Penelope in Book 19; the Book 18 speech offers generalized, potentially truthful self-description rather than another invented identity. | Describe a series of false and strategically partial self-presentations without the insecure exact count and duration. |
| R2-002 | 218–224 and note L218 | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MODERATE` | 0.94 | “Not until too late did she lay to heart that folly” changes Helen’s mental action. The note further recasts divine instigation and ignorance of the outcome as “deception,” although no deceiver is identified here. ## Especially Successful Renderings

- **23.187–204:** The construction of the rooted olive bed is concrete and lucid, preserving the sequence from living tree through chamber, bedpost, inlay, and oxhide strap.
- **23.233–240:** The Poseidon-wrecked swimmers simile retains its agency, physical detail, emotional reversal, and application to Penelope’s recognition.
- **23.267–284:** Teiresias’ prophecy is relayed with excellent completeness, including the oar’s mistaken identity, the sacrifices, the return home, and the gentle death from the sea.

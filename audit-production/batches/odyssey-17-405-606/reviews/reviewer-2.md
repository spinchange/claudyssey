# Reviewer 2 — *Odyssey* 17.405–606

The translation is vigorous and generally faithful, with especially strong handling of the Egyptian raid, Antinous’ assault, and Penelope’s account of the household’s depletion. I found six firm, localized issues, all minor. No fixed-wording-only formula deviations are counted.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 407–411 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.95 | The note calls Antinous’ footstool display “applauded,” but the poem supplies no applause or approval. | After the display, `οἱ δʼ ἄλλοι πάντες δίδοσαν`; at 481–487 they rebuke his violence. | Remove “applauded” and distinguish the threat from the others’ charity. |
| R2-002 | 525–527 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.98 | “He swears he has heard” upgrades the stranger’s claim into an oath. | `στεῦται δʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἀκοῦσαι` means he claims or professes to have heard. | Use “he claims,” “he says,” or “he professes.” |
| R2-003 | 550 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note says Penelope pays the clothing promise in Book 23, but Eurynome clothes Odysseus there. | At 23.154–155 `Εὐρυνόμη ταμίη` puts the mantle and tunic around him. | Credit Eurynome while retaining Penelope’s promise in the formula chain. |
| R2-004 | 582 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.93 | “He asks you to wait” softens `ἄνωγεν`, which says the stranger bade or instructed Penelope to wait. | `ἀλλά σε μεῖναι ἄνωγεν ἐς ἠέλιον καταδύντα`. | Use “He bids/tells you to wait.” |
| R2-005 | 586–588 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.91 | The note says Penelope reasons from a premise her hall disproves, as though she denied such men exist. She says no other mortals behave `ὧδε`, to this degree; the suitors are the standard, not a counterexample. | `οὐ γάρ πού τινες ὧδε ... ἀτάσθαλα μηχανόωνται`. | Explain that Penelope calls the suitors unmatched in recklessness. |
| R2-006 | 606 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note says the interview is in “the next book,” but Book 18 intervenes and the interview begins in Book 19. | Book 17 closes at 606; the interview is realized in Book 19. | Replace with “Book 19” or “after the events of Book 18.” |

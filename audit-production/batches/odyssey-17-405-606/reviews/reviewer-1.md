# Reviewer 1 Audit — *Odyssey* 17.405–606

The translation is vivid and generally faithful, especially in the Egyptian tale, the footstool assault, and Penelope’s response to the sneeze omen. I found six localized minor issues: two in the verse and four in the notes. No fixed-register wording differences are counted.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | Note L407 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.92 | The note calls Antinous’ display of the footstool “applauded,” inventing an audience response. The other suitors instead proceed to give the beggar food, and after the stool is thrown they condemn Antinous. | At 409 Antinous merely displays the stool: `θρῆνυν ἑλὼν ὑπέφηνε`. At 411 `οἱ δʼ ἄλλοι πάντες δίδοσαν`; at 481–483 all react indignantly to the assault. | Remove “applauded”; retain the well-supported points that the threat is premeditated and directed at the suppliant. |
| R1-002 | 455 | OMISSION | MINOR | 0.91 | “You would not give so much as a grain of salt from your own stores” omits the recipient: Antinous would not give it even to the person standing beside or dependent upon him. | `οὐ σύ γʼ ἂν ἐξ οἴκου σῷ ἐπιστάτῃ οὐδʼ ἅλα δοίης` includes the dative `σῷ ἐπιστάτῃ`. | Restore the social sting, e.g. “You would not give even salt from your own house to a man standing at your side.” |
| R1-003 | 525 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.92 | “He swears he has heard” turns Eumaeus’ report into an oath. Here `στεῦται` means that the stranger maintains or insists that he has heard the news. | `στεῦται δʼ Ὀδυσῆος ἀκοῦσαι`. There is no oath formula or divine witness. | Use “he insists he has heard,” “he claims to have heard,” or similar wording. |
| R1-004 | Note L550 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.88 | The note says Penelope pays the accumulated clothing “debt” in Book 23. She does not dress Odysseus there: Eurynome supplies his mantle and tunic, while Penelope tests him through the secret of the bed. | At 17.550 Penelope promises `ἕσσω μιν χλαῖνάν τε χιτῶνά τε`. In Book 23 Eurynome dresses Odysseus; Penelope’s decisive test concerns the immovable bed. | Preserve the formula links, but do not claim Penelope literally fulfills the clothing promise in Book 23. |
| R1-005 | Note L570 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.97 | The note says Odysseus dictates that the interview be “alone.” Odysseus specifies sunset, proximity, and the fire, but Eumaeus independently proposes that Penelope speak with the stranger alone. | Odysseus requests sunset and a seat nearer the fire at 569–573. Eumaeus adds `οἴην πρὸς ξεῖνον` at 583–584. | Distinguish the contributions: Odysseus sets the time and physical setting; Eumaeus recommends privacy. |
| R1-006 | Note L606 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note says the firelight interview is set for “the next book.” Book 18 intervenes; Penelope’s extended interview with Odysseus occurs in Book 19. | Line 606 closes the present day. The ensuing book is the Irus and suitor episode of Book 18. | Change “the next book” to “Book 19” or “later that night, after the events of Book 18.” |

# Reviewer 2 — Odyssey 10.192–382

## Overall assessment

The translation is consistently accurate across Circe's first appearance, the
transformation of the crew, Hermes' intervention, and Odysseus' confrontation
with Circe. I found one firm issue, in an explanatory note: it assigns Hermes a
unique role that the poem itself disproves. The verse rendering has no firm
material fidelity problem in this sample.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 10.277 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.92 | The note calls Hermes “the only god who assists in all the wanderings.” Hermes assists here, but he neither assists in every wandering episode nor is he the only divinity to assist Odysseus during the wanderings: most plainly, Leucothea directly saves him in Book 5. The exclusivity claim is therefore false. | At 10.277 `Ἑρμείας χρυσόρραπις ἀντεβόλησεν` introduces Hermes' help in this episode only; nothing in the line supports a poem-wide exclusive role. | Narrow the claim to Hermes' distinctive intervention on Aeaea, or specify the more limited pattern actually meant without claiming that he alone assists throughout the wanderings. |

Counts by severity: **MINOR 1; MODERATE 0; MAJOR 0; CRITICAL 0. Total: 1.**

## Especially successful renderings

1. **10.236–240** precisely preserves the drug's purpose and the cruel split
   between transformed pig bodies and an unchanged mind: `νοῦς ἦν ἔμπεδος,
   ὡς τὸ πάρος περ` becomes “their minds stayed steady, exactly as before.”
2. **10.293–301** clearly retains every operative step in Hermes' instructions:
   the wand-stroke, sword-rush, bed invitation, demanded oath, and danger of
   emasculation.
3. **10.337–345** accurately carries Odysseus' conditional refusal and the
   force of his oath demand through to Circe's immediate compliance.

# Adjudication Report

Reviewer 1 proposed four findings and Reviewer 2 proposed one. All five
proposals identify distinct issues, and all five are sustained. The verse at
10.225 collapses Polites' trustworthiness into affection; the 10.205 note
turns leadership of a lot-assigned search party into a standing deputy rank;
the 10.277 note gives Hermes a false exclusive role in the wanderings; the
10.297 and 10.343 notes misdescribe Calypso's voyage-safeguarding oath as a
precondition for sex; and the 10.304 note denies the protective drug's
explicit causal action. No fixed-wording-only formula deviation is counted.

## Calibration Implications

Coordinated near-synonyms should retain distinct characterization when the
Greek deliberately pairs affection with dependability. Temporary, expressly
assigned leadership does not by itself establish a permanent military rank.
Poem-wide exclusivity claims about divine action require comparison with other
helpers and episodes. Verbal recurrence does not establish identical narrative
function: Book 5's oath protects a departure plan, whereas the Aeaea oath
governs an immediate sexual bargain. Finally, an unstaged action may remain
explicitly causal; the absence of dramatized ingestion cannot override Hermes'
statement that moly prevents Circe's enchantment.

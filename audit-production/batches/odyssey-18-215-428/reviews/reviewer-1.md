# Reviewer 1 Audit — *Odyssey* 18.215–428

The translation is strong in narrative sequence, physical action, and rhetorical voice, especially in Penelope’s gift extraction and Odysseus’ work challenge. I found six localized minor issues: three in the verse and three in the notes. No fixed-wording formula differences are counted.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | Note L235 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.98 | The note claims that “father, son, and family friend” all pray the same wish. Book 17 is Telemachus quoting Menelaus’ Book 4 speech, not a new paternal prayer. | Book 17.123 introduces Menelaus’ quoted reply; 17.132 repeats Menelaus’ 4.341 invocation. | Describe Book 17 as Telemachus’ recitation of Menelaus, followed here by his own appropriation. |
| R1-002 | 274 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.95 | “This bitter shame” changes `ἄχος`, pain or grief, into shame. | `τόδʼ αἰνὸν ἄχος κραδίην καὶ θυμὸν ἱκάνει`. | Use “terrible grief” or “bitter pain.” |
| R1-003 | 389 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.84 | “I will finish you off for good” turns a nonspecific threat to work harm into an explicit death threat. | `ἦ τάχα τοι τελέω κακόν` promises to accomplish evil against him, not specifically death. | Use “I’ll soon do you harm” or “I’ll make you pay.” |
| R1-004 | 406 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.86 | “You are possessed” gives emphatic `δαιμόνιοι` a supernatural diagnosis; madness and divine goading are separately stated. | `δαιμόνιοι, μαίνεσθε`; line 407 separately says `θεῶν νύ τις ὔμμʼ ὀροθύνει`. | Use “Strange men—you have gone mad.” |
| R1-005 | Note L406 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.99 | “Ten hours before it matters” supplies an exact interval absent from the poem. | Evening and the next-day sequence are narrated without hourly duration. | Use a nonquantified description. |
| R1-006 | Note L420 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.97 | The note makes Amphinomus pour the wine. He proposes the libation, but Moulius mixes and serves it. | Amphinomus speaks at 412–421; Moulius serves at 423–425. | Credit Amphinomus with the proposal and Moulius with service. |

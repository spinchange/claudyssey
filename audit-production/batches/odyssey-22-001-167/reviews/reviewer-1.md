# Reviewer 1

The passage is highly effective and generally faithful, especially in its combat sequencing, rhetorical escalation, and preservation of concrete objects. I found three limited semantic problems: one unclear agency relation, one strengthened claim of certainty, and one architectural interpretation presented more specifically than the Greek supports.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 89–91 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.80 | “To see if he might give way” leaves the subordinate subject naturally attachable to Amphinomus, although it is Odysseus whom Amphinomus hopes to make yield. Context permits recovery, but the Greek agency is clearer than the English. | In `εἴ πώς οἱ εἴξειε θυράων`, `οἱ` is the dative beneficiary referring to Amphinomus, while the unexpressed subject of `εἴξειε` is Odysseus: “[to see] whether Odysseus might yield to him from the doors.” | Name Odysseus or recast explicitly: “to see if Odysseus might yield the doors to him.” |
| R1-002 | 139–141 | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.87 | “I am certain” strengthens Melanthius’ expressly qualified belief into certainty. His confidence may be contextually strong, but the source still marks the statement as what he thinks. | `ἔνδον γάρ, ὀΐομαι, οὐδέ πη ἄλλῃ` means “for inside, I think/suppose, and nowhere else.” | Preserve the epistemic qualification: “for it is inside, I think—and nowhere else—”. |
| R1-003 | 136 note; 142–143 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.81 | “Smoke-vents” assigns a specific function to the hall’s openings or narrow ways, and the note compounds this by calling them “the servant’s secret ways.” Neither smoke nor a servant-only secret route is expressed in the Greek. | `ἀνὰ ῥῶγας μεγάροιο` denotes ascent through the hall’s gaps, openings, crevices, or narrow passages; it contains no word for smoke or secrecy. | Use a neutral architectural term such as “through the narrow passages/openings of the hall,” and mark any smoke-vent or servant-route interpretation as conjectural. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. Lines 34–41 preserve the indictment’s accumulating structure—house, slave-women, wife, gods, and human vengeance—and land forcefully on the repeated cords of death.
2. Lines 54–67 handle Eurymachus’ compensation offer and Odysseus’ refusal with excellent rhetorical clarity, preserving the Greek’s payment vocabulary as it turns from restitution to retribution.
3. Lines 79–88 render Eurymachus’ death with unusually close control of the physical sequence: liver, dropped sword, table, spilled feast, forehead, chair, and descending mist.

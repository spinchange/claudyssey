# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 22.1–167

## Overall Assessment

The translation handles the opening slaughter with excellent physical clarity, rhetorical force, and tactical momentum. The ten sustained findings are divided between three localized verse problems and seven explanatory-note errors involving morphology, chronology, staging, recurrence, or interpretive overstatement.

Twelve firm reviewer IDs resolve to ten distinct proposal keys. Two true duplicate pairs were merged. All ten distinct proposals are sustained, though several categories or severities are modified. The lineation proposal at 74–78 is normalized to GRAMMAR because the material problem is loss of the new first-person-plural subject; the ambiguity proposal at 89–91 is likewise normalized to GRAMMAR and reduced to MINOR because context permits recovery.

At 142–143, “smoke-vents” remains a plausible resolution of disputed architectural language and is not independently condemned. The sustained finding concerns the note’s unsupported promotion of that possibility into “the servant’s secret ways.”

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No firm proposal was rejected, left uncertain, or unresolved. No fixed-wording-only FORMULA deviation was counted.

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **22.8–25:** Antinous’ interrupted drink, the arrow through his neck, the blood, overturned table, fouled feast, and futile search for weapons are staged with exceptional physical clarity.
2. **22.45–67:** Eurymachus’ attempt to isolate blame and offer restitution, followed by Odysseus’ absolute refusal, preserves the financial vocabulary and the opposed speech acts.
3. **22.79–125:** The deaths of Eurymachus and Amphinomus, Telemachus’ tactical withdrawal, retrieval of armor, and Odysseus’ transition from bow to spear retain the Greek’s momentum and concrete detail.

## Calibration Implications

- Morphological claims in notes must distinguish finite verbs from participles even when the verse itself is accurate.
- Exact book counts should follow the actual duration of a disguise rather than the hero’s broader absence from the narrative.
- Prospective actions marked by `μέλλω` should not be described as already completed for dramatic effect.
- “Verbatim” requires identity of wording, not merely a strongly marked recurrence.
- Contiguous narration does not license exact minute counts.
- When a new finite verb introduces a new subject and action, English ellipsis must not leave the previous object attached across the line break.
- Pronoun ambiguity should be categorized by the underlying grammatical relationship and moderated when context reliably restores the intended referent.
- Epistemic verbs such as `ὀΐομαι` should not become certainty.
- Disputed architectural vocabulary may be resolved poetically, but notes must mark functional, secret, or socially restricted interpretations as conjecture.
- Formula-history claims remain auditable when they make a substantive uniqueness argument; this is distinct from fixed-wording enforcement.

# Reviewer 1 Audit — Odyssey 15.1–185

## Overall Assessment

This is a strong, generally accurate translation that preserves the passage's narrative sequence, tactical detail, social exchanges, and omen interpretation. The most definite translation-level defect is the expansion of singular indefinite `τινα` at 15.31 into an explicitly plural prediction. The notes contain three firmer problems: an unsupported claim that Athena is strategically exaggerating Penelope's family pressure, a plainly incorrect line-distance claim about the later Sidonian episode, and a confused attribution of the formula at 15.181. Fixed-wording-only formula differences were excluded from the findings.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| R1-001 | 15.31–32 | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.99 | Greek `τινα ... ἀνδρῶν μνηστήρων` predicts that the earth will cover “some one of the suitor-men” before Telemachus reaches the city. “More than one of the suitor-men” turns the singular indefinite into an explicit plural and therefore enlarges the predicted body count. This is a semantic addition, not merely a fixed-formula wording difference. | Translate the phrase with a singular or number-neutral indefinite, such as “some one of the suitors” or “a suitor.” |
| R1-002 | Note to 15.16 (`[^L16]`) | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.95 | The note recasts Athena's direct report—Penelope's father and brothers urge her to marry Eurymachus—as “at best, a strategic exaggeration” and says Athena is manipulating Telemachus. Nothing in the Greek marks the report as deceptive, and Penelope's resistance does not negate pressure from her family. The note replaces the text's warning with an unsupported account of divine dishonesty and materially changes how the scene is read. | Present the family pressure and Eurymachus' exceptional gifts as Athena's stated reason for urgency. If proposing manipulation as an interpretation, label it explicitly as speculative and supply textual grounds. |
| R1-003 | Note to 15.113 (`[^L113]`) | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note says the Sidonian identity of Eumaeus' treacherous nurse returns “fifty lines later” at 15.425–429. From 15.113–119 to 15.425–429 is more than three hundred lines, not fifty. | Replace “fifty lines later” with “more than three hundred lines later,” or omit the interval. |
| R1-004 | Note to 15.180 (`[^L180]`) | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.94 | The note treats “then even there I would pray to you as to a god” as though it echoes a prior promise by Telemachus to the disguised Athena and calls Odyssey 8.467 merely its “counterpart.” In fact, 15.181 repeats the verse used at 8.467 by Odysseus to Nausicaa. The asserted Telemachus–Athena antecedent confuses the passage's speaker and intertextual relation. | State directly that Telemachus reuses Odysseus' line to Nausicaa at 8.467; do not attribute the formula to an earlier Telemachus–Athena promise unless a distinct passage is identified. |

## Concerns Considered

These points fall below the firm-finding threshold but deserve editorial attention.

| Location | Confidence | Concern |
|---|---:|---|
| Note to 15.7 (`[^L7]`) | 0.58 | Saying Odysseus “lay awake testing Eumaeus for a cloak” compresses the sequence: the cloak test occurs in the evening storytelling before the party sleeps. The wording is loose, though it does not seriously distort the scene. |
| 15.44 and note (`[^L44]`) | 0.64 | “A kick of his heel” is somewhat stronger than `λὰξ ποδὶ κινήσας`, and the note's description of the gesture as affectionate is interpretive rather than explicit. Familiarity is plausible, so this is not firm. |
| 15.78 | 0.68 | `κῦδός ... καὶ ἀγλαΐη καὶ ὄνειαρ` names glory, splendor/distinction, and benefit; “the glory and the profit” may collapse `ἀγλαΐη` into the general idea of honors. The preceding “honors” partly compensates. |
| 15.104–105 and note (`[^L104]`) | 0.70 | “Worked all over with figures” narrows `παμποίκιλοι` / `ποικίλμασιν`, which guarantees elaborate variegated ornament but not necessarily representational figures. The note then strengthens the possible connection to Helen's battle-weaving in Iliad 3.125–128. Figural embroidery is possible, so this remains a concern rather than a finding. |
| 15.171 | 0.68 | `ὑποφθαμένη` means that Helen forestalls Menelaus in speaking. “Was before him, and spoke out” can sound merely spatial, although the attached note restores the intended competition in interpretation. |
| 15.174 and note (`[^L174]`) | 0.67 | “Fattened in the house” is narrower than `ἀτιταλλομένην`, “reared/nurtured in the house.” The bird's great size makes the rendering conceivable, but fattening is not itself stated. |

## Notable Successes

1. Lines 27–42 clearly preserve Athena's ambush warning, the geography of the suitors' watch, and the tactical instruction to land away from the city.
2. Lines 67–85 effectively carry Menelaus' measured hospitality, his rejection of both detaining and driving off a guest, and the practical offer of a chariot tour.
3. Lines 160–181 preserve the omen's essential components—the eagle from the right, the domestic goose, Helen's interpretation, Odysseus' imminent vengeance, and the stronger possibility that he is already home.

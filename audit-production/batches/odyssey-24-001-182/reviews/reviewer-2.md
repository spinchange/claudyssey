# Reviewer 2

The translation is consistently strong in its handling of the underworld procession, Achilles’ funeral, and Amphimedon’s retelling of the web stratagem. Five material issues remain: a moderate comparative-attachment error concerning Antilochus and four minor explanatory-note distortions involving Hermes’ wand, chronology, returned kings, and Laertes’ shroud. Fixed-register wording differences have not been counted.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |

| R2-001 | 1–5 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.97 | The note says Hermes’ wand “put Calypso’s island to sleep.” Book 5 repeats the wand’s power to charm or awaken eyes, but Hermes does not use it to put Calypso, her household, or the island to sleep. The note converts a stated capacity into an unstaged action. | The recurrence at 5.47–48 merely describes the wand; here `τῇ ῥʼ ἄγε κινήσας` says Hermes moves and leads the shades with it. | Say that the wand carried on Calypso’s island now guides the suitors, without claiming it previously put the island to sleep. |
| R2-002 | 78–79 | `GRAMMAR` | `MODERATE` | 0.99 | “Whom you honored beyond all your other companions after Patroclus died” turns a ranking into a temporal sequence. ## Especially Successful Renderings

- **24.1–14:** Hermes’ procession and the bat simile retain the shades’ sound, collective motion, underworld itinerary, and exhausted spectral condition.
- **24.36–76:** Agamemnon’s account of Achilles’ death and funeral preserves the sustained ritual sequence, its divine participants, and its emotional scale.
- **24.125–146:** Amphimedon’s web narrative successfully preserves its formulaic relation to the earlier tellings while maintaining the suitor’s accusatory perspective.

## Concerns Considered

| 69–70 | 0.62 | “Moved in armor” may weaken the vigorous force of `τεύχεσιν ἐρρώσαντο` during the heroes’ movement around the pyre. | The armored ritual motion, surrounding pyre, mixed foot and mounted participants, and great clamor remain explicit. |
| 80–84 note | 0.61 | Saying sailors “will steer by” Achilles’ tomb adds a navigational use where the Greek says only that the monument will be visible from far out at sea. | A conspicuous coastal tomb can naturally function as a landmark, and the note presents the claim as literary interpretation rather than technical navigation data. |
| 109 note | 0.59 | “A hundred picked men” adds a number where Agamemnon says only that the shades are selected men of one age. | The traditional suitor total is close to one hundred, so the note can be read as conventional round-number shorthand rather than an exact source claim. |
| 149–150 | 0.64 | “The border of the land” may suggest an island or coastal boundary, while `ἀγροῦ ἐπʼ ἐσχατιήν` means the remote edge of the countryside or cultivated territory. | The swineherd’s house and the surrounding farm context prevent a consequential geographic relocation. |
| 173–174 | 0.70 | “Shouted out threats” and “however much he pleaded” strengthen neutral or directive `ὁμοκλέομεν ἐπέεσσι` and `ἀγορεύοι` into threatening and supplicatory speech acts. | The suitors’ hostile attempt to prevent delivery of the bow makes both intensifications contextually plausible, and the operative demand remains accurate. |

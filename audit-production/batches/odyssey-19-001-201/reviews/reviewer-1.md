# Reviewer 1 Audit — *Odyssey* 19.1–201

The translation is consistently lucid and attentive to Homeric structure. The firm problems are localized: three verse-level semantic shifts and four explanatory-note claims contradicted by the poem or register. No fixed-wording-only formula deviations are counted.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 36 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The claim that Telemachus is “finally allowed to sense a theophany” overlooks his recognition of Athena in Book 1. | At 1.322–323 Telemachus `ὀίσατο γὰρ θεὸν εἶναι`, guessed she was a god. | Distinguish this indirect radiance from his earlier recognition. |
| R1-002 | 56 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.98 | The note invents Odysseus’ memory of the chair being delivered. The poem names its maker but gives no delivery scene or evidence that he witnessed one. | `ἥν ποτε τέκτων / ποίησ’ Ἰκμάλιος` says only that Icmalius made the chair. | Retain the family-object interpretation without a remembered delivery. |
| R1-003 | 71 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.99 | “Woman possessed” gives emphatic `δαιμονίη` an unsupported supernatural diagnosis. | It is a forceful address expressing outrage or astonishment. | Use a forceful non-supernatural address. |
| R1-004 | 104 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | Calling this identity formula its “last” asking is false; Laertes asks it again in Book 24. | The same formula recurs at 24.298. | Describe the wife’s climactic asking without claiming finality. |
| R1-005 | 113 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.88 | “The flocks throw firm young” transfers `ἔμπεδα` from constancy of reproduction to physical quality of offspring. | In `τίκτῃ δ’ ἔμπεδα μῆλα`, `ἔμπεδα` is adverbial. | Render the flocks bearing young unfailingly or steadily. |
| R1-006 | 141 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | “The poem’s only twice-told tale” is contradicted when Amphimedon tells the web story a third time in Book 24. | The story recurs at 24.129–146 after Books 2 and 19. | Call this the second telling and note the third to come. |
| R1-007 | 160–161 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.87 | “A house that Zeus grants glory to” redirects `κῦδος` from Telemachus to the house. | `τῷ τε Ζεὺς κῦδος ὀπάζει` makes the man the recipient. | Make Telemachus the recipient. |

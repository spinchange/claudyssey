# Reviewer 2 Fidelity Audit — *Odyssey* 19.1–201

The verse is largely strong. The firm issues are localized and minor: unsupported note additions, weakened lexical choices, a transferred modifier, and a grammatical shift in Zeus’s favor.

## Findings

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 19.55 and note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.99 | “Where she always sat” invents a habitual seating place. | The chair is set by the fire and `ἔνθʼ ἄρʼ ἐφῖζε`, there she sat. | Remove “always.” |
| R2-002 | 19.56 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.98 | The note says Odysseus remembers the chair being delivered; only its maker and construction are stated. | Lines 56–58 name Icmalius and the footrest. | Omit the delivery memory. |
| R2-003 | 19.71 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.99 | “Woman possessed” makes `δαιμονίη` a supernatural diagnosis. | It is a forceful vocative followed by a question about anger. | Use a nonsupernatural emphatic address. |
| R2-004 | 19.87–88 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.91 | “Mischief” weakens culpable `ἀτασθάλλουσα`, reckless or outrageous misconduct. | No woman acting recklessly escapes Telemachus’ notice. | Use “recklessness” or “outrage.” |
| R2-005 | 19.104 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The formula history reverses speaker/addressee at 1.170 and falsely calls this the last asking before its recurrence at 24.298. | Telemachus asks disguised Athena at 1.170; Laertes asks at 24.298. | Correct both claims. |
| R2-006 | 19.109–114 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.96 | The note turns a simile into a factual twenty-year reign by Penelope. | `κλέος ... ὥς ... βασιλῆος` compares her fame to a just king’s. | Preserve analogy without asserting kingship. |
| R2-007 | 19.113 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.92 | “Flocks throw firm young” transfers `ἔμπεδα` from unfailing reproduction to firm offspring. | `τίκτῃ δʼ ἔμπεδα μῆλα`. | Use “bear young without fail.” |
| R2-008 | 19.141 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | Calling the web story the poem’s only twice-told tale ignores its third telling in Book 24 and other repetitions. | It appears in Books 2, 19, and 24. | Call this the second telling. |
| R2-009 | 19.160–161 | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.96 | “A house that Zeus grants glory to” transfers favor from Telemachus to the house. | `τῷ` refers to the grown man able to care for the house. | Make Telemachus the recipient. |

# Reviewer 1

## Overall assessment

The translation is consistently accurate and often resourceful across the
opening of Odysseus' narrative. It preserves the argument and social force of
the proem, the hard causal sequence of the Ciconian defeat, the temptation and
coercion of the Lotus-eater episode, and the unusually technical survey of the
Cyclopes' offshore island. I found one localized unsupported image in the
verse and one explanatory-note claim that contradicts the passage's expressly
mixed visual and auditory evidence.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R1-001 | 9.51-52 | `ADDITION` | `MINOR` | 0.90 | “In the morning mist” adds atmospheric mist. The Greek marks when the Cicones arrived—at dawn or early in the morning—but supplies no mist here. “Morning” preserves the time, so the unsupported scenic addition is limited. | `ἦλθον ... ἠέριοι` means that they came early/in the morning; no noun or modifier denotes mist. | Use “at dawn,” “early in the morning,” or another temporal expression without mist. |
| R1-002 | 9.166-167 note | `MISTRANSLATION` | `MINOR` | 0.91 | The note calls the reconnaissance “aural,” although the passage explicitly combines sight and hearing: Odysseus and his men look toward the land and perceive smoke before the text mentions human and animal voices. The note's own later discussion of smoke as a visible sign makes the categorical label especially misleading. | `ἐλεύσσομεν ... καπνόν` (“we looked/saw ... smoke”) is visual; `φθογγήν` then supplies the voices of the Cyclopes, sheep, and goats. | Describe the reconnaissance as mixed visual and auditory evidence: visible smoke together with human and animal voices. |

## Especially successful renderings

1. Lines 39-44 keep the brutal economy of the Ciconian raid and, crucially,
   preserve `ἠνώγεα` as the operative “I ordered,” followed by the crew's
   refusal to obey.
2. Lines 94-104 render the Lotus-eater sequence with excellent causal clarity,
   from the fruit's effect on desire and memory through Odysseus' forced,
   tearful removal of the men and his preventive command to the rest.
3. Lines 116-141 sustain the extended practical survey of the offshore island,
   including its unused fertility, shipbuilding counterfactual, anchorage,
   mooring equipment, spring, and poplars, without flattening the passage into
   generic landscape.

# Reviewer 2

## Overall assessment

The translation is consistently attentive to syntax, narrative sequence, and the
technical detail of travel, fighting, and landfall. I found one localized firm
issue: the rendering of the Cicones' dawn arrival adds mist that is not in the
Greek. Two smaller possibilities were considered but remain below threshold.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 9.51-52 | `ADDITION` | `MINOR` | 0.89 | “In the morning mist” adds a misty atmospheric setting. The Greek says that the Cicones came early in the morning; the simile supplies their multitude, not mist. The translation retains morning timing, so the unsupported added detail is limited. | `ἦλθον ... ὅσα φύλλα καὶ ἄνθεα ... ἠέριοι` (“they came ... as many as leaves and flowers ... early in the morning”) | Render the adverb temporally, e.g. “They came at dawn, thick as leaves and flowers in season.” |

Counts by severity: **1 MINOR**; **0 MODERATE**; **0 MAJOR**; **0 CRITICAL**.

Especially successful renderings:

1. **9.39-42** preserves the blunt sequence of sacking, killing, seizure, and
   equal division without sanitizing Odysseus' raid.
2. **9.98-104** keeps the coercive rescue from the lotus, the physical binding,
   and the rapid return to ordered rowing lucid across the sentence.
3. **9.116-141** handles the island survey's agricultural and nautical detail
   with unusual precision, including the unoccupied land, anchor-stones,
   stern-cables, and harbor spring.

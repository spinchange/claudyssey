# Reviewer 2 Fidelity Review

The verse translation is consistently strong across the Proteus encounter, the reports of the Achaean homecomings, and Telemachus' exchange with Menelaus. Agency, sequence, syntax, and rhetorical force are generally preserved. I found one firm issue: an explanatory note makes a demonstrably false uniqueness claim about ambrosia's practical use in Homer. Formula-register-only differences are excluded.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 441 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.99 | The note calls the seal-stench episode “the only use of ambrosia as a practical remedy in Homer.” The *Iliad* uses ambrosia practically more than once, including an especially close parallel in which Thetis drips ambrosia and nectar into Patroclus' nostrils to preserve his corpse. Athena later gives Achilles ambrosia to prevent hunger. The note's account of the present deodorizing action is correct, but its Homer-wide uniqueness claim is false. | At *Odyssey* 4.445-446, Eidothea puts `ἀμβροσίην ὑπὸ ῥῖνα` under each man's nose and destroys the seals' smell. At [*Iliad* 19.38-39](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0012.tlg001.perseus-grc2%3A19.1-19.72?right=perseus-eng4), `Πατρόκλῳ δʼ αὖτʼ ἀμβροσίην καὶ νέκταρ ἐρυθρὸν / στάξε κατὰ ῥινῶν, ἵνα οἱ χρὼς ἔμπεδος εἴη`: ambrosia and nectar are dripped into Patroclus' nostrils so his body remains intact. *Iliad* 19.347-353 also gives ambrosia to Achilles so hunger will not reach him. | Remove the uniqueness claim. The note can accurately call this an unusually comic or deodorizing use of ambrosia without claiming it is Homer's only practical application. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

- **Lines 454-470:** Proteus' capture, successive transformations, exhaustion, questioning, and Menelaus' reply preserve the action, agency, and repeated homecoming language with close fidelity.
- **Lines 499-537:** The contrasting fates of Ajax and Agamemnon remain clear, including Poseidon's initial rescue of Ajax, the fatal boast, Agamemnon's joyful landing, Aegisthus' watchman, and the banquet ambush.

# Reviewer 1 Fidelity Review

This is a strong and generally careful rendering of *Odyssey* 15.186–371. It handles the rapid genealogy, seafaring sequence, and shifts between Telemachus and the hut with particular clarity. I find two firm material issues: one localized displacement of an epithet and one consequential reversal of who performs the act of supplication. No fixed-wording-only formula deviations are counted.

| R1-001 | 15.233–234 | LEXICAL | MINOR | 0.84 | “The Fury who strikes the house” replaces the Greek epithet’s explicit frightfulness with a specific house-directed action that the adjective does not denote. “Fury” itself preserves some of the dread, so the net loss is limited, but the added domestic target materially changes the characterization in this compressed genealogy. | `θεὰ δασπλῆτις Ἐρινύς`: `δασπλῆτις` means horrid or frightful, not “house-striking.” | Render the phrase as “the dreadful/frightful Fury,” without assigning her a house as the object of a blow. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. Lines 195–201 preserve the guest-friendship argument, the travelers’ growing concord, Nestor’s comic hospitality, and Telemachus’ urgency in fluent English; “aching to host me” is especially effective for `ἱέμενος φιλέειν`.
2. Lines 245–253 navigate the dense Amphiaraus–Cleitus–Polypheides genealogy cleanly while retaining the gods’ love, the fatal bribe, Dawn’s abduction, and Polypheides’ supremacy as a seer.
3. Lines 319–324 give the servant-skills catalogue a crisp rhetorical build and preserve its social conclusion in “all that lesser men do, waiting on the great.”

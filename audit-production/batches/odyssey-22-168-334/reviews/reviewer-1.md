# Reviewer 1

The verse translation is consistently strong, particularly in the rapid combat sequences and the handling of embedded speeches. The firm issues are confined to explanatory notes that overstate certainty, miscount or misdescribe verse structure, or claim more narrative corroboration than the poem supplies.

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. Lines 173–200 preserve the grim mechanics of Melanthius’ capture and suspension while carrying Eumaeus’ dawn-formula taunt with clarity and tonal force.
2. Lines 226–240 render Athena’s rebuke, continued testing, and swallow-like ascent with excellent control of both martial rhetoric and movement.
3. Lines 265–309 keep the volleys, named kills, two grazing wounds, aegis-induced rout, and paired animal similes unusually lucid without sacrificing momentum.

## Concerns Considered

| 184–186 and note | 0.64 | “The stitches of its straps rotted out” supplies decay as the cause of `ῥαφαὶ ... λέλυντο`, which directly says that the seams or stitching had come loose. | The shield is explicitly old and marked by `ἄζη`, so deterioration is strongly supported even if “rotted” is more specific than the verb. |
| 219–223 | 0.58 | “Will not walk free” may suggest imprisonment, whereas `πολεύειν` more neutrally denies the women continued movement or life in Ithaca’s town. | Within a threat encompassing killing, confiscation, and expulsion, the English still conveys exclusion from ordinary civic life without committing clearly to incarceration. |
| 260–264 | 0.63 | “Now it is mine to say it: throw!” turns the deliberative `εἴποιμι ... ἀκοντίσαι`—“I would say that we too should throw”—into a sharper imperative and leaves “we too” implicit. | The battlefield context makes the utterance an operative counter-volley order, and the following action clearly includes Odysseus with the other defenders. |
| 302–306 and note | 0.70 | “Men delight to watch the hunting” adds explicit spectatorship where `χαίρουσι ... ἀνέρες ἄγρῃ` says that men rejoice in the hunt or quarry; the note builds an audience-complicity reading on “watch.” | Since the vultures perform the killing while men are introduced separately, observation is a plausible contextual relation, though not lexically explicit. |
| 325 | 0.60 | “A death that lays men low” replaces the harsh or pitiless quality of `θάνατον ... δυσηλεγέα` with a generic result of death. | The menacing force and certainty of Odysseus’ death sentence remain intact, leaving the loss of adjectival nuance of uncertain materiality. |

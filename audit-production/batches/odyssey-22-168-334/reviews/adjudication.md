# Adjudication — *Odyssey* 22.168–334

## Overall Assessment

The translation is consistently strong through Melanthius’ capture, Athena’s appearance, the exchange of volleys, and the rout. The five sustained findings are confined to explanatory notes except for one consequential scope error in Leodes’ plea.

Nine firm reviewer IDs resolve to six distinct proposal keys. Three duplicate pairs were merged. Five proposals are sustained and one is rejected. The epistemic-certainty and Leodes-scope findings are normalized to GRAMMAR; the invented casualty count is normalized to ADDITION. The claim that Book 21 “certified every word” of Leodes’ plea is raised to MODERATE because it prejudges the scene’s central question of culpability.

R2-003 is rejected. Each of the two suitor volleys does strike the doorpost, door, and wall. The note says every “volley,” not every individual cast, and note L277 explicitly acknowledges the two grazing wounds in the second volley.

Adjudication status: COMPLETE. No proposal remains unresolved. No fixed-wording-only FORMULA deviation was counted.

## Rejected and Unresolved Proposals

R2-003 is rejected. At 22.256–259 the first volley is wholly nullified and hits the doorpost, door, and wall. At 22.273–280 many casts in the second volley are nullified, the same three architectural impacts recur, and two additional casts graze defenders. The note’s sentence can accurately describe the repeated architectural pattern without claiming that no spear touches a person; its separate note at L277 explicitly describes both grazes.

No proposal remains unresolved.

The following concerns remain below threshold:

| Lines | Concern | Reason not counted |
|---|---|---|
| 22.184–186 | “The stitches of its straps rotted out” specifies decay as the cause of the loosened seams. | The shield is explicitly old and deteriorated, making decay a plausible contextual explanation. |
| 22.219–223 | “Will not walk free” may suggest imprisonment rather than a broader denial of civic movement or continued life. | Within a threat encompassing killing, confiscation, and exclusion, the wording conveys coercive loss of ordinary life without decisively selecting incarceration. |

| 22.260–264 | “Now it is mine to say it: throw!” sharpens the deliberative `εἴποιμι ... ἀκοντίσαι` and leaves “we too” implicit. | The battle context and immediately following collective action preserve the operative proposal and Odysseus’ inclusion. |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **22.173–200:** Melanthius’ capture, backward binding, suspension near the roof-beams, and Eumaeus’ dawn-formula taunt retain precise physical sequencing and grim tonal control.
2. **22.224–240:** Athena’s rebuke, withheld victory, continued testing, and swallow-like ascent preserve both her martial aggression and deliberately limited intervention.

## Calibration Implications

- Epistemic verbs such as `ὀΐομαι` must not become categorical knowledge in explanatory notes.
- Exact casualty counts require explicit textual support; plural narration cannot be converted into a guessed number.
- Claims about line-by-line poetic structure should be checked against the Greek lineation.
- A note elsewhere in the same apparatus may clarify and limit an apparently broad statement; the notes should be read as a coherent whole.
- Narrator corroboration must be limited to what the narrator actually verifies, especially when a character’s culpability is disputed.
- Adjectival scope over coordinated infinitives must be preserved when narrowing the adjective to one verb materially changes a speaker’s claim.
- No fixed-register-only formula variation should enter semantic adjudication.

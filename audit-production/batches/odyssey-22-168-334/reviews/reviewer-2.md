# Reviewer 2

The verse translation is consistently strong through the capture of Melanthius, Athena’s appearance, and the rout. Firm issues are limited to one consequential scope error in Leodes’ plea and several notes that overstate certainty, casualty counts, volley outcomes, or the narrator’s corroboration. No fixed-wording-only formula deviations are counted.

| ID | Lines | Category | Severity | Confidence | Finding | Greek evidence | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| R2-001 | 22.205–210 note | GRAMMAR | MINOR | 0.88 | The note first accurately says Odysseus is “guessing” that Mentor is Athena, but then says he acts “while knowing” who his interlocutor is. This turns an explicitly marked supposition into certainty. | `ὀϊόμενος λαοσσόον ἔμμεν Ἀθήνην` means “thinking/supposing that it was Athena, rouser of armies.” | Retain “believing” or “supposing,” or present certain recognition as interpretation rather than grammatical fact. |
| R2-002 | 22.211–223 note | ADDITION | MINOR | 0.98 | “Even now — four dead” invents a casualty count. Before Agelaus’ threat, three deaths have been individually named, while 22.116–118 additionally describes Odysseus dropping suitors one after another without giving a number. | `μνηστήρων ἕνα γʼ αἰεὶ ... βάλλε ... τοὶ δʼ ἀγχιστῖνοι ἔπιπτον` describes plural casualties but supplies no exact total. | Say “with suitors already dead” or “after the first deaths,” without a numerical count. |
| R2-003 | 22.247–280 note | MISTRANSLATION | MINOR | 0.94 | “Every volley lands in doorpost, door, and wall” overstates the battle pattern. Athena nullifies every cast in the first volley, but only many in the second; Amphimedon and Ctesippus wound Telemachus and Eumaeus. The house does not absorb every volley without human contact. | The first volley has `τὰ ... πάντα ἐτώσια`; the second has only `τὰ ... πολλὰ ἐτώσια`, followed by the two hits at 277–280. | Restrict the architectural image to the first volley, or say that most subsequent casts strike the house while two graze the defenders. |
| R2-004 | 22.310–319 note; cf. 21.144–147 | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.99 | The note says the poem “certified every word” of Leodes’ plea, including that he tried to stop the suitors. Book 21 certifies only that he alone hated their outrages and was indignant with them. His claim of active intervention is made here by Leodes himself and is not narrator-verified. This materially prejudges the scene’s central question of his culpability. | At 21.146–147 the narrator gives `ἀτασθαλίαι ... ἐχθραὶ ἔσαν` and `πᾶσιν ... νεμέσσα`; only Leodes’ present plea supplies `ἄλλους / παύεσκον μνηστῆρας`. | Distinguish the narrator-certified dislike of the outrages from Leodes’ uncorroborated claim that he repeatedly intervened. |
| R2-005 | 22.313–315 and note | MISTRANSLATION | MODERATE | 0.97 | “I never said a word to any woman” makes Leodes claim total noncommunication. His denial concerns reckless speech or conduct toward the women. The translation preserves the qualification only for the action, not for the speech, and the note treats the stronger English claim as true. | `εἰπεῖν οὐδέ τι ῥέξαι ἀτάσθαλον` coordinates saying and doing under `ἀτάσθαλον`: he denies saying or doing anything reckless. | Use “I never said or did anything reckless to any woman in your halls.” |

## Especially Successful Renderings

1. **22.168–200:** Melanthius’ capture, backward binding, suspension near the roof-beams, and Eumaeus’ second-person torture-taunt are rendered with precise physical sequencing.
2. **22.224–240:** Athena’s rebuke, withheld victory, continued testing, and swallow-like perch preserve both her aggression and her deliberately limited intervention.
3. **22.265–309:** The defenders’ counter-volley, Philoetius’ retributive “guest-gift,” Athena’s aegis, and the cattle-and-vulture similes form a clear, forceful account of the rout.

## Concerns Considered

| Lines | Confidence | Concern | Why not counted |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 22.173–177 note | 0.59 | “Not death — storage” could imply that Odysseus spares Melanthius, although the suspension is prolonged torture before his later killing. | The note immediately stresses that he remains alive only “to wait,” so it can be read as contrasting immediate death with temporary confinement. |
| 22.184–186 | 0.64 | “The stitches of its straps rotted out” assigns decay as the cause of `ῥαφαὶ ... λέλυντο`, which literally says the seams or stitching had come loose. | The shield’s age and mold-like deterioration make rotting a plausible contextual explanation. |
| 22.219–223 | 0.69 | “Will not walk free” adds an explicit loss-of-liberty interpretation to the threat that Mentor’s wife and daughters will not be allowed to go about Ithaca. | Killing, expulsion, confinement, and enslavement all remain possible mechanisms; “walk free” broadly conveys coercive exclusion without decisively selecting one. |
| 22.226 note | 0.63 | Calling Athena’s rebuke “the poem’s only direct set-piece linking this hall to Troy” is an expansive structural claim, especially since Odysseus mentions Troy at 22.35. | “Set-piece” can reasonably distinguish Athena’s extended Trojan résumé from a brief geographical reference. |
| 22.299–306 | 0.65 | “Men delight to watch the hunting” makes the men explicit spectators, while `χαίρουσι ... ἄγρῃ` says that men delight in the hunt or prey without specifying their precise role. | Since vultures perform the attack in the simile, observation is a plausible way for human beings to participate in the scene. |
